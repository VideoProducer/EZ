Radon Testing

Radon testing measures indoor concentrations of naturally occurring radon gas, a radioactive soil gas that can accumulate in buildings. Testing typically involves placing a passive long-term test device in the lowest occupied area of a home for several months. Health Canada publishes a national radon guideline concentration level; verify the current threshold with Health Canada or a licensed radon measurement professional, as specific numerical values are not reproduced here. CMHC also provides guidance on radon awareness for Canadian homeowners. Where concentrations exceed the guideline, mitigation measures such as sub-slab depressurization may be recommended. Buyers, sellers, and strata owners should confirm testing and mitigation obligations with a qualified professional and verify any current numeric thresholds directly with Health Canada (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is radon testing legally required before selling a home in British Columbia?

No. Neither the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, nor the RESA Rules impose a statutory duty on sellers or licensees to conduct radon testing prior to listing or selling residential property in BC. However, under RESA a licensee must disclose material latent defects known to them, and if a seller is aware of elevated radon levels that information may be material and must be disclosed. Verify disclosure obligations for your situation with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

What radon concentration level requires mitigation in Canada?

Health Canada has established a guideline of 200 becquerels per cubic metre (Bq/m³) (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) as the radon reference level above which remedial action is recommended. This is a federal health guideline, not a BC statute, but it is widely referenced in BC real estate practice. Verify current Health Canada guidelines at canada.ca before acting.

Verify with: Radon
Does the BC Property Transfer Tax Act require radon testing or disclosure?

No. The Property Transfer Tax Act (PTTA), RSBC 1996, c. 378, governs the tax payable on title registration and does not impose radon testing or disclosure requirements. Radon testing and disclosure obligations, if any, arise under common law, the Real Estate Services Act, or contractual terms in the purchase contract. Verify with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Radon
Can a buyer rescind a contract if radon testing reveals high levels after subject removal?

This depends on the contract terms and whether the seller or licensee knew of elevated radon and failed to disclose it. Under BC common law, fraudulent or negligent misrepresentation may give rise to rescission, but absent a contractual condition or statutory breach there is generally no automatic right to rescind after all subjects have been removed. Verify your contractual rights and remedies with a BC lawyer before acting.

Verify with: Radon
Are strata corporations in BC required to conduct radon testing in common property?

No. The Strata Property Act (SPA), SBC 1998, c. 43, and the Strata Property Regulation do not mandate radon testing of common property or strata units. A strata council has a statutory duty to manage and maintain common property and common assets (s. 3 SPA), and prudent risk management may include testing, but there is no explicit radon testing requirement. Verify the strata's duties and your rights with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Radon
Does a BC real estate licensee have to recommend radon testing to a buyer client?

RESA and the RESA Rules require licensees to act in the best interests of their clients and to provide competent service, but do not explicitly mandate recommending radon testing. Best practice under BCFSA guidance includes informing buyers of the availability of home inspections (which may include radon testing) and material health or safety risks. Verify current BCFSA practice standards at bcfsa.ca or consult a BC lawyer.

Verify with: Radon
If I am a landlord in BC, must I test for radon under the Residential Tenancy Act?

No. The Residential Tenancy Act (RTA), SBC 2002, c. 78, requires landlords to provide and maintain residential premises in a reasonable state of repair and comply with health and safety standards, but does not explicitly require radon testing. WorkSafeBC and local health authorities may have guidelines for certain occupancies. Verify your obligations under the RTA and applicable health standards with a BC lawyer or licensed property manager.

Verify with: Radon
Are home inspectors in BC required to test for radon as part of a standard inspection?

No statutory requirement exists in BC mandating home inspectors to include radon testing in a standard pre-purchase inspection. The scope of a home inspection is typically defined by contract and professional standards (e.g., the standards of practice of the inspector's professional body). Radon testing is usually an optional add-on service. Verify the scope of your inspection agreement and current professional standards with your inspector and a BC lawyer.

Verify with: Radon
Can elevated radon levels affect property value or marketability in BC?

This depends on buyer perception, disclosure practices, and local market conditions; there is no BC statute that prescribes a radon-based devaluation formula. Under the Real Estate Services Act a licensee must disclose known material latent defects, and if elevated radon is known and material it must be disclosed, which may affect marketability. Verify disclosure and valuation questions with a BC lawyer, notary, or accredited appraiser.

Verify with: Radon
Where can I find official guidance on radon testing and mitigation in British Columbia?

Health Canada provides the federal radon guideline (200 Bq/m³, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) and testing/mitigation resources at canada.ca/radon. The BC Centre for Disease Control and regional health authorities offer BC-specific information and resources. Neither the Real Estate Services Act nor BC Ministry of Finance publications prescribe radon testing protocols. Verify current BC and federal guidance with those agencies or a BC environmental health professional.
