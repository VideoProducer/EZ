Strata

Annual General Meeting (AGM)

The mandatory yearly meeting of strata lot owners at which the budget is approved, council is elected, and key resolutions are voted on. Notice and procedural requirements are set out in the Strata Property Act.

Frequently Asked Questions

What legislation governs Annual General Meetings (AGMs) for strata corporations in British Columbia?

AGMs for strata corporations in BC are governed by the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and its Regulation. The Act sets out the mandatory requirements for notice, quorum, voting, and the business that must be conducted at each AGM. Individual strata corporations may also have bylaws that supplement, but cannot contradict, the Act's provisions.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
How much notice must a strata corporation give owners before holding an AGM in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act, a strata corporation must give owners at least 14 days' written notice before an AGM, unless the strata's bylaws specify a longer period. The notice must include the AGM agenda, proposed budget, and any resolutions to be voted on. Providing less notice than required can invalidate the meeting and its resolutions.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
What mandatory business must be conducted at a BC strata corporation's AGM?

The Strata Property Act requires that each AGM address, at minimum, the election of a new strata council, approval of the annual budget (including contributions to the contingency reserve fund), and any other business set out in the Act or the corporation's bylaws. Owners must also receive the financial statements and any depreciation report updates as part of the AGM package. Additional resolutions, such as bylaw amendments, may be added to the agenda.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Can a BC strata corporation hold its AGM electronically or by a combination of in-person and remote participation?

Yes. Amendments to the Strata Property Act permit strata corporations to hold AGMs electronically, or using a hybrid format combining in-person and electronic participation, provided the strata's bylaws allow for it or owners otherwise consent. Owners participating electronically must be able to hear and be heard by other participants, satisfying the meeting's procedural requirements. Strata corporations should review their bylaws and the current Regulation to confirm the precise rules that apply.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
What quorum is required for a valid AGM under the Strata Property Act?

Under the Strata Property Act, quorum for an AGM is reached when owners of at least one-third of the strata lots are present in person or by proxy. If quorum is not achieved within a reasonable time after the scheduled start, the meeting may be adjourned in accordance with the Act's provisions. A reconvened meeting following an adjournment for lack of quorum may proceed with those present, subject to the Act's rules.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
How are votes counted at a BC strata AGM, and can owners vote by proxy?

At a BC strata AGM, each strata lot generally carries one vote, regardless of its size or assessed value, unless the strata's Schedule of Voting Rights provides otherwise. The Strata Property Act expressly permits owners to appoint another person as a proxy to vote on their behalf at the AGM. Different types of resolutions — ordinary, three-quarters, or unanimous — require different levels of approval, as specified in the Act.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Must a BC strata corporation include a proposed budget in the AGM notice package?

Yes. The Strata Property Act requires that the proposed budget for the upcoming fiscal year be included with the AGM notice sent to owners. The budget must show the strata's estimated operating expenses and the proposed contributions to the contingency reserve fund. Owners vote to approve the budget at the AGM, and the approved budget determines the strata fees payable by each owner.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
What happens if a BC strata corporation fails to hold an AGM within the required timeframe?

The Strata Property Act requires a strata corporation to hold an AGM not later than two months after the end of each fiscal year. Failure to hold an AGM within this period is a contravention of the Act, and owners may apply to the BC Supreme Court or the Civil Resolution Tribunal for an order compelling the strata corporation to hold the meeting. Continued non-compliance may expose the strata corporation and its council members to further legal consequences.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Can a real estate licensee in BC attend or participate in a strata AGM on behalf of an owner client?

A licensed real estate professional regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) under the Real Estate Services Act may attend an AGM as a proxy for an owner client, provided the owner validly appoints them as proxy in accordance with the Strata Property Act. However, a licensee acting in this capacity is representing the owner's interests, not providing strata management services, and must comply with all applicable duties under RESA and the BCFSA's rules. Licensees should confirm that acting as proxy does not create any conflict with their agency obligations.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Are minutes from a BC strata AGM available to prospective purchasers, and how are they obtained?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, a strata corporation must make its minutes available for inspection, and a prospective purchaser or their agent can request a Form B Information Certificate, which must be accompanied by specified documents including the most recent AGM minutes and current budget. The Form B is typically obtained through the strata manager or strata council, and the strata corporation is required to provide it within a set number of days of receiving the request and the applicable fee. Reviewing AGM minutes helps purchasers understand strata governance, financial health, and any pending issues or resolutions.
