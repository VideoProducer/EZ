BC Land Title and Survey Authority (LTSA)

The BC Land Title and Survey Authority (LTSA) is a statutory corporation established under BC legislation to operate the province's land title and survey systems. Under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, it administers the registration of titles, charges, and survey plans across British Columbia. The LTSA oversees the Land Title Office, through which ownership interests and encumbrances are recorded, and the Surveyor General function, which governs cadastral surveying. Registration through the LTSA provides indefeasibility of title, a foundational protection for property owners in BC. Verify current operational details, fees, and any legislative amendments with a BC lawyer, notary, or the LTSA directly at ltsa.ca.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the BC Land Title and Survey Authority (LTSA)?

The LTSA is the statutory authority responsible for operating British Columbia's land title and survey systems under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. It administers the registration of land titles, charges (such as mortgages and liens), and survey plans across the province through the Land Title Office and the Surveyor General's Office. The LTSA maintains the legal record of land ownership and interests in BC. Verify operational details and current services at ltsa.ca or with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: LTSA
Is the LTSA a government ministry or a private corporation?

The LTSA operates as a statutory corporation under provincial legislation, separate from direct government ministries, though it performs a public function under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. As of 2026-07-27 (verify current), it is responsible for administering BC's land title and survey systems on behalf of the Province. For its precise legal status and governance structure, verify with a BC lawyer, notary, or consult current provincial publications at gov.bc.ca.

Verify with: LTSA
What is the role of the LTSA in a real estate transaction?

The LTSA registers transfers of title, mortgages, liens, easements, and other charges on real property in BC under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. When a property is sold or refinanced, the buyer's lawyer or notary submits documents to the LTSA for registration to legally transfer or encumber title. The LTSA maintains the official, publicly searchable record of ownership and interests. Verify specific registration requirements and timelines with your BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: LTSA
Can I search who owns a property in BC through the LTSA?

Yes. The LTSA maintains publicly accessible land title records under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, which include registered owner information, legal descriptions, and registered charges. As of 2026-07-27 (verify current), title searches can be ordered online through ltsa.ca or through a BC lawyer or notary. Personal information is subject to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), SBC 2003, c. 63, and collection and use must comply with applicable privacy laws.

Verify with: LTSA
What is a title search, and why is it important?

A title search is a review of the LTSA's official land title register for a specific property, showing the registered owner, legal description, charges (mortgages, liens, easements), and any statutory restrictions or encumbrances under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Buyers and lenders rely on title searches to confirm ownership, identify debts or encumbrances, and ensure clear title before completing a transaction. Your BC lawyer or notary will typically order and review a title search as part of a real estate purchase or refinance.

Verify with: LTSA
Does the LTSA guarantee the accuracy of land title information?

British Columbia operates an indefeasible title system (often called Torrens title) under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, which means the LTSA's register is the conclusive record of title and registered interests, subject to limited statutory exceptions. Once registered, title is generally guaranteed by the Province, and the Land Title and Survey Authority Assurance Fund may compensate persons who suffer loss due to registration errors (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current eligibility and limits). Verify the scope of the assurance and any exclusions with a BC lawyer or notary before relying on this protection.

Verify with: LTSA
What is a parcel identifier (PID) and how is it used?

A parcel identifier (PID) is a unique nine-digit number assigned by the LTSA to each distinct parcel of land in BC under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. The PID is used to identify and search properties in the LTSA's land title register and is required for title searches, property transfers, and registration of charges. You can find a property's PID on the title certificate, property tax assessment notice, or by searching the LTSA database at ltsa.ca. Verify the correct PID with your BC lawyer or notary before any registration.

Verify with: LTSA
How do I register a mortgage or charge against a property in BC?

Mortgages, liens, easements, and other charges must be registered with the LTSA under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, to be legally enforceable against the land and subsequent purchasers. Registration is typically completed electronically by a BC lawyer or notary who prepares and submits the required forms and documents to the LTSA. As of 2026-07-27 (verify current), registration fees apply and are set by regulation. Consult your BC lawyer or notary for the correct procedure, forms, and current fees.

Verify with: LTSA
What is the Surveyor General's Office, and how does it relate to the LTSA?

The Surveyor General's Office is part of the LTSA and is responsible for administering BC's cadastral survey system, including the review and approval of survey plans filed with the Land Title Office under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, and the Land Surveyors Act, RSBC 1996, c. 249 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current statute citation). It ensures that legal survey plans meet statutory standards and accurately define property boundaries. For questions about survey requirements, subdivision, or boundary disputes, verify with a BC land surveyor, lawyer, or notary.

Verify with: LTSA
Are LTSA records and fees the same across all of British Columbia?

Yes. The LTSA operates a centralized land title system for the entire province under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, so registration processes, title records, and LTSA fees (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current fee schedule at ltsa.ca) are consistent province-wide. Note that property transfer tax (PTT) under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, and municipal property taxes are separate charges administered by the BC Ministry of Finance and local governments, respectively. Verify current LTSA fees and any applicable taxes or levies with your BC lawyer, notary, or at gov.bc.ca.

Verify with: LTSA
