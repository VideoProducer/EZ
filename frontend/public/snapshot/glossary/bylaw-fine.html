Strata

Bylaw Fine

A monetary penalty a strata corporation may impose on an owner or tenant who contravenes its bylaws or rules, as authorized under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. Before levying a fine, the strata corporation must follow procedural requirements set out in the Act, including providing written notice of the contravention and an opportunity for the person to be heard. Maximum fine amounts per contravention are established in the Act and its regulations; verify current amounts with a BC lawyer or licensed professional, as figures may have changed. Fines may be assessed on a continuing basis for ongoing contraventions, subject to limits prescribed under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43 — verify current details with a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a bylaw fine in British Columbia strata property?

A bylaw fine is a monetary penalty a strata corporation may impose on an owner or tenant for contravening the strata's bylaws or rules, as permitted under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. The fine is enforceable only if the strata follows procedural requirements set out in the Act and Strata Property Regulation. Verify current amounts and procedures with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
What procedural steps must a strata corporation follow before imposing a bylaw fine in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, section 135, a strata must give written particulars of the alleged contravention and a reasonable opportunity for the person to be heard or present a written response before imposing a fine. The strata council must then consider any response before making a decision. Verify the exact procedural requirements in the Strata Property Regulation and with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Is there a maximum amount a strata corporation can impose as a bylaw fine in BC?

The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the Strata Property Regulation set a maximum bylaw fine amount; this limit is subject to periodic adjustment by regulation. As of 2026-07-27, verify the current maximum with a BC lawyer, notary, or by reviewing the current Strata Property Regulation before acting. The strata's bylaws must also authorize the imposition of fines.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Can a strata corporation fine a tenant for a bylaw violation in BC?

Yes. Under section 131 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, the strata corporation may impose a bylaw fine on a tenant who contravenes the bylaws or rules, following the same notice and hearing procedures required for owners. The strata may also hold the owner responsible for the tenant's contravention. Verify enforcement options with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
What happens if an owner or tenant refuses to pay a bylaw fine in BC?

Unpaid bylaw fines are treated as a strata fee under section 116 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the strata may register a certificate of lien against the owner's strata lot to collect the debt. The strata may ultimately enforce the lien through proceedings under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Verify collection and enforcement procedures with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Can a strata corporation impose a continuing or daily bylaw fine in BC?

The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, does not expressly authorize automatic continuing daily fines; each separate contravention generally requires a separate notice and opportunity to be heard under section 135. Some stratas attempt to impose daily or continuing fines in their bylaws, but enforceability may be challenged. Verify the enforceability of any continuing fine provision with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Must a strata corporation's bylaws specifically authorize fines before the strata can impose them in BC?

Yes. A strata corporation may impose fines only if its bylaws expressly permit them, and the bylaws must comply with the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the Strata Property Regulation. Standard Schedule of Bylaw provisions may or may not include fine authority, so each strata should review and amend its bylaws as needed. Verify your strata's specific bylaw provisions with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Can an owner or tenant dispute a bylaw fine imposed by a strata corporation in BC?

Yes. An owner or tenant may apply to the Civil Resolution Tribunal (CRT) under section 123.2 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, to challenge the validity or amount of a bylaw fine if the strata did not follow procedural requirements or the fine is unreasonable. The CRT has exclusive jurisdiction for strata property disputes up to a certain monetary limit (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify dispute resolution options with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Are bylaw fines tax-deductible for owners or considered taxable income for the strata corporation in BC?

Bylaw fines paid by an owner or tenant are generally not tax-deductible personal expenses, and fines collected by the strata corporation are typically not considered taxable income but are treated as strata fee revenue under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. Tax treatment depends on federal and provincial tax law, so verify with a licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Can a strata corporation impose a bylaw fine retroactively for past contraventions in BC?

A strata may impose a fine for a past contravention, but it must comply with section 135 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, by providing written particulars and an opportunity to be heard before the fine is levied. The strata cannot impose a fine for conduct that occurred before the bylaw authorizing fines was validly passed. Verify procedural fairness and bylaw timing issues with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
