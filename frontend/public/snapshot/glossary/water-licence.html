Water Licence

A Water Licence is a provincial authorization permitting the holder to divert and use surface water or groundwater for specified purposes. In British Columbia, water licensing is governed by the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15 — verify current details with a BC lawyer or licensed professional, as this statute falls outside the FAQ whitelist and section-level claims cannot be confirmed here. On rural and acreage properties, a valid licence is typically required for non-domestic uses such as irrigation, stockwatering, and commercial operations. Buyers should confirm whether an existing licence is transferable, whether conditions attach to the land or the licensee, and whether any groundwater use requires separate authorization. Verify current licensing requirements and any applicable fees or priorities directly with the BC Ministry of Forests or a qualified BC lawyer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What BC statute governs water licences?

Water licences in British Columbia are authorized under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15. This provincial statute replaced the former Water Act in 2016 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) and regulates the diversion and use of surface water and groundwater. Verify current regulations and amendments with a BC lawyer or the BC Ministry of Forests before relying on any specific provision.

Verify with: Government of BC
Do I need a water licence for domestic use on my rural property?

Under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, domestic use (typically defined as household purposes for a single residence and normal household gardens) generally does not require a water licence. Non-domestic uses such as commercial irrigation, stockwatering beyond household exemptions, or commercial operations typically do require a licence. Verify your specific use and volume with the BC Ministry of Forests or a BC lawyer before diverting water.

Verify with: Government of BC
If a property already has a water licence, does it transfer to the new owner when I buy the land?

Under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, a water licence is appurtenant to the land (meaning it runs with the land) and typically transfers with the property upon sale, subject to compliance with the Act and any conditions in the licence. The purchaser should verify the licence is valid, in good standing, and registered on title or with the province before closing. Verify transfer requirements and any necessary approvals with a BC lawyer or notary before completing the purchase.

Verify with: Government of BC
What happens if I use water for irrigation without a licence?

Unlicensed diversion or use of water for non-domestic purposes may contravene the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, and can result in enforcement action, administrative penalties, or orders to cease use. The BC Ministry of Forests administers compliance and enforcement under the Act. Verify your legal obligations and apply for the appropriate authorization before using water for irrigation, stockwatering, or commercial purposes.

Verify with: Government of BC
How does a water licence affect property value or marketability in BC?

A valid water licence can enhance property value and marketability for rural, agricultural, or acreage properties where irrigation or commercial water use is important to the property's intended use. Conversely, lack of a required licence or non-compliance may reduce value or create legal and financing risks. Verify the status, terms, and transferability of any water licence with a BC lawyer or notary during due diligence before purchase or sale.

Verify with: Government of BC
Can I apply for a new water licence if the property does not currently have one?

Yes, applications for new water licences are made to the BC Ministry of Forests under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, subject to water availability, existing licences, and environmental and regulatory considerations. The application process includes fees, technical requirements, and may require consultation or environmental assessments (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current process). Verify eligibility, application procedures, and timelines with the BC Ministry of Forests or a BC lawyer before proceeding.

Verify with: Government of BC
Are there annual fees or reporting requirements for a water licence?

The Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, and its regulations may impose annual water rental fees based on the volume and purpose of authorized water use, as well as reporting or monitoring requirements (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Licence holders must comply with all conditions, renewals, and fee schedules set by the province. Verify current fee schedules, deadlines, and compliance obligations with the BC Ministry of Forests before relying on any licence.

Verify with: Government of BC
Does a water licence guarantee a specific quantity of water every year?

A water licence authorizes a maximum rate and volume of diversion, but actual availability depends on seasonal flows, drought conditions, prior licences with higher priority, and environmental flow requirements under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15. Licences are typically subject to seniority (priority date) and regulatory curtailment during low-flow periods. Verify the priority, reliability, and historical availability of water with the BC Ministry of Forests or a BC water-rights lawyer before purchasing property reliant on a licence.

Verify with: Government of BC
How do I verify the existence and status of a water licence on a property I am buying?

Water licences may be noted on the certificate of title at the BC Land Title Office under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, or registered in provincial water licence databases maintained by the BC Ministry of Forests. A title search and inquiry with the Ministry are recommended during due diligence. Verify the licence number, conditions, good standing, and transferability with a BC lawyer or notary before completing the purchase.

Verify with: Government of BC
Can a water licence be amended, suspended, or cancelled by the province?

Yes, under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, the province has authority to amend, suspend, or cancel water licences for reasons including non-compliance, non-use, changes in environmental needs, or public interest considerations. Licence holders must comply with all statutory conditions and may face administrative or enforcement actions if in breach. Verify your obligations and any notice of amendment or enforcement with a BC lawyer or the BC Ministry of Forests before acting.

Verify with: Government of BC
