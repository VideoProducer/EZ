Process & General

BCFSA

The BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) is the provincial regulatory body responsible for overseeing the financial services sector in British Columbia, including real estate services. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, BCFSA administers licensing, professional conduct standards, investigations, and disciplinary processes for real estate licensees in BC. BCFSA assumed the regulatory functions previously held by the Real Estate Council of British Columbia (RECBC); verify the exact transition date and current regulatory scope with a BC lawyer or by consulting BCFSA directly at bcfsa.ca.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the BCFSA?

The BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) is the provincial regulator for the financial services sector in British Columbia, including real estate services. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, BCFSA oversees licensing, professional conduct, investigations, and discipline for real estate professionals. BCFSA assumed the regulatory functions of the former Real Estate Council of British Columbia (RECBC) on August 1, 2021 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), consolidating real estate oversight with other financial services regulation in BC.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
What does BCFSA regulate in BC real estate?

BCFSA regulates real estate services in British Columbia under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules. Its regulatory functions include licensing real estate professionals, setting professional conduct standards, conducting compliance audits, investigating complaints, and imposing discipline. BCFSA does not regulate property transactions themselves, land titles, or conveyancing by lawyers and notaries.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
Who needs a BCFSA licence to practise real estate in BC?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, individuals providing real estate services in BC for compensation generally require a BCFSA licence. This includes real estate trading representatives, managing brokers, strata property managers, and rental property managers. Verify current licensing categories and exemptions with BCFSA or a BC lawyer before acting.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
When did BCFSA replace the Real Estate Council of BC (RECBC)?

BCFSA assumed the regulatory functions of the Real Estate Council of British Columbia (RECBC) on August 1, 2021 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The consolidation merged real estate oversight into the broader BC Financial Services Authority, which also regulates other financial services sectors in the province. All former RECBC rules and licensing frameworks were transferred to BCFSA under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
Can BCFSA discipline a licensed real estate professional?

Yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules, BCFSA has the authority to investigate professional conduct and impose discipline, including fines, licence suspensions, licence cancellations, and conditions on practice. Disciplinary decisions are published on the BCFSA website. Verify current disciplinary processes and appeal rights with a BC lawyer or by consulting BCFSA directly.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
Does BCFSA handle consumer complaints about real estate agents?

Yes. BCFSA receives and investigates consumer complaints about licensed real estate professionals under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42. BCFSA's complaint and investigation process is set out in the RESA Rules. If a complaint involves unlicensed activity, fraud, or criminal conduct, BCFSA may refer the matter to law enforcement or other authorities.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
Where can I verify if a BC real estate professional is licensed by BCFSA?

You can verify current licensing status through the BCFSA public registry, available at www.bcfsa.ca (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current URL). The registry shows the licence type, status, brokerage affiliation, and any disciplinary history for real estate trading representatives, managing brokers, strata managers, and rental managers licensed under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42. Always confirm current licence status before engaging a real estate professional.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
Does BCFSA regulate real estate appraisers in BC?

No. BCFSA does not regulate real estate appraisers. Real estate appraisers in BC are regulated by the Appraisal Institute of Canada (AIC) and other professional bodies, not by BCFSA under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42. Verify the credentials and regulatory status of appraisers with the appropriate professional body or a BC lawyer before relying on an appraisal.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
Can BCFSA set continuing education requirements for BC real estate licensees?

Yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules, BCFSA has the authority to set education, licensing, and relicensing requirements, including mandatory continuing professional education for real estate professionals. Verify current continuing education requirements and deadlines directly with BCFSA or through your managing broker before your licence renewal.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
What is the difference between BCFSA and a real estate board or association in BC?

BCFSA is the statutory regulator under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, responsible for licensing, professional conduct, and discipline of real estate professionals. Real estate boards (such as the Real Estate Board of Greater Vancouver or the Victoria Real Estate Board) are member associations that provide MLS® services, advocacy, and professional development, but do not hold statutory regulatory authority. BCFSA enforces the law; boards provide member services and market data.

Verify with: BC Financial Services Authority
