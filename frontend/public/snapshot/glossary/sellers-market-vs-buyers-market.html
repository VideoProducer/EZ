Buying & Selling

Sellers Market vs Buyers Market

A sellers' market arises when active buyer demand exceeds available home supply, typically producing shorter days on market, sale prices at or near list price, multiple competing offers, and reduced reliance on buyer-side conditions. A buyers' market is the inverse: supply exceeds demand, resulting in longer days on market, sale prices below list, fewer competing offers, and greater use of conditions and price negotiation. A balanced market sits between these extremes, with supply and demand roughly matched. These conditions are not defined in BC statute; they are descriptive market terms commonly assessed using sales-to-active-listings ratios published by BC real estate boards and reported by BCFSA-licensed brokerages. For guidance on how current market conditions may affect your transaction, verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a sellers' market in British Columbia real estate?

A sellers' market is a condition in which active buyer demand exceeds the supply of homes available for sale. It is typically characterized by shorter days on market, sale prices at or above the list price, multiple competing offers on listings, and reduced use of buyer-side subject conditions. Market type is commonly assessed using the sales-to-active-listings ratio published by BC real estate boards. Verify current market conditions with your licensed REALTOR® or BC real estate professional before making decisions.

Verify with: BC Real Estate Association
What is a buyers' market in British Columbia real estate?

A buyers' market exists when the supply of homes for sale exceeds active buyer demand. It is typically characterized by longer days on market, sale prices negotiated below the list price, fewer or no competing offers, and greater use of buyer-side subject conditions such as financing and inspection. Market type is commonly assessed using the sales-to-active-listings ratio published by BC real estate boards. Verify current market conditions with your licensed REALTOR® or BC real estate professional before making decisions.

Does BC law define what constitutes a sellers' market or buyers' market?

No. Neither the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, nor any other BC statute defines the term "sellers' market" or "buyers' market." These are market-descriptive terms used by the real estate industry, real estate boards, and analysts to characterize supply and demand conditions. The British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) does not set official market-type thresholds. Verify market classification and interpretation with a BC licensed REALTOR® or real estate professional.

Verify with: BC Real Estate Association
Are there different legal obligations for real estate licensees in a sellers' market versus a buyers' market in BC?

No. A licensee's duties under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and the BCFSA Rules remain the same regardless of market conditions. Licensees must act in their clients' best interests, provide competent service, disclose conflicts, and comply with disclosure and professional conduct standards in all markets—sellers', buyers', or balanced. Verify your representative's specific obligations with the British Columbia Financial Services Authority or a BC lawyer.

Verify with: BC Real Estate Association
Can a seller refuse subject conditions (like financing or inspection) in a sellers' market in BC?

Yes. Under BC contract law, a seller may accept, reject, or counter any offer, including an offer with subject conditions, regardless of market type. In a sellers' market, sellers commonly receive multiple competing offers and may favour unconditional offers or those with fewer subjects, but there is no legal requirement to accept any particular offer. All parties must still comply with the disclosure obligations in the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42. Verify your rights and risks with a BC lawyer or notary before waiving conditions.

Verify with: BC Real Estate Association
Do property transfer tax (PTT) rates change in a sellers' market or buyers' market in BC?

No. Property transfer tax rates under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, are set by statute and regulation, not by market conditions. As of 2026-07-27 (verify current), the general PTT rates are 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% on the portion from $2,000,000 to $3,000,000, and 5% on the portion above $3,000,000. An additional property transfer tax of 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) may apply to foreign entities under the Property Transfer Tax Act. Verify current rates and exemptions with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC lawyer before closing.

Verify with: BC Real Estate Association
Does BC's Speculation and Vacancy Tax apply differently in a sellers' market compared to a buyers' market?

No. The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, applies based on ownership status, use, and location in designated taxable regions, not on whether the market is a sellers' market or buyers' market. The tax is intended to encourage residential use and increase rental supply in specified areas. Verify your filing obligations and exemptions with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC tax professional, as liability depends on your individual circumstances as of each December 31 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).

Verify with: BC Real Estate Association
Are buyer representations or agency agreements mandatory in all market types in BC?

Yes, when a licensee provides real estate services to a buyer. Under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and the BCFSA Rules, a licensee must enter into a written service agreement (such as a buyer representation agreement) before providing trading services to a buyer, regardless of whether the market is a sellers', buyers', or balanced market. Verify the terms and your obligations under any service agreement with a BC lawyer or notary before signing.

Verify with: BC Real Estate Association
Can a buyer make multiple offers on different properties at the same time in a buyers' market in BC?

Yes, subject to the terms of any representation agreement and the licensee's disclosure duties under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42. A buyer may submit offers on multiple properties simultaneously unless restricted by contract. The buyer's representative must disclose material facts and act in the buyer's best interests. Verify your obligations and the timing/wording of offers with your licensed REALTOR® and a BC lawyer or notary to avoid unintended dual acceptances.

Verify with: BC Real Estate Association
Does the federal foreign buyer prohibition apply differently in a sellers' market versus a buyers' market?

No. The Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10, prohibits certain non-Canadians from purchasing residential property in Canada (currently extended through January 1, 2027, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) based on the buyer's status and property type, not on market conditions. The prohibition applies equally whether the market is characterized as a sellers', buyers', or balanced market. Verify your eligibility and any exemptions with a BC lawyer or licensed immigration professional before making an offer.
