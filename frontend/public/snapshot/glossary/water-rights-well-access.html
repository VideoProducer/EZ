Water rights / well access

Water rights in BC are legal entitlements to access and use water from sources such as wells, creeks, or streams, and are governed by provincial water legislation. The Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15 falls outside the whitelist; verify the governing statute and licensing requirements with a BC lawyer. For rural properties used in equestrian or agricultural operations, well capacity and any water licences attached to the land are material facts that must be disclosed. Whether a water licence transfers with the property is a legal question — verify current details with a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What BC statute governs water rights and well access for rural properties used for horse operations or irrigation?

Water rights in British Columbia are governed by the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, which replaced the former Water Act in 2016. The Act establishes a licensing regime for surface water and groundwater use, including wells used for irrigation, livestock watering, and domestic purposes. Verify current licensing requirements and exemptions with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Forests, as rules vary by use type, volume, and date of first beneficial use.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Do I need a water licence in BC to operate a well for watering horses on my rural property?

Under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, non-domestic groundwater use (including livestock watering) that began after February 29, 2016, generally requires a groundwater licence unless an exemption applies. Wells used solely for domestic purposes on land where the owner resides may be exempt. Verify your specific use, start date, and volume with the BC Ministry of Forests or a BC lawyer before relying on any exemption.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
What is the difference between a 'water right' and 'well access' in BC real estate transactions?

A 'water right' typically refers to a statutory licence or authorization under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, granting the legal entitlement to divert and use a specified quantity of water from a defined source. 'Well access' generally refers to the physical infrastructure (the drilled well) and the legal ability to draw groundwater, which may or may not require a separate licence depending on use type and timing. Verify whether a current licence exists, whether it transfers with title, and whether ongoing use complies with provincial law by consulting a BC lawyer or notary during the conveyance process.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Are water licences or well authorizations registered on title in BC?

Water licences issued under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, are not automatically registered on title in the BC Land Title Office, though they are recorded in the province's water licence database (GWELLS and the Water Rights Licensing System). A well tag number and construction details may be recorded in the provincial Groundwater Wells and Aquifers (GWELLS) application, but buyers should independently verify licence status and conditions. Always conduct a water licence search through the BC Ministry of Forests and confirm compliance with a BC lawyer or notary before completing a rural property purchase.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can a water licence or well be transferred when I buy a BC rural property?

Under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, water licences are generally appurtenant to the land (meaning they run with the land title) and transfer automatically upon conveyance of the property, but only if the licence was validly issued and remains in good standing. Well infrastructure transfers as a fixture unless expressly excluded in the contract of purchase and sale. Verify the licence number, licence conditions, transfer requirements, and any required Ministry approvals or notifications with a BC lawyer or notary and the BC Ministry of Forests before closing.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
What due diligence should a buyer perform regarding water rights and wells on a BC rural property?

Buyers should obtain a water licence search from the BC Ministry of Forests, a well tag report from the provincial GWELLS database, and a current water quality test (bacteriological and chemical) from an accredited lab. The Contract of Purchase and Sale should include conditions for satisfactory review of water quantity, quality, licence status, and compliance with the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15. Verify all findings and draft appropriate contract language with a BC lawyer or notary before removing subject conditions.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
What is a 'well tag number' in BC and why does it matter?

A well tag number is a unique identifier assigned by a qualified well driller when a well is constructed and reported to the province under the Groundwater Protection Regulation, B.C. Reg. 299/2004 (made under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15). The well tag links the physical well to construction records, depth, yield, aquifer characteristics, and location data stored in the provincial GWELLS database. Buyers should verify the well tag number, confirm the well record matches the property, and review the driller's report for capacity and construction compliance with a BC lawyer or qualified water professional.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can municipal water service replace or supplement well water for horse operations in BC, and does this affect zoning?

Connection to municipal water is governed by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, and individual municipal bylaws, which may permit or require connection in serviced areas. If the property is within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, permitted uses (including horse operations and irrigation) must comply with ALR Use Regulation, and water source does not change underlying zoning or ALR restrictions. Verify municipal service availability, connection costs, and ALR/zoning compliance with the local government, the Agricultural Land Commission, and a BC lawyer or notary before proceeding.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
What happens if a BC property's well runs dry or has insufficient capacity for my intended use?

Well capacity and reliability are matters of physical fact and contractual allocation of risk, not typically covered by statute. Under the Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, a groundwater licence (if required) specifies a licensed quantity, but does not guarantee physical availability during drought or aquifer depletion. Buyers should include a condition in the Contract of Purchase and Sale for satisfactory well flow testing (often measured in gallons per minute or litres per second) and review historical yield, seasonal variation, and aquifer sustainability with a qualified hydrogeologist or well professional and a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Are there restrictions on drilling new wells or expanding water use on BC rural properties?

The Water Sustainability Act, SBC 2014, c. 15, and Groundwater Protection Regulation, B.C. Reg. 299/2004, require that new wells be constructed by qualified well drillers, reported to the province, and comply with setback and construction standards. New or expanded non-domestic groundwater use (e.g., irrigation for a larger area or additional livestock) may require a groundwater licence or authorization. Verify current licensing thresholds, aquifer capacity, local government bylaws, and any Critical Groundwater Area designations with the BC Ministry of Forests and a BC lawyer or notary before drilling or expanding use.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
