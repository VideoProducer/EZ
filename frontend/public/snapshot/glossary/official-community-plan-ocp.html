Official Community Plan (OCP)

An Official Community Plan (OCP) is a statement of objectives and policies adopted by bylaw by a local government to guide planning and land use management within its jurisdiction, as authorized under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. The OCP shapes future decisions on zoning, development permits, and infrastructure, but it is not itself a zoning bylaw and does not override existing zoning. Amendments to zoning must still be consistent with the OCP, but the OCP alone does not change what a landowner may build or do with their property. For how an OCP may affect a specific parcel or proposed development, verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed land use professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an Official Community Plan (OCP) in British Columbia?

An Official Community Plan (OCP) is a statement of objectives and policies adopted by bylaw by a local government under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, to guide decisions on planning and land use management within its jurisdiction. The OCP frames future zoning, development permits, and infrastructure decisions, but it is not the same as zoning. Local governments in BC are required to adopt an OCP for their jurisdictions under the Local Government Act.

Verify with: Local Government Planning
Is an OCP the same as zoning?

No. An OCP is a broad policy document that sets out objectives and policies for land use and community development, while zoning is a specific regulatory tool that determines what uses are permitted on individual parcels. Under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, zoning bylaws must be consistent with the OCP. The OCP guides zoning decisions, but zoning is the actual regulation that property owners must comply with.

Verify with: Local Government Planning
Which statute governs Official Community Plans in BC?

Official Community Plans are governed by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. The Act sets out requirements for the content, adoption, amendment, and implementation of OCPs by BC municipalities and regional districts. Verify the current provisions and any amendments with a BC lawyer or municipal planning department before relying on specific sections.

Verify with: Local Government Planning
Must a local government adopt an Official Community Plan?

Yes. Under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, every municipality and regional district in British Columbia must adopt an Official Community Plan by bylaw for the areas within their jurisdiction. The Act sets out mandatory content and procedural requirements for adoption and amendment. Verify current requirements with your local government or a BC lawyer.

Verify with: Local Government Planning
Can an OCP restrict what I build on my property?

An OCP itself does not directly restrict what you can build; it is a policy document, not a regulation. However, under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, zoning bylaws and development permit areas must be consistent with the OCP, and these regulatory tools do impose binding restrictions on land use and development. If you are considering development, verify zoning and OCP consistency with your local government or a BC lawyer.

Verify with: Local Government Planning
How does an OCP affect rezoning applications?

Under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, a local government may not adopt a zoning bylaw that is inconsistent with its Official Community Plan. If a rezoning application proposes a use inconsistent with the OCP, the local government must first amend the OCP before approving the rezoning. The OCP designation for a property is therefore a key factor in assessing the feasibility of a rezoning. Verify the OCP designation and rezoning process with your local government or a BC lawyer.

Verify with: Local Government Planning
What topics must an Official Community Plan address?

Under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, an OCP must include statements and map designations for approximate locations, amount, and type of residential, commercial, industrial, institutional, agricultural, recreational, and public utility land uses. OCPs may also include policies on affordable housing, greenhouse gas reduction targets, social well-being, and other matters. Verify the specific content requirements and any recent amendments with your local government or a BC lawyer.

Verify with: Local Government Planning
How is an OCP amended?

An OCP is amended by bylaw under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, following a public process that typically includes a public hearing. The amendment bylaw must be consistent with the Act's procedural requirements, and amendments often require referrals to other agencies or governments. Verify the current amendment process and timelines with your local government or a BC lawyer before initiating an OCP amendment application.

Verify with: Local Government Planning
Does an OCP designation guarantee that my property can be developed for that use?

No. An OCP designation reflects policy intent, not a legal entitlement to develop. Under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, actual development permissions are determined by zoning bylaws, development permits, building permits, and other approvals, all of which must be consistent with the OCP but involve separate application processes. Verify your property's zoning, any development permit area designations, and applicable approvals with your local government or a BC lawyer before proceeding.

Verify with: Local Government Planning
Can I challenge or appeal an Official Community Plan or OCP amendment?

There is no statutory appeal right to challenge the adoption or amendment of an OCP itself under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. However, judicial review may be available in limited circumstances if the local government failed to follow proper procedures or acted outside its jurisdiction. Public participation is typically through the public hearing process before adoption. Verify your options with a BC lawyer before pursuing any legal challenge.
