Strata Documents

Council Meeting Minutes

Strata council meeting minutes are the written record of the regular meetings held by the elected strata council throughout the year — typically monthly or quarterly. These minutes are where the day-to-day management of the building is recorded: maintenance issues reported, repair quotes discussed, bylaw complaints received, and neighbour disputes noted. Council minutes are among the most revealing documents in a strata purchase. Request the last two years. Look for recurring complaints about the same issue — this often signals a systemic problem the strata has not resolved.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are strata corporations in BC legally required to keep council meeting minutes?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), a strata corporation is required to keep minutes of all strata council meetings, including the date of each meeting and the decisions made. These minutes must be retained as part of the strata corporation's records and made available to eligible persons on request.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Who is entitled to request and receive strata council meeting minutes in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act, owners, mortgagees of a strata lot, and prospective purchasers who have an accepted offer to purchase are entitled to request records including council meeting minutes. A strata corporation must make these records available for inspection and provide copies, typically within a prescribed timeframe set out in the Strata Property Act Regulation.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
How are council meeting minutes typically obtained during a strata property purchase in BC?

Council meeting minutes are commonly obtained as part of the Form B Information Certificate package or through a separate document request to the strata corporation or its property manager. A buyer or their licensee may request the minutes directly, and the strata corporation is obligated under the Strata Property Act to provide access to these records. Reviewing at least two years of minutes is widely recommended to identify recurring or unresolved issues.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
What kinds of issues are typically disclosed in BC strata council meeting minutes?

Council meeting minutes record the day-to-day management decisions of the strata corporation, including maintenance and repair matters, contractor quotes, bylaw complaints received, neighbour disputes, financial discussions, and any motions passed or deferred. Because these are the working records of the elected council, they often reveal issues that may not appear in other strata documents such as the Form B Information Certificate.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Can a BC real estate licensee advise a buyer to skip reviewing council meeting minutes to speed up a transaction?

A licensee licensed under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and regulated by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) has a duty to act in the best interests of their client and to disclose all known material information. Advising a buyer to forgo reviewing council meeting minutes without explaining the potential risks could conflict with a licensee's duties under RESA and BCFSA conduct standards. Licensees should ensure buyers are informed about the significance of these records.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
How far back should a buyer request council meeting minutes when purchasing a strata property in BC?

Reviewing at least two years of council meeting minutes is widely recommended by strata industry professionals, as this period is generally sufficient to identify recurring problems, ongoing disputes, or deferred maintenance that a strata corporation has not resolved. There is no statutory minimum review period prescribed by the Strata Property Act, but a longer review period provides a more complete picture of the strata corporation's management history.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
What should a buyer look for in BC strata council meeting minutes to identify potential red flags?

Buyers should look for recurring references to the same maintenance issue, which may signal a systemic problem that has not been effectively addressed, as well as repeated bylaw complaints involving the same unit or owner, unresolved water ingress or building envelope concerns, and discussions about special levies or underfunded contingency reserve funds. Under the Strata Property Act, the contingency reserve fund is meant to cover major anticipated and unanticipated repair and replacement costs, so minutes reflecting underfunding or deferred repairs warrant careful attention.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Are strata council meeting minutes different from annual general meeting (AGM) minutes in BC?

Yes. Council meeting minutes are records of the regular meetings held by the elected strata council to manage the corporation's ongoing affairs, whereas AGM minutes record the proceedings of the annual general meeting of all owners as required under the Strata Property Act. Both sets of minutes are part of the strata corporation's official records and are separately accessible, but council minutes typically contain more detail about day-to-day operational and maintenance decisions.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Can personal information about individual owners or residents appear in BC strata council meeting minutes, and are there privacy considerations?

Yes, council meeting minutes may contain personal information such as names associated with bylaw complaints or disputes, which engages BC's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA). Strata corporations collecting, using, and disclosing personal information must do so in accordance with PIPA, and individuals handling strata records — including licensees and buyers who receive copies — should treat any personal information contained in the minutes with appropriate discretion and use it only for the purpose for which it was obtained.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
What happens if a strata corporation in BC refuses to provide council meeting minutes to an entitled person?

If a strata corporation refuses a valid request for council meeting minutes from an entitled person, that person may apply to the Civil Resolution Tribunal (CRT), which has jurisdiction over strata property disputes under the Civil Resolution Tribunal Act, to compel the corporation to provide access to the records. The Strata Property Act imposes an obligation on strata corporations to make records available, and non-compliance can result in orders from the CRT requiring disclosure.
