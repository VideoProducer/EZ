Strata Documents

Council Meeting Minutes

Strata council meeting minutes are the written record of decisions and discussions from strata council meetings, as governed by the Strata Property Act (SPA), SBC 1998, c. 43. The SPA requires a strata corporation to keep minutes of council meetings; verify current retention and access requirements directly against the SPA or with a BC lawyer or notary. These minutes typically document maintenance issues, repair decisions, bylaw enforcement matters, and neighbourhood disputes handled by the council. Buyers commonly review council minutes when conducting strata document due diligence to identify recurring or unresolved issues. The number of years of minutes to request should be confirmed with a BC lawyer or notary, as no specific period is stated here to avoid error.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are strata council meeting minutes required by law in British Columbia?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, section 35, the strata council must prepare and retain minutes of each council meeting. The minutes must record council decisions and must be available for owner inspection. Verify current requirements under the Strata Property Act and Strata Property Regulation with a BC lawyer or notary before relying on this information.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Can I request copies of strata council meeting minutes before purchasing a strata unit in BC?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, section 36, an owner or a person authorized in writing by an owner or purchaser may inspect strata records, including council meeting minutes, and may request copies at the strata's expense schedule. A Form B Information Certificate, required under section 59 of the Strata Property Act, does not automatically include council minutes, so purchasers typically request them separately. Verify the current procedure and any fees with the strata corporation or a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
How long must a strata corporation keep council meeting minutes in BC?

Under section 35(2)(c) of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and section 4.1 of the Strata Property Regulation, a strata corporation must retain council meeting minutes for at least six years (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Some strata corporations retain minutes longer as a best practice. Verify current retention requirements under the Strata Property Regulation with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
What information should be included in strata council meeting minutes under BC law?

Section 35(1) of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, requires that council meeting minutes be prepared and that they record council decisions. The Act does not prescribe a specific format, but best practice includes the date, attendees, decisions made, and action items. Verify current best practices and compliance requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or strata management professional.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Are strata council meeting minutes confidential in British Columbia?

Generally, no. Under section 36 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, owners and authorized persons (including prospective purchasers) have the right to inspect most strata records, including council minutes. However, section 36(3) permits a strata corporation to refuse access to portions of records that disclose personal information about an identifiable individual who is not the person requesting the record, subject to certain exceptions. Verify the scope of access and privacy protections under the Strata Property Act and the Personal Information Protection Act, SBC 2003, c. 63, with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
How far back should a purchaser request council meeting minutes when buying a strata property in BC?

There is no statutory minimum for purchaser due diligence, but best practice in BC is to request at least the last two years of council meeting minutes to identify recurring issues, deferred maintenance, bylaw complaints, or neighbour disputes. This review helps assess the strata corporation's governance and potential future liabilities. Verify the scope of your pre-purchase due diligence with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Can a strata council hold 'in camera' sessions and exclude those minutes from owner inspection in BC?

The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, does not expressly authorize 'in camera' sessions or exempt minutes from inspection, but section 36(3) allows a strata to refuse access to portions that disclose personal information about an identifiable individual, subject to exceptions. Councils may redact or withhold sensitive personal information, but general business decisions must be recorded and accessible. Verify the lawful scope of redaction under the Strata Property Act and the Personal Information Protection Act, SBC 2003, c. 63, with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
What should a purchaser look for when reviewing strata council meeting minutes in BC?

Purchasers should review minutes for recurring complaints about the same issue (e.g., water ingress, noise, parking disputes), evidence of deferred or inadequate maintenance, bylaw enforcement patterns, insurance claims, and special levy discussions. Recurring problems often signal systemic issues the strata corporation has not resolved. Verify the significance of any findings with a BC lawyer, notary, or qualified home inspector before making a purchase decision.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
If council meeting minutes are incomplete or missing, what recourse does a BC strata owner have?

Under section 35 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, the council has a duty to prepare and retain minutes. An owner may request the strata comply, and if the strata refuses or fails to maintain proper records, the owner may apply to the Civil Resolution Tribunal (CRT) for an order requiring compliance or other relief. Verify current dispute resolution options under the Strata Property Act and the Civil Resolution Tribunal Act, SBC 2012, c. 25, with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Are draft or unapproved council meeting minutes accessible to owners in BC?

The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, requires that council minutes be prepared and accessible under section 36, but it does not specify whether draft minutes must be provided before approval. Best practice is for strata corporations to make approved minutes available; draft minutes may contain errors or incomplete information. Verify your strata corporation's policy and your rights under the Strata Property Act with a BC lawyer or notary.
