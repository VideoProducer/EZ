Cash Offer

A cash offer is a purchase in which the buyer pays the full purchase price from their own funds at completion, without obtaining mortgage financing. Because no lender approval is required, the buyer may choose to waive a financing condition, which can make the offer more attractive to sellers in competitive markets. Regardless, the standard conveyancing process under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250 still applies, including title transfer and registration. Property Transfer Tax is payable under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378; verify current rates and any applicable exemptions with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional. Cash buyers are also subject to federal anti-money-laundering source-of-funds requirements; verify current obligations with a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is a cash offer in British Columbia real estate?

A cash offer means the buyer intends to purchase the property without obtaining a mortgage, paying the full purchase price from their own available funds at completion. Under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and BCFSA Rules, a buyer making a cash offer typically removes any 'subject to financing' condition, which can make the offer more attractive to sellers. The buyer must still complete standard conveyancing through a BC lawyer or notary, pay Property Transfer Tax under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, and satisfy anti-money-laundering source-of-funds requirements. Verify current conveyancing requirements and your legal obligations with a BC lawyer or notary before submitting a cash offer.

Do I still need to pay Property Transfer Tax (PTT) on a cash purchase in BC?

Yes. The Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, applies to all BC real estate transfers, regardless of whether the buyer uses a mortgage or pays cash. As of 2026-07-27 — verify current, PTT is calculated on the fair market value of the property at generally 1% on the first $200,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), 2% on the portion between $200,000 and $2,000,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), 3% on the portion between $2,000,000 and $3,000,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), and 5% on the portion above $3,000,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). First-time home buyers may qualify for a full or partial exemption under the Property Transfer Tax Act; verify eligibility and current thresholds with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before closing.

Do I need to provide proof of funds when making a cash offer in BC?

Yes. Under BC's anti-money-laundering framework and the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, your BC lawyer or notary public is required to verify the source of your funds as part of their professional obligations. You must provide acceptable documentation showing you have sufficient cash available to complete the purchase (e.g., recent bank statements, investment account statements, proof of proceeds from a prior sale). The specific documentation required depends on the amount and source; verify the exact documents your lawyer or notary needs before your completion date. Failure to satisfy source-of-funds verification can delay or prevent closing.

Does a cash offer mean I don't need a lawyer or notary in BC?

No. Under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, every BC real estate transfer must be registered in the Land Title Office, which requires proper conveyancing by a BC lawyer or notary public. Your lawyer or notary conducts title searches, registers the transfer, ensures compliance with the Property Transfer Tax Act and anti-money-laundering obligations, and protects your legal interests. Paying cash only removes the financing condition; all other legal steps remain mandatory. Verify your legal representation early in the offer process with a BC lawyer or notary.

Can a non-Canadian make a cash offer on residential property in BC?

As of 2026-07-27 — verify current, the federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10, prohibits most non-Canadians from purchasing residential property in Canada, with limited exceptions, through January 1, 2027 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). If you are a non-Canadian and fall within an exception, you may make a cash offer but will also be subject to the BC Additional Property Transfer Tax of 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, unless exempt. Verify your eligibility under both federal and provincial law with a BC lawyer or notary and the BC Ministry of Finance before making an offer.

Does a cash offer exempt me from the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

No. The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, is an annual tax based on property ownership and occupancy status in designated taxable regions, not on how you financed the purchase. If your property is in a taxable area and you do not meet exemption criteria (e.g., principal residence, long-term rental), the tax applies whether you paid cash or used a mortgage. As of 2026-07-27 — verify current, the tax rates and designated areas are set by BC Ministry of Finance regulation. Verify your exemption status and filing obligations with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional annually.

Is a cash offer binding immediately, or can I still back out?

Under BC contract law, a cash offer becomes a binding contract once accepted by the seller, unless you include conditions (subjects) that allow you to withdraw. Removing the financing condition—common in cash offers—means you cannot cancel for lack of mortgage approval, but you may still include other conditions such as subject to satisfactory home inspection, subject to review of strata documents (under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43), or subject to lawyer review. If you fail to complete a firm, unconditional cash offer, you risk forfeiting your deposit and facing legal action for breach of contract. Verify the enforceability and risks of your specific offer with a BC lawyer or notary before signing.

Does the BC Home Flipping Tax apply if I pay cash for a property?

Yes, if you sell the property within a certain period. The Home Flipping Tax Act, SBC 2024 (effective January 1, 2025 — verify current), imposes an income inclusion rule on profits from the sale of residential property held for less than a specified period, with limited exemptions (e.g., death, divorce, disability, involuntary job relocation). How you financed the original purchase (cash or mortgage) does not affect your liability under this Act. Verify the current holding period threshold, exemptions, and federal and provincial tax treatment with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before purchasing with intent to resell.

If I make a cash offer, do I still get a First-Time Home Buyer Property Transfer Tax exemption in BC?

Yes, if you meet all other eligibility criteria under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378. The exemption is based on factors such as being a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, being a first-time buyer, intending to occupy the property as your principal residence, and the property's fair market value. As of 2026-07-27 — verify current, the full exemption applies up to $835,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) with a partial exemption up to $860,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Whether you pay cash or use a mortgage does not affect exemption eligibility. Verify current thresholds and your eligibility with your BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before closing.

Are there any advantages to a cash offer in a competitive BC real estate market?

A cash offer can be more attractive to sellers because it removes the risk that the buyer will fail to obtain mortgage financing, and it often allows for a faster closing timeline. Under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, the listing agent must present all offers to the seller; sellers may prefer cash offers for certainty and speed, but they are not legally required to accept the highest or any specific offer. Cash buyers still face the same legal obligations—title transfer, Property Transfer Tax under the Property Transfer Tax Act, anti-money-laundering verification, and compliance with all BC statutes. Verify the strategic and legal implications of structuring your offer with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed REALTOR® before submitting.
