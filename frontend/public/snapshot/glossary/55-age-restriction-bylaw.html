Strata

55+ Age Restriction Bylaw

A strata corporation in British Columbia may pass a bylaw restricting residency to persons aged 55 or older, as permitted under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. This exception exists within the Act's broader framework governing age-related bylaws for seniors' housing. Not all age-restriction bylaws are permitted; only those meeting the specific criteria set out in the Strata Property Act remain valid. Buyers and owners should review any such bylaw carefully before purchasing, as it may restrict who may reside in the unit. Verify current details and applicable provisions with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a 55+ age-restriction bylaw in British Columbia strata corporations?

A 55+ age-restriction bylaw is a bylaw filed under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, that limits occupancy of strata units to persons aged 55 years or older (and their spouses or other permitted occupants). The Strata Property Act permits age restrictions for housing intended for seniors, while general age restrictions for other age groups are no longer allowed in BC. Verify current bylaw wording and enforcement details with a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing in a 55+ strata.

Are 55+ age-restriction bylaws still legal in BC?

Yes. The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, continues to permit age-restriction bylaws for seniors' housing, specifically 55+ developments. BC legislation ended general age-restriction bylaws that discriminated against families with children, but 55+ restrictions remain explicitly permitted for seniors' housing. Verify the specific bylaw is properly registered and enforceable with a BC lawyer or notary before relying on it.

Verify with: BCFSA — BC Government Ends Rental and Age Restrictions on Strata Properties
Can a strata corporation enforce a 55+ age-restriction bylaw against owners who are under 55?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a validly registered 55+ age-restriction bylaw can restrict occupancy, not ownership. An owner under 55 may own the unit but generally cannot occupy it themselves; enforcement typically involves fines or court orders if the bylaw is breached. Verify enforcement procedures, exemptions, and bylaw validity with a BC lawyer before purchasing or occupying.

Does a 55+ age-restriction bylaw apply to spouses or caregivers who are under 55?

Many 55+ bylaws include exemptions permitting spouses, dependent children, or live-in caregivers under 55 to reside with a qualifying resident aged 55 or older, but the exact wording varies by strata corporation. The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, does not mandate uniform exemption language. Verify the specific bylaw's terms and any exemptions in writing with the strata corporation and a BC lawyer or notary before moving in.

Verify with: BCFSA — BC Government Ends Rental and Age Restrictions on Strata Properties
Can a strata corporation pass a new 55+ age-restriction bylaw after the building is already occupied?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation can pass a new age-restriction bylaw by a ¾ vote at a general meeting, but existing owners and tenants typically have grandfathered rights to continue occupying if they do not meet the age requirement. The bylaw applies to future occupants and transfers. Verify grandfathering provisions, voting requirements, and the bylaw's legal effect with a BC lawyer or notary before proceeding.

How does a buyer confirm a strata property has a valid 55+ age-restriction bylaw?

A buyer should review the strata's registered bylaws in the Form B (Information Certificate) provided under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and confirm the age-restriction bylaw is filed in the Land Title Office. The Form B must disclose all bylaws and rental or age restrictions. Verify the bylaw's wording, registration, and enforceability with a BC lawyer or notary, and ensure your realtor or licensee obtains a current Form B before you remove subjects.

Are there property tax benefits for 55+ age-restricted strata properties in BC?

Age restrictions themselves do not create automatic property tax benefits, but qualifying seniors may be eligible for the BC Home Owner Grant or other provincial programs depending on age, income, and residency (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Eligibility is determined by the BC Home Owner Grant Act and BC Ministry of Finance rules, not solely by the strata bylaw. Verify current grant thresholds, age requirements, and application procedures with the BC Ministry of Finance or a licensed tax professional.

Can a strata corporation change or repeal a 55+ age-restriction bylaw?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation can amend or repeal an age-restriction bylaw by a ¾ vote at a general meeting, following proper notice and filing procedures. Repealing the bylaw removes the age restriction for future occupants. Verify voting requirements, notice periods, and the legal and market impact of a bylaw change with a BC lawyer or notary before voting or purchasing.

Does the federal Foreign Buyer Ban apply to 55+ age-restricted strata units?

Yes. The Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10 (currently extended through January 1, 2027 as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), generally prohibits non-Canadians from purchasing residential property in Canada, including 55+ strata units, subject to exemptions. The federal ban operates independently of BC strata bylaws. Verify current exemptions, eligibility, and compliance requirements with a BC lawyer or licensed tax professional before a non-Canadian purchaser makes an offer.

Who enforces a 55+ age-restriction bylaw in a BC strata corporation?

The strata corporation enforces its own bylaws under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, typically through its strata council, which may levy fines, demand compliance, or seek court orders for breaches. The British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) does not enforce strata bylaws; enforcement is a civil matter between the strata and the owner or occupant. Verify enforcement options, fines, and dispute resolution procedures with a BC lawyer or notary if a bylaw dispute arises.
