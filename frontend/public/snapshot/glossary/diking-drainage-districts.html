Diking / Drainage Districts

Diking and drainage districts are statutory bodies established under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, to manage flood protection and drainage infrastructure such as dikes, pumps, and drainage channels within defined geographic boundaries. These districts levy assessments against properties that benefit from their works, which may appear as charges on title or tax notices affecting real property in BC. Buyers of land within or adjacent to such a district should confirm the existence, scope, and any outstanding levies before completing a purchase. Verify current assessment amounts, district boundaries, and governance details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a diking district or drainage district in British Columbia?

A diking district or drainage district is a special-purpose local government created under BC's Drainage, Ditch and Dike Act, RSBC 1996, c. 103, to manage flood protection infrastructure such as dikes, pumps, and drainage works within a defined geographic area. These districts levy annual taxes or tolls on properties that benefit from the flood protection and drainage services. Verify current boundaries and rates with the specific district or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — Diking Authorities for New Dikes Policy
How are diking and drainage districts created in BC?

Diking and drainage districts are established by Letters Patent issued under the Drainage, Ditch and Dike Act, RSBC 1996, c. 103, following a petition by landowners or a local government resolution. The Minister responsible (currently the Minister of Water, Land and Resource Stewardship as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) grants the Letters Patent, which define the district's boundaries and powers. Verify the formation process for a specific area with a BC lawyer, notary, or the local government before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — Diking Authorities for New Dikes Policy
Do I have to pay diking or drainage district taxes if my property is within the district?

Yes. Under the Drainage, Ditch and Dike Act, RSBC 1996, c. 103, properties within a diking or drainage district are subject to annual levies (taxes or tolls) set by the district's board of trustees to fund maintenance, operations, and capital works. These levies are typically collected on the municipal property tax notice and are separate from general municipal taxes. Verify the current levy amount and assessment method for your property with the district office or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — Diking Authorities for New Dikes Policy
Who manages a BC diking or drainage district?

Each diking or drainage district is governed by an elected or appointed board of trustees as provided by the Drainage, Ditch and Dike Act, RSBC 1996, c. 103, and the district's Letters Patent. The trustees are responsible for establishing budgets, setting levies, maintaining infrastructure, and making operational decisions. Verify the current trustees and meeting schedules for a specific district with the district office or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — Diking Authorities for New Dikes Policy
Does a diking or drainage district affect my property title or land use in BC?

Being within a diking or drainage district does not typically restrict land use or appear as a charge on title under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, but it does impose an ongoing statutory obligation to pay annual district levies. Flood protection works may also affect floodplain designations or zoning under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Verify any land use, title, or zoning impacts with a BC lawyer, notary, or local government before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — Diking Authorities for New Dikes Policy
Can a property be removed from a diking or drainage district in BC?

Removal of a property from a diking or drainage district generally requires an amendment to the district's Letters Patent by the Minister under the Drainage, Ditch and Dike Act, RSBC 1996, c. 103, which typically occurs only if the property no longer benefits from the works or if district boundaries are redrawn. Individual exemptions are rare and discretionary. Verify the process and eligibility for boundary changes with a BC lawyer, notary, or the district office before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — Diking Authorities for New Dikes Policy
What happens if I don't pay my diking or drainage district levy in BC?

Unpaid diking or drainage district levies are treated like unpaid property taxes under the Drainage, Ditch and Dike Act, RSBC 1996, c. 103, and may result in penalties, interest, and ultimately a tax sale of the property by the local government or district. The levy is a statutory charge that takes priority over most private liens. Verify current collection and enforcement procedures with the district office, local government, or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — Diking Authorities for New Dikes Policy
Are diking and drainage districts the same as improvement districts in BC?

No. Diking and drainage districts are governed by the Drainage, Ditch and Dike Act, RSBC 1996, c. 103, and focus exclusively on flood protection and drainage. Improvement districts, governed by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, may provide a broader range of services such as water supply, fire protection, or recreation. Verify the legal structure and powers of a specific district with a BC lawyer, notary, or the district office before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — Diking Authorities for New Dikes Policy
Do I need to disclose a diking or drainage district levy when selling property in BC?

Yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and RESA Rules administered by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), licensees must disclose all material latent defects and financial obligations, including diking or drainage district levies, to prospective buyers. Sellers should include district levy amounts and contact information in the Property Disclosure Statement or similar documentation. Verify disclosure obligations with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — Diking Authorities for New Dikes Policy
Where can I find information about a specific diking or drainage district in BC?

Contact the district office directly (many are listed on local government websites or the BC Ministry of Water, Land and Resource Stewardship website as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), review the district's Letters Patent and annual budgets, or request a title search and property tax assessment from your municipality. The BC government's diking safety program and floodplain mapping resources are also available at www.gov.bc.ca. Verify current contact details and records with a BC lawyer, notary, or the local government before acting.
