Property Types

Guest house / carriage house

A guest house or carriage house is a secondary detached dwelling unit located on the same parcel of land as a principal residence. Local governments regulate whether such structures are permitted and under what conditions, including setbacks, height limits, and maximum floor area, through zoning bylaws enacted under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. BC Bill 44 (Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023) introduced changes affecting secondary suite and small-scale multi-unit housing permissions across many BC municipalities — verify current details with a BC lawyer or licensed tax professional. If rented to a tenant, the unit may be subject to the Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78. Rental income may have tax implications; verify current details with a licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a guest house, carriage house, and a laneway house in BC?

All three are secondary dwellings on a lot, but terminology and zoning rules vary by municipality under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. A guest house or carriage house is typically detached and may be above a garage, while a laneway house usually has access from a rear lane. Verify the specific definitions, zoning permissions, and use restrictions with your local municipal planning or development department and a BC lawyer or notary before proceeding.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can I rent out a guest house or carriage house on my BC property?

Rental is permitted only if local zoning bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, allow it and the structure complies with building codes. If rented, the Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78, governs the landlord-tenant relationship, except if the unit shares a kitchen or bathroom with the owner or the owner's immediate family. Verify current municipal bylaws and consult a BC lawyer or notary before renting.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does a guest house or carriage house count as a separate property for BC property transfer tax (PTT)?

No. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, PTT is assessed on the entire property (lot) at the time of transfer, not on individual structures. A guest house or carriage house on the same legal parcel as the principal residence is included in the single PTT calculation. Verify with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance if you are subdividing or stratifying the lot.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can a guest house or carriage house qualify for the BC First-Time Home Buyer PTT exemption?

The full First-Time Home Buyer Exemption under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, applies to properties up to $835,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), and partial exemptions up to $860,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The exemption applies to the entire property; a guest house or carriage house on the same parcel is included if all statutory conditions (citizenship, residency, no prior principal residence ownership) are met. Verify eligibility and current thresholds with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before acting.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Is a guest house or carriage house subject to the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

Under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, the tax applies to residential properties in designated taxable regions if they are not the owner's principal residence or exempt (e.g., long-term rental). A guest house or carriage house on the same parcel as your principal residence may be exempt if the entire property is your principal residence, or may qualify for a rental exemption if rented long-term. Verify your specific circumstances and current exemptions with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before the annual declaration deadline.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Do I need municipal approval to build a guest house or carriage house in BC?

Yes. All construction requires compliance with local zoning bylaws and a building permit under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, and municipal bylaws. Each municipality sets rules on lot size, setbacks, height, floor area, parking, and whether secondary dwellings are permitted. Verify requirements with your municipal planning department and consult a BC lawyer or notary before starting construction.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can a guest house or carriage house be mortgaged separately from the main house in BC?

Typically no, unless the secondary dwelling is on a separate legal parcel created by subdivision or strata conversion under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Lenders mortgage the entire property (lot and all structures) as a single security. Verify financing options and legal structure with a BC lawyer, notary, and your mortgage lender before proceeding.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does building a guest house or carriage house affect my BC Home Owner Grant?

The BC Home Owner Grant under the Home Owner Grant Act applies to your principal residence. If the guest house or carriage house is on the same parcel and you occupy the main dwelling as your principal residence, the grant typically continues. If you rent the main house and move into the secondary dwelling, or rent both, eligibility may change. Verify your specific situation with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before your annual property tax filing.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
What is the BC Home Flipping Tax, and does it apply to a property with a guest house or carriage house?

The Home Flipping Tax Act, SBC 2024 (effective January 1, 2025 — verify current), imposes income attribution on profits from residential property disposals within a specified holding period. The tax applies to the entire property (lot and all structures), including any guest house or carriage house, unless an exemption applies (e.g., death, separation, disability, involuntary disposition). Verify current exemptions, holding-period thresholds, and reporting obligations with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before selling.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Are there BC privacy laws I must follow if I rent a guest house or carriage house?

Yes. The Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), SBC 2003, c. 63, governs how landlords collect, use, and disclose tenants' personal information (e.g., rental applications, credit checks, references). Landlords must obtain consent, use information only for stated purposes, and safeguard it. Verify compliance obligations and best practices with a BC lawyer, notary, or the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for BC before collecting tenant information.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
