Insurance

Liability Coverage

Personal liability coverage on a home insurance policy provides protection if someone is injured on the insured property or the homeowner accidentally damages someone else's property. Standard limits are $1M; $2M is recommended; $5M for pools, dogs, or rental units. Liability coverage follows the homeowner off-property too (e.g. accidentally injuring someone on a hike). Confirming liability limits and considering an umbrella policy is common practice for high-net-worth situations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is liability coverage on a BC home insurance policy and what does it protect against?

Liability coverage on a BC home insurance policy protects the homeowner if someone is injured on their property or if they accidentally damage someone else's property. It can also extend off-property — for example, if the homeowner accidentally injures someone during a recreational activity like hiking. This coverage is arranged privately through insurers and is not mandated by BC statute, but it is a standard component of most residential policies in British Columbia.

What liability coverage limit is typically recommended for homeowners in British Columbia?

Standard home insurance policies in BC commonly include a $1,000,000 personal liability limit, though a $2,000,000 limit is broadly recommended by insurance professionals for greater protection. Homeowners with elevated risk factors — such as a swimming pool, a dog, or a rental unit on the property — are often advised to consider a $5,000,000 limit or an umbrella policy. Specific limit recommendations should be discussed with a licensed insurance broker, as individual circumstances vary.

Does personal liability coverage on a BC home insurance policy protect the homeowner away from their property?

Yes, personal liability coverage typically follows the insured homeowner off their property, meaning incidents that occur elsewhere — such as accidentally injuring another person during an outdoor activity — may still be covered. This off-property protection is a common feature of standard residential policies in British Columbia, though exact scope depends on the specific policy wording. Homeowners should review their policy documents carefully and consult their insurer or broker to confirm the extent of off-property coverage.

Why might a BC homeowner with a swimming pool, dog, or rental unit need higher liability limits?

Certain property features and activities — including swimming pools, dogs, and rental units — are considered higher-risk by insurers because they statistically increase the likelihood of injury or property damage claims. In British Columbia, a homeowner who rents out a unit may also face additional obligations under tenancy law, and an injury to a tenant or their guest could result in a significant liability claim. Insurers and brokers in BC commonly recommend $5,000,000 in personal liability coverage or an umbrella policy for homeowners in these situations.

What is an umbrella liability policy and when is it relevant for BC homeowners?

An umbrella policy is a supplemental insurance product that provides an additional layer of liability coverage above and beyond the limits of a standard home insurance policy. In British Columbia, umbrella policies are commonly considered by high-net-worth homeowners, those with rental properties, or anyone whose personal circumstances create elevated liability exposure. The umbrella policy kicks in once the underlying home insurance liability limit is exhausted, offering broader financial protection.

How does liability coverage relate to strata lot ownership in British Columbia?

In British Columbia, strata lot owners should be aware that a strata corporation's master insurance policy — required under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) — covers the strata corporation itself and common property, but does not typically cover the personal liability of individual owners within their strata lot. Each strata lot owner should maintain their own home insurance policy with adequate personal liability coverage to protect against claims arising from incidents within their unit or attributable to them. Owners can review the strata corporation's insurance details by requesting information under the Strata Property Act.

Is a BC licensee required to confirm a client's liability coverage limits under RESA or BCFSA rules?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and the rules administered by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), real estate licensees in BC are not specifically mandated to verify or advise on a client's personal liability insurance limits, as insurance advice falls outside the scope of a licensee's licensed activities. However, a licensee may appropriately inform a buyer or seller of the general importance of reviewing their liability coverage as part of responsible homeownership. Any detailed insurance guidance should be referred to a licensed insurance professional.

If a guest is injured on a BC homeowner's property, what role does personal liability coverage play?

If a guest sustains an injury on a BC homeowner's property — for example, slipping on an icy walkway — the homeowner's personal liability coverage can respond to cover legal defence costs and any damages awarded, up to the policy limit. Tort liability in BC is governed by common law and relevant provincial statutes, and liability claims can be significant, making adequate coverage limits important. Homeowners should ensure their policy limits are sufficient to cover potential legal costs and damage awards in their specific circumstances.

Does liability coverage apply to BC homeowners who rent out a suite or secondary dwelling on their property?

BC homeowners who rent out a suite or secondary dwelling should confirm with their insurer that their personal liability coverage extends to rental activities, as some standard home insurance policies may exclude or limit coverage when a portion of the property is rented out. Failing to disclose rental activity to an insurer could result in a denied claim. Given the additional liability exposure that comes with tenants — including the potential for tenant or visitor injury — higher liability limits or a separate landlord policy are commonly recommended in British Columbia.

What is the relationship between liability coverage limits and high-net-worth homeowners in BC real estate transactions?

For high-net-worth individuals purchasing property in British Columbia, confirming and potentially increasing personal liability coverage limits is considered sound risk management practice, as their personal assets may be more exposed in the event of a significant liability judgment. Insurance professionals in BC commonly recommend umbrella policies for such buyers, which provide coverage above standard home insurance limits. While BCFSA-licensed real estate licensees may flag this as a general consideration, detailed advice on appropriate coverage levels should come from a licensed insurance broker.
