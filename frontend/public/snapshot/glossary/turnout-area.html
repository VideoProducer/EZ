Property Types

Turnout area

General term for any fenced enclosure where horses are allowed to move freely outside of stalls, including paddocks, pens, and small pastures.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a turnout area in the context of BC horse property real estate listings?

In BC real estate, a turnout area is a general term for any fenced enclosure — such as a paddock, pen, or small pasture — where horses can move freely outside of their stalls. When a listing describes a turnout area, it typically signals that the property is configured for equestrian use and has dedicated outdoor space for horses to exercise. Buyers should verify the size, fencing condition, and drainage of any turnout area during their due diligence.

Does a property with a turnout area in BC likely fall within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR)?

Many BC properties with turnout areas are located within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), as horse facilities and pastures are common forms of farm use. The ALR is administered by the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) under the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36), which governs what uses are permitted on ALR land. Buyers should confirm the property's ALR status directly with the ALC and verify that any existing or planned turnout area complies with permitted farm use requirements.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can a BC buyer construct a new or expanded turnout area on ALR land without ALC approval?

Under the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36), farm use — including infrastructure that supports horse keeping such as turnout areas — is generally permitted on ALR land without a non-farm use application, provided it meets the ALC's definition of farm use. However, the specific structures, grading, or drainage works associated with a turnout area may also require local government permits. Buyers and owners should consult both the ALC and their local municipality or regional district to confirm what approvals are needed.

How should a BC real estate licensee disclose the condition of a turnout area when listing a horse property?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and BCFSA rules, a licensee has a duty to disclose all known material latent defects affecting a property, which could include structural fencing failures, drainage problems, or soil contamination within a turnout area. Licensees must not misrepresent the condition or size of a turnout area in marketing materials. Sellers and their licensees should ensure any known issues with the turnout area are accurately communicated to prospective buyers.

Is a turnout area considered part of the farm operation for BC farm classification and property tax purposes?

In BC, properties used for farming — including equestrian operations where horses are kept or bred commercially — may qualify for farm classification under BC Assessment's framework, which can significantly reduce property taxes. A turnout area used as part of an active horse operation could support a farm classification application, but eligibility depends on meeting gross farm income thresholds set by the province. Owners should consult BC Assessment directly for current farm classification requirements and thresholds.

What Property Transfer Tax (PTT) rules apply when a BC buyer purchases a horse property that includes a turnout area?

The BC Property Transfer Tax Act applies to purchases of horse properties with turnout areas in the same way it applies to other real property transfers, using the standard tiered rates of 1% on the first portion of fair market value, 2% on the next portion, 3% on the portion above a higher threshold, and an additional 2% on the residential value exceeding $3,000,000. Farm land that qualifies under the BC Farm Land Exemption may be eligible for a PTT exemption on the farm portion; buyers should consult the current BC Ministry of Finance guidance to determine whether any exemption applies. The presence of a turnout area alone does not trigger or eliminate any specific PTT exemption.

Can a strata lot in BC legally include or share a turnout area for horses?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), a strata corporation may designate outdoor areas as common property or limited common property, but whether horses and turnout areas are permitted depends entirely on the strata corporation's bylaws, which are binding on all owners. Many strata corporations in BC restrict or prohibit livestock, including horses, through their bylaws. Buyers interested in keeping horses should carefully review the strata's bylaws and any existing usage restrictions before purchasing.

What due diligence should a BC buyer conduct regarding a turnout area before removing subject conditions?

Before removing subjects, a BC buyer should verify the turnout area's fencing integrity, soil drainage, size relative to the number of horses intended, and compliance with any local zoning or ALR requirements. Buyers should also confirm whether the turnout area is included in the legal lot boundaries by reviewing the land title and survey plan through the BC Land Title and Survey Authority. If the property is in the ALR, confirming that the turnout area constitutes permitted farm use under the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36) is an important step.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does BC's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) have any relevance when a licensee markets a property with a turnout area to prospective buyers?

BC's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) requires licensees to collect, use, and disclose the personal information of both sellers and prospective buyers only for purposes that a reasonable person would consider appropriate in the circumstances. When marketing a horse property with a turnout area — for example, by sending property details or promotional materials to a targeted list of equestrian buyers — licensees must ensure they have appropriate consent for using individuals' contact information. Any unsolicited commercial electronic messages sent to promote such listings must also comply with Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL).

If a BC horse property with a turnout area is inherited through an estate, what rules govern the transfer?

When a BC horse property — including any turnout areas — is transferred through an estate, the process is governed by the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA) of BC, which sets out the rules for intestate succession, probate, and the duties of an executor or administrator. The executor is responsible for managing and ultimately transferring the property in accordance with the will or, if there is no will, the intestacy provisions of WESA. Any PTT implications on a transfer through an estate should be reviewed against the BC Property Transfer Tax Act, as certain transfers to related individuals may qualify for exemptions.
