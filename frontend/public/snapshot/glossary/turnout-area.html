Property Types

Turnout area

A turnout area is a general industry term for any fenced enclosure on an equestrian or agricultural property where horses are permitted to move freely outside of stalls. Common examples include paddocks, pens, and small pastures. Where the land falls within the Agricultural Land Reserve, its use and configuration may be subject to oversight under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36; verify current requirements with a BC lawyer or licensed professional. Local zoning bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, may also govern permitted structures and fencing standards — verify current details with a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does British Columbia have specific provincial legislation that defines or regulates 'turnout areas' for horses on real property?

British Columbia does not have a single provincial statute that comprehensively defines or regulates horse turnout areas as a standalone term. Zoning and permissible use of land for agricultural purposes, including horse facilities, are generally governed by municipal bylaws under the Local Government Act (RSBC 2015, c. 1) and, where applicable, the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, if the property is within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR). Verify current zoning requirements and ALR restrictions with the local government and the Agricultural Land Commission, and consult a BC lawyer or notary for land-use compliance.

If a property listing advertises a 'turnout area,' does that automatically mean the land is zoned for agricultural or equestrian use in BC?

No. The presence of a fenced enclosure described as a turnout area does not automatically confirm that the property is zoned for agricultural or equestrian use. Zoning is determined by the local government under the Local Government Act (RSBC 2015, c. 1), and permitted uses vary by jurisdiction and zone. Verify the property's zoning designation, permitted uses, and any applicable Agricultural Land Reserve restrictions with the municipality and the Agricultural Land Commission before purchase or use.

Are there BC Property Transfer Tax exemptions specifically for properties with turnout areas or equestrian facilities?

The Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, does not provide a specific exemption based solely on the presence of turnout areas or equestrian facilities. General exemptions—such as the First-Time Home Buyer Exemption (up to $835,000 as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) or the farm exemption for qualifying agricultural land and buildings—may apply if all statutory conditions are met. Verify eligibility for any farm or agricultural exemption with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before relying on it.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
If I buy a rural property in BC with a turnout area, am I eligible for the BC Home Owner Grant?

Eligibility for the BC Home Owner Grant (under the Home Owner Grant Act) depends on whether the property is your principal residence, meets the definition of a 'residence' under the Act, and satisfies other conditions such as citizenship or permanent residency. The presence of a turnout area or equestrian facility does not disqualify you, but if the property is classified as farm or used primarily for commercial agricultural purposes, different rules or exemptions may apply. Verify current eligibility criteria and property classification with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC notary or lawyer.

Does the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax apply to properties with turnout areas used for horses?

The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, applies to residential properties in designated taxable regions, and exemptions depend on use, ownership, and occupancy—not on the presence of equestrian facilities. If the property is classified as residential and located in a taxable region, the tax may apply unless a statutory exemption (e.g., principal residence, long-term rental) is met. Verify your property's classification, taxable region status, and exemption eligibility with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Are there special disclosure requirements under BC real estate licensing law when selling a property with a turnout area?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the BCFSA Rules, licensees must disclose all known material latent defects and comply with duties of disclosure, confidentiality, and reasonable care. While 'turnout area' itself is not a regulated term, material facts about the property—such as zoning restrictions, ALR status, structural issues with fencing or shelters, or non-compliance with local bylaws—must be disclosed if known. Verify disclosure obligations for your specific transaction with your licensed real estate professional, BC lawyer, or notary.

If my property is in the BC Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), can I build or expand a turnout area without approval?

Land within the Agricultural Land Reserve is subject to the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, and most non-farm uses or site alterations require approval from the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC). Construction or expansion of fencing, shelters, or other improvements in a turnout area may require ALC approval if it is considered a non-farm use or involves soil removal, fill placement, or subdivision. Verify current ALC requirements and apply for any necessary approvals before beginning work; consult a BC lawyer, notary, or the ALC directly.

Does the federal Foreign Buyer Ban apply to agricultural properties with turnout areas in BC?

The Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10 (currently extended through January 1, 2027 — verify current), prohibits certain non-Canadians from purchasing residential property in Canada, subject to exceptions. The Act's application to agricultural or rural properties with equestrian facilities depends on whether the property meets the definition of 'residential property' under the federal Act and whether any exceptions apply. Verify the property's classification and your eligibility under the federal Act with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before purchase.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can a strata corporation in BC restrict or prohibit the use of common property as a turnout area for horses?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation may pass bylaws governing the use and enjoyment of common property, strata lots, and limited common property, provided the bylaws are not contrary to the Act or human rights legislation. Unless the strata's bylaws or use restrictions explicitly permit equestrian use of common property, the strata corporation may prohibit it. Verify the current registered bylaws and rules, and consult a BC lawyer or notary if you wish to propose or challenge a bylaw amendment.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
If I lease rural property in BC for equestrian use including a turnout area, does the BC Residential Tenancy Act apply?

The Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78 (RTA), applies to residential tenancy agreements; it generally does not apply to agreements for agricultural land or commercial equestrian facilities unless the agreement is primarily for residential occupation. If the lease is solely or primarily for agricultural, equestrian, or commercial use (not as a dwelling), the RTA likely does not apply, and the tenancy is governed by common law and the terms of the lease. Verify the nature of your tenancy agreement and applicable law with a BC lawyer or notary before entering into or enforcing the lease.
