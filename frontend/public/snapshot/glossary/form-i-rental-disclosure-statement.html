Strata Documents

Form I — Rental Disclosure Statement

Form I is required when a strata lot has previously been rented and is being sold. The seller must disclose the rental history. This matters because some older strata buildings have rental restriction bylaws that were grandfathered — a unit that was already rented before the bylaw was passed may retain the right to be rented. Understanding a unit's rental history and rental rights is important for investor buyers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Form I — Rental Disclosure Statement in British Columbia?

Form I is a prescribed disclosure form under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and its Regulation that a seller must provide to a buyer when a strata lot has previously been rented. The form discloses the rental history of the unit, including whether the lot was rented before any rental restriction bylaw came into effect. This information is critical because a unit that was lawfully rented before a rental restriction bylaw was passed may retain a grandfathered right to continue being rented.

Why does rental history matter in a BC strata building with rental restriction bylaws?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), a strata corporation may pass bylaws restricting or prohibiting rentals, but those bylaws cannot retroactively remove an existing owner's right to rent if the unit was already being rented at the time the bylaw was adopted. A strata lot that was rented before the restriction bylaw was passed may retain a grandfathered rental entitlement that runs with that specific owner, not necessarily with the unit itself. Form I helps a buyer understand whether any such grandfathered rental right exists and the circumstances under which it was established.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Who is responsible for completing and delivering Form I in a BC strata sale?

The seller of the strata lot is responsible for completing and delivering Form I — Rental Disclosure Statement to the buyer, as required under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and its Regulation. The seller must provide the form before the buyer enters into a contract to purchase the strata lot. A real estate licensee acting for the seller has a duty under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and BCFSA Rules to ensure their client fulfils required disclosure obligations.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
When must Form I be delivered to the buyer — before or after signing the contract of purchase and sale?

Form I must be delivered to the buyer before the buyer enters into the contract of purchase and sale for the strata lot, as set out in the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) Regulation. Delivering the form after the contract is signed does not satisfy the statutory requirement. Buyers who do not receive Form I at the correct time should seek guidance on their rights under the Strata Property Act before proceeding.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Does a grandfathered rental right transfer automatically to a new buyer when a strata lot is sold in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), grandfathered rental rights that arose because an owner was renting before a restriction bylaw was passed are generally personal to the owner and do not automatically transfer to a subsequent buyer. Once the strata lot is sold, the new owner typically becomes subject to the strata corporation's current rental restriction bylaw. This is one of the key reasons Form I is important — it allows a buyer to understand the rental history without assuming the rental right will continue after purchase.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
What information must a seller disclose on Form I in British Columbia?

Form I requires the seller to disclose whether the strata lot has been rented, the period during which it was rented, and whether it was being rented at the time any applicable rental restriction bylaw was adopted by the strata corporation, as prescribed under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) Regulation. The form is designed to give the buyer a factual picture of the lot's rental history so they can assess any grandfathered entitlements and future rental viability. Sellers must complete the form honestly and accurately, as misrepresentation can expose them to legal liability.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Is Form I the same as Form B — Information Certificate in a BC strata transaction?

No — Form I and Form B are separate prescribed forms under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) Regulation and serve different purposes. Form B — Information Certificate is issued by the strata corporation and provides information about the strata lot and corporation, including bylaw details, contingency reserve fund balances, and pending special levies. Form I — Rental Disclosure Statement is completed by the seller and specifically addresses the rental history of the strata lot, making it a seller's disclosure document rather than a strata corporation document.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
What are the consequences for a seller who fails to provide Form I before the contract is signed in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), if a seller fails to provide Form I to the buyer before the contract of purchase and sale is entered into, the buyer may have the right to rescind the contract within a prescribed period after receiving the form. This right of rescission is intended to protect buyers from being bound to a contract without adequate rental disclosure information. Sellers and their licensees should ensure Form I is delivered at the correct time to avoid triggering rescission rights or other legal consequences.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
How does Form I affect an investor buyer evaluating whether a BC strata lot can be rented out?

Form I gives an investor buyer factual information about the strata lot's rental history, which is essential context for understanding the building's rental bylaw environment and any potential grandfathered entitlements, as contemplated by the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43). However, because grandfathered rental rights are generally personal to the existing owner and do not pass to a new buyer, an investor should also obtain and review the strata corporation's current bylaws — typically through Form B — before making purchasing decisions. Understanding the interplay between Form I and the current bylaws helps an investor accurately assess whether the unit can lawfully be rented after the sale closes.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Do real estate licensees in BC have any specific obligations regarding Form I under RESA or BCFSA Rules?

Real estate licensees in BC are governed by the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and the rules and requirements administered by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), which merged with the former Real Estate Council of BC on August 1, 2021. While Form I is a seller's statutory obligation under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), a licensee acting for the seller has a professional duty to inform their client of the requirement and facilitate timely delivery to the buyer before the contract is signed. A licensee acting for the buyer should advise their client to review Form I carefully and, where relevant, seek legal advice on the implications of the disclosed rental history.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
