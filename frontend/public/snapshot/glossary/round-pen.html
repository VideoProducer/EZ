Property Types

Round pen

A circular fenced area used for horse training, groundwork, conditioning, and controlled movement exercises.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a round pen on a BC property need to be disclosed to a buyer during a real estate transaction?

Yes, a round pen is a material physical feature of the property that a licensee must disclose if it could reasonably affect a buyer's decision. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and BCFSA conduct standards, licensees are obligated to disclose known material latent defects and relevant property characteristics. Sellers should also inform their licensee of any structural or permitting concerns related to the round pen's construction.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Is a round pen considered a farm building or structure under BC's Agricultural Land Reserve rules?

A round pen used for horse training may qualify as a farm structure if the property is operated as a farm within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), as horse-related activities can constitute a farm use under the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36). Whether a specific round pen qualifies depends on the nature and scale of the equestrian operation and how the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC) classifies the use. Property owners in the ALR should confirm the classification directly with the ALC before constructing or marketing the structure.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Could a round pen affect the assessed value of a BC rural property for Property Transfer Tax purposes?

A round pen that is a permanent, fixed structure may be considered part of the improvements on a property and could influence the assessed value used to calculate Property Transfer Tax under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act. PTT is calculated at 1% on the first portion of the fair market value, 2% on the next portion, and 3% and additional amounts on higher values — consult the current BC Ministry of Finance guidance for exact thresholds. Buyers should be aware that permanent equestrian infrastructure, including a round pen, may factor into the taxable fair market value.

Can a round pen be located on ALR land in BC, and does its construction require ALC approval?

Constructing a round pen on ALR land in BC is generally permitted if it supports an on-farm equestrian or agricultural use, since the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36) allows farm buildings and structures that are ancillary to farm use without requiring a non-farm-use application. However, if the round pen is intended to support a commercial riding school or other use that may not qualify as farm use, ALC approval may be required. Landowners should contact the ALC directly to confirm whether their specific intended use complies with current ALR regulations.

Would a round pen on a strata lot in BC require strata corporation approval before installation?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), any alteration to a strata lot or common property typically requires approval from the strata corporation in accordance with the strata's bylaws. A round pen on a strata lot would almost certainly require written strata approval, as it constitutes a significant physical alteration, and many strata bylaws restrict structures of this nature. Prospective buyers and owners should review the strata's bylaws and obtain any required approvals before installing a round pen.

How should a BC real estate licensee describe a round pen in a property listing to avoid misrepresentation?

Under RESA and BCFSA conduct standards, a licensee must ensure that all statements in a listing are accurate and not misleading, which means the round pen's size, material, condition, and any relevant permit status should be described truthfully. Overstating the pen's condition or implying it has been permitted when it has not could constitute misrepresentation under RESA. Licensees should verify material facts about the structure with the seller and confirm those representations before publication.

Does a round pen require a building permit in BC, and how does this affect a property sale?

Building permit requirements for a round pen in BC are governed by local municipal or regional district bylaws and, in some cases, the BC Building Code, depending on the structure's size, permanence, and footing type. A round pen without a required permit could create complications during a property sale, as buyers and licensees may identify it as an unpermitted structure during due diligence. Sellers should check with their local authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) to determine whether their round pen required a permit and whether any retroactive approvals are needed.

If a BC property with a round pen is inherited through an estate, are there any special considerations for the sale?

When a property with a round pen is transferred through an estate in BC, the executor or personal representative has authority to sell the property under the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA). The round pen, as part of the property's improvements, would pass as part of the estate asset, and the executor must ensure accurate disclosure of its condition and permit status in any listing. Beneficiaries and executors should work with a licensee who is familiar with estate sales under RESA to ensure compliance with all disclosure obligations.

Can a BC real estate licensee collect personal information from buyers specifically interested in equestrian properties with round pens?

Yes, a licensee may collect personal information from buyers, including their interest in equestrian features like a round pen, but must do so in compliance with the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) of BC. This means collecting only the information reasonably necessary for the purpose, obtaining consent, and safeguarding that data appropriately. If the licensee wishes to send promotional electronic messages about equestrian listings to those buyers, they must also comply with Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) consent requirements.

How is a round pen typically addressed in a BC real estate contract of purchase and sale — is it included or excluded?

In BC, a round pen that is permanently affixed to the land is generally treated as a fixture and would be included in a sale unless specifically excluded in the Contract of Purchase and Sale. If the round pen is a portable or modular structure, it may be considered a chattel and should be explicitly listed as included or excluded to avoid disputes. Licensees acting for either party should ensure the contract clearly addresses the round pen's status to meet their obligations under RESA and avoid misunderstandings at completion.
