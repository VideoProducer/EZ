Property Types

Round pen

A round pen is a circular, fully enclosed fenced area on equestrian or agricultural properties, used for horse training, groundwork, conditioning, and controlled movement exercises. It is a common feature on rural properties, including those located within the Agricultural Land Reserve under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36. Its presence may affect property classification, permitted uses, and assessed value; verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a round pen considered a permitted structure on land in the BC Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR)?

Under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, and ALC regulations, structures that support farm use—such as round pens for horse training and conditioning—are generally permitted on ALR land without requiring ALC approval, provided they are used for bona fide agricultural purposes. The definition and approval requirements can vary by regional panel and specific parcel conditions; verify current ALC Use Regulation and local panel policies with a BC lawyer or directly with the Agricultural Land Commission before construction.

Do I need a building permit from my BC municipality to construct a round pen?

Permit requirements are determined by the local government under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, and municipal bylaws. Many BC municipalities do not require a building permit for open agricultural fencing structures like round pens, but some impose setback, height, or lot-coverage rules. Verify current bylaws and permit requirements with your municipal planning or building department before beginning construction.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does adding a round pen to my BC residential property affect my property assessment or taxes?

BC Assessment values land and improvements annually; a round pen may be classified as a farm improvement or ancillary structure depending on property class and use. If your property is classified as residential (Class 1) under the BC Assessment Act, the round pen's contributory value is typically minimal; if classified as farm (Class 9), it supports agricultural use. Verify the impact on your specific assessment with BC Assessment or a BC property tax professional.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can I claim the BC Home Owner Grant if my property includes a round pen used for personal horse training?

Eligibility for the BC Home Owner Grant (administered under the Home Owner Grant Act) depends on the property being your principal residence and classified as Class 1 (residential) as of December 31 of the previous year (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). A round pen for personal use does not disqualify you, but if the property is classified as farm (Class 9) or mixed-use, different grant rules or rates may apply. Verify your classification and grant eligibility with BC Assessment or a BC notary before applying.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
If I sell my BC property with a round pen, does the round pen affect the Property Transfer Tax (PTT) calculation?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, PTT is calculated on the fair market value of the entire property transfer, including land and all improvements. A round pen is an improvement that contributes to total market value; BC Assessment and appraisers consider all structures when determining fair market value. The PTT rate structure and any exemptions (e.g., first-time home buyer) apply to the total consideration (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current rates and exemptions with a BC lawyer or notary).

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Do zoning bylaws in BC regulate the size or location of a round pen on rural residential land?

Yes. Local governments exercise zoning authority under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Bylaws commonly regulate setbacks from property lines, watercourses, and roadways, as well as maximum height and lot coverage for accessory structures. Round pens may be subject to these rules even if no building permit is required. Verify current zoning bylaw provisions—including setbacks, maximum dimensions, and permitted accessory uses—with your municipal planning department before construction.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can I build a round pen on strata common property or limited common property in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, alterations to common property or limited common property generally require a resolution of the strata corporation (often a 3/4 vote) and compliance with the strata's bylaws and rules. A round pen would typically constitute a significant alteration. Review your strata's registered bylaws, rules, and Form B (strata plan), and obtain strata council or owner approval and any required municipal permits before proceeding; verify the process with a BC lawyer or notary familiar with strata law.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
If I lease farmland in BC and install a round pen, who owns the round pen at the end of the lease?

Ownership of improvements made by a tenant is generally governed by the lease agreement and common-law principles of fixtures. In BC, a round pen may be a fixture (attached to land) or chattel (removable) depending on construction method and permanence; the lease should specify whether tenant improvements remain with the landlord or may be removed. Verify the terms of your agricultural lease and your rights regarding improvements with a BC lawyer before installation.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does the federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act affect purchasing BC land with a round pen for horse training?

The Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10 (currently in force and extended through January 1, 2027 — as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), prohibits non-Canadians from purchasing residential property but includes exemptions for certain agricultural and recreational land depending on size, zoning, and intended use. A property used primarily for horse training may fall outside the prohibition if it is zoned and classified as agricultural. Verify eligibility, zoning classification, and current exemptions with a BC real estate lawyer or notary before entering a purchase contract.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Are there liability considerations under BC law if someone is injured in my round pen?

BC's common law and the Occupiers Liability Act, RSBC 1996, c. 337, impose a duty on occupiers to take reasonable care that persons entering premises are reasonably safe. If you permit others to use your round pen—whether as guests, students, or renters—you owe a duty of care; liability insurance and waivers may be prudent risk-management tools but do not eliminate all legal duties. Verify current occupier liability standards, insurance requirements, and waiver enforceability with a BC lawyer and a licensed insurance broker before allowing third-party use.
