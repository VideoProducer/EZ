Knob and Tube Wiring

Knob and tube wiring is an early electrical system found in some BC homes built before approximately 1950. It uses ceramic knobs and tubes to route conductors and lacks a ground wire. Many home insurers treat active knob and tube as a material risk factor that can affect coverage availability or premiums; lender underwriting policies vary similarly. A licensed electrician should assess the system before insurance or financing decisions are made. Remediation or full replacement costs vary widely by property size and condition — verify current pricing with licensed contractors. Knob and tube is commonly noted in home-inspection reports on older properties. For insurance and lending implications specific to your transaction, verify current requirements with your insurer, lender, and a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is knob and tube wiring, and why does it matter in BC real estate transactions?

Knob and tube is an early electrical wiring system used in BC homes built before approximately 1950, characterized by ceramic knobs and tubes routing conductors with no ground wire. It matters in BC real estate transactions because most insurers will not write home insurance policies on active knob and tube without a remediation plan, and many lenders refuse to provide mortgages without it being removed. A home inspection by a licensed electrician is the standard step before insurance or lender underwriting decisions. Verify current insurer and lender requirements with a BC mortgage broker or insurance broker before acting.

Does BC law require sellers to disclose knob and tube wiring to buyers?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules, licensees must disclose known material latent defects; active knob and tube wiring is typically considered a material latent defect because it affects insurability, financing, and safety. The Property Condition Disclosure Statement (a form promulgated by the BC Real Estate Association, not a statutory form) commonly asks about electrical systems and updates. Verify disclosure obligations for your specific transaction with a BC lawyer, notary, or your managing broker before listing or making an offer.

Verify with: Technical Safety BC
Can I get home insurance in BC if my property has knob and tube wiring?

Most BC insurers will not issue a home insurance policy on a property with active knob and tube wiring unless the seller or buyer provides a written remediation plan from a licensed electrician, often with a timeline for full replacement. Some insurers may decline coverage outright. Verify current underwriting criteria and any available exceptions with a licensed BC insurance broker before waiving subjects on an offer, as the absence of insurance will typically void mortgage financing commitments.

Verify with: Technical Safety BC
How much does it typically cost to replace knob and tube wiring in a BC home?

Full replacement of knob and tube wiring in BC typically costs between $8,000 and $25,000 or more (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), depending on the size of the home, accessibility of walls and ceilings, and the scope of electrical panel upgrades required. These are market estimates; actual costs vary by contractor, municipality permit fees, and whether drywall repair or asbestos abatement is required. Obtain written quotes from at least two licensed BC electricians and confirm permit requirements with your local building department before budgeting.

Verify with: Technical Safety BC
Will my mortgage lender require knob and tube wiring to be removed before funding?

Many BC mortgage lenders — including those underwriting insured mortgages through Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) — require knob and tube wiring to be fully removed or deactivated before advancing funds, because the property cannot be insured. Some lenders may accept a holdback arrangement where a portion of the mortgage is held in trust until an electrician certifies completion and an Electrical Safety Certificate is issued. Verify your lender's specific policy and any holdback terms in writing with your BC mortgage broker or lender before removing subjects on an offer.

Verify with: Technical Safety BC
Does a BC home inspector have to identify knob and tube wiring in the inspection report?

While no BC statute mandates the content of a private home inspection report, knob and tube wiring is a standard line item in inspection reports on pre-1950 properties, and professional standards for home inspectors generally require identifying visible, accessible knob and tube. Buyers should hire a licensed electrician for a separate electrical inspection if the general home inspector flags any knob and tube, as walls and ceilings may hide additional runs. Verify the scope of any home inspection and consider an electrical-specific inspection before removing subjects on your Contract of Purchase and Sale.

Verify with: Technical Safety BC
Can I remove knob and tube wiring myself, or do I need a licensed electrician in BC?

Under BC's safety regulatory framework, only a licensed electrician holding a valid BC Electrical Permit may perform electrical work, and all new wiring must be inspected and certified by Technical Safety BC (the provincial electrical safety authority). Do-it-yourself electrical work is illegal and voids home insurance; it also exposes the homeowner to civil liability and municipal bylaw enforcement. Always hire a licensed BC electrician and obtain the required permits from your municipality and an Electrical Safety Certificate from Technical Safety BC before closing or listing.

Verify with: Technical Safety BC
If I buy a BC home 'as is,' am I still responsible for replacing the knob and tube wiring?

Yes. An 'as is' clause in a BC Contract of Purchase and Sale (typically 'as is, where is') means the buyer accepts the property in its current condition and waives the right to claim against the seller for most defects, but it does not relieve the buyer of the practical obligation to obtain insurance and financing. If your lender or insurer requires knob and tube removal, you remain responsible for that cost post-closing. Verify the enforceability and scope of any 'as is' clause with a BC lawyer or notary, and confirm lender and insurer requirements in writing before removing subjects.

Verify with: Technical Safety BC
Does the BC Property Transfer Tax Act give any relief or exemption if I have to replace knob and tube wiring after closing?

No. The Property Transfer Tax Act (PTTA), RSBC 1996, c. 378, levies tax on the fair market value of the property at the time of registration; exemptions (First-Time Home Buyer, Newly Built Home) relate to buyer status and property use, not post-closing repair costs. Knob and tube replacement is a capital improvement that may reduce the effective purchase price in negotiation, but it does not trigger a refund or adjustment of Property Transfer Tax after the fact. Verify current PTTA exemptions and rates with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before closing.

Verify with: Technical Safety BC
Can a strata corporation in BC force an owner to remove knob and tube wiring from their unit?

Under the Strata Property Act (SPA), SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation may pass bylaws governing alterations and safety, and the council has a statutory duty to repair and maintain common property and ensure the safety of the strata. If knob and tube wiring in a unit poses a safety risk or affects the building's insurability, the strata may require the owner to remediate under a bylaw or issue a council resolution; enforceability depends on the bylaw's validity and reasonableness. Verify the strata's bylaws, minutes, and any notices with a BC lawyer or notary, and review the strata's insurance policy and any engineering reports before purchasing a strata unit with knob and tube wiring.
