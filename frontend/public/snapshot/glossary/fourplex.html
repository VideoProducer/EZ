Property Types

Fourplex

A fourplex is a single building with four separate dwelling units. BC's 2024 housing legislation requires most municipalities to allow up to four units on lots in areas served by transit. A fourplex can be on one title or stratified into four titles. CMHC offers special insured mortgage programs for owner-occupied 2–4 unit buildings with as little as 5–10% down, which makes fourplexes attractive for first-time investors.

Frequently Asked Questions

What BC legislation requires municipalities to allow fourplex construction on residential lots?

The BC government's 2024 housing legislation, specifically the amendments to the Local Government Act through Bill 44 (the Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023), requires most municipalities in BC to permit up to four dwelling units on lots in areas served by transit and in urban zones previously restricted to single-family use. This significantly expanded where fourplexes can be built as of right, removing the need for rezoning applications in qualifying areas. Property owners should confirm their specific lot's eligibility with the applicable municipality, as some exemptions and local conditions apply.

Can a fourplex in BC be stratified so each unit has its own title?

Yes, a fourplex can be stratified under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), converting each of the four dwelling units into a separate strata lot with its own title, while common property and common assets are shared among all strata lot owners through a strata corporation. Once stratified, the strata corporation is governed by the Strata Property Act and its Regulation, including requirements around bylaws, annual general meetings, depreciation reports, and contingency reserve funds. The stratification process requires a strata plan to be deposited with the Land Title Office.

What Property Transfer Tax applies when purchasing a fourplex in BC?

Under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act, PTT is calculated at 1% on the first $200,000 of the fair market value, 2% on the portion between $200,000 and $2,000,000, 3% on the portion between $2,000,000 and $3,000,000, and an additional 2% on the residential portion exceeding $3,000,000. If the fourplex qualifies as a newly built home and the purchase price falls within the applicable threshold under the Newly Built Home Exemption (currently up to $1,100,000 for a full exemption), a PTT exemption may be available. Consult the current BC Ministry of Finance guidance for exact eligibility conditions and thresholds applicable to multi-unit newly built properties.

Can a first-time home buyer claim a PTT exemption when purchasing a fourplex in BC?

The First-Time Home Buyers' Program under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act provides a full PTT exemption for eligible purchasers on properties up to $835,000, with a partial exemption available on properties valued between $835,000 and $860,000. However, the program requires the property to be used as the purchaser's principal residence, and a fourplex's eligibility may depend on how the property is classified and used. Consult the BC Ministry of Finance for current guidance on whether a multi-unit building qualifies under the program's residency and use requirements.

What CMHC mortgage insurance options are available for someone purchasing a fourplex in BC?

CMHC offers insured mortgage programs for owner-occupied residential properties with two to four units, which can include a fourplex where the purchaser occupies one unit, with down payment requirements as low as 5% for certain purchase price thresholds, though higher down payments may apply depending on the total purchase price. These programs make fourplexes more accessible to first-time investors by reducing the upfront capital required compared to conventional commercial financing. Consult CMHC directly or a licensed mortgage broker for current eligibility criteria, insurance premiums, and purchase price caps, as these figures are subject to change.

How does a BC real estate licensee's duty of disclosure apply when a client is buying or selling a fourplex?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and the rules administered by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), a licensee must disclose all known material latent defects and must act in accordance with their agency duties, whether representing a buyer, seller, or acting as a limited dual agent. For a fourplex, material facts could include issues with tenancies under the Residential Tenancy Act, zoning compliance, outstanding strata bylaw violations if stratified, or permit history. Licensees must also provide clients with the required disclosure forms as mandated by BCFSA.

If a stratified fourplex is sold in BC, what strata documents must typically be provided to a buyer?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), a buyer of a strata lot in a stratified fourplex is entitled to receive a Form B Information Certificate from the strata corporation, which discloses items such as strata fees, the amount in the contingency reserve fund, any outstanding levies, and registered bylaws. Upon completion, the seller must also provide a Form F Certificate of Payment confirming all strata fees and assessments have been paid. If the strata corporation is required to have a depreciation report, a current or properly waived report must also be disclosed.

Can a fourplex be built on Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) land in BC?

Land within the Agricultural Land Reserve is subject to the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36) and the regulations administered by the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC), which impose significant restrictions on non-farm use and subdivision. Constructing a fourplex on ALR land would likely constitute a non-farm use requiring ALC approval, and non-adhering residential use rules limit the number and size of residences permitted on ALR parcels. Property owners with land in the ALR should contact the ALC directly to determine what residential uses are permitted before proceeding with any fourplex development.

Are tenants in a fourplex protected under BC tenancy law if the owner decides to stratify or sell the building?

Existing tenants in a fourplex are protected under the Residential Tenancy Act, and landlords must comply with all prescribed notice requirements and grounds for tenancy termination, including any applicable provisions triggered by conversion to strata ownership or sale. The Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and related regulations also impose requirements when residential buildings are converted to strata, including notice obligations to tenants. Owners contemplating stratification or sale should review both statutes carefully to ensure full compliance with tenant rights.

Verify with: BC Government — Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing
How does BC's privacy legislation apply to a landlord or licensee collecting tenant information for a fourplex?

Under the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) of BC, landlords and real estate licensees are required to collect, use, and disclose tenants' personal information only for identified purposes, with appropriate consent, and must protect that information using reasonable security measures. This applies to rental applications, credit checks, and any other personal data gathered in connection with renting units in a fourplex. If a licensee sends commercial electronic messages to prospective tenants or buyers, Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) also applies and requires express or implied consent before sending such messages.
