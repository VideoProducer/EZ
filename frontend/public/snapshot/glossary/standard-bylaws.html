Strata

Standard Bylaws

Standard bylaws are the default rules governing a strata corporation's operations, as set out in the Schedule of Standard Bylaws to the Strata Property Act (SPA), SBC 1998, c. 43. They apply automatically to every strata corporation upon creation unless subsequently amended or replaced. A strata corporation may alter its standard bylaws by passing the required resolution type specified under the SPA and filing the amendment at the Land Title Office under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Until such amendments are registered, the standard bylaws remain in full legal effect and are enforceable against owners, tenants, and occupants. Verify current bylaw requirements and amendment procedures with a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are Standard Bylaws in British Columbia?

Standard Bylaws are the default bylaws set out in the Schedule to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, that automatically apply to every strata corporation in BC. They govern matters such as the use and enjoyment of strata lots and common property, rentals, and pets unless the strata corporation amends or replaces them by the required resolution and registers the changes at the Land Title Office. Verify the current Schedule content and any amendments with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Do Standard Bylaws apply automatically to all strata corporations in BC?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, the Standard Bylaws in the Schedule apply to a strata corporation from the date it is created unless the developer or the strata corporation amends or replaces them by resolution and registers the amended or replacement bylaws at the Land Title Office. Until registration of a change, the Standard Bylaws remain in force.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
How can a strata corporation amend or replace the Standard Bylaws?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation may amend or repeal a bylaw, or create a new bylaw, by a resolution passed by a three-quarter vote at an annual or special general meeting (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current voting thresholds). The amended or new bylaws must then be filed in the Land Title Office to take effect. Verify current filing requirements and procedures with a BC lawyer or notary before proceeding.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Where can I find the full text of BC's Standard Bylaws?

The full text of the Standard Bylaws is set out in the Schedule to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, which is published on the BC Laws website (www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca). The Schedule is updated from time to time by the BC legislature, so verify you are reading the current in-force version as of the date you need it with a BC lawyer, notary, or by checking the official Queen's Printer website.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Can a developer register different bylaws before the first AGM?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a developer may file bylaws that differ from the Standard Bylaws at the time the strata plan is deposited or shortly thereafter, and those bylaws will govern the strata corporation instead of the Standard Bylaws. Once the strata corporation holds its first annual general meeting, the owners may amend or replace those developer-filed bylaws by the required resolution. Verify filing timelines and procedures with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
What topics do the Standard Bylaws typically cover?

The Standard Bylaws in the Schedule to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, address use and enjoyment of strata lots and common property, the keeping of pets, age restrictions, rental restrictions, repair and maintenance obligations, and other matters. Each topic may be amended or replaced by the strata corporation subject to the voting thresholds and registration requirements in the Act. Review the current Schedule and verify any strata-specific amendments with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Are Standard Bylaws the same as strata corporation rules?

No. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, bylaws and rules are distinct: bylaws are enacted or amended by a three-quarter vote (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) and must be filed at the Land Title Office, whereas rules may be made or amended by a majority vote and do not require Land Title Office registration. Rules cannot alter a bylaw or contradict the Act. Verify the distinction and current requirements with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
Do Standard Bylaws restrict rentals or pets?

The Standard Bylaws in the Schedule to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, include provisions on the keeping of pets and historically permitted rental restrictions, subject to certain limitations in the Act. Changes to provincial legislation (including amendments regarding rental restriction bylaws) mean the current state of rental and pet bylaw enforceability depends on the date of bylaw enactment and subsequent statutory amendments (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the specific text of the Standard Bylaws Schedule and any amendments with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
What happens if a strata corporation's bylaws conflict with the Strata Property Act?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, any bylaw provision that is inconsistent with the Act or its regulations is of no effect to the extent of the inconsistency. This applies equally to Standard Bylaws and to any amended or replacement bylaws. If you believe a bylaw conflicts with the Act, verify with a BC lawyer or notary before relying on or challenging the bylaw.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
How do I know if my strata corporation has amended the Standard Bylaws?

Amended or replacement bylaws must be filed at the Land Title Office under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and you may order a copy of the registered bylaws from the Land Title Office or request them from your strata corporation (strata corporations are required to provide copies to owners and purchasers under the Act). Compare the registered bylaws to the Standard Bylaws Schedule to identify differences. Verify current access procedures and fees with the Land Title Office or a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Strata Property Act
