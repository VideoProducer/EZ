Pasture / Grazing Land

Pasture or grazing land in British Columbia refers to open, typically unfenced or fenced land used for the longer-term grazing and exercise of livestock, including horses. Such land may fall within the Agricultural Land Reserve, regulated under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, which restricts non-farm uses without Commission approval. Zoning classifications for this land type are governed locally under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Buyers should verify current ALC status, permitted uses, and any applicable Property Transfer Tax exemptions under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before completing a purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is pasture / grazing land in the context of BC real estate?

Pasture / grazing land refers to a larger, fenced open area used for longer-term grazing and exercise of horses or other livestock. In BC, such land may be subject to regulation under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, if it is located within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR). Verify zoning and use restrictions with your local government and the Agricultural Land Commission before purchasing or changing the use of such property.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does pasture / grazing land in BC qualify for any property tax relief?

Pasture and grazing land used for farm purposes may qualify for farm property tax classification under the BC Assessment Act and local government bylaws, which can result in lower property tax rates. Eligibility typically requires that the land be used for a qualifying farm use and meet minimum size and income thresholds set by BC Assessment and the local government (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify current eligibility criteria and application deadlines with BC Assessment and your local government before acting.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Is pasture / grazing land in the Agricultural Land Reserve subject to use restrictions?

Yes. Land within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), governed by the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, is restricted primarily to agricultural uses, including pasture and grazing. Non-farm uses, subdivision, or soil removal typically require permission from the Agricultural Land Commission. Verify whether a specific parcel is in the ALR and what uses are permitted by consulting the ALC's online map and contacting the Agricultural Land Commission directly.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Do I need to disclose that a property includes pasture / grazing land when selling in BC?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules administered by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), licensees must disclose all known material latent defects. If the pasture or grazing land has characteristics affecting its value or use—such as ALR designation, covenants, or environmental conditions—those must be disclosed. Verify disclosure obligations for your specific transaction with a BC lawyer, notary, or your licensed real estate professional before listing.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does the Property Transfer Tax apply differently to pasture / grazing land in BC?

The Property Transfer Tax Act (PTTA), RSBC 1996, c. 378, applies to all transfers of land in BC, including pasture and grazing land, at the standard graduated rates (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Farm property is not exempt from Property Transfer Tax unless a specific statutory exemption applies, such as transfers between related individuals or qualifying corporate reorganizations. Verify whether any exemptions apply to your transaction with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before completing the purchase.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can foreign buyers purchase pasture / grazing land in BC?

The federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10, prohibits certain non-Canadians from purchasing residential property (currently extended through January 1, 2027 as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), but this prohibition generally does not apply to commercial or agricultural land unless it includes a residential dwelling of four or fewer units. Verify whether a specific pasture / grazing property is subject to the federal prohibition or BC's Additional Property Transfer Tax (foreign buyer tax) with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before making an offer.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does zoning affect what I can do with pasture / grazing land in BC?

Yes. Local government zoning bylaws, enacted under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, regulate permitted uses, minimum parcel sizes, and structures on pasture and grazing land. Even if land is in the ALR, local zoning may impose additional restrictions on buildings, fencing, and accessory uses. Verify the zoning designation and permitted uses with the local government planning department before purchasing or developing pasture / grazing land.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Is pasture / grazing land subject to the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, applies to residential properties in designated taxable regions; working farms and agricultural land are generally exempt if they qualify under the farm classification for property tax purposes (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Pasture or grazing land without a residential improvement, or land meeting the farm exemption criteria, is typically not subject to the tax. Verify your specific property's status with the BC Ministry of Finance or a licensed tax professional before the annual declaration deadline.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can I subdivide pasture / grazing land in BC?

Subdivision of land, including pasture and grazing parcels, requires approval under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, and compliance with local government bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. If the land is in the Agricultural Land Reserve, subdivision also requires approval from the Agricultural Land Commission under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36. Verify subdivision requirements and application processes with your local government and, if applicable, the Agricultural Land Commission before proceeding.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Do environmental regulations apply to pasture / grazing land in BC?

Yes. Pasture and grazing operations may be subject to federal and provincial environmental regulations, including those governing water use, manure management, and riparian area protection. While a comprehensive review of environmental law is beyond real estate compliance, buyers and sellers should verify any environmental covenants, contamination, or regulatory compliance issues with a BC lawyer or environmental consultant before closing. The Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and RESA Rules require licensees to disclose known material facts, including environmental conditions.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
