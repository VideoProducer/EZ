First-Time Home Buyer Incentives

Several programs may assist first-time buyers in British Columbia. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act (RSBC 1996, c. 378), the First-Time Home Buyers' Program may provide a full or partial exemption on property transfer tax for eligible residential purchases; verify current thresholds with BC Ministry of Finance / gov.bc.ca, as dollar figures change (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The BC Newly Built Home Exemption under the same Act may also apply to qualifying purchases of newly built principal residences and is not restricted to first-time buyers, though eligibility may overlap; verify current thresholds with BC Ministry of Finance / gov.bc.ca (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Federally, the First Home Savings Account (FHSA), the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP), and the First-Time Home Buyers' Tax Credit are administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (FCAC / CRA); verify current contribution limits, withdrawal limits, and credit amounts directly with CRA (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Eligibility conditions vary across all programs. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the BC Property Transfer Tax First-Time Home Buyers' Program and how much can I save?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, the BC Property Transfer Tax First-Time Home Buyers' Program provides a full exemption from property transfer tax on residential property up to $500,000 of fair market value, with a partial exemption tapering to $860,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Eligibility requires that you are a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, have lived in BC for 12 consecutive months immediately before the registration date or filed two income tax returns as a BC resident in the past six years, have never owned an interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, and will occupy the property as your principal residence. Verify current eligibility requirements and thresholds with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — First-Time Home Buyers' Program
Can I claim both the First-Time Home Buyers' Program and the Newly Built Home Exemption at the same time?

Yes, under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, a qualifying first-time home buyer purchasing a newly built principal residence may be eligible for both the BC Property Transfer Tax First-Time Home Buyers' Program (up to $860,000 fair market value, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) and the BC Newly Built Home Exemption (up to $1,150,000 fair market value, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) concurrently where eligibility for both programs overlaps. The Newly Built Home Exemption is not restricted to first-time buyers and applies to any qualifying buyer purchasing a newly built principal residence. Verify which exemption provides the greatest benefit for your transaction with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — First-Time Home Buyers' Program
What is the federal First Home Savings Account (FHSA) and how does it help BC first-time home buyers?

The First Home Savings Account (FHSA) is a federal tax-advantaged registered savings account administered by the Canada Revenue Agency that allows eligible first-time home buyers to contribute and deduct up to $8,000 per year (lifetime limit $40,000, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for the purpose of purchasing a first home. Contributions are tax-deductible, and qualifying withdrawals (including investment growth) to purchase a first home are tax-free. Because this is a federal program under the Income Tax Act (Canada), eligibility and contribution rules apply across Canada, including BC. Verify current FHSA contribution limits, withdrawal rules, and eligibility with a licensed tax professional or the Canada Revenue Agency before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — First-Time Home Buyers' Program
How does the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) work for first-time buyers in BC?

The Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) is a federal program administered by the Canada Revenue Agency under the Income Tax Act (Canada) that allows eligible individuals to withdraw up to $60,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) from their Registered Retirement Savings Plan (RRSP) to buy or build a qualifying home without immediate tax consequences. The withdrawn amount must be repaid to the RRSP over a period of up to 15 years, beginning the second year after withdrawal. Because this is a federal program, it applies to first-time home buyers across Canada, including BC. Verify current HBP withdrawal limits, repayment schedules, and eligibility with a licensed tax professional or the Canada Revenue Agency before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — First-Time Home Buyers' Program
What is the federal First-Time Home Buyers' Tax Credit and am I eligible if I buy in BC?

The First-Time Home Buyers' Tax Credit is a non-refundable federal income tax credit administered by the Canada Revenue Agency under the Income Tax Act (Canada) that allows eligible first-time home buyers to claim up to $10,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) on their personal income tax return, resulting in a maximum federal tax credit of $1,500 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Eligibility generally requires that neither you nor your spouse or common-law partner owned and lived in another home as a principal residence in the year of acquisition or the preceding four calendar years. Because this is a federal program, it applies to qualifying first-time home buyers purchasing anywhere in Canada, including BC. Verify current credit amounts and eligibility with a licensed tax professional or the Canada Revenue Agency before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — First-Time Home Buyers' Program
Do I have to be a Canadian citizen to qualify for the BC First-Time Home Buyers' Program exemption?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, to qualify for the BC Property Transfer Tax First-Time Home Buyers' Program, you must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident of Canada at the time of registration. You must also have lived in BC for 12 consecutive months immediately before the registration date or filed two income tax returns as a BC resident in the past six years, never owned an interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world, and intend to occupy the property as your principal residence within 92 days. Verify your specific eligibility and residency status with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — First-Time Home Buyers' Program
What does 'never owned a principal residence anywhere in the world' mean for the BC first-time home buyer exemption?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, to qualify for the BC Property Transfer Tax First-Time Home Buyers' Program, you must never have owned an interest in a principal residence anywhere in the world at any time. This includes any ownership interest (sole, joint, or beneficial) in any residential property you used as your principal residence, whether in Canada or abroad. If you previously owned any interest in a principal residence, you are not eligible for this exemption. Verify your ownership history and eligibility with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — First-Time Home Buyers' Program
Can I use the BC first-time home buyer exemption if I'm buying a strata unit (condo)?

Yes, under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, the BC Property Transfer Tax First-Time Home Buyers' Program applies to residential property, including strata lots (condominiums) governed by the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, provided all other eligibility criteria are met (Canadian citizen or permanent resident, BC residency, never owned a principal residence anywhere in the world, and occupation as principal residence). The fair market value thresholds (full exemption up to $500,000, partial exemption tapering to $860,000, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) apply to the strata lot purchase. Verify current eligibility and thresholds with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — First-Time Home Buyers' Program
How long do I have to move into the property to keep the BC first-time home buyer exemption?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, to qualify for and retain the BC Property Transfer Tax First-Time Home Buyers' Program exemption, you must occupy the property as your principal residence within 92 days of the registration date and continue to occupy it as your principal residence for at least one year after registration. If you fail to meet these occupancy requirements, you may be required to repay the exempted tax. Verify current occupancy rules and exceptions with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before acting.

Verify with: BC Government — First-Time Home Buyers' Program
Where can I verify the current dollar thresholds and eligibility rules for BC first-time home buyer incentives?

For the BC Property Transfer Tax First-Time Home Buyers' Program and BC Newly Built Home Exemption, verify current fair market value thresholds, eligibility criteria, and exemption amounts at www.gov.bc.ca or by contacting the BC Ministry of Finance directly. For federal programs (FHSA, Home Buyers' Plan, First-Time Home Buyers' Tax Credit), verify current contribution limits, withdrawal limits, credit amounts, and eligibility with the Canada Revenue Agency at www.canada.ca or a licensed tax professional. For transaction-specific advice, consult a BC lawyer or notary public before acting.
