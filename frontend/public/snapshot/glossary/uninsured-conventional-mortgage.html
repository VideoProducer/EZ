Financing

Uninsured / Conventional Mortgage

A conventional (uninsured) mortgage is one where the borrower provides a down payment of at least 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) of the purchase price, meaning the loan-to-value ratio falls at or below the threshold at which mortgage default insurance is required. Because no insurer such as CMHC backs the loan, the lender bears the full credit risk. Federally regulated lenders must apply the federal mortgage stress test when qualifying borrowers; confirm current qualifying rate rules with FCAC or a licensed mortgage professional. Individual lender underwriting criteria may impose additional conditions beyond the federal minimum. Verify current thresholds and qualifying requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed mortgage professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an uninsured or conventional mortgage in British Columbia?

An uninsured or conventional mortgage is a mortgage loan where the borrower provides a down payment of 20% or more of the purchase price (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), meaning default mortgage insurance is not required. While BC does not regulate mortgage insurance directly, federally regulated lenders lending conventional mortgages must still apply the federal mortgage stress test under the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) guidelines. Verify current down payment thresholds and stress test rules with a BC mortgage professional or licensed financial advisor before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Do I need mortgage default insurance if I put down 20% or more in BC?

No, if you provide a down payment of 20% or more of the property purchase price (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), mortgage default insurance from Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) or private insurers is not required. CMHC and mortgage insurance requirements are governed by federal statute and regulations, not BC provincial law. Verify current insurance thresholds with your lender or a BC mortgage broker before finalizing your financing.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Does the federal mortgage stress test apply to conventional mortgages in BC?

Yes, for federally regulated lenders (banks, trust companies, and loan companies governed by federal legislation), the mortgage stress test applies to all mortgages, including conventional (uninsured) mortgages, under guidelines published by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) as of 2026-07-27 — verify current. The stress test requires qualifying at the higher of the contract rate plus 2% or the Bank of Canada's published qualifying rate (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify current qualifying rates and lender-specific criteria with your lender or a BC mortgage broker before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Are there any BC provincial laws that regulate conventional mortgage terms or interest rates?

BC does not directly regulate mortgage contract terms, interest rates, or underwriting criteria for conventional mortgages; these are primarily governed by federal banking laws and lender policies. The British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) regulates mortgage brokers under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules, ensuring brokers meet licensing, disclosure, and conduct standards. For questions about your mortgage contract or rate, verify details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed mortgage professional.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Will I pay lower interest rates with a conventional mortgage compared to a high-ratio mortgage?

Interest rates on conventional mortgages are set by individual lenders and vary based on market conditions, borrower creditworthiness, and lender policies; BC provincial law does not prescribe or cap mortgage interest rates. Federally regulated lenders may offer different rates for insured versus uninsured mortgages depending on their risk assessment and funding costs. Verify current rate offerings and eligibility with multiple lenders or a BC licensed mortgage broker before committing.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Does using a conventional mortgage affect my BC Property Transfer Tax obligations?

No, the type of mortgage (conventional or insured) does not directly affect Property Transfer Tax (PTT) obligations under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378; PTT is calculated on the purchase price or fair market value of the property, not the mortgage structure. The fair market value threshold for the First-Time Home Buyer Exemption is up to $835,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), and the Newly Built Home Exemption applies up to $1,100,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify current PTT exemptions and rates with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC lawyer or notary before completing your purchase.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Can I get a conventional mortgage for a property on BC's Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR)?

Yes, conventional mortgages are available for properties within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), governed by the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, but lender-specific policies may impose additional qualifying criteria or restrictions based on permitted land use. Some lenders may require appraisals that account for ALR restrictions on subdivision, non-farm use, or resale, which can affect property valuation. Verify lender eligibility and ALR land use restrictions with a BC mortgage broker and the Agricultural Land Commission before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Are there any BC rebates or grants specifically for conventional mortgage borrowers?

BC does not currently offer provincial rebates, grants, or tax credits specifically tied to conventional (uninsured) mortgages; the BC Home Owner Grant Act provides an annual property tax reduction for qualifying owner-occupiers regardless of mortgage type, subject to income and assessed value thresholds (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Federal programs such as the First-Time Home Buyers' Incentive (a shared-equity mortgage program administered by CMHC) historically applied only to insured mortgages, though program availability and rules change. Verify current provincial and federal homebuyer assistance programs with the BC Ministry of Finance, CMHC, or a BC lawyer or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Does BC law require my mortgage broker to disclose if a conventional mortgage is suitable for me?

Yes, under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules enforced by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), licensed mortgage brokers owe a duty to act in the client's best interests, provide full disclosure of material facts, and ensure suitability of mortgage products based on the client's financial circumstances. Mortgage brokers must comply with BCFSA Rules regarding conflicts of interest, remuneration disclosure, and client documentation (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify your broker's licensing status at www.bcfsa.ca and discuss suitability and disclosure obligations with your broker or a BC lawyer before signing any mortgage documents.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
If I refinance my home in BC with a conventional mortgage, are there tax implications?

Refinancing a conventional mortgage does not trigger Property Transfer Tax under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, because no change in beneficial ownership occurs; however, interest paid on funds borrowed for investment purposes may have federal income tax implications under the Income Tax Act (Canada), which is outside BC's legislative jurisdiction. If you use refinanced funds for purposes such as renovating a rental property or investing, verify federal tax deductibility and reporting obligations with a licensed tax professional or accountant. BC does not impose a provincial tax on mortgage refinancing transactions (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).
