BC Assessment vs Market Value

BC Assessment, established under provincial authority, mails every BC property owner an annual notice each January showing the property's assessed value. That assessed value is determined using July 1 of the prior year as the valuation date and relies on mass appraisal methods applied across comparable properties — verify current methodology with BC Assessment or a licensed appraiser. Market value, by contrast, reflects the price a willing buyer and seller would agree upon in a current, open-market transaction, based on recent comparable sales. Because the assessment date lags the present day and uses broad statistical models rather than individual appraisals, assessed value and current market value can differ materially depending on prevailing market conditions. Neither figure is inherently more "correct"; they serve different purposes. For tax or transactional decisions, verify current assessed and market values with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between BC Assessment value and market value?

BC Assessment value is the assessed value assigned by BC Assessment (a Crown corporation) for property taxation purposes, using mass appraisal methods and a valuation date of July 1 of the preceding year. Market value is the price a willing buyer and willing seller would agree to in an open-market transaction on the current date, based on recent comparable sales and present market conditions. The two figures can differ significantly because BC Assessment's valuation is retrospective (July 1 snapshot) while market value reflects current conditions (verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting).

Verify with: BC Assessment Authority
Which value – BC Assessment or market value – is used to calculate Property Transfer Tax in BC?

Property Transfer Tax under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, is calculated on the fair market value of the property at the time of registration, not the BC Assessment value. Fair market value is typically the purchase price in an arm's-length sale, or if the transaction is not at arm's length, an appraisal or evidence of market value may be required. Verify the specifics for your transaction with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment Authority
Can I use my BC Assessment value to price my home for sale?

BC Assessment value is not designed to set listing prices; it reflects July 1 of the prior year and uses mass appraisal, not individual property inspections or current market trends. A licensed BC real estate professional (regulated under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and overseen by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority) will prepare a comparative market analysis based on recent comparable sales to help determine current market value. Verify current market conditions with a licensed real estate professional before listing.

Verify with: BC Assessment Authority
Why is my BC Assessment value lower (or higher) than what similar homes are selling for today?

BC Assessment uses a valuation date of July 1 of the previous year, so a home assessed in January 2026 reflects market conditions as of July 1, 2025 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). If the market has risen or fallen since that snapshot, or if individual property features (renovations, condition, location nuances) differ from mass appraisal assumptions, assessed value and current sale prices will diverge. Verify your property's current market value with a licensed BC real estate professional or appraiser before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment Authority
Does BC Assessment value affect how much property tax I pay?

Yes. Under the BC Home Owner Grant Act and municipal taxation bylaws authorized by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, your annual property tax is calculated by multiplying your assessed value by the municipal tax rate (mill rate). However, the mill rate is adjusted each year by local governments to meet budget needs, so a higher assessed value does not automatically mean proportionally higher taxes if all properties in the area increased similarly. Verify your municipality's current tax rates and exemptions with your local government or a BC tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment Authority
Can I appeal my BC Assessment if I think it does not reflect my home's market value?

Yes. BC Assessment provides an independent review and appeal process if you believe your assessed value is incorrect; the deadline to file a complaint is typically January 31 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). You may present evidence such as recent comparable sales, appraisals, or property condition reports. Verify current complaint deadlines and procedures at www.bcassessment.ca or consult a BC lawyer, notary, or property appraiser before filing.

Verify with: BC Assessment Authority
Is BC Assessment value the same as an appraisal?

No. BC Assessment value is produced by mass appraisal—applying statistical models and general market data to large groups of properties—while a formal appraisal is an individual, detailed valuation conducted by a qualified appraiser who inspects the property and analyzes current comparable sales. Lenders, courts, and some regulatory processes (e.g., estate valuations under the Wills, Estates and Succession Act, SBC 2009, c. 13) typically require a formal appraisal, not the BC Assessment value. Verify which valuation method is required for your purpose with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed appraiser before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment Authority
Which value is used for mortgage lending – BC Assessment or market value?

Mortgage lenders in Canada (regulated federally and subject to CMHC underwriting guidelines where applicable) base loan-to-value calculations on current market value, typically verified by a formal appraisal or automated valuation model at the time of financing. BC Assessment value is not used for underwriting purposes because it reflects July 1 of the prior year and may not capture recent market changes or property-specific features. Verify current lender requirements with your mortgage broker or lender and a BC lawyer or notary before finalizing financing.

Verify with: BC Assessment Authority
Does the Speculation and Vacancy Tax use BC Assessment value or market value?

The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, bases the tax on the BC Assessment value of the property for the relevant tax year (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The tax rate (currently 2% for foreign owners and satellite families, 0.5% for Canadian citizens and permanent residents who are not members of a satellite family, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) is applied to that assessed value if the property is vacant or underutilized in a designated taxable region. Verify your eligibility, exemptions, and current rates at www.gov.bc.ca or with a BC tax professional before the annual declaration deadline.

Verify with: BC Assessment Authority
If I buy a property for less than its BC Assessment value, do I pay Property Transfer Tax on the lower amount?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, PTT is calculated on the greater of the purchase price or the fair market value at the time of transfer. If you purchase below BC Assessment but the transaction is at arm's length and reflects genuine market value (e.g., distress sale, property condition issues), the lower purchase price may be accepted; however, if the Province suspects the price does not reflect fair market value, it may reassess and charge PTT on a higher amount. Verify your specific transaction with a BC lawyer or notary before completion to ensure correct PTT calculation and disclosure.
