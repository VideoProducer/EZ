HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit)

A Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) is a revolving credit facility secured by a registered charge against residential property under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, allowing a borrower to draw, repay, and redraw funds up to an approved limit. Interest rates are typically variable, and minimum payments are commonly structured as interest-only during the draw period; verify current terms with a licensed mortgage professional. Federally regulated lenders are subject to underwriting and disclosure requirements overseen by the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC); verify current rules at fcac.gc.ca. The secured charge must be registered in the BC Land Title Office to be enforceable against the property. Specific loan-to-value limits and stress-test requirements applicable to HELOCs at federally regulated institutions should be verified with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed mortgage professional, as these figures change and no specific percentage is stated here.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a HELOC and how does it differ from a traditional mortgage in British Columbia?

A HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit) is a revolving line of credit secured by the equity in your BC home, allowing you to borrow, repay, and re-borrow up to an approved limit. Unlike a traditional mortgage with fixed payments toward principal and interest, a HELOC typically requires only minimum interest payments and carries a variable interest rate. Federally regulated lenders (banks, federal credit unions) must comply with underwriting and disclosure rules set by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) and the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC). Verify current product terms and regulatory requirements with your lender and a BC lawyer or notary before entering any HELOC agreement.

Are HELOCs in BC regulated at the provincial or federal level?

HELOCs offered by federally regulated financial institutions (banks, federally incorporated credit unions) are governed by federal laws, including the Bank Act, SC 1991, c. 46, and supervised by OSFI and the FCAC. BC provincial law—such as the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250—governs the registration of the mortgage or charge securing the HELOC on BC land. The British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) regulates BC credit unions and mortgage brokers under the Financial Institutions Act, RSBC 1996, c. 141, and the Mortgage Brokers Act, RSBC 1996, c. 313, but does not set underwriting rules for federally regulated lenders. Verify the regulatory status of your lender and the applicable disclosure and consumer-protection rules with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed financial professional.

Verify with: Home Equity Line of Credit
What is the maximum loan-to-value (LTV) ratio allowed for a HELOC in British Columbia?

Under OSFI Guideline B-20 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), federally regulated lenders are generally limited to a combined loan-to-value (LTV) ratio of 65% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for a stand-alone HELOC on an owner-occupied residential property. When a HELOC is combined with a traditional mortgage (often called a "readvanceable" or "combo" product), the total debt secured by the property cannot exceed 80% LTV (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) at origination, with the HELOC portion capped at 65% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Provincial credit unions and private lenders may have different LTV limits; the BCFSA does not publish a uniform provincial LTV cap for HELOCs. Verify your lender's specific LTV policy and current OSFI guidelines with your lender, mortgage broker, and a BC lawyer or notary before proceeding.

Verify with: Home Equity Line of Credit
Do I need a BC lawyer or notary to register a HELOC on my property?

Yes. Under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, any mortgage or charge (including a HELOC) must be registered in the BC Land Title Office to secure the lender's interest in the property. In British Columbia, both lawyers and notaries public are authorized to prepare and file land-title documents, provide independent legal advice, and ensure the borrower understands the nature and consequences of the charge. Verify the scope of services, fees, and regulatory requirements with a BC lawyer or notary before signing your HELOC documents.

Verify with: Home Equity Line of Credit
Can I use a HELOC to purchase an investment property in BC, and are there tax implications?

A HELOC secured by your principal residence can provide funds for any lawful purpose, including the purchase of an investment property in BC, subject to your lender's approval and compliance with applicable LTV and underwriting rules. Interest paid on funds borrowed for income-producing purposes may be deductible for federal income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act, RSC 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), while interest on personal-use borrowing is generally not deductible (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The investment property itself may be subject to BC's Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, and federal capital-gains tax on disposition. Verify all tax consequences, deductibility rules, and reporting obligations with a licensed tax professional or accountant before using HELOC funds for investment purposes.

Verify with: Home Equity Line of Credit
If I default on my HELOC, can the lender foreclose on my BC home?

Yes. Because a HELOC is secured by a registered mortgage or charge under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, the lender has the legal right to enforce the security if you default on the credit agreement. In British Columbia, mortgage enforcement is governed by common law and the Law and Equity Act, RSBC 1996, c. 253, which provides for judicial foreclosure or court-ordered sale procedures. The lender must follow proper notice and court procedures; timelines and redemption rights depend on the specifics of your default and the court process. Verify your rights, obligations, and available remedies (including refinancing, sale, or negotiated forbearance) with a BC lawyer immediately if you are at risk of default.

Verify with: Home Equity Line of Credit
Are HELOC interest rates fixed or variable in BC, and how are they set?

HELOCs in British Columbia typically carry variable interest rates, often expressed as the lender's prime rate plus or minus a margin (spread). The Bank of Canada's policy interest rate influences the prime rates set by individual lenders, but each federally regulated or provincial lender sets its own prime and HELOC margin (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) requires federally regulated lenders to disclose the interest-rate calculation method, any rate changes, and associated fees in the credit agreement. Verify your lender's current prime rate, margin, rate-adjustment frequency, and disclosure obligations before drawing on your HELOC.

Verify with: Home Equity Line of Credit
Can I be charged Property Transfer Tax (PTT) when I set up or increase a HELOC in BC?

No. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, PTT is triggered by a transfer or deemed transfer of beneficial ownership of land, not by the registration of a mortgage, charge, or HELOC. Registering a new HELOC or increasing the authorized credit limit does not transfer title and therefore does not attract PTT (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). However, Land Title Office registration fees will apply to register or amend the charge. Verify current registration fees and confirm that no transfer of beneficial interest is occurring with your BC lawyer or notary before registration.

Verify with: Home Equity Line of Credit
If I sell my BC home, must I pay out my HELOC at closing?

Yes, in the ordinary course of a residential sale. The purchaser's lender will require clear title under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, meaning all existing mortgages and charges (including your HELOC) must be discharged at or before completion unless the parties agree otherwise and the new lender consents. Your lawyer or notary will calculate the HELOC payout (principal drawn, accrued interest, and any discharge or prepayment fees) on the closing statement of adjustments and arrange for the discharge to be filed in the Land Title Office. Verify the exact payout amount, timing, and any penalties with your HELOC lender and your BC lawyer or notary well before the completion date.

Verify with: Home Equity Line of Credit
Are there disclosure or cooling-off rights for HELOC borrowers in British Columbia?

Federally regulated lenders must comply with disclosure requirements under the Cost of Borrowing (Banks) Regulations, SOR/2001-101, and Cost of Borrowing (Canadian Credit Unions) Regulations, SOR/2014-249, enforced by the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC), including pre-contractual disclosure of interest rates, fees, and calculation methods (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). British Columbia does not have a statutory cooling-off period for HELOC agreements; once you sign, the contract is binding subject to its terms and applicable consumer-protection law. BC credit unions are subject to BCFSA oversight under the Financial Institutions Act, RSBC 1996, c. 141, but cooling-off rights, if any, are determined by the credit union's bylaws and the loan agreement. Verify all disclosure documents, your right to independent legal advice, and any cancellation or prepayment terms with your lender and a BC lawyer or notary before signing.
