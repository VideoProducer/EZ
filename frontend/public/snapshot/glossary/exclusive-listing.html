Exclusive Listing

An exclusive listing is a property marketed for sale through a single brokerage without being posted to the MLS® System or REALTOR.ca. Because exposure is limited to that brokerage's own buyer pool and private network, sellers typically accept reduced market reach in exchange for privacy, off-market testing, or discretion in estate situations. In BC, licensees handling exclusive listings remain bound by the agency, disclosure, and conduct obligations set out in the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and must comply with client privacy requirements under PIPA, SBC 2003, c. 63. Whether reduced exposure affects the final sale price in any particular transaction is a market question; verify current market conditions and implications with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an exclusive listing in British Columbia real estate?

An exclusive listing is a listing agreement where a property is represented by one brokerage but not published on the MLS® System or REALTOR.ca, limiting exposure to that brokerage's buyer pool and private network. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and BCFSA Rules, the same agency and disclosure obligations apply as with MLS® listings. Sellers often choose this arrangement for privacy, off-market test pricing, or estate situations, though it typically results in reduced market exposure. Verify current BCFSA requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before entering an exclusive listing agreement.

Does an exclusive listing in BC still require a written listing agreement?

Yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and BCFSA Rules, all listing agreements—whether exclusive, MLS®, or otherwise—must be in writing and signed by the seller and the brokerage. The agreement must contain mandatory terms including the listing price, commission structure, term, and agency disclosure. Failure to use a written agreement is a regulatory breach. Verify current BCFSA form requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before signing.

Are BC brokerages required to disclose that an exclusive listing will not be on MLS®?

Yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and BCFSA Rules, brokerages owe a duty of full disclosure to clients, which includes explaining that an exclusive listing will not appear on MLS® or REALTOR.ca and that this materially limits market exposure and may impact sale price. The brokerage must ensure the seller provides informed consent to this limited marketing approach. Verify current BCFSA disclosure standards with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before proceeding.

Can a BC seller cancel an exclusive listing agreement early?

It depends on the terms of the written listing agreement. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), the listing contract governs the term and any cancellation provisions; early termination without the brokerage's consent may expose the seller to damages or commission liability if a buyer is later procured during the term or a holdover period. Verify the specific cancellation terms in your exclusive listing agreement with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before attempting to cancel.

Does the BC Property Transfer Tax (PTT) apply differently to properties sold via exclusive listing?

No. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act (PTTA), RSBC 1996, c. 378, PTT applies to all taxable property transfers in BC regardless of whether the property was marketed via exclusive listing, MLS®, or privately. The tax is calculated on the fair market value or consideration paid, whichever is greater. Verify current PTT rates and exemptions (e.g., First-Time Home Buyer Exemption thresholds as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before closing.

Are exclusive listings in BC subject to the same commission disclosure rules as MLS® listings?

Yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and BCFSA Rules, all listing agreements—exclusive or MLS®—must clearly disclose the commission or remuneration payable to the brokerage and how it is calculated. The brokerage must also disclose whether a commission will be offered to a cooperating brokerage representing a buyer (which is often zero or reduced in exclusive listings). Verify current BCFSA commission disclosure requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before signing.

Can a BC brokerage use an exclusive listing to avoid cooperating with buyer's agents?

Exclusive listings are not subject to MLS® cooperation rules, so the listing brokerage may choose not to offer or share commission with cooperating buyer brokerages, though this is a business decision rather than a regulatory requirement. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), the brokerage must still act in the seller's best interests, and limiting cooperation may reduce buyer traffic and sale price. Verify the cooperation terms in any exclusive listing agreement with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before proceeding.

Do exclusive listings in BC require a Property Disclosure Statement (PDS)?

The Property Disclosure Statement is not mandated by provincial statute for most residential sales in BC, but under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and BCFSA Rules, brokerages must ensure material latent defects are disclosed to buyers, and many brokerages require sellers to complete a PDS as part of their standard practice. Whether a property is on MLS® or exclusive listing does not change the seller's and brokerage's disclosure obligations. Verify current BCFSA disclosure standards and PDS practices with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before listing.

If a BC property is sold via exclusive listing, does the federal ban on foreign buyers still apply?

Yes. The Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10 (currently extended through January 1, 2027 — verify current), applies to all residential property purchases in Canada by non-Canadians, regardless of marketing method (exclusive listing, MLS®, or private sale). Violation may result in criminal penalties and forced divestiture. Verify current federal eligibility, exemptions, and effective dates with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before closing.

Does a BC exclusive listing affect a buyer's financing or mortgage approval?

No. Lenders assess the property's appraised value, the buyer's creditworthiness, income, and down payment, not the marketing method. However, exclusive listings may yield fewer comparable sales data points for the appraiser, potentially affecting the appraisal. Verify mortgage approval requirements and appraisal processes with your lender and a BC mortgage broker or licensed financial professional before making an offer.
