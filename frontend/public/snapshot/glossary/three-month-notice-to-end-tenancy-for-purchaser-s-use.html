Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use

The Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use is a notice form a landlord must serve under the Residential Tenancy Act when a purchaser of the rental property, or a close family member of the purchaser, intends in good faith to occupy the rental unit. As of August 21, 2024, the required notice period is three months and the tenant's dispute period is 21 days. The notice is generated using the Residential Tenancy Branch's Landlord Use Web Portal (form RTB-32P). The purchaser or close family member must occupy the unit for at least 12 months following the effective date of the notice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use in British Columbia?

Under the Residential Tenancy Act (RSBC 2002, c. 78), the Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use is a formal notice a landlord must serve on a tenant when a purchaser of the rental property, or a close family member of the purchaser, genuinely intends to occupy the rental unit as their primary residence. The required notice period is three months, and the notice must be generated through the Residential Tenancy Branch's Landlord Use Web Portal using form RTB-32P. The purchaser or qualifying family member must then occupy the unit for a minimum of 12 consecutive months following the effective date of the notice.

Who qualifies as a 'close family member' of the purchaser for the purposes of this notice in BC?

Under the Residential Tenancy Act, 'close family member' is defined to include the purchaser's spouse, child, parent, or the parent or child of the purchaser's spouse. The definition does not extend to more distant relatives such as cousins, aunts, or uncles. Landlords and purchasers should confirm the current statutory definition directly in the Residential Tenancy Act to ensure the intended occupant qualifies before serving the notice.

How long does a tenant have to dispute the Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use in BC?

As of August 21, 2024, a tenant who receives this notice has 21 days from the date the notice is served to file a dispute application with the Residential Tenancy Branch. If the tenant does not file a dispute within that 21-day window, they lose the right to challenge the notice through the Residential Tenancy Branch's dispute resolution process. Tenants wishing to dispute must act promptly and file an application for dispute resolution using the Residential Tenancy Branch's online or in-person filing process.

What form must be used to issue a Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use in BC, and how is it generated?

The notice must be generated using the Residential Tenancy Branch's Landlord Use Web Portal, which produces the prescribed form RTB-32P. Landlords cannot simply draft their own version of this notice, as the Residential Tenancy Act requires use of the approved form. Using the web portal ensures the notice contains all mandatory information required under the Residential Tenancy Act and its regulations.

What monetary compensation, if any, is a tenant entitled to receive when given a Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use in BC?

Under the Residential Tenancy Act, a tenant who receives a valid Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use is entitled to receive one month's rent as compensation from the landlord. This compensation must be paid on or before the effective date of the notice. Failure by the landlord to pay the required compensation can give the tenant grounds to dispute the notice or seek remedies through the Residential Tenancy Branch.

What are the consequences in BC if the purchaser or their close family member does not occupy the rental unit for the required 12 months after the notice takes effect?

Under the Residential Tenancy Act, if the purchaser or their qualifying close family member fails to occupy the rental unit for a minimum of 12 consecutive months following the effective date of the notice, the former tenant may be entitled to seek compensation through the Residential Tenancy Branch. The landlord or purchaser who used the notice in bad faith, or failed to meet the 12-month occupancy requirement, may be ordered to pay the former tenant an amount equivalent to 12 months' rent. The Residential Tenancy Branch handles disputes and compensation orders arising from such failures.

Can a tenant remain in the rental unit in BC if they dispute the Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use within the 21-day period?

Yes, if a tenant files a valid dispute application with the Residential Tenancy Branch within the 21-day dispute period, they are generally entitled to remain in the rental unit until the dispute is resolved through the Residential Tenancy Branch's dispute resolution process. The tenancy is not automatically ended simply because the notice was served; it requires either the tenant's compliance or a final decision from the Residential Tenancy Branch upholding the notice. Tenants should retain proof of their dispute filing.

Does the Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use apply to strata lots in BC?

Yes, the Residential Tenancy Act applies to rental units that are strata lots, meaning a purchaser of a strata lot can serve a Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use on a tenant occupying that strata lot, provided all requirements of the Act are met. However, landlords and purchasers of strata lots should also be aware of the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and the strata corporation's bylaws, which may impose additional rules governing rentals and occupancy within the strata development. Compliance with both the Residential Tenancy Act and the Strata Property Act is required.

What role, if any, does a licensed real estate professional in BC have in relation to this notice?

Licensed real estate professionals in BC are governed by the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and regulated by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and they may advise purchaser clients about the existence and general requirements of the Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use as part of a real estate transaction. However, the actual issuance, service, and legal interpretation of the notice is governed by the Residential Tenancy Act and falls outside the scope of real estate licensee services, meaning tenants and purchasers should consult the Residential Tenancy Branch or a legal professional for specific guidance. Licensees must not provide legal advice regarding the notice's validity or enforceability.

Is the Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use available to a purchaser who has not yet completed the purchase of the property in BC?

Under the Residential Tenancy Act, it is the landlord who must serve the Three-Month Notice to End Tenancy for Purchaser's Use, and the notice is connected to the purchaser's genuine intention to occupy the unit following completion of the sale. The notice is typically served by the current landlord (seller) on behalf of the purchaser's intended use, and its validity depends on the good-faith occupancy intention of the purchaser at the time of service. Parties should consult the Residential Tenancy Branch's current guidance and their legal advisors to ensure the notice is served at the appropriate stage of the transaction in compliance with the Residential Tenancy Act.
