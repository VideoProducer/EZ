Price Per Square Foot

Price per square foot ($/sqft) is calculated by dividing a property's sale price by its finished square footage and is commonly used as a quick market comparison metric. However, it is a blunt tool that does not account for lot size, views, condition, layout, age, finishing quality, or — in strata properties governed by the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43 — ongoing strata fees and the building's maintenance history. Two properties at identical $/sqft figures may differ substantially in value; verify current figures with a BC-licensed appraiser. A professional Comparative Market Analysis prepared by a registrant under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42 provides a more reliable basis for pricing decisions than $/sqft alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is price per square foot ($/sqft) in BC real estate?

Price per square foot is the total sale price of a property divided by its finished square footage, expressed as a dollar-per-square-foot figure. It is a quick comparison metric used to gauge relative pricing between properties, but it is a blunt tool that ignores many critical factors such as lot size, view, condition, layout, age, finishing quality, and strata fees. A professional Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) prepared by a licensed real estate professional under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, is far more reliable for valuation purposes. Verify any valuation or pricing strategy with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before acting.

Is price per square foot regulated or defined in BC real estate law?

No BC statute—including the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, or the Strata Property Act (SPA), SBC 1998, c. 43—defines or regulates the calculation or use of price per square foot as a metric. It is an industry-standard comparison tool, not a legal standard or approved valuation method. Licensed real estate professionals regulated by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) under RESA and the RESA Rules must provide accurate representations of property characteristics, including square footage, but $/sqft itself is not a mandated disclosure. Verify square footage measurements and valuation methods with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before relying on $/sqft comparisons.

Does BC law require sellers or licensees to disclose price per square foot?

No. Neither the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, nor the RESA Rules mandate disclosure of price per square foot. However, licensees must not make misrepresentations under RESA and the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) Rules, so any square footage figure used to calculate $/sqft must be accurate and verifiable. The Strata Property Act (SPA), SBC 1998, c. 43, does not impose $/sqft disclosure obligations on strata corporations or sellers. Verify disclosure obligations for your transaction with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before acting.

Why is price per square foot considered a 'blunt' metric in BC?

Price per square foot ignores numerous value drivers that materially affect property price, including lot size, view corridors, building condition, layout efficiency, age, finishing quality, zoning (e.g., under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1), strata fees (relevant under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43), and location-specific amenities. For example, a $750/sqft condo in a building with deferred maintenance or known defects (disclosed under SPA ss. 59–60) is not comparable to $750/sqft in a newly built, code-compliant building. A professional Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) prepared under the standards of the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, accounts for these variables and is far more reliable. Verify valuation methods with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before acting.

Can I use price per square foot to determine how much Property Transfer Tax (PTT) I will pay in BC?

No. Property Transfer Tax (PTT) under the Property Transfer Tax Act (PTTA), RSBC 1996, c. 378, is calculated on the total fair market value or purchase price of the property, not on a per-square-foot basis. PTT rates are progressive: 1% on the first $200,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), 2% on the portion from $200,000 to $2,000,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), 3% on the portion from $2,000,000 to $3,000,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), and 5% on the portion above $3,000,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), plus an Additional PTT of 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for foreign entities or taxable trustees under PTTA s. 2.02. Exemptions for first-time home buyers and newly built homes also depend on total fair market value thresholds. Verify PTT calculations and exemption eligibility with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Does the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) use price per square foot?

No. The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, calculates the SVT based on the BC assessed value of the property (as determined by BC Assessment for property tax purposes), not on price per square foot or actual sale price. The SVT rate is 2% of assessed value for foreign owners and satellite families, and 0.5% for Canadian citizens or permanent residents who are not BC residents (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Price per square foot is irrelevant to SVT liability, exemptions, or declarations. Verify SVT obligations and exemptions with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Can I compare a house and a strata condo using price per square foot in BC?

You can calculate $/sqft for both, but the comparison is highly misleading because houses include lot value (land title under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250) and condos do not separately transfer land title—they include a strata lot and share of common property under the Strata Property Act (SPA), SBC 1998, c. 43. Strata properties also carry monthly strata fees, special levies, and shared liability for building defects (SPA ss. 59, 158–161), none of which are reflected in $/sqft. A house may have lower $/sqft but higher maintenance and property tax costs; a condo may have higher $/sqft but lower direct maintenance costs and higher strata fees. Verify comparative valuation methods with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before acting.

Does BC law say how to measure square footage for calculating price per square foot?

No BC statute—including the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, or the Strata Property Act (SPA), SBC 1998, c. 43—prescribes a mandatory method for measuring residential square footage. Industry practice in BC typically follows guidelines such as those from the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA) or the Real Estate Board of Greater Vancouver (REBGV), which generally measure above-grade finished living space, excluding garages, unfinished basements, and balconies—but practices vary. Misrepresentation of square footage can constitute professional misconduct under RESA and the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) Rules. Verify the measurement standard and source (e.g., builder plans, BC Assessment, appraisal, or laser measurement) with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before relying on any $/sqft figure.

Can a buyer refuse to complete a purchase in BC if the actual price per square foot differs from what was advertised?

A buyer's right to refuse completion depends on whether there was a material misrepresentation of square footage (not $/sqft itself) that induced the contract, which may be a breach of contract or a misrepresentation actionable under common law and the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42. If the contract of purchase and sale specifies square footage or incorporates representations made by the seller or licensee, and the actual measurement is materially different, the buyer may have remedies including rescission, damages, or specific performance, depending on the contract terms and the nature of the misrepresentation. Price per square foot is a derived figure, not typically a contractual term. Verify your contractual rights and remedies with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Should I use price per square foot to decide how much to offer on a BC property?

Price per square foot should be used only as a preliminary sanity check, not as the basis for an offer. A professional Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) prepared by a licensed real estate professional under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) Rules will account for recent comparable sales, property condition, location, zoning (Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1), strata status (Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43), and market trends—factors that $/sqft ignores. An appraisal, home inspection, and review of title and disclosure documents (Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250; SPA ss. 59–60) are also critical to determining fair value. Verify valuation and offer strategy with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before acting.
