Firm Deal

A contract of purchase and sale with no remaining subjects or conditions — binding on both buyer and seller. From this point forward the buyer's deposit is at risk and the seller cannot accept other offers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly makes a contract of purchase and sale a 'firm deal' in British Columbia?

A contract of purchase and sale becomes a firm deal in BC when all subjects and conditions — such as financing approval, home inspection, or strata document review — have been either fulfilled or waived by the party who holds the benefit of each condition. At that point no conditions remain outstanding, and both buyer and seller are legally bound to complete the transaction. Licensees operating under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and regulated by the BCFSA must ensure clients understand this shift from a conditional to an unconditional contract before subjects are removed.

Verify with: BCREA — British Columbia Real Estate Association
Once a deal goes firm in BC, is the buyer's deposit protected if the seller backs out?

Once a deal is firm, the seller is contractually obligated to complete; if the seller wrongfully refuses, the buyer may pursue remedies including specific performance or damages through the BC Supreme Court. The deposit held in trust under RESA and its Rules must be released in accordance with the contract terms or a court order — a licensee cannot unilaterally release disputed funds. Because the deposit is at risk for the buyer if they default, both parties should understand their obligations before subjects are removed.

Can a seller accept a backup offer or continue to market the property after a deal goes firm in BC?

Once a deal is firm in BC, the seller is bound by the contract and cannot accept another offer to purchase the same property on the same terms, as doing so could expose them to significant legal liability for breach of contract. A seller may receive and hold a backup offer that is explicitly conditional on the firm deal collapsing, but the existing firm contract remains in full force. Licensees have professional obligations under RESA and BCFSA Rules to accurately represent the status of a property to prospective buyers.

What happens to a buyer's deposit if they fail to complete after a deal goes firm in BC?

When a deal is firm and the buyer fails to complete without a lawful excuse, the seller is generally entitled to pursue the deposit as liquidated damages or to seek additional damages through the courts, depending on how the contract is drafted. Under RESA and its Rules, deposits are held in a licensee's brokerage trust account and may only be released to the entitled party by mutual written agreement of both parties or pursuant to a court order. Buyers should obtain independent legal advice before removing subjects to fully understand their financial exposure.

Does Property Transfer Tax (PTT) become payable when a deal goes firm, or only at completion in BC?

Under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act, PTT is triggered and payable at the time of registration of the transfer at the Land Title Office, which occurs on the completion date — not when the deal goes firm. However, the obligation to pay PTT is determined by the terms set out in the firm contract, and buyers should assess their PTT liability (at tiers of 1%, 2%, 3%, and an additional 2% on the residential portion of the fair market value exceeding $3,000,000) before removing subjects. Exemptions such as the First-Time Home Buyers' Program (full exemption up to $835,000) or the Newly Built Home Exemption (up to $1,100,000) should be confirmed with the BC Ministry of Finance prior to the deal going firm.

If a strata property deal goes firm in BC, what happens if the buyer later discovers an issue in the strata documents they had not yet reviewed?

Once a strata property deal is firm, the buyer has waived or fulfilled all conditions, including any strata document review condition; they generally cannot rescind the contract solely because they later find issues in the strata documents. Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), documents such as the Form B Information Certificate, depreciation report, and bylaws are typically provided during the subject period for review. Buyers are strongly encouraged to thoroughly review all strata documents and seek independent advice before waiving a strata document review condition, as doing so is a key component of converting a conditional offer into a firm deal.

Can a BC licensee advise a client to remove subjects and go firm without fully explaining the consequences?

No — under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and BCFSA regulatory requirements, licensees have a duty to act in their client's best interests and to ensure clients are informed of material consequences before making decisions, including the removal of subjects. Advising a client to go firm without explaining that the deposit becomes at risk and the contract becomes fully binding could constitute a breach of the licensee's professional obligations. BCFSA has the authority to investigate and discipline licensees whose conduct falls below the required standard of care.

In BC, can a deal go firm on a property located in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR)?

Yes, a contract of purchase and sale on ALR land can go firm in BC, but the firm deal does not override any restrictions imposed under the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36). Buyers should ensure that any required Agricultural Land Commission approvals — such as non-farm use applications or subdivision approvals — are addressed as conditions before the deal goes firm, because the ALC's jurisdiction over ALR land continues independently of the contract. Parties should consult the ALC directly for guidance on specific restrictions applicable to a given parcel.

What role does BCFSA play if a dispute arises over a firm deal in BC — for example, over deposit release?

BCFSA, as the regulator under RESA, oversees the conduct of real estate licensees, including their obligations to maintain deposits in trust and to not release those funds without proper authority. If a dispute arises between buyer and seller over a deposit following a firm deal, BCFSA does not adjudicate civil contract disputes — that is a matter for the BC courts or arbitration as specified in the contract. Licensees who improperly release trust funds may face disciplinary action by BCFSA, separate from any civil liability.

Verify with: BCREA — British Columbia Real Estate Association
If a buyer in BC dies after a deal goes firm but before completion, what happens to the contract?

Under the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA) of BC, the rights and obligations of a deceased person generally pass to their estate, meaning a firm contract of purchase and sale would typically become an obligation of the buyer's estate rather than being automatically voided. The executor or administrator of the estate would be responsible for completing the transaction or negotiating a resolution with the seller. Parties in this situation should seek independent legal advice promptly, as the specific terms of the contract and the estate's circumstances will govern the outcome.
