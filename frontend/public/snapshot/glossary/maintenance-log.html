Strata Documents

Maintenance Log

A maintenance log is a record of repairs and upkeep completed on a strata corporation's common property over time. The Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) requires strata corporations to repair and maintain common property, but does not expressly mandate a standardised maintenance log format; verify current requirements with a BC lawyer or notary. In practice, some strata corporations maintain detailed records while others do not. A thorough maintenance history helps prospective buyers assess whether common property has been consistently maintained or whether work has been deferred. Deferred maintenance can increase the likelihood of future special levies being approved by the strata corporation. Maintenance records are commonly included in the strata document package requested during a purchase transaction in BC.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a strata corporation in BC legally required to maintain a maintenance log?

The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, does not explicitly require a strata corporation to keep a formal "maintenance log." However, under s. 35 of the SPA, a strata corporation must keep adequate books and records, and under s. 36, must prepare a depreciation report (unless a 3/4 vote waives it), which inherently relies on maintenance history. Verify with a BC lawyer or notary whether your strata's record-keeping obligations extend to a specific maintenance log format.

Can a buyer request a maintenance log when purchasing a strata unit in BC?

Yes. Under s. 59 of the Strata Property Act and the Strata Property Regulation, a buyer may request Form B (the Information Certificate) and other strata documents, which commonly include maintenance records if the strata keeps them. The strata corporation is not statutorily required to provide a formal maintenance log if one does not exist, but prudent buyers often ask for any available maintenance history. Verify the scope of available records with the strata or a BC lawyer before completing a purchase.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
How does a maintenance log relate to the depreciation report required under BC's Strata Property Act?

Under s. 94 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation must obtain a depreciation report at least once every fiscal year that is a reporting year (unless waived by 3/4 vote). A depreciation report estimates the repair and replacement cost of common property and common assets; a detailed maintenance log helps the report's author assess the condition and lifespan of building components. Verify depreciation report requirements and timelines with a BC lawyer, notary, or qualified depreciation report provider.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
What information is typically included in a strata corporation's maintenance log?

A maintenance log commonly includes dates of repairs, descriptions of work performed, the common property element serviced (e.g., roof, elevator, boiler), contractor names, and costs. Because the Strata Property Act does not prescribe a specific format, the level of detail varies by strata. Verify what records your strata maintains by requesting documents under s. 36 of the SPA or by consulting with the strata council.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Can poor maintenance history shown in a log affect a strata unit's resale value?

Yes, in practice. Evidence of deferred maintenance increases the likelihood that a strata corporation will levy special assessments to fund overdue repairs, which can reduce buyer demand and affect market value. Under s. 108 of the Strata Property Act, a strata may raise a special levy by 3/4 vote to pay for repairs; buyers review maintenance logs and depreciation reports to assess this risk. Verify the financial and legal implications with a BC real estate lawyer or notary before listing or purchasing.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Are maintenance logs considered part of the strata's books and records under BC law?

Section 35 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, requires a strata corporation to "prepare and retain adequate books and records." While the Act does not list "maintenance log" by name, records of expenditures, contracts, and repairs are generally included in prudent record-keeping. Verify your strata's specific obligations and record retention policies with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
How long must a strata corporation in BC keep maintenance records?

The Strata Property Regulation does not specify a minimum retention period for maintenance logs. Under s. 36(3) of the Strata Property Act, certain financial records (annual financial statements, depreciation reports) must be kept for at least six years, but maintenance logs are not explicitly covered. Many well-managed stratas retain maintenance records indefinitely for historical reference. Verify retention best practices with a BC strata management professional or lawyer.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Can an owner access the strata's maintenance log under BC's Strata Property Act?

Yes. Under s. 36(1) of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, an owner, tenant, or person authorized in writing by an owner may inspect the books and records of the strata corporation at a mutually agreeable time. If the strata maintains a maintenance log, it is generally considered part of the books and records. Verify access procedures and any applicable fees with your strata council or a BC lawyer.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Does BC law define what constitutes "deferred maintenance" in a strata context?

No. Neither the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, nor the Strata Property Regulation provides a statutory definition of "deferred maintenance." In practice, the term refers to necessary repairs or replacements postponed beyond their recommended or useful life, often identified in a depreciation report prepared under s. 94 of the SPA. Verify the condition of specific building components with a qualified building envelope consultant, engineer, or BC lawyer before purchasing or assessing special levy risk.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Can a real estate licensee in BC be disciplined for failing to advise a buyer to review a strata's maintenance log?

Potentially, yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules administered by the BC Financial Services Authority, a licensee must act in the client's best interests and provide competent service; advising a buyer client to review all available strata documents—including maintenance records—is considered prudent practice. The BCFSA may investigate complaints of professional misconduct. Verify your obligations or file a complaint at www.bcfsa.ca or consult a BC lawyer if you believe a licensee failed in their duties.
