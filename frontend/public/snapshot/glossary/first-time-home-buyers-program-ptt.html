First Time Home Buyers' Program (PTT)

A provincial tax exemption available under the Property Transfer Tax Act (RSBC 1996, c. 378) for qualifying first-time home buyers in BC. As of 2026-07-27 — verify current, a full exemption may apply on properties up to $835,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) fair market value, with a partial exemption phasing out up to $860,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Maximum potential savings reach $8,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) per qualifying transaction. Eligibility conditions include residency, citizenship, and occupancy requirements; verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional. This exemption is distinct from the federal First Home Savings Account and Home Buyers' Plan, which are savings vehicles governed by federal legislation — this is a provincial tax exemption only. Confirm all thresholds and eligibility criteria at BC Ministry of Finance / gov.bc.ca (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the First Time Home Buyers' Program (PTT) in British Columbia?

The First Time Home Buyers' Program is a Property Transfer Tax (PTT) exemption available under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, for qualifying first-time purchasers of BC residential property. As of 2026-07-27 — verify current, eligible buyers receive a full exemption on properties up to $835,000 fair market value, with a partial exemption phasing out to $860,000, for maximum savings of up to $8,000. This is a provincial tax exemption administered by the BC Ministry of Finance, distinct from federal savings programs such as the First Home Savings Account (FHSA) or Home Buyers' Plan (HBP). Verify current thresholds and eligibility criteria with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before relying on this exemption.

Verify with: Government of BC
Who qualifies as a 'first-time home buyer' under the BC PTT exemption?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, a first-time home buyer is generally an individual who has never owned an interest in residential property anywhere in the world and who is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident. The applicant must also occupy the property as their principal residence within a specified period and continue to occupy it for at least one year (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Specific ownership history and residency rules apply; verify your eligibility with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before completing your purchase.

Verify with: Government of BC
What are the fair market value thresholds for the BC First Time Home Buyers' PTT exemption?

Effective April 1, 2024 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), the full exemption applies to properties with a fair market value up to $835,000, and a partial exemption phases out for properties valued between $835,000 and $860,000 under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378. Properties valued over $860,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) do not qualify for any portion of the first-time buyer exemption. These thresholds are set by regulation and may change; always verify the current limits with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC lawyer or notary before relying on the exemption.

Verify with: Government of BC
How much money can a first-time home buyer save using the BC PTT exemption?

The maximum tax savings under the First Time Home Buyers' Program is up to $8,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), which corresponds to the Property Transfer Tax otherwise payable on a qualifying property at the program's upper threshold under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378. The actual savings depend on the purchase price and whether the buyer receives the full or partial exemption. Verify the precise calculation for your purchase price with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before closing.

Verify with: Government of BC
What is the residency or occupancy requirement for the BC First Time Home Buyers' PTT exemption?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, qualifying first-time buyers must occupy the property as their principal residence and continue to do so for at least one year (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Failure to meet the occupancy requirement may result in repayment of the exempted tax plus penalty and interest. Specific exceptions and enforcement details are set out in the Act and regulations; verify your obligations with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before you purchase.

Verify with: Government of BC
Does the BC First Time Home Buyers' PTT exemption apply to all types of property?

The exemption under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, generally applies to residential property, including detached homes, townhouses, condominiums (stratified properties under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43), and certain other dwelling types, provided they will be the buyer's principal residence. Vacant land, recreational properties, and properties not intended for principal residence typically do not qualify (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify eligibility for your specific property type with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before applying for the exemption.

Verify with: Government of BC
Can a non-Canadian claim the BC First Time Home Buyers' PTT exemption?

No. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, applicants for the First Time Home Buyers' exemption must be Canadian citizens or permanent residents (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Foreign nationals, including temporary residents and non-permanent visa holders, are not eligible for this exemption. Note that additional Property Transfer Tax (the 'Additional PTT' at 20% as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) may apply to foreign nationals or foreign corporations under separate provisions of the Act; verify your status and obligations with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance.

Verify with: Government of BC
Is the BC First Time Home Buyers' PTT exemption the same as the federal First Home Savings Account (FHSA)?

No. The BC First Time Home Buyers' Program is a provincial Property Transfer Tax exemption under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, which reduces or eliminates the tax payable on registration of title. The First Home Savings Account (FHSA) is a federal registered savings plan administered under the Income Tax Act (Canada) that allows tax-deductible contributions and tax-free withdrawals for a first home purchase. These are separate programs with different rules, administered by different levels of government; verify eligibility and benefits for each with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Verify with: Government of BC
How do I apply for the BC First Time Home Buyers' PTT exemption?

Application for the exemption is made at the time of property registration through the BC Land Title Office, typically by completing the appropriate forms as part of the property transfer process under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, and the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Your lawyer or notary handling the conveyance will usually prepare and file the exemption claim on your behalf (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the required documentation, declarations, and filing procedures with your BC lawyer or notary before closing to ensure the exemption is claimed correctly.

Verify with: Government of BC
What happens if I sell or stop living in the property before the one-year occupancy period ends?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, if you fail to occupy the property as your principal residence for the required period (currently one year as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), you may be required to repay the exempted Property Transfer Tax, plus penalty and interest. Limited exceptions may apply for circumstances such as death, employment relocation, or relationship breakdown, as set out in the Act and regulations. Verify your specific obligations and any available relief with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before making decisions that would affect your occupancy.

Verify with: Government of BC
