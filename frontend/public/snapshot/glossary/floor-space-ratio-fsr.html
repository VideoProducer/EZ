Floor Space Ratio (FSR)

Floor Space Ratio (FSR) is the ratio of a building's total floor area to the area of the lot on which it sits. For example, an FSR of 0.5 applied to a 6,000 sq ft lot would permit up to 3,000 sq ft of total floor area (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). FSR limits are established by local government zoning bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, and vary by zone and municipality. A higher FSR allows greater buildable floor area on the same lot. FSR is a key metric for developers, property owners, and parties planning new construction or renovation. Verify applicable FSR for a specific property with the relevant local government or a BC lawyer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Floor Space Ratio (FSR) in British Columbia real estate?

Floor Space Ratio (FSR) is the ratio of a building's total floor area to the size of the lot on which it sits. For example, an FSR of 0.5 on a 6,000 square foot lot permits up to 3,000 square feet of total floor area. FSR is set by municipal zoning bylaws under authority granted by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, and determines the maximum permitted floor area on a lot. Verify current FSR limits for any specific property with the local municipality or a BC lawyer or notary before planning construction or renovation.

Verify with: BC Government Housing
Who sets FSR limits in British Columbia?

FSR limits are set by individual municipalities through their zoning bylaws, under authority granted by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Each municipality (e.g., City of Vancouver, District of North Vancouver, City of Victoria) adopts its own zoning bylaws that specify FSR for different zones and neighborhoods. FSR limits vary widely across BC municipalities and even within different zones of the same municipality. Verify the applicable FSR for any property by reviewing the municipal zoning bylaw or consulting with a BC lawyer, notary, or municipal planning department.

Verify with: BC Government Housing
Does the province of BC set a standard FSR for all municipalities?

No. The Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, grants municipalities the authority to regulate land use, density, and building form through zoning bylaws, but does not impose a standard provincial FSR. Each municipality independently determines FSR limits based on local planning objectives, community plans, and zoning designations. Verify the FSR applicable to any lot by consulting the relevant municipal zoning bylaw or a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Government Housing
How does FSR affect what I can build on my lot?

FSR determines the maximum total floor area permitted on your lot, regardless of the number of storeys or building footprint. For instance, if your lot is 5,000 square feet and the zoning bylaw permits an FSR of 0.6, the total allowable floor area is 3,000 square feet (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current municipal rules). This floor area can be distributed over one or more storeys, subject to other zoning regulations such as height limits, setbacks, and lot coverage. Verify the specific FSR and all other applicable zoning requirements with the local municipality or a BC lawyer or notary before commencing design or construction.

Verify with: BC Government Housing
Does FSR include basements, garages, or balconies?

What counts toward FSR calculation varies by municipality and is defined in each municipal zoning bylaw under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Some municipalities exclude basements, unenclosed balconies, or garages from FSR calculations, while others include all enclosed floor area regardless of use or level. Verify the definition of "floor area" or "floor space" in the applicable municipal zoning bylaw or consult with a BC lawyer, notary, or municipal planner before finalizing building plans.

Verify with: BC Government Housing
Can FSR be increased or varied for my property?

FSR may be increased through municipal processes such as rezoning, development permits with variance, or density bonus provisions, all governed by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Some municipalities offer higher FSR in exchange for public amenities, affordable housing units, or compliance with green building standards. Any increase in FSR requires municipal approval and is subject to public consultation and council approval processes. Verify the process, timelines, and requirements for FSR increases with the local municipality or a BC lawyer or notary before applying.

Verify with: BC Government Housing
How does Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing (SSMUH) legislation affect FSR in BC?

The Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023 (BC Bill 44) requires most BC municipalities to allow small-scale multi-unit housing (up to 3–6 units depending on lot characteristics) in areas previously zoned for single-family homes, effective July 1, 2024 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The legislation sets minimum FSR requirements for affected lots, though specific FSR rules vary by municipality and lot size. Verify the applicable SSMUH FSR and zoning rules with the local municipality or a BC lawyer or notary before planning multi-unit development.

Verify with: BC Government Housing
Does FSR affect property value or Property Transfer Tax in BC?

FSR does not directly determine property value or Property Transfer Tax (PTT) liability, but higher permitted FSR typically increases a lot's development potential and market value. PTT is calculated on the fair market value of the property at the time of registration under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, regardless of FSR. Higher FSR may lead to higher assessed or market value, which in turn affects PTT payable. Verify PTT obligations and current exemption thresholds with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before completing a purchase.

Verify with: BC Government Housing
Where can I find the FSR for a specific property in BC?

FSR for a specific property is found in the municipal zoning bylaw applicable to that property, available from the local municipality's planning department or online zoning map and bylaw database. Each municipality administers its own zoning bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Title documents (available through the BC Land Title and Survey Authority) do not typically show FSR, as zoning is a municipal regulatory matter, not a registered interest on title. Verify the current FSR and zoning designation for any property by contacting the municipality or consulting a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Government Housing
Can FSR differ within the same municipality?

Yes. Municipal zoning bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, typically designate multiple zoning categories (e.g., residential, commercial, mixed-use) with different FSR limits for each zone and sometimes for specific sub-areas or neighborhoods. For example, downtown or transit-oriented areas often permit higher FSR than low-density residential neighborhoods. Verify the zoning designation and corresponding FSR for any lot by reviewing the municipal zoning bylaw and official community plan, or by consulting a BC lawyer, notary, or municipal planner.
