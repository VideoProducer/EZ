Form K — Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities

Form K — Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities is a prescribed form under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, that an owner must provide to a tenant before the tenant occupies a strata lot. By receiving Form K, the tenant acknowledges that the strata corporation's bylaws and rules are binding on them in the same manner as on an owner. Where a tenant breaches those bylaws or rules, the strata corporation may levy a fine against the owner of the strata lot rather than the tenant directly; verify current fine amounts and enforcement procedures with a BC lawyer or notary. Buyers purchasing a tenanted strata unit should confirm that the existing tenant has been served with Form K, as failure to do so may affect the enforceability of bylaw obligations against that tenant. For the prescribed form and current procedural requirements, verify details against the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and consult a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Form K in British Columbia strata law?

Form K is the "Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities" required under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the Strata Property Regulation. It is a prescribed form that must be given to every tenant in a strata building to inform them that they are bound by the strata corporation's bylaws and rules just as an owner would be. The form serves as official notice of the tenant's obligations under the Strata Property Act.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Who is legally required to give Form K to a tenant in a strata unit?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the Strata Property Regulation, the landlord (owner of the strata lot) or the strata corporation must provide Form K to the tenant. The specific delivery obligation is set out in the Strata Property Regulation. Verify the current delivery requirements and timing with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
When must Form K be delivered to a tenant?

The Strata Property Regulation under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, prescribes when Form K must be delivered to a tenant. The general requirement is that it must be provided before or at the start of a tenancy. Verify the precise timing requirement in the current Strata Property Regulation with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Are tenants in a strata building legally bound by the strata bylaws and rules?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, tenants and occupants of a strata lot must comply with the strata corporation's bylaws and rules to the same extent as an owner must comply. Form K serves as formal notice of this legal obligation. Failure by a tenant to comply can result in consequences for both the tenant and the owner.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
What happens if a tenant violates strata bylaws or rules?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, if a tenant violates strata bylaws or rules, the strata corporation may levy fines against the owner of the strata lot. The owner is responsible for their tenant's compliance with bylaws and rules. The strata corporation may also pursue dispute resolution or other enforcement mechanisms set out in the Strata Property Act; verify the current enforcement procedures with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
As a buyer of a tenanted strata unit, why should I care about Form K?

If you are purchasing a strata lot with an existing tenant, you inherit the tenancy and the legal obligation to ensure the tenant complies with strata bylaws and rules under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. If the tenant has not received Form K or does not understand their obligations, you may face fines or enforcement action from the strata corporation for the tenant's violations. Verify that Form K has been properly delivered and that the tenant is aware of their responsibilities before completing the purchase.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Can a strata corporation fine an owner for their tenant's breach of bylaws even if the owner gave Form K?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, the owner is responsible for ensuring that their tenant complies with strata bylaws and rules, regardless of whether Form K was delivered. Delivery of Form K is a statutory requirement, but it does not absolve the owner of liability for a tenant's violations. The strata corporation may levy fines against the owner for the tenant's non-compliance.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Is Form K the same as a tenancy agreement or lease?

No. Form K is a statutory notice required under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, that informs the tenant of their obligation to comply with strata bylaws and rules. A tenancy agreement or lease is governed by the Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78, and sets out the contractual terms between landlord and tenant (rent, duration, responsibilities, etc.). Both documents serve different purposes and are governed by different statutes.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Where can I obtain a copy of the official Form K in British Columbia?

The prescribed Form K is published in the Strata Property Regulation under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. Copies of the official form are available from the BC government's website (www.gov.bc.ca) and may be provided by the strata corporation or the owner's legal advisor. Verify that you are using the current version of the form with a BC lawyer or notary before delivering it to a tenant.

Verify with: BC Government Strata Housing
Does Form K apply to short-term rentals or only long-term tenancies in a strata building?

The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, applies to all occupants of a strata lot, including short-term renters, and they must comply with strata bylaws and rules. However, the specific delivery requirement for Form K is set out in the Strata Property Regulation, and the application to short-term rentals may depend on the type of occupancy and strata bylaws (many strata corporations restrict or prohibit short-term rentals). Verify the current regulation and your strata's bylaws with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.
