Licensed Residential Builder

A Licensed Residential Builder is a builder licensed by BC Housing under the Homeowner Protection Act, RSBC 1996, c. 193, to construct and sell new homes in British Columbia. Licensing under that Act requires builders to meet ongoing requirements related to new home warranty coverage, education, and conduct standards. Builder licence status and history are publicly searchable through the BC Housing Licensee Registry at bchousing.org. Buyers of presale or newly built homes should verify a builder's current licence status directly through that registry before entering into a purchase contract, and should confirm any applicable warranty obligations with a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Licensed Residential Builder in British Columbia?

A Licensed Residential Builder is a builder licensed by BC Housing under the Homeowner Protection Act, SBC 1998, c. 29, to construct new homes for sale in BC. Licensing requires compliance with statutory warranty obligations, mandatory education, and conduct standards. Builder licence status and history are publicly searchable through the BC Housing Licensee Registry at www.bchousing.org.

Verify with: Licensing
Is a builder licence required to build new homes for sale in BC?

Yes. Under the Homeowner Protection Act and its regulations, a person must hold a valid residential builder licence from BC Housing to construct or cause to be constructed a new home for sale in British Columbia. Building without a valid licence where required is prohibited and subject to enforcement. Verify current licensing requirements and exemptions with BC Housing or a BC lawyer before acting.

Verify with: Licensing
What statutory warranty does a Licensed Residential Builder provide?

Under the Homeowner Protection Act, Licensed Residential Builders must enrol each new home in third-party home warranty insurance that provides statutory warranty coverage including defects in materials and labour, building envelope defects, and structural defects for prescribed periods. The warranty insurance protects homebuyers if the builder defaults on warranty obligations. Verify current warranty terms and coverage limits with BC Housing or a BC lawyer before acting.

Verify with: Licensing
How can I verify if a builder is licensed in BC?

BC Housing maintains a publicly searchable online Licensee Registry that shows current licence status, licensing history, and any disciplinary actions for residential builders. The registry is accessible at www.bchousing.org. Verify the builder's licence status directly through the official registry before entering into any contract for new home construction.

Verify with: Licensing
What education requirements must a Licensed Residential Builder meet?

The Homeowner Protection Act and its regulations require residential builders to complete mandatory education as a condition of initial licensing and ongoing licence renewal. Specific course requirements and exemptions are set by BC Housing and may vary by licence class and builder experience. Verify current education requirements with BC Housing before acting.

Verify with: Licensing
Can BC Housing discipline or suspend a Licensed Residential Builder?

Yes. Under the Homeowner Protection Act, BC Housing has authority to investigate complaints, impose conditions on a builder's licence, suspend or cancel a licence, or impose administrative penalties for non-compliance with the Act, regulations, or licence conditions. Disciplinary actions and licence status changes are recorded in the public Licensee Registry. Verify the regulatory framework and any specific case details with BC Housing or a BC lawyer before acting.

Verify with: Licensing
Does a Licensed Residential Builder licence apply to renovations or repairs?

The Homeowner Protection Act primarily regulates the construction of new homes for sale, not general renovations or repairs. A residential builder licence is typically not required for renovation work unless the work constitutes construction of a new home as defined in the Act and regulations. Verify whether specific work requires a builder licence with BC Housing or a BC lawyer before acting.

Verify with: Licensing
What is the difference between a Licensed Residential Builder and a licensed real estate professional in BC?

A Licensed Residential Builder is licensed by BC Housing under the Homeowner Protection Act to construct new homes for sale. A licensed real estate professional (managing broker, associate broker, or representative) is licensed by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, to provide real estate services such as selling, buying, or leasing real property. These are separate regulatory regimes with different licensing bodies.

Verify with: Licensing
Are owner-builders exempt from the Licensed Residential Builder requirement?

The Homeowner Protection Act and its regulations provide an owner-builder exemption for individuals who build a home for their own occupation and do not intend to sell it within a prescribed period. Specific conditions, time limits, and documentation apply to the owner-builder exemption. Verify eligibility and current exemption rules with BC Housing or a BC lawyer before acting, as improper use of the exemption may result in enforcement action.

Verify with: Licensing
Where can I find more information about Licensed Residential Builders in BC?

BC Housing administers the residential builder licensing program and maintains comprehensive resources at www.bchousing.org, including the Licensee Registry, application guides, and regulatory bulletins. For legal interpretation of the Homeowner Protection Act or advice on a specific transaction or dispute, verify details with a BC lawyer or notary public. For general consumer information, contact BC Housing directly.
