CMHC Mortgage Default Insurance

If a borrower's down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price the mortgage must be insured against default. In Canada this insurance is provided by CMHC, Sagen, or Canada Guaranty. The premium is added to the mortgage and ranges from 2.8% to 4% of the mortgage amount depending on the down payment size. On a $700,000 home with 5% down the CMHC premium is roughly $26,600 added to the mortgage. This protects the lender — not the borrower — but it allows purchases with less than 20% down.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is CMHC Mortgage Default Insurance and why is it required in British Columbia?

CMHC Mortgage Default Insurance is mandatory in Canada, including British Columbia, when a borrower's down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price. It protects the lender — not the borrower — against loss if the borrower defaults on the mortgage. The insurance is provided by one of three approved insurers: CMHC, Sagen, or Canada Guaranty, and the premium is added to the mortgage principal rather than paid upfront.

Verify with: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation
What are the current CMHC insurance premium rates in BC, and how are they calculated?

The premium ranges from 2.8% to 4% of the insured mortgage amount, depending on the size of the down payment — the smaller the down payment, the higher the premium percentage. For example, on a $700,000 home purchased with a 5% down payment, the insured mortgage amount would be $665,000, generating a premium of approximately $26,600 that is added to the mortgage balance. Borrowers should consult their lender or mortgage broker for the precise rate tier that applies to their specific loan-to-value ratio.

Verify with: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation
Is there a maximum purchase price limit for CMHC-insured mortgages in BC?

Yes. Under federal rules, high-ratio insured mortgages are only available for properties with a purchase price below the applicable federal threshold; as of 2026, properties priced at $1,500,000 or more are not eligible for CMHC mortgage default insurance. Because many properties in Metro Vancouver and other BC markets exceed this threshold, buyers of higher-priced homes must have a minimum 20% down payment to obtain conventional (uninsured) financing. Buyers should confirm the current federal threshold with their lender, as it is set by federal regulation rather than BC statute.

Verify with: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation
Does CMHC mortgage default insurance affect how much Property Transfer Tax a BC buyer pays?

CMHC mortgage default insurance itself does not reduce or eliminate Property Transfer Tax (PTT) payable under BC's Property Transfer Tax Act. PTT is calculated on the fair market value of the property at standard rates of 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion up to $2,000,000, 3% on the portion between $2,000,000 and $3,000,000, and an additional 2% on any residential value above $3,000,000. Separate exemptions, such as the First-Time Home Buyer exemption (for eligible properties up to $835,000) or the Newly Built Home exemption (up to $1,100,000), may reduce PTT but are governed by the Property Transfer Tax Act independently of mortgage insurance.

Verify with: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation
Do BC first-time home buyers still need CMHC insurance if they qualify for the First-Time Home Buyer PTT exemption?

Yes — the First-Time Home Buyer exemption under BC's Property Transfer Tax Act reduces or eliminates the Property Transfer Tax payable on eligible purchases up to $835,000, but it has no bearing on whether CMHC mortgage default insurance is required. If a first-time buyer's down payment is less than 20% of the purchase price, federal rules still require the mortgage to be insured regardless of any PTT exemption. The two programs operate independently under different legislative frameworks.

Verify with: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation
Can a CMHC-insured mortgage be used to purchase a strata lot in British Columbia?

Yes, a CMHC-insured mortgage can be used to purchase a strata lot, such as a condominium or townhouse, provided the property meets the insurer's eligibility criteria. Strata lots in BC are governed by the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), and matters such as strata bylaws, contingency reserve funds, and depreciation reports are relevant to the overall financial health of the strata corporation. Lenders and insurers may review strata documents — including Form B (Information Certificate) — as part of their underwriting process to assess the financial stability of the strata.

Verify with: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation
What disclosure obligations does a BC real estate licensee have regarding CMHC mortgage default insurance?

BC real estate licensees are regulated by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and its Rules, which require licensees to act honestly and in good faith and to disclose material information relevant to a trade in real estate. While licensees are not mortgage professionals and cannot give mortgage advice, they should ensure buyers understand that an insured mortgage is required when the down payment is under 20%, as this is a material fact that can affect financing and the total cost of a purchase. Licensees who also hold a mortgage broker licence are subject to additional obligations under the Mortgage Brokers Act.

Verify with: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation
If a borrower defaults on a CMHC-insured mortgage in BC, what legal process does the lender follow to recover the property?

In British Columbia, mortgage enforcement is judicial — there is no power-of-sale process as exists in some other provinces. If a borrower defaults, the lender must commence foreclosure proceedings through the BC Supreme Court, governed by the BC Supreme Court Civil Rules and the Law and Equity Act. CMHC mortgage default insurance means that after the lender completes the foreclosure and suffers a loss, CMHC pays the lender's claim — but CMHC may then seek to recover that loss from the defaulting borrower.

Verify with: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation
Does CMHC mortgage default insurance apply to properties located in BC's Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR)?

A property's location within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), administered under BC's Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36), does not automatically disqualify it from CMHC mortgage default insurance, but the insurer's eligibility rules and the lender's underwriting criteria will apply. ALR properties are subject to use restrictions imposed by the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC), which may affect the property's marketability and value — both factors lenders and insurers consider. Buyers of ALR properties should consult the ALC directly regarding permitted uses and consult their lender regarding insurability.

Verify with: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation
How does CMHC mortgage default insurance interact with privacy obligations when a BC licensee collects a client's financial information?

When a BC real estate licensee collects a client's personal financial information — such as income details or credit information relevant to their ability to obtain a CMHC-insured mortgage — the licensee must handle that information in accordance with BC's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), which governs the collection, use, and disclosure of personal information by private-sector organizations in BC. PIPA requires that personal information be collected only for identified purposes, with the individual's consent, and be protected by reasonable security safeguards. Licensees should limit collection to what is reasonably necessary and must not disclose client financial information to third parties without appropriate authorization.
