Kitec Plumbing

A brand of aluminum-core plumbing pipe widely installed from the 1990s to 2007, subject to a class action settlement due to premature failures. Presence of Kitec may affect insurance and resale.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Kitec plumbing and why is it a concern for BC home buyers?

Kitec is a brand of flexible piping with an aluminum core and plastic inner and outer layers, widely installed in BC homes from the mid-1990s until approximately 2007. It was subject to a North American class action settlement after reports of premature corrosion, fitting failures, and potential water damage. Its presence in a property can affect home insurance availability and resale value in British Columbia.

Are BC real estate licensees required to disclose known Kitec plumbing to buyers?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and the standards of conduct enforced by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), licensees acting as agents must disclose all known material latent defects — including defects that could make a property dangerous or unfit for habitation — to buyers. Because Kitec plumbing failures can cause significant water damage, known presence of Kitec would generally be considered a material latent defect triggering disclosure obligations. Licensees should review BCFSA guidance on disclosure duties to ensure full compliance.

Does a BC seller have a legal obligation to disclose Kitec plumbing to a buyer?

In British Columbia, sellers are generally obligated to disclose known material latent defects, including conditions that could render a property dangerous or unfit for its intended use, and concealing such defects can give rise to civil liability. While the Property Disclosure Statement used in most residential transactions asks sellers about known defects in plumbing and water systems, sellers should be truthful about known Kitec installations. Buyers and sellers should consult their licensees and legal counsel regarding the specific disclosure obligations applicable to their transaction.

How can a buyer in BC determine whether a property contains Kitec plumbing?

The most reliable method is to retain a qualified home inspector or licensed plumber to physically inspect visible piping, fittings, and mechanical areas such as utility rooms and under sinks, looking for the distinctive orange or blue flexible pipes and fittings stamped with 'Kitec' or brand names such as 'PlumbBetter', 'IPEX', or 'AmbioComfort'. Buyers can also review any available strata documentation, building permits, or previous inspection reports. In strata properties, the strata corporation's records obtainable under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) may reference plumbing issues or remediation work.

How does Kitec plumbing affect strata corporations and common property in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), the strata corporation is responsible for the repair and maintenance of common property and common assets, which can include main plumbing supply lines running through common areas. If Kitec plumbing is present in common property, the strata corporation may face significant remediation costs, which could affect the contingency reserve fund and potentially result in special levies against strata lot owners. Buyers of strata lots should review Form B (Information Certificate) and the most recent depreciation report to assess whether Kitec plumbing has been identified and what remediation, if any, is planned or completed.

Can a BC strata corporation's depreciation report address Kitec plumbing replacement costs?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and its Regulation, a depreciation report must include an inventory of the strata corporation's common assets and components, along with projected maintenance, repair, and replacement costs over a 30-year period. If Kitec plumbing is identified in common property or shared infrastructure, a properly prepared depreciation report should reflect anticipated replacement costs and timelines. Prospective buyers of strata units should carefully review the depreciation report and contingency reserve fund balance to understand how plumbing remediation costs may affect them financially.

Does the presence of Kitec plumbing affect home insurance for BC properties?

Many BC property insurers treat Kitec plumbing as a material risk and may decline coverage, impose exclusions for water damage related to plumbing failure, or require replacement before issuing or renewing a policy. Insurance decisions are made at the insurer's discretion based on their underwriting criteria, which can vary between companies. Buyers and owners should confirm with their insurance broker whether Kitec plumbing in a property will affect their coverage terms or premiums.

Was there a class action settlement related to Kitec plumbing that applied to BC homeowners?

Yes. A North American class action lawsuit against Kitec manufacturers and distributors resulted in a settlement fund, and Canadian homeowners, including those in British Columbia, were eligible to submit claims for reimbursement of replacement costs up to defined limits. The settlement claims period has now closed, and new claims are generally no longer being accepted. BC homeowners who have not yet replaced Kitec plumbing should consult a licensed plumber to assess the current condition of their systems and explore remediation options.

Does Kitec plumbing need to be disclosed in a Form B Information Certificate for a BC strata property?

Form B, issued under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), provides a buyer with key financial and legal information about a strata corporation but does not specifically require a line-item disclosure of plumbing types. However, if the strata corporation is aware of Kitec plumbing in common property and it represents a known defect or pending significant expenditure, that information may be reflected in the strata's minutes, depreciation report, or contingency reserve fund planning documents that accompany or supplement Form B. Buyers are strongly advised to review all strata records available to them before completing a purchase.

How should a BC real estate licensee handle a listing where the seller is uncertain whether the property contains Kitec plumbing?

Under BCFSA's standards of conduct for licensees under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), a licensee must act honestly and with reasonable care, and should not represent the plumbing system's condition as defect-free if there is genuine uncertainty. The licensee should recommend that the seller retain a plumber or home inspector to assess the plumbing before listing, and should ensure that any uncertainty about the plumbing type is accurately reflected in the Property Disclosure Statement rather than left unanswered or misrepresented. Licensees should consult current BCFSA guidance on disclosure obligations when dealing with material latent defect situations.
