Kitec Plumbing

Kitec plumbing refers to a brand of composite pipe, typically featuring an aluminum layer bonded between layers of plastic, that was installed in many BC homes and strata buildings during the 1990s and into the mid-2000s. The product became associated with premature failure, including fitting corrosion and pipe degradation, leading to a class action settlement; verify current details regarding that settlement with a BC lawyer or licensed professional. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, strata corporations have maintenance and repair obligations that may be engaged where Kitec is present. Buyers and owners should be aware that insurers may impose conditions, exclusions, or higher premiums for properties containing Kitec — verify current insurer requirements directly. Disclosure obligations for licensees are governed by the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and BCFSA guidance; known material latent defects, which may include Kitec, must be disclosed. Replacement costs can be significant; verify current estimates with a licensed plumber or contractor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Kitec plumbing?

Kitec is a brand of aluminum-core plumbing pipe and fittings installed widely in BC homes and condos from approximately the mid-1990s to 2007 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current installation period). It was subject to a North American class action settlement due to premature corrosion and failures. The presence of Kitec in a property may affect resale, financing, and homeowner insurance availability.

Verify with: Government of Canada Consumer Product Safety
Do I have to disclose Kitec plumbing when selling my BC home or strata unit?

Under BC's Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules, a licensee must disclose known material latent defects. If you, as the seller, are aware of Kitec plumbing, it is generally considered a material latent defect because it may affect property value, insurability, and safety. Verify current disclosure obligations with a BC lawyer, notary, or your licensed real estate professional before listing.

Verify with: Government of Canada Consumer Product Safety
Does the Strata Property Act require disclosure of Kitec in common property?

The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, requires a strata corporation to provide certain records and an Information Certificate (Form B) to buyers. If Kitec is present in common property or units and the strata council is aware of it, that information should be disclosed through the Information Certificate or depreciation report. Verify current strata disclosure requirements with a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing.

Verify with: Government of Canada Consumer Product Safety
Can I get homeowner insurance if my BC property has Kitec plumbing?

Insurance coverage for properties with Kitec plumbing varies by insurer; some insurers may decline coverage, restrict coverage, or charge higher premiums (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current insurer policies). This is a private-market decision and is not governed by a specific BC statute. Verify current insurability and policy terms with a licensed insurance broker or agent before purchasing or renewing a policy on a property with Kitec.

Verify with: Government of Canada Consumer Product Safety
Is there a BC government program to help pay for Kitec replacement?

As of 2026-07-27, there is no provincial BC government grant or subsidy program specifically for Kitec plumbing replacement (verify current programs with BC Housing or the BC Ministry of Finance). The original class action settlement fund closed in 2012 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Homeowners should verify eligibility for any current federal (e.g., CMHC) or municipal retrofit programs with a BC lawyer, notary, or the relevant agency.

Verify with: Government of Canada Consumer Product Safety
Does the presence of Kitec affect my property transfer tax in BC?

The Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, bases the tax on the fair market value of the property; the presence of Kitec may reduce fair market value, which could in turn reduce the PTT liability. However, the PTTA itself does not provide a specific exemption or credit for Kitec. Verify current PTT calculations and valuation with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before completing a transaction.

Verify with: Government of Canada Consumer Product Safety
Can a buyer back out of a purchase if Kitec is discovered during the inspection period?

BC real estate contracts (typically the Contract of Purchase and Sale approved by the BC Real Estate Association) commonly include a subject clause for satisfactory inspection. If Kitec is discovered and the buyer is not satisfied, the buyer may remove themselves from the contract during the subject removal period, subject to the specific wording of the clause. Verify the enforceability and current contract terms with a BC lawyer or notary before waiving subjects.

Verify with: Government of Canada Consumer Product Safety
Do strata corporations in BC have to replace Kitec in common property?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation has a statutory duty to repair and maintain common property and common assets. If Kitec is present and poses a safety or insurance risk, the strata may be required to repair or replace it, typically through a special levy or use of the contingency reserve fund. Verify current obligations and funding mechanisms with a BC lawyer, notary, or strata property manager before proceeding.

Verify with: Government of Canada Consumer Product Safety
Is Kitec still permitted to be installed in new construction in BC?

Kitec is no longer manufactured or approved for installation; it was withdrawn from the North American market in 2007 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). BC's building code (administered under provincial and municipal authority) requires compliance with current plumbing standards, which do not include Kitec. Verify current BC Building Code plumbing requirements with a BC licensed plumber, building official, or lawyer before any new installation.

Verify with: Government of Canada Consumer Product Safety
Where can I find more information about the Kitec class action settlement?

The Kitec class action settlement in Canada concluded with a claims deadline in 2012 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current status). Information may still be available through the settlement administrator's archived website or through counsel who administered the settlement. For current legal advice on remedies, replacement costs, or disclosure obligations, verify with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.
