Property Types

Private beach access

A claim of private beach access describes a situation where a property owner holds a legal right to use a beach area that is neither publicly owned nor publicly managed. Such rights are typically recorded against title under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, and may take the form of a right-of-way, easement, or restrictive covenant attached to the land. The exact nature, boundaries, and permitted uses of any such access are determined by the registered instrument, not by informal custom or advertisement. Buyers should obtain and review all title documents before relying on any representation of private beach access. Verify current details with a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is private beach access in British Columbia?

Private beach access refers to a right to use a beach or foreshore that is not publicly owned or managed, typically through a deeded access right, easement, or covenant registered on title under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. The right must be documented on the registered title or in a separate legal instrument to be enforceable. Verify the specific legal form and enforceability of any private beach access right with a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing a property.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Is private beach access always included with waterfront property ownership in BC?

No. In British Columbia, ownership of upland property does not automatically include ownership of or access to the foreshore or beach; the Crown may own the foreshore below the natural boundary (high water mark) under common law and the Land Act, RSBC 1996, c. 245. Private beach access must be expressly granted by deed, easement, covenant, or license registered under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Always verify ownership and access rights on the registered title with a BC lawyer or notary before assuming access is included.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
How can I verify if a BC property has private beach access?

Order a certified copy of the registered title and title search from the BC Land Title and Survey Authority, which operates under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, to review registered easements, covenants, or rights-of-way that grant beach access. A BC lawyer or notary can review the title documents and any accompanying plans to confirm the legal extent and enforceability of the access right. Verify current access rights and any restrictions with a BC lawyer or notary before completing a purchase.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can private beach access be shared with other property owners?

Yes. Private beach access may be granted as a shared easement or right-of-way benefiting multiple properties, or it may be restricted to a single property; the scope is determined by the registered instrument under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. If the property is part of a strata corporation, shared beach access may also be common property or limited common property under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. Verify the specific sharing arrangements, maintenance obligations, and user restrictions with a BC lawyer or notary before relying on the access.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does a real estate licensee in BC have to disclose private beach access details?

Yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules administered by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), licensees must not mislead and must provide material information to clients and customers. Private beach access—including whether it is deeded, the extent of the right, and any known restrictions—is typically material and must be disclosed accurately. Verify disclosure obligations and the specifics of any beach access right with a BC lawyer or notary and consult the current BCFSA Rules (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can private beach access rights be extinguished or lost in BC?

Yes. Private beach access rights may be extinguished by abandonment, release, expiry (if time-limited), cancellation by court order, or non-use under certain common-law doctrines, though registered easements under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, are generally not lost by mere non-use alone. Changes to title, Crown land tenure, or zoning under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, may also affect access rights. Verify the current status and enforceability of any beach access right with a BC lawyer or notary before relying on it.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Is property tax affected by private beach access in BC?

Property tax assessment is determined by BC Assessment Authority based on market value and property characteristics, which may reflect the enhanced value attributable to private beach access; however, no specific provincial statute mandates a separate tax rate solely for beach access. The BC Home Owner Grant Act provides a property tax grant for qualifying principal residences, regardless of beach access (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current eligibility and amounts). Verify how beach access affects your specific property assessment and tax with BC Assessment or a BC lawyer.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can a strata corporation in BC restrict use of private beach access designated as limited common property?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation may pass bylaws regulating the use and enjoyment of limited common property, including private beach access, provided the bylaws are not significantly unfair and comply with the Act. Bylaws must be filed in the Land Title Office to be enforceable against owners and occupants (Strata Property Act, s. 119 and s. 120). Verify the current strata bylaws, rules, and any restrictions on beach access with a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing a strata lot.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does the foreign buyer ban apply differently if a property has private beach access?

No. The Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10 (federal), prohibits non-Canadians from purchasing residential property in Canada (currently extended through January 1, 2027 as of 2026-07-27 — verify current status), regardless of whether the property includes private beach access. The prohibition is based on the residential use of the property, not its amenities. Verify your eligibility and any exemptions under the federal Act with a BC lawyer or licensed tax professional before purchasing.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
What should a buyer do if private beach access is advertised but not on the registered title?

The buyer should immediately request written confirmation and documentation of the access right from the seller and have a BC lawyer or notary review the registered title and all supporting documents under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. If the access is not registered or legally enforceable, the buyer may refuse to complete the purchase, negotiate a price reduction, or require the seller to register the access as a condition of closing. Never rely on verbal representations or advertisements alone; verify all access rights on the registered title with a BC lawyer or notary before removing subjects or waiving conditions.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
