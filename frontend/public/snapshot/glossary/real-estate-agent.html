Process & General

Real Estate Agent

In British Columbia, a real estate agent is a person licensed under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), to provide real estate services such as buying, selling, leasing, or managing property on behalf of clients. Licensing does not require membership in a local real estate board or the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA). Agents who voluntarily join CREA through a local board may use the registered REALTOR® trademark and access certain CREA member-only services; agents who are not CREA members cannot use that trademark or those services. Verify current licensing requirements and membership details with BCFSA at bcfsa.ca or a BC lawyer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who regulates real estate agents in British Columbia?

All real estate agents in British Columbia are licensed and regulated by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42. The BCFSA oversees licensing, professional standards, and compliance for all real estate professionals in the province. Verify current BCFSA licensing requirements at www.bcfsa.ca or with a BC lawyer before relying on any licensing information.

What is the difference between a real estate agent and a REALTOR® in BC?

Every REALTOR® in BC is a licensed real estate agent regulated by the BCFSA under the Real Estate Services Act, but not every BC real estate agent is a REALTOR®. A REALTOR® is a licensed agent who has also chosen to join the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA) through a local real estate board and agrees to abide by CREA's Code of Ethics and Standards of Business Practice. Only CREA members may use the REALTOR® trademark and access REALTOR.ca and certain MLS® system privileges.

Do I need to hire a REALTOR®, or can I work with any BC real estate agent?

You may work with any real estate agent licensed by the BCFSA under the Real Estate Services Act—REALTOR® membership is optional and does not affect an agent's provincial licensing or legal authority to represent buyers or sellers. The key difference is that REALTOR® members have access to CREA's REALTOR.ca platform and certain MLS® services, and they commit to CREA's Code of Ethics. Verify the licensing status of any agent through the BCFSA public registry at www.bcfsa.ca before entering into a service agreement.

What law governs the services a BC real estate agent can provide?

The Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and the Rules made under it establish the scope of practice, licensing, disclosure obligations, fiduciary duties, and professional conduct standards for all BC real estate agents. The BCFSA enforces these provisions and may discipline licensees for misconduct. Verify current RESA Rules and BCFSA bulletins at www.bcfsa.ca or consult a BC lawyer for interpretation of specific provisions.

Are BC real estate agents required to carry insurance?

Yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act and BCFSA Rules, all licensed real estate agents in BC must maintain errors and omissions (E&O) insurance as a condition of licensure. The BCFSA sets minimum coverage requirements, which are subject to change. Verify current insurance thresholds and coverage details directly with the BCFSA or your BC lawyer, notary, or licensed insurance professional.

Verify with: Canadian Real Estate Association — The Difference Between a REALTOR® and a Real Estate Agent
Can a BC real estate agent represent both the buyer and the seller in the same transaction?

Under the Real Estate Services Act and BCFSA Rules, dual agency (representing both buyer and seller) is permitted in BC only with informed written consent from both parties and full disclosure of the conflict. The agent must act fairly and disclose all relevant information, but their ability to advocate for either party is limited. Verify disclosure and consent requirements under current RESA Rules and consult a BC lawyer or notary before entering a dual-agency arrangement.

What happens if a BC real estate agent breaches their duties?

The BCFSA has authority under the Real Estate Services Act to investigate complaints, impose discipline (including fines, licence suspension, or cancellation), and order restitution. Clients may also have civil remedies for breach of contract or fiduciary duty. Verify complaint procedures and timelines at www.bcfsa.ca or with a BC lawyer before taking action.

Do BC real estate agents need to protect my personal information?

Yes. BC real estate agents and brokerages are subject to British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), SBC 2003, c. 63, which governs the collection, use, and disclosure of personal information in the private sector. Agents must obtain consent, limit collection to reasonable purposes, and safeguard your data. Verify current PIPA obligations and your privacy rights with the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for BC or a BC lawyer.

Can a BC real estate agent send me marketing emails without my permission?

No. Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL), SC 2010, c. 23, requires express or implied consent before sending commercial electronic messages, including real estate marketing emails. There are limited exceptions for existing business relationships. Verify CASL consent rules and exemptions with the Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission (CRTC) at www.fightspam.gc.ca or consult a lawyer versed in federal CASL compliance.

Where can I verify that a real estate agent is licensed in BC?

You can verify any BC real estate agent's licence status, disciplinary history, and brokerage affiliation through the public registry maintained by the BCFSA at www.bcfsa.ca. The BCFSA registry is updated regularly and is the authoritative source for licensing information under the Real Estate Services Act. Always confirm current licence status before signing a representation agreement or any contract.
