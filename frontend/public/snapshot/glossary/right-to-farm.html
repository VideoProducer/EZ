Right to Farm

Right to Farm is a legal protection established under BC's Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, which shields qualifying farm operators from nuisance-related complaints — such as those involving odour, noise, or dust — arising from normal farm practices. This protection applies where the farm operation is conducted in a reasonable manner and in accordance with generally accepted farm practices. The Act works alongside the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, which governs land use within the Agricultural Land Reserve. The protection does not apply where practices are unreasonable or inconsistent with accepted standards. Buyers of properties near designated farming areas should be aware that these protections may limit recourse against neighbouring farm operations. Verify current scope, eligibility conditions, and any administrative processes with a BC lawyer or licensed professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act in British Columbia?

The Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, is a British Columbia statute that protects farmers from nuisance lawsuits when they carry on normal farm practices in permitted farming areas. Under this Act, a farmer is not liable in nuisance to any person for any odour, noise, dust, or other disturbance resulting from an agricultural operation conducted as part of a farm business in accordance with normal farm practices and in a manner consistent with proper and accepted customs and standards. Does the Right to Farm Act protect all farm activities in British Columbia?

The Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, provides protection only when the farm operation is conducted (a) in accordance with normal farm practices, (b) as part of a farm business, and (c) on land in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) or on land designated for agricultural use by a municipal zoning bylaw. The protection does not extend to activities that are not normal farm practices or that are carried on in an unreasonable manner. What are 'normal farm practices' under BC's Right to Farm legislation?

Under the Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, 'normal farm practice' means a practice that is conducted by a farm business in a manner consistent with proper and accepted customs and standards as established and followed by similar farm businesses under similar circumstances, and makes use of innovative technology in a manner consistent with proper advanced farm management practices and agricultural sustainability. The British Columbia Farm Industry Review Board (BCFIRB) has authority to determine whether a practice qualifies as normal. Can a neighbour sue a farmer for noise or odour under BC's Right to Farm Act?

Under section 2(1) of the Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, a farmer is not liable in nuisance to any person for odour, noise, dust, or other disturbance resulting from an agricultural operation if the operation is conducted as part of a farm business, in accordance with normal farm practices, and on land in the ALR or designated for farming. A neighbour may still complain to the BC Farm Industry Review Board (BCFIRB) if they believe the farm practice is not normal or not conducted reasonably. What is the role of the BC Farm Industry Review Board in Right to Farm disputes?

Under the Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, the British Columbia Farm Industry Review Board (BCFIRB) has jurisdiction to hear and determine complaints about whether a farm operation is being carried on in accordance with normal farm practices. BCFIRB may order the farmer to cease or modify a practice if it finds the practice is not normal or is being conducted unreasonably. Does purchasing property near farmland in BC mean accepting farm nuisances?

British Columbia's Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, protects farmers from nuisance liability for odour, noise, dust, or other disturbances caused by normal farm practices, even if those practices commenced after a neighbour purchased nearby land. Purchasers of property near farmland in the Agricultural Land Reserve or municipally designated farm zones should be aware that they may have limited legal recourse for typical farm-related impacts. Does the Right to Farm Act apply to land outside the Agricultural Land Reserve in BC?

Under section 1(1) of the Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, the definition of 'farm operation' includes agricultural operations carried on in the Agricultural Land Reserve (governed by the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36) or on other land that is classified as a farm, is designated for farm use, or is actually used for farm purposes under an enactment. Land outside the ALR may still qualify if it is designated for agricultural use by a municipal zoning bylaw or meets other statutory criteria. Are there any exceptions to the nuisance protection under BC's Right to Farm Act?

The Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, does not provide absolute immunity; the protection applies only when the farm operation is conducted in accordance with normal farm practices and in a reasonable manner. If a farm practice is found by the BC Farm Industry Review Board not to be normal or to be conducted unreasonably, the farmer may lose the nuisance protection and could be subject to an order to cease or modify the practice. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

How does the Right to Farm Act affect real estate disclosures in British Columbia?

While the Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, provides legal protection to farmers, real estate licensees in BC are required under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and BCFSA Rules to provide material latent defect disclosures and fulfill their duties to clients; proximity to active farmland or the Agricultural Land Reserve is commonly disclosed as a material fact that may affect a buyer's use and enjoyment of property. Verify current disclosure obligations with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before acting.

Can a municipality restrict farm activities despite the Right to Farm Act in BC?

Under section 917 of the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, a municipality's zoning bylaw must not prohibit farm use of land in a farm zone or in the Agricultural Land Reserve unless specifically authorized, and the Farm Practices Protection (Right to Farm) Act, RSBC 1996, c. 131, limits municipal authority to impose nuisance-based restrictions on normal farm practices. However, municipalities retain authority to regulate farm activities through health, safety, and other valid regulatory mechanisms.
