Polybutylene (Poly-B) Piping

A type of grey plastic plumbing pipe installed in many BC homes from roughly 1978–1998, now known to be prone to failure. Replacement is often recommended and may affect insurability and insurance premiums.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Poly-B piping and why is it a concern in BC homes?

Polybutylene (Poly-B) piping is a grey plastic plumbing material installed in many BC homes between approximately 1978 and 1998. It is now known to degrade over time, particularly at fittings and joints, leading to leaks, water damage, and potential mould issues. Because of its failure-prone nature, many insurers in BC are reluctant to provide coverage for homes that still contain it, or they may charge higher premiums.

Verify with: BC Housing
Are BC real estate licensees required to disclose the presence of Poly-B piping when selling a property?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and BCFSA conduct standards, licensees have a duty to disclose known material latent defects that are not discoverable by a reasonable inspection. Poly-B piping, if known to be present and known to be prone to failure, would likely qualify as a material latent defect requiring disclosure to buyers. Licensees should consult current BCFSA guidance to ensure they meet their disclosure obligations accurately.

Verify with: BC Housing
Does the presence of Poly-B piping affect home insurance in British Columbia?

Yes, many BC insurers treat Poly-B piping as a significant risk factor and may decline to issue a new policy, limit coverage for water damage, or charge substantially higher premiums for homes that contain it. Insurance requirements and underwriting criteria are set by individual insurers, not by BC statute, so buyers and sellers should consult directly with an insurer or broker to understand the current impact on coverage.

Verify with: BC Housing
If a strata building in BC has Poly-B piping in common property, who is responsible for replacing it?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), the strata corporation is responsible for maintaining and repairing common property, including plumbing that runs through common areas. If Poly-B piping forms part of the common property or limited common property as defined by the strata plan, the cost of replacement would typically be funded through the contingency reserve fund or a special levy approved by the strata corporation. Owners of individual strata lots are generally responsible for Poly-B piping located within their own unit, subject to the strata's bylaws.

Verify with: BC Housing
Should Poly-B piping be identified in a strata corporation's depreciation report in BC?

Yes, under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and its Regulation, depreciation reports must identify the condition of major common property components and project their repair or replacement costs over a 30-year horizon. Poly-B piping in common property would typically be flagged as a component requiring attention, with estimated replacement costs included in the long-term financial planning for the strata corporation.

Verify with: BC Housing
Will replacing Poly-B piping affect the Property Transfer Tax payable on a BC property?

Replacing Poly-B piping is a renovation cost borne by the seller or buyer and does not directly alter how Property Transfer Tax (PTT) is calculated under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act, which is based on the fair market value of the property at the time of transfer. However, if the presence of Poly-B piping materially affects the negotiated purchase price, that lower fair market value would form the basis for the PTT calculation. Consult the current BC Ministry of Finance guidance for exact PTT rates and thresholds applicable to the transaction.

Verify with: BC Housing
How can a buyer in BC determine whether a home they are purchasing contains Poly-B piping?

A buyer can hire a qualified home inspector to visually identify Poly-B piping, which is typically grey in colour and may be marked with the designation 'PB' and a series of numbers on the pipe. In a strata context, buyers can also review the Form B Information Certificate issued under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), strata minutes, and any depreciation report for references to Poly-B piping in common property. Sellers and their licensees should disclose known Poly-B piping in accordance with BCFSA disclosure standards under RESA.

Verify with: BC Housing
Can a buyer in BC make an offer conditional on Poly-B piping being replaced before completion?

Yes, a buyer may negotiate a subject clause or condition in the Contract of Purchase and Sale that requires the seller to replace the Poly-B piping prior to completion, or alternatively negotiate a price adjustment to account for the anticipated replacement cost. The specific terms of any such condition are a matter of contract negotiation between the parties. Licensees acting for either party must ensure they are meeting their duties under RESA and BCFSA Rules when drafting or advising on contractual conditions.

Verify with: BC Housing
Does Poly-B piping need to be disclosed on the Property Disclosure Statement in BC?

The BC Property Disclosure Statement (PDS) asks sellers to disclose known defects and conditions affecting the property, and sellers should disclose known Poly-B piping and any associated damage or failures. Sellers who are aware of Poly-B piping but do not disclose it may face liability if a buyer later suffers loss as a result. BCFSA and RESA standards also require licensees to ensure that material facts known to them are not concealed from the buyer.

Verify with: BC Housing
If a BC seller is an estate administrator selling a property under WESA, are there still Poly-B disclosure obligations?

Under the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA) of BC, an executor or administrator is obligated to act in good faith and in the best interests of the estate and beneficiaries, which includes not misrepresenting property conditions. While an estate may have limited knowledge of the property's condition, the licensee representing the estate still has disclosure obligations under RESA and BCFSA standards, including disclosing known material latent defects such as Poly-B piping. Estate sellers are encouraged to make reasonable inquiries and disclose any Poly-B piping they are aware of to avoid potential liability.
