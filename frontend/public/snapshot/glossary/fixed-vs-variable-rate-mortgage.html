Fixed vs Variable Rate Mortgage

A fixed-rate mortgage holds the interest rate constant throughout the contracted term, so each scheduled payment's interest portion does not change during that term. A variable-rate mortgage ties the rate to the lender's prime rate, which moves in response to the Bank of Canada's policy rate; depending on the product structure, either the payment amount or the interest-versus-principal split within each payment will shift as prime moves. Fixed-rate products offer payment predictability; variable-rate products carry rate-movement risk during the term. Both product types are available through federally regulated lenders across Canada; FCAC and the Bank of Canada publish general guidance on how each structure works. For advice on which structure suits your circumstances, verify current rate conditions and product terms with a licensed mortgage professional or BC lawyer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between a fixed-rate and a variable-rate mortgage in BC?

A fixed-rate mortgage holds the interest rate constant for the full term of the contract (commonly 1 to 5 years as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), so the interest portion of each scheduled payment does not change during that term. A variable-rate mortgage has an interest rate tied to the lender's prime rate, which moves with the Bank of Canada's policy rate; depending on the product, either the payment amount or the share of each payment going to interest versus principal will change as prime moves. Both product types are offered across federally regulated lenders in Canada and are described in the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada's mortgage guidance.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Does BC law require lenders to disclose whether a mortgage is fixed or variable rate?

Federally regulated financial institutions in Canada must comply with disclosure rules under the Bank Act (SC 1991, c. 46) and regulations enforced by the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada, which include clear disclosure of mortgage terms, including whether the rate is fixed or variable. For BC credit unions and other provincially regulated lenders, the British Columbia Financial Services Authority oversees mortgage disclosures under applicable BC statutes and BCFSA Rules. Verify current disclosure obligations for your lender type with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed mortgage professional before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Are there BC property transfer tax (PTT) differences between buying a home with a fixed versus a variable-rate mortgage?

No. The Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, calculates PTT based on the fair market value of the property at the time of registration, not on the type of mortgage financing the buyer uses. Whether a buyer chooses a fixed-rate or variable-rate mortgage does not affect the PTT payable or eligibility for the First-Time Home Buyer Exemption or Newly Built Home Exemption. Verify current PTT rates and exemption thresholds (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Can I switch from a variable-rate to a fixed-rate mortgage during my term in BC?

Whether you can switch depends on the terms of your mortgage contract, not on BC provincial law. Many lenders allow borrowers to convert from a variable-rate to a fixed-rate mortgage mid-term without penalty, but the new fixed rate and any conditions are set by the lender's policies and your contract terms. Review your mortgage agreement and verify current conversion options and costs with your lender or a licensed mortgage professional before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Does the BC Home Flipping Tax treat fixed and variable-rate mortgages differently?

No. The Home Flipping Tax Act, SBC 2024 (effective January 1, 2025 — verify current), applies to the disposition of residential property in BC held for less than the prescribed period, based on the gain realized and the holding period, not on the type of mortgage financing used. The choice between a fixed-rate and a variable-rate mortgage does not affect liability under the Home Flipping Tax. Verify current holding-period thresholds and exemptions with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
If the Bank of Canada raises its policy rate, how does that affect my variable-rate mortgage in BC?

A variable-rate mortgage has an interest rate tied to the lender's prime rate, which typically moves in tandem with the Bank of Canada's policy rate. When the Bank of Canada raises its policy rate, most lenders increase their prime rate, and your variable-rate mortgage cost will rise; depending on your product, either your payment amount will increase or a larger share of each fixed payment will go to interest instead of principal, extending your amortization. Verify how your specific variable-rate product responds to rate changes with your lender or a licensed mortgage professional before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Are fixed-rate mortgages subject to the same prepayment and portability rules as variable-rate mortgages in BC?

Prepayment privileges, penalties, and portability rights are determined by the mortgage contract and the lender's policies, not by BC provincial statute. Both fixed-rate and variable-rate mortgages can have prepayment options (e.g., lump-sum payments, increased payments) and portability features, but the specific terms, penalty calculations (e.g., three-month interest or interest-rate differential for fixed; typically three-month interest for variable), and conditions vary by lender and product. Review your mortgage agreement and verify current terms with your lender or a licensed mortgage professional before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Does BC's Speculation and Vacancy Tax or the federal Foreign Buyer Ban distinguish between fixed and variable-rate mortgages?

No. The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, imposes an annual tax based on property ownership, residency, and use, not on the type of mortgage financing. Similarly, the Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10 (currently extended through January 1, 2027 — verify current), restricts certain non-Canadian purchases but does not differentiate between fixed-rate and variable-rate mortgages. Verify current SVT and Foreign Buyer Ban rules with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Which mortgage type—fixed or variable—typically has a lower starting interest rate in BC?

Historically, variable-rate mortgages have often been offered at lower starting interest rates than fixed-rate mortgages of equivalent term length, because lenders price in less interest-rate risk over the term. However, actual rates depend on the lender, the borrower's credit profile, market conditions set by the Bank of Canada's policy rate, and the specific mortgage product. Verify current fixed and variable rates with multiple lenders or a licensed mortgage broker before acting, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current.

Verify with: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada
Does the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada provide tools to compare fixed versus variable-rate mortgages for BC buyers?

Yes. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) publishes mortgage guidance, calculators, and educational materials on its website (www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency) to help Canadian borrowers understand fixed-rate and variable-rate mortgage products, payment scenarios, and total interest costs over time. These tools are general and not BC-specific, but they apply to federally regulated lenders operating in BC. Verify how FCAC resources apply to your specific mortgage scenario with a licensed mortgage professional before acting.
