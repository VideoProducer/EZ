Flood Insurance

Flood insurance — sometimes called overland water coverage — protects against property damage when water enters at ground level from sources such as rivers overflowing their banks, snowmelt runoff, or pooling rainfall. In British Columbia, this coverage is not included in standard home insurance policies and must be added as a separate endorsement; verify current availability and terms with a licensed insurance professional. Properties situated within designated flood-hazard areas — including parts of some Fraser Valley and Sea-to-Sky corridor communities — may face restricted coverage or significantly higher premiums, with insurer underwriting decisions referencing floodplain maps published by the Province of BC and local governments under authority including the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. The federal government has publicly indicated development of a national flood insurance program intended to serve high-risk properties currently uninsurable in the private market; verify the current status of that program with CMHC or a licensed insurance professional before relying on its availability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is flood (overland water) insurance in British Columbia?

Flood insurance, also called overland water insurance, covers damage caused when water enters a property at ground level from sources such as rivers overflowing, snowmelt, or heavy rainfall pooling on the surface. In BC, this coverage is not included in standard home insurance policies and must be added as a separate endorsement. Verify current policy terms and availability with a licensed insurance agent or broker before purchasing.

Is flood insurance mandatory for BC homeowners?

No BC statute currently mandates flood insurance for residential property owners. However, if a property is financed by a federally regulated lender and is located in a designated flood-hazard area, the lender may require flood insurance as a condition of the mortgage under the lender's internal risk-management policies (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify lender requirements and municipal floodplain designations with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed mortgage professional before acting.

How do BC floodplain maps affect my ability to buy flood insurance?

The Province of BC and local governments publish floodplain maps that identify designated flood-hazard areas; private insurers reference those maps when underwriting overland water coverage. Properties in mapped high-risk zones (for example, parts of Sumas Prairie, Chilliwack, Pemberton, and Squamish) may be uninsurable for overland water or face significantly higher premiums (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the specific flood designation and insurability of a property with a licensed insurance agent or broker and consult local government records before acting.

Does my standard BC home insurance policy automatically cover flood damage?

No. Standard home insurance policies in British Columbia do not automatically include coverage for overland water (flood) damage; this coverage must be added as a separate endorsement. Water damage from internal sources (for example, burst pipes) is typically covered under standard policies, but surface water intrusion is not (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the terms of your specific policy with a licensed insurance agent or broker.

What is the federal National Flood Insurance Program, and is it available in BC today?

The Government of Canada has announced development of a National Flood Insurance Program intended to provide coverage for high-risk properties currently uninsurable in the private market. As of 2026-07-27, the program is still in development and not yet operational (verify current). For current availability and eligibility, verify with the federal Department of Public Safety Canada, CMHC, or a licensed insurance professional.

Can a strata corporation in BC purchase flood insurance for a strata building?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation must obtain and maintain property insurance on common property and common assets, and may insure against perils the strata corporation considers advisable. Flood (overland water) insurance is not mandated by the Act but may be added as an endorsement if the insurer offers it and the strata council determines it is prudent (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify current strata insurance requirements and endorsement options with a licensed insurance agent, broker, or BC lawyer.

If I am a buyer, how do I find out whether a BC property is in a floodplain?

Floodplain maps are published by the Province of BC and local governments; many municipalities make these maps available online or at municipal offices. A buyer may also request that the seller or the seller's real estate licensee disclose known flood-hazard designations; under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and RESA Rules, licensees must disclose known material latent defects, which may include flood risk if known (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify floodplain status and disclosure obligations with the local government, a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional before acting.

Does the BC provincial Property Transfer Tax or speculation tax interact with flood insurance?

No. The Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, and the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, do not create exemptions, credits, or additional liability based on whether a property is in a floodplain or has flood insurance (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Flood risk may, however, affect property valuation and insurability, which are separate considerations. Verify tax obligations and property valuation with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Can a landlord in BC require a tenant to purchase flood insurance?

Under the Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78, a tenancy agreement may not require a tenant to purchase insurance for the landlord's building or for the landlord's property (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). A landlord may require a tenant to obtain tenant contents insurance, but overland water coverage for the structure itself remains the landlord's responsibility if desired. Verify tenancy agreement terms and obligations with a BC lawyer, notary, or the Residential Tenancy Branch before acting.

Where can I verify current flood insurance premium costs and eligibility for my BC property?

Flood insurance premiums and eligibility vary by insurer, property location, construction, elevation, proximity to water, and floodplain designation; there is no single provincial schedule (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Contact multiple licensed insurance agents or brokers for quotes, review municipal floodplain maps, and consult the Insurance Bureau of Canada (IBC) or the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) for educational resources. Verify all details with a licensed insurance professional before purchasing coverage.
