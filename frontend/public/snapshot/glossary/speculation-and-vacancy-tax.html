Speculation and Vacancy Tax

An annual provincial tax on owners of residential property in designated BC communities who do not occupy or rent out their property, established under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46. Tax rates and the list of affected communities are set by regulation; verify current rates, community coverage, and declaration deadlines with the BC Ministry of Finance at gov.bc.ca or a licensed tax professional, as these figures change. An annual declaration is required by a prescribed deadline each year; failure to declare may result in the owner being assessed at a higher rate — verify current deadlines and consequences with the BC Ministry of Finance. Owners who are foreign owners or untaxed worldwide earners are subject to a higher rate than BC residents or Canadian citizens and permanent residents; all specific percentages should be verified as current at gov.bc.ca (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Payment is due by a prescribed date following the declaration period. Exemptions may apply based on occupancy, rental use, or owner status; eligibility conditions and any restrictions on appealing assessments for prior tax years should be confirmed with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) is an annual provincial tax on owners of residential property in 59 designated BC communities who do not live in or rent out their home, established under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46. The tax applies to properties that are vacant or underutilized for six months or more of the calendar year. All owners in designated areas must file an annual declaration by March 31 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), even if exempt. Verify current rates and exemptions with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
What are the 2026 and 2027 tax rates for the Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

For the 2026 tax year, the rate is 3% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for foreign owners and satellite families (untaxed worldwide income earners), and 1% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for BC residents and Canadian citizens or permanent residents. For the 2027 tax year, the rate rises to 4% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for foreign owners and satellite families, and remains 1% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for BC residents and Canadian citizens or permanent residents. Verify current rates with the BC Ministry of Finance or a licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
Which communities are subject to the Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

The Speculation and Vacancy Tax applies in 59 designated taxable regions (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) across BC, including Metro Vancouver, Capital Regional District (Greater Victoria), Nanaimo, Kelowna, West Kelowna, Abbotsford, Chilliwack, Mission, and portions of the Fraser Valley and Okanagan. The full list is maintained by the BC Ministry of Finance. Verify whether a specific property falls within a designated area by consulting the province's official SVT map or a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
When is the Speculation and Vacancy Tax declaration due, and when is payment due?

Under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, the annual declaration is due by March 31 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for the previous calendar year. Payment, if tax is owed, is due on the first business day of July (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Late or missing declarations may result in penalties and interest. Verify current deadlines and filing procedures with the BC Ministry of Finance or a licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
Who is exempt from the Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

Common exemptions include BC residents who occupy the property as their principal residence, Canadian citizens or permanent residents who occupy it as principal residence, long-term rental tenancies (minimum 6 months in an arm's length tenancy), owners with qualifying disabilities, and certain other exemptions listed in the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46. An annual declaration is still required even if an exemption applies. Verify exemption eligibility for your specific situation with the BC Ministry of Finance, a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
Can I appeal my Speculation and Vacancy Tax assessment for prior years?

BC Budget 2026 announced that owners cannot appeal SVT assessments for the 2023, 2024, or 2025 tax years (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) if they made a 'no exemption applies' declaration for those years. This measure was introduced as part of Budget 2026 legislative amendments. For other tax years or circumstances, verify appeal rights and procedures with the BC Ministry of Finance, a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
How is the Speculation and Vacancy Tax calculated?

The SVT is calculated as a percentage of the property's assessed taxable value (BC Assessment) for the tax year, at the applicable rate: 3% or 4% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for foreign owners and satellite families, or 1% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for BC residents and Canadian citizens or permanent residents, depending on the year. The tax applies only to the portion of ownership subject to SVT if exemptions or partial exemptions apply. Verify calculation details and your assessed value with BC Assessment, the BC Ministry of Finance, or a licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
What happens if I fail to file the annual Speculation and Vacancy Tax declaration?

Failure to file the annual declaration by March 31 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) may result in a deemed liability for the full SVT amount, plus penalties and interest, even if the owner would have qualified for an exemption. The BC Ministry of Finance administers enforcement under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46. Verify late-filing options, penalties, and reinstatement procedures with the BC Ministry of Finance, a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
Does renting out my property exempt me from the Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

Renting the property under a residential tenancy agreement for at least six months (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) of the calendar year to an arm's length tenant may qualify as an exemption under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46. Short-term vacation rentals do not qualify. Documentation and annual declaration are still required. Verify rental exemption requirements and documentation with the BC Ministry of Finance, a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
Is the Speculation and Vacancy Tax the same as property transfer tax or the BC home flipping tax?

No. The Speculation and Vacancy Tax (SVT) is an annual tax on underutilized residential property under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46. Property transfer tax (PTT) is a one-time tax on property purchases under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378. The BC home flipping tax is a capital gains–style tax on short-hold sales under the Home Flipping Tax Act, SBC 2024 (effective January 1, 2025 — verify current). Verify the applicability of each tax to your transaction with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Government
