Strata

Rental Restriction Bylaws (Prohibited 2022)

Following amendments to the Strata Property Act in November 2022, strata bylaws restricting the rental of strata lots are generally no longer enforceable, with limited exceptions such as short-term rental restrictions and certain age restrictions. Verify current rules with the Province and review the bylaws registered against title before assuming a rental is permitted.

Frequently Asked Questions

What changed about rental restriction bylaws in BC strata corporations in November 2022?

Amendments to the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) that came into force in November 2022 made most strata bylaws that restrict or prohibit the rental of strata lots unenforceable. This means a strata corporation can no longer enforce a bylaw that prevents an owner from renting their strata lot to a long-term residential tenant, even if that bylaw was validly registered before the amendments. Owners and buyers should review the current wording of the Strata Property Act and its Regulation to understand the full scope of this change.

Are there any exceptions that still allow a strata corporation in BC to restrict rentals after the 2022 amendments?

Yes, the 2022 amendments to the Strata Property Act preserved the ability of strata corporations to restrict or prohibit short-term rentals, meaning rentals typically for periods shorter than those defined as residential tenancies under applicable legislation. Strata corporations that qualify as age-restricted communities under the Strata Property Act may also retain certain restrictions connected to occupancy age requirements. Because exceptions are fact-specific, buyers and owners should review the actual registered bylaws and consult current BC Government guidance before assuming any restriction is valid or invalid.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
Do pre-existing rental restriction bylaws registered before November 2022 still apply in BC strata buildings?

No — following the November 2022 amendments to the Strata Property Act, pre-existing bylaws that restrict long-term residential rentals became generally unenforceable regardless of when they were registered on title. A strata corporation cannot enforce such a bylaw simply because it predates the legislative change. However, buyers should still obtain and review a Form B Information Certificate to understand all bylaws registered against the strata lot, as other types of restrictions may remain valid.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
Can a BC strata corporation pass a new bylaw after November 2022 to reimpose rental restrictions?

Under the Strata Property Act as amended in November 2022, strata corporations are generally prohibited from passing new bylaws that restrict or prohibit the rental of strata lots to long-term residential tenants. Any such new bylaw would be unenforceable to the same extent as the pre-existing ones the amendments targeted. Strata corporations retain the ability to regulate short-term rentals and to enforce other lawful bylaw provisions unrelated to long-term rental restrictions.

What is a Form B Information Certificate and why is it relevant to rental restriction bylaws in BC?

A Form B Information Certificate is a document produced by a strata corporation under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) that discloses key information about a strata lot, including the bylaws and rules in force at the time of issuance. Even though long-term rental restriction bylaws are generally unenforceable following the November 2022 amendments, reviewing the Form B helps buyers identify what bylaws exist on the books, including any short-term rental restrictions or other provisions that may still be enforceable. Relying solely on the Form B without cross-referencing the current state of the Strata Property Act may lead to misunderstandings about what restrictions actually apply.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
How does the 2022 change to rental restriction bylaws affect buyers purchasing a strata unit in BC as a rental investment?

Buyers purchasing a BC strata lot for long-term residential rental purposes generally benefit from the November 2022 Strata Property Act amendments, as they can no longer be prohibited from renting out their unit by a strata bylaw targeting long-term tenancies. However, buyers must still confirm whether the strata corporation has enforceable short-term rental restrictions, age-restriction provisions, or other bylaws that could affect their intended use. Conducting thorough due diligence, including obtaining and reviewing the Form B and all registered bylaws, remains essential before completing a purchase.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
Does the prohibition on rental restriction bylaws under the 2022 BC amendments apply to all types of strata properties?

The November 2022 amendments to the Strata Property Act broadly apply to strata corporations in British Columbia, but certain categories — such as age-restricted strata communities that meet the criteria set out in the Act — may retain some occupancy-related restrictions. Bare land stratas and other specialized strata forms are also subject to the Strata Property Act, so the amendments generally apply to them as well, though the practical effect may vary. Buyers and strata councils should confirm applicability to their specific strata with reference to the current text of the Strata Property Act and its Regulation.

What role does the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) play regarding rental restriction bylaws in strata properties?

The BCFSA regulates real estate licensees in BC under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and its Rules, and licensees are required to be knowledgeable and provide accurate information to clients about matters material to a transaction, which includes the enforceability of strata bylaws. A licensee who misrepresents the effect of a rental restriction bylaw — for example, incorrectly advising a client that a rental ban remains enforceable after the November 2022 Strata Property Act amendments — could be in breach of their professional obligations under RESA. The BCFSA does not administer the Strata Property Act itself; strata governance disputes are addressed through the Civil Resolution Tribunal or the courts.

Can a BC strata corporation still restrict short-term rentals such as those listed on platforms like Airbnb after the 2022 amendments?

Yes — the November 2022 amendments to the Strata Property Act specifically preserved the ability of strata corporations to pass and enforce bylaws restricting short-term rentals, which are typically rentals of a duration shorter than that of a residential tenancy under applicable BC legislation. A strata corporation may therefore still prohibit owners from listing their strata lot on short-term rental platforms by passing an appropriate bylaw. Buyers intending to use a strata lot for short-term rental purposes must carefully review the registered bylaws and current municipal short-term rental regulations, as additional restrictions may apply at both the strata and municipal level.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
If a strata corporation attempts to enforce an unenforceable rental restriction bylaw against an owner in BC, what recourse does the owner have?

An owner facing enforcement of a bylaw that is unenforceable under the amended Strata Property Act may apply to the Civil Resolution Tribunal (CRT), which has jurisdiction to resolve strata property disputes in British Columbia, to seek a determination that the bylaw cannot be enforced. If the matter involves more complex legal issues or higher-value claims, the owner may alternatively pursue remedies through the BC Supreme Court. Owners in this situation should review the current provisions of the Strata Property Act and the Civil Resolution Tribunal Act to understand the applicable process and time limits.
