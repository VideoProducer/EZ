Mortgage Stress Test (B-20)

The Mortgage Stress Test is a qualifying rate requirement established under the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) Guideline B-20, which applies to federally regulated lenders in Canada. Under this guideline, borrowers must demonstrate they can afford payments calculated at the greater of their contract rate plus a prescribed percentage buffer, or a minimum qualifying rate set by OSFI — verify current rates and buffer amounts with a licensed mortgage professional or FCAC. The test applies regardless of down payment size for federally regulated lenders. Its purpose is to help ensure borrowers retain repayment capacity if interest rates rise after the mortgage is issued. Because the qualifying rate is higher than the actual contract rate, the test typically reduces the maximum loan amount a borrower can obtain. OSFI may update Guideline B-20 thresholds periodically; verify the current qualifying rate and buffer with OSFI, FCAC, or a licensed tax professional before relying on any specific figures (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Mortgage Stress Test (B-20) in British Columbia?

The B-20 Guideline is a federal prudential standard issued by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) that applies to federally regulated lenders across Canada, including in British Columbia. It requires borrowers seeking an uninsured mortgage to qualify at the greater of the contract rate plus 2% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) or a benchmark rate set by OSFI. This test reduces the maximum amount a borrower can qualify for but is intended to ensure borrowers can withstand future interest rate increases. Verify current qualifying rate requirements with a BC mortgage broker or lender before applying.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Does the B-20 stress test apply to all mortgage lenders in BC?

The B-20 Guideline applies only to federally regulated financial institutions such as banks, federal credit unions, and federally incorporated trust companies. Credit unions regulated under the BC Financial Institutions Act and private or alternative lenders may follow different underwriting standards set by their own regulators or internal policies. Verify the qualification criteria that apply to your specific lender with a BC mortgage broker or licensed professional before proceeding.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
How does the stress test affect how much I can borrow to buy a home in BC?

The stress test typically reduces the maximum loan amount for which a borrower qualifies, because the lender must assess affordability at a higher interest rate than the actual contract rate. For example, if your contract rate is 5.0% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), you must qualify at 7.0% (contract plus 2%) or the OSFI benchmark rate, whichever is higher. This means your debt servicing ratios—calculated under the B-20 Guideline and any applicable lender policies—must remain within acceptable limits at the higher rate. Verify your specific borrowing capacity with a BC mortgage broker or lender.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Is the B-20 stress test a BC provincial law?

No. The B-20 Guideline is a federal prudential standard issued by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) under the authority of the Bank Act, SC 1991, c. 46, and related federal financial institution statutes. It is not enacted under any British Columbia statute, though it affects mortgage qualification for BC homebuyers who apply to federally regulated lenders. Verify the applicable requirements for your transaction with a BC mortgage broker, notary, or lawyer.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Does the stress test apply to insured mortgages in BC (mortgages with CMHC or other default insurance)?

Yes. Insured mortgages—those with less than 20% down payment and insured by Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC), Sagen, or Canada Guaranty—are subject to similar stress-test requirements set by the mortgage insurers and by federal regulation. The qualifying criteria for insured mortgages are governed by federal policy and insurer guidelines, not by the B-20 Guideline itself, but the effect is comparable. Verify current insured-mortgage qualification rules with a BC mortgage broker or lender before applying.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Can I avoid the B-20 stress test by using a BC credit union or private lender?

BC credit unions regulated under the BC Financial Institutions Act, RSBC 1996, c. 141, and overseen by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) are not directly subject to the federal B-20 Guideline. However, many BC credit unions and private lenders voluntarily apply similar stress-test standards, and all lenders must comply with their own regulator's prudential requirements and internal lending policies. Verify the specific qualification criteria and rates with your chosen lender or a BC mortgage broker before proceeding.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Does the B-20 stress test affect refinancing or renewing my existing mortgage in BC?

Straight renewals with your existing lender—where you do not increase your principal or change lenders—typically do not require re-qualification under the B-20 stress test. If you refinance, switch lenders, or increase your mortgage amount, federally regulated lenders will generally apply the current stress-test qualifying rate at the time of application. Verify the requirements for your specific refinance or renewal scenario with your lender or a BC mortgage broker.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Where can I find the current OSFI benchmark rate used in the B-20 stress test?

The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) publishes the benchmark qualifying rate on its website (www.osfi-bsif.gc.ca). As of 2026-07-27 (verify current), the rate is updated periodically and may differ from the Bank of Canada policy rate. Always verify the current benchmark rate and how it applies to your mortgage application with your lender or a BC mortgage broker before finalizing your offer on a property.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Does BC's Property Transfer Tax Act or any other BC real estate statute reference the B-20 stress test?

No. The Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, and other BC real estate statutes do not reference or regulate the B-20 Guideline, which is a federal prudential measure. The stress test affects mortgage qualification and borrowing capacity but does not change BC property transfer tax calculations, exemptions, or registration requirements. Verify property transfer tax obligations separately with the BC Ministry of Finance, a BC notary, or lawyer.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
If I am a foreign buyer subject to BC's Additional Property Transfer Tax, does the B-20 stress test still apply?

Yes. The B-20 Guideline mortgage stress test and BC's Additional Property Transfer Tax under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, are separate requirements. Foreign nationals, foreign corporations, and taxable trustees purchasing residential property in specified areas of BC must pay an additional 20% property transfer tax (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) and may also be subject to the federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10 (currently extended through January 1, 2027 — verify current). If you obtain financing from a federally regulated lender, the B-20 stress test will apply to your mortgage qualification. Verify all requirements—federal foreign buyer prohibition, BC additional PTT, and mortgage qualification—with a BC lawyer, notary, and mortgage broker before proceeding.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
