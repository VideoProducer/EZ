Mortgage Stress Test (B-20)

A federally mandated qualifying rate test under OSFI's B-20 Guideline, requiring borrowers to qualify at the greater of the contract rate plus 2% or the benchmark rate set by the regulator. The test reduces borrowing capacity but cushions borrowers against future rate increases.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Mortgage Stress Test (B-20) and does it apply to all BC home buyers?

The Mortgage Stress Test is a federally mandated qualifying requirement under OSFI's B-20 Guideline that applies to federally regulated lenders across Canada, including those operating in British Columbia. Borrowers must qualify at the greater of their contract rate plus 2% or the minimum qualifying rate set by OSFI, whichever is higher. The test applies to most insured and uninsured mortgages at federally regulated financial institutions, though mortgages from provincially regulated lenders such as some BC credit unions may be subject to different provincial oversight standards.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Does the B-20 Stress Test apply to mortgages obtained through BC credit unions?

BC credit unions are provincially regulated under the Financial Institutions Act and overseen by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and they are not directly subject to OSFI's B-20 Guideline in the same manner as federally regulated banks. However, BCFSA has encouraged provincially regulated lenders in BC to apply similar prudent underwriting standards, and individual credit unions may adopt their own qualifying rate policies. Borrowers should confirm the specific qualifying criteria directly with their credit union.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
How does the B-20 Stress Test affect how much a BC buyer can borrow?

Because borrowers must qualify at a rate higher than their actual contract rate — specifically the greater of the contract rate plus 2% or the OSFI benchmark rate — their maximum eligible loan amount is reduced compared to qualifying at the contract rate alone. This means a BC buyer may qualify for a smaller mortgage than the listed price of a property requires, directly influencing purchasing power in markets such as Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley. Buyers should obtain a pre-approval from their lender to understand exactly how much they can borrow under the current stress test parameters.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Does the B-20 Stress Test apply when a BC homeowner renews or switches their mortgage to a different lender?

Under OSFI's B-20 Guideline, the stress test generally applies when a borrower switches their uninsured mortgage to a new federally regulated lender at renewal, meaning the borrower must re-qualify at the higher qualifying rate with the new institution. If a borrower renews with their existing federally regulated lender, the stress test may not be re-applied in the same way, depending on the lender's policies and whether the mortgage is insured or uninsured. Borrowers in BC considering switching lenders at renewal should confirm the qualifying requirements with both the existing and prospective lenders.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Can a BC real estate licensee advise a client on whether they will pass the B-20 Stress Test?

BC real estate licensees are licensed under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), and their scope of practice covers real estate trading services, not mortgage or financial advice. Providing specific guidance on whether a client will qualify under OSFI's B-20 Guideline would constitute mortgage brokering or financial advice, which requires separate licensing under the Mortgage Brokers Act in BC. Licensees should refer clients to a licensed mortgage broker or their financial institution for stress test qualification assessments.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Does the B-20 Stress Test interact with the BC Property Transfer Tax or First-Time Home Buyer exemptions?

The B-20 Stress Test and BC Property Transfer Tax (PTT) obligations are separate and independent requirements: the stress test is a federal mortgage qualifying rule, while PTT is imposed under BC's Property Transfer Tax Act on the registration of property transfers. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, first-time home buyers may qualify for a full PTT exemption on properties with a fair market value up to $835,000, and a newly built home exemption is available on qualifying properties up to $1,100,000. However, a buyer's ability to qualify under B-20 does not alter their PTT obligations or exemption eligibility, and both requirements must be considered separately.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Does the B-20 Stress Test apply differently to purchases of strata lots in BC compared to freehold properties?

The B-20 Stress Test applies based on the nature of the mortgage and the lender, not on whether the property is a strata lot governed by BC's Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) or a freehold property. However, lenders may factor strata-related carrying costs such as monthly strata fees into their total debt service calculations when assessing a borrower's ability to qualify under the stress test. Buyers of strata lots in BC should ensure their lender is aware of all strata fees, which are disclosed in documents such as the Form B (Information Certificate) issued by the strata corporation under the Strata Property Act.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
How does the B-20 Stress Test affect buyers purchasing agricultural land in BC's Agricultural Land Reserve?

The B-20 Stress Test applies to the mortgage qualification process regardless of whether the property is located within or outside BC's Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), which is administered by the Agricultural Land Commission under the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36). However, agricultural properties within the ALR may have restrictions on use, subdivision, and non-farm residential development that can affect lender assessments of the property's value and suitability as security. Buyers should consult both their lender and the Agricultural Land Commission directly regarding any restrictions that may influence financing on ALR properties.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
If a BC borrower fails the B-20 Stress Test at one federally regulated lender, can they simply use a private lender instead?

Private lenders in BC, such as mortgage investment corporations or individual investors, are generally not federally regulated institutions and therefore are not directly subject to OSFI's B-20 Guideline, meaning they may lend to borrowers who do not meet stress test thresholds. However, private mortgage lending typically involves higher interest rates, shorter terms, and potentially less favourable conditions compared to institutional lending. Borrowers considering private lending in BC should work with a mortgage broker licensed under BC's Mortgage Brokers Act and carefully assess the associated risks.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
Does the B-20 Stress Test have any interaction with BC's foreclosure process if a borrower later defaults?

The B-20 Stress Test is a pre-lending qualifying standard intended to reduce the risk of default by ensuring borrowers can sustain payments at higher rates; it does not alter the legal process that applies if default does occur. In British Columbia, foreclosure is a judicial process governed by the BC Supreme Court Civil Rules and the Law and Equity Act, and lenders must apply to the BC Supreme Court for an Order Nisi of foreclosure rather than exercising a power-of-sale as in some other provinces. The stress test's purpose is precisely to provide a financial buffer so that borrowers are less likely to find themselves in a foreclosure situation due to rising interest rates.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions
