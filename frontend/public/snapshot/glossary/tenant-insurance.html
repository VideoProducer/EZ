Tenant Insurance

Tenant insurance is a personal lines policy that covers a tenant's own belongings, personal liability, and additional living expenses if a rental unit becomes uninhabitable. A landlord's building policy does not extend to a tenant's possessions or personal liability. Under the Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78, landlords may include a requirement to maintain tenant insurance as a term of the tenancy agreement; verify the current legislative provisions with a BC lawyer or notary. In strata buildings, the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, permits strata corporations to pass bylaws that can hold an occupant responsible for damage caused to other strata lots or common property, making tenant insurance particularly relevant. Premium costs vary by insurer and coverage level; verify current pricing with a licensed insurance professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a BC landlord require a tenant to have tenant insurance?

Yes. Under the Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78, a landlord may include a term in the tenancy agreement requiring the tenant to maintain tenant (renter's) insurance. This requirement must be stated in the tenancy agreement at the start of the tenancy. Verify the specific wording and enforceability with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

What does tenant insurance typically cover in BC?

Tenant insurance generally covers the tenant's personal belongings against loss or damage, personal liability if the tenant is found legally responsible for injury or property damage to others, and additional living expenses if the rental unit becomes uninhabitable. The landlord's insurance covers the building structure, not the tenant's possessions or liability. Verify coverage details with a licensed insurance broker.

Why is tenant insurance important in a strata building in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and strata bylaws, an occupant (including a tenant) can be held responsible for damage they cause to common property or other strata lots. Tenant insurance provides liability coverage for such claims and protects the tenant's personal property. Verify the strata's bylaws and insurance requirements with the landlord or strata council.

How much does tenant insurance usually cost in BC?

Typical premiums for tenant insurance in BC range from approximately CAD $15 to $30 per month (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), depending on coverage limits, location, and the insurer. Actual costs vary based on individual circumstances and the value of personal property insured. Obtain quotes from licensed insurance brokers to confirm current rates.

Is tenant insurance mandatory by law in British Columbia?

No. There is no BC statute that makes tenant insurance mandatory for all tenants. However, under the Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78, a landlord may require it as a term of the tenancy agreement. Verify whether your tenancy agreement contains such a requirement before acting.

What happens if a tenant does not obtain insurance when the tenancy agreement requires it?

Failure to comply with a term of the tenancy agreement requiring tenant insurance may be considered a breach of the agreement under the Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78. The landlord may serve notice or apply to the Residential Tenancy Branch for dispute resolution. Verify the consequences and dispute resolution process with a BC lawyer, notary, or the Residential Tenancy Branch.

Does a landlord's insurance policy in BC cover a tenant's personal belongings?

No. A landlord's property insurance typically covers only the building structure and the landlord's own contents, not the tenant's personal belongings or liability. Tenants need their own tenant insurance to protect their possessions and obtain liability coverage. Verify coverage details with a licensed insurance broker.

Verify with: IBC — Glossary of Common Terms · BC Gov — Residential Tenancies (RTB)
Can a strata corporation in BC require tenants to carry insurance?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation may pass bylaws requiring occupants, including tenants, to obtain and maintain insurance. Such bylaws are enforceable if properly passed and filed. Verify the strata's bylaws and any insurance requirements with the strata council or a BC lawyer.

What is 'additional living expense' coverage in tenant insurance?

Additional living expense (ALE) coverage reimburses a tenant for reasonable extra costs (such as temporary accommodation and meals) if the rental unit becomes uninhabitable due to an insured peril, such as fire or flood. Coverage limits and conditions vary by policy. Verify specific ALE terms and limits with a licensed insurance broker before purchasing a policy.

Verify with: IBC — Glossary of Common Terms · BC Gov — Residential Tenancies (RTB)
Where can a BC tenant obtain tenant insurance?

BC tenants can obtain tenant (renter's) insurance from licensed insurance brokers and insurance companies operating in British Columbia. The British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) regulates insurance providers in the province. Verify the licensing status of any broker or insurer at www.bcfsa.ca before purchasing a policy.
