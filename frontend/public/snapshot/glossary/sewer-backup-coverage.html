Sewer Backup Coverage

Sewer backup coverage is an endorsement (add-on) to a home insurance policy that covers damage caused when municipal sewers back up into a home — typically through floor drains, basement fixtures, and lower-level plumbing. Sewer backup is not included in standard BC home policies. Typical premium for the endorsement is approximately CAD $50–$300 per year. The endorsement is particularly relevant for homes with finished basements, especially in flood-prone areas of Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is sewer backup coverage included in a standard BC home insurance policy?

No, sewer backup coverage is not included in a standard BC home insurance policy and must be purchased as a separate endorsement (add-on). Without this endorsement, damage caused by municipal sewer water backing up through floor drains or basement fixtures is generally excluded from coverage. BC homeowners should review their policy wording carefully with their insurer to confirm what is and is not included.

How much does a sewer backup endorsement typically cost in British Columbia?

In British Columbia, the annual premium for a sewer backup endorsement typically ranges from approximately CAD $50 to $300 per year, depending on the insurer, the property's location, and risk factors such as whether the home has a finished basement or is situated in a flood-prone area. Premiums may be higher in higher-risk municipalities such as those in Metro Vancouver or the Fraser Valley. Homeowners should obtain quotes from multiple insurers to compare coverage terms and pricing.

Does a BC strata corporation's insurance policy cover sewer backup damage inside individual strata lots?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), a strata corporation is required to obtain property insurance on common property and on the strata lots themselves, but the extent of that coverage — including whether it includes a sewer backup endorsement — depends on the specific policy the strata corporation holds. Even where strata insurance covers some sewer backup damage, it may not cover an owner's personal belongings, betterments, or improvements inside the unit. Strata lot owners should request a copy of the strata's insurance certificate and consider purchasing their own unit owner (HO6-equivalent) policy with a sewer backup endorsement.

Must a BC real estate licensee disclose to a buyer that a property has previously experienced sewer backup damage?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and the duties imposed by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), a licensee acting for a buyer must disclose all known material latent defects, which can include prior sewer backup damage that materially affects the value or habitability of the property. A seller's agent must also advise the seller of their obligation to disclose known material latent defects to potential buyers. Failure to make required disclosures can result in regulatory action by the BCFSA.

Why is sewer backup coverage considered especially important for homes in Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley?

Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley contain many low-lying areas, aging municipal sewer infrastructure, and properties that experience significant rainfall and periodic flooding, all of which increase the risk of municipal sewer systems becoming overwhelmed and backing up into homes. Finished basements — common in these regions — are particularly vulnerable because sewer backup typically enters through floor drains and lower-level plumbing fixtures. The combination of high-value finished living space and elevated geographic risk makes the endorsement especially relevant for homeowners in these areas.

Does sewer backup coverage in BC cover overland flooding, such as water entering through doors or windows?

No, sewer backup coverage and overland flood coverage are distinct endorsements in BC; sewer backup coverage applies specifically to damage caused by water or sewage that backs up through a home's internal drain or plumbing system connected to the municipal sewer, not water that enters a property from the exterior ground surface. Overland flooding — water entering through doors, windows, or foundation walls due to surface water or rising water bodies — typically requires a separate overland water or flood endorsement. Homeowners should confirm the precise scope of each endorsement with their insurer.

When a strata lot owner experiences sewer backup damage, who is responsible — the owner or the strata corporation?

Responsibility depends on whether the sewer backup originates from common property plumbing (such as shared drain lines) or from within the strata lot itself, and on the strata corporation's bylaws, which are governed by the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43). If the cause is a common property defect or the strata corporation's failure to maintain shared infrastructure, the strata corporation may bear responsibility; if the source is within the owner's lot, the owner may be responsible. Owners should review the strata's bylaws, obtain a Form B Information Certificate to understand the strata's insurance coverage, and carry their own sewer backup endorsement as an additional safeguard.

Can a BC buyer make an offer to purchase conditional on confirming the property has sewer backup coverage available from insurers?

Yes, a buyer in BC can include a subject condition (subject to financing, inspection, or other conditions) in their Contract of Purchase and Sale requiring confirmation that the property is insurable and that a sewer backup endorsement is available, particularly for homes with finished basements or in high-risk areas. This is a negotiated contractual term between the parties and is not mandated by statute, but licensees governed by the BCFSA and RESA have a duty to inform clients of conditions that may protect their interests. If an insurer declines to offer sewer backup coverage for a specific property, that information is material to a buyer's decision.

Does a BC strata corporation's Form B Information Certificate disclose whether the strata's insurance includes sewer backup coverage?

A Form B Information Certificate, required under section 59 of the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), must include information about the strata corporation's insurance, typically by attaching the insurance certificate or summary. Whether that insurance certificate explicitly itemizes a sewer backup endorsement depends on the strata's specific policy and how the insurer documents coverage. Buyers of strata lots should review the attached insurance documents carefully and ask the strata or their licensee whether sewer backup is confirmed as an included coverage.

Does the BC Property Transfer Tax apply differently to a home purchase where the property has a history of sewer backup claims?

No, the BC Property Transfer Tax Act applies to the fair market value of the property being transferred and does not vary based on the insurance claims history of the property, including prior sewer backup incidents. The standard PTT rates — 1% on the first $200,000, 2% on the portion from $200,000 to $3,000,000, 3% on the portion above $3,000,000, and an additional 2% on residential property value above $3,000,000 — are calculated solely on the purchase price or fair market value. A history of sewer backup claims may affect insurability or property value, but it has no direct effect on the PTT calculation.
