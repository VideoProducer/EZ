Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)

A federal program allowing first-time home buyers to withdraw up to $60,000 tax-free from their RRSP to purchase or build a qualifying home, repayable over 15 years. The withdrawal limit was increased to $60,000 effective April 16, 2024.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) and how does it apply to BC residents purchasing their first home?

The Home Buyers' Plan (HBP) is a federal program administered by the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) that allows eligible first-time home buyers across Canada, including those in British Columbia, to withdraw up to $60,000 tax-free from their Registered Retirement Savings Plan (RRSP) to purchase or build a qualifying home. The $60,000 individual limit — or up to $120,000 combined for two qualifying buyers purchasing together — was established effective April 16, 2024. The withdrawn amount must be repaid to the RRSP over a 15-year repayment period, or the unpaid portion is added to the buyer's taxable income each year. The HBP is a federal program and is not governed by BC statutes such as the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) or the BC Property Transfer Tax Act.

Can a BC buyer combine the HBP with the BC First-Time Home Buyers' Property Transfer Tax exemption?

Yes, a BC buyer may be eligible to use the HBP and the BC First-Time Home Buyers' Property Transfer Tax exemption simultaneously, as they are separate programs governed by different authorities. The BC First-Time Home Buyers' exemption under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act provides a full exemption from Property Transfer Tax for qualifying purchasers on homes priced up to $835,000, with a partial exemption available on homes priced above that threshold up to a prescribed limit. Eligibility criteria for each program are set independently — the HBP is governed by the federal Income Tax Act and administered by the CRA, while the PTT exemption is governed by the BC Property Transfer Tax Act and administered by the BC Ministry of Finance. Buyers should confirm their eligibility for each program separately with the CRA and the BC Ministry of Finance.

Does using the HBP affect the amount of Property Transfer Tax a BC buyer must pay?

No, using the HBP does not reduce or offset the Property Transfer Tax (PTT) owing on a property purchase in British Columbia, as these are entirely separate programs. PTT in BC is calculated under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act at 1% on the first $200,000 of the fair market value, 2% on the portion between $200,000 and $2,000,000, 3% on the portion between $2,000,000 and $3,000,000, and an additional 2% on the residential portion exceeding $3,000,000. The HBP simply allows the buyer to access RRSP funds tax-free to help fund their purchase, including potentially covering closing costs such as PTT where not otherwise exempted. Buyers should consult the BC Ministry of Finance for confirmation of current PTT rates and thresholds.

Are BC real estate licensees permitted to provide advice to clients about whether they should use the HBP?

BC real estate licensees licensed under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and regulated by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) are not permitted to provide tax, legal, or financial planning advice, as doing so falls outside their authorized scope of practice. The HBP involves federal income tax rules under the Income Tax Act and personal financial planning considerations that require advice from a qualified tax professional or financial advisor. A licensee may inform a client that the HBP exists as a general educational matter, but should direct clients to the CRA and a qualified advisor for personalized guidance. Providing tax advice could expose a licensee to regulatory consequences under RESA and the BCFSA Rules.

Can a BC buyer use HBP funds withdrawn from their RRSP to pay the deposit held in a real estate licensee's trust account?

HBP withdrawal proceeds are deposited directly into the buyer's personal bank account by their RRSP issuer and may then be used for any eligible purpose related to the qualifying home purchase, including a deposit. Under RESA and the BCFSA Rules, deposits received by a BC real estate licensee in connection with a real estate transaction must be held in a brokerage's designated trust account and disbursed in accordance with the terms of the contract. The source of deposit funds — including RRSP proceeds received through the HBP — does not alter the licensee's obligations regarding trust account handling. Buyers should ensure HBP funds are received in sufficient time to meet deposit deadlines specified in the Contract of Purchase and Sale.

Does the HBP apply to purchases of strata lots in BC, such as condominiums or townhomes governed by the Strata Property Act?

Yes, the HBP can generally be used toward the purchase of a strata lot in British Columbia, including apartments and townhomes, provided the property qualifies as an eligible property under the federal Income Tax Act and the buyer meets all HBP eligibility conditions as determined by the CRA. Strata lots in BC are governed by the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), which establishes the legal framework for strata corporations, common property, and ownership — but this provincial legislation does not impose restrictions on the source of funds a buyer uses to complete a strata purchase. Buyers purchasing strata lots should also review strata documents such as Form B (Information Certificate) and depreciation reports, which are required disclosures under the Strata Property Act, as part of their due diligence. Confirm HBP eligibility for the specific property type with the CRA.

What is the 15-year repayment obligation under the HBP, and what happens if a BC buyer fails to make their annual repayment?

Under the HBP, the total amount withdrawn from an RRSP must be repaid over a 15-year period, generally beginning in the second calendar year after the year of withdrawal, with a minimum annual repayment amount set by the CRA based on the total amount withdrawn. If a buyer does not make their required annual repayment to their RRSP, the amount that should have been repaid is included in their taxable income for that year, resulting in a federal and potentially provincial income tax liability. This repayment obligation is governed by the federal Income Tax Act and administered by the CRA — it is not a BC statutory requirement under any provincial real estate legislation. Buyers should monitor their HBP repayment schedule through their annual CRA Notice of Assessment and consult a tax professional if they anticipate difficulty meeting repayments.

Can a BC buyer use the HBP to purchase agricultural land located within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR)?

Whether a property located within BC's Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) qualifies under the HBP is determined by the CRA based on federal Income Tax Act criteria, not by the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36) or the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC). The ALC administers the ALR and imposes restrictions on subdivision, non-farm use, and residential use of agricultural land under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, which may affect the nature and use of the property being purchased. A buyer should confirm with the CRA whether the specific ALR property qualifies as an eligible home under the HBP, and separately consult the ALC regarding any applicable land-use restrictions. These are two independent regulatory frameworks operating at the federal and provincial levels respectively.

If a BC buyer receives HBP funds and the purchase falls through, what are their obligations?

If an HBP withdrawal is made but the qualifying home purchase does not complete, the CRA requires that the withdrawn funds be returned to an RRSP by December 31 of the year following the year of withdrawal, or the amount will be included in the buyer's taxable income for that year. This obligation arises under the federal Income Tax Act and is administered by the CRA — it is independent of any remedies or obligations that may arise under BC real estate law, such as forfeiture of a deposit governed by the terms of the Contract of Purchase and Sale. BC real estate licensees regulated by the BCFSA under RESA have obligations regarding the handling of trust funds in a failed transaction but have no role in the buyer's federal tax obligations related to the HBP. Buyers in this situation should contact the CRA promptly and seek advice from a qualified tax professional.

Is personal information collected from a BC buyer in connection with the HBP process subject to BC privacy legislation?

Personal information collected by a BC real estate licensee or brokerage from a buyer in connection with a real estate transaction — including information related to the buyer's use of the HBP — is subject to the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) of British Columbia, which governs how private-sector organizations collect, use, and disclose personal information. Licensees must collect only the personal information necessary for the transaction, obtain the individual's consent, and safeguard that information in accordance with PIPA. HBP-specific financial and tax information shared directly with the CRA or an RRSP issuer is governed by federal privacy legislation, specifically the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA), as those are federally regulated entities. Licensees regulated by the BCFSA under RESA should ensure their privacy practices comply with PIPA at all times.
