First Home Savings Account (FHSA)

A federal registered account allowing first-time home buyers to contribute up to $8,000 per year (to a lifetime limit of $40,000) on a tax-deductible basis, with tax-free withdrawals toward a qualifying home purchase. Can be combined with the Home Buyers' Plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a First Home Savings Account (FHSA) and how does it work for BC buyers?

The FHSA is a federal registered account that allows eligible first-time home buyers across Canada, including those in British Columbia, to contribute up to $8,000 per year to a lifetime maximum of $40,000, with contributions being tax-deductible and qualifying withdrawals being tax-free. The account is governed by federal Income Tax Act rules, not BC provincial legislation, so the contribution and withdrawal mechanics are the same in BC as elsewhere in Canada. BC buyers use the FHSA alongside provincial programs, but the account itself is administered through federally regulated financial institutions.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
Can a BC buyer use an FHSA together with the federal Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)?

Yes, a qualifying BC buyer can use both an FHSA and the Home Buyers' Plan in the same home purchase transaction, allowing them to combine tax-free FHSA withdrawals with an RRSP withdrawal under the HBP rules. These are separate federal programs governed by the Income Tax Act, and using one does not preclude using the other for the same qualifying home purchase. Buyers should confirm their specific eligibility for both programs with a qualified tax advisor, as individual circumstances vary.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
Does using an FHSA affect eligibility for BC's First-Time Home Buyers' Property Transfer Tax exemption?

The BC First-Time Home Buyers' Property Transfer Tax (PTT) exemption is a separate provincial program governed by the BC Property Transfer Tax Act, and FHSA use does not directly affect eligibility for it. Under the PTT Act, a first-time buyer may qualify for a full exemption on properties with a fair market value up to $835,000, subject to residency, citizenship, and property-use requirements set out in that Act. Buyers should verify their PTT exemption eligibility independently of their FHSA status with the BC Ministry of Finance.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
Can an FHSA be used to purchase a strata lot in BC?

Yes, a qualifying strata lot — such as a condominium unit or townhouse governed by the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) — can be a qualifying home for FHSA withdrawal purposes, provided it meets the federal definition of a qualifying home under the Income Tax Act. The Strata Property Act governs the ownership structure and strata corporation rules in BC but does not impose additional restrictions on how a buyer funds their purchase. Buyers should ensure the strata lot meets all federal FHSA qualifying home criteria before making a withdrawal.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
How might a BC real estate licensee ethically discuss the FHSA with a buyer client?

A BC real estate licensee licensed under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and regulated by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) may explain what an FHSA is in general, educational terms as part of their service to a buyer client. However, licensees must not provide tax or investment advice, as doing so falls outside the scope of licensed real estate services and could constitute unlicensed activity in another regulated profession. The BCFSA's professional conduct guidelines under RESA require licensees to refer clients to qualified tax and financial professionals for FHSA-specific advice.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
Can a BC buyer use FHSA funds to purchase agricultural land within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR)?

Whether FHSA funds can be used depends on the federal qualifying home definition under the Income Tax Act, not on ALR status; however, purchasing land in BC's Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) involves significant restrictions under the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36), including limitations on residential use and subdivision. If the property qualifies as a residential home under federal FHSA rules, the funds may technically be used, but the buyer must also comply with all ALR land-use restrictions administered by the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC). Buyers considering ALR properties should consult the ALC directly regarding permitted uses before proceeding.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
What happens to a BC resident's FHSA if they do not purchase a home?

Under the federal Income Tax Act rules, if an FHSA holder has not made a qualifying home purchase within a set period (currently by December 31 of the year the account holder turns a specified age, or within a set number of years of opening the account), the account must be closed and the funds transferred to an RRSP or RRIF on a tax-deferred basis, or withdrawn as taxable income. This is a federal rule applicable uniformly across Canada, including BC. Holders in this situation should consult a qualified tax advisor for guidance on the transfer and tax implications.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
Does BC's Speculation and Vacancy Tax interact with FHSA use in any way?

BC's Speculation and Vacancy Tax is an annual provincial tax on residential properties in designated BC regions, assessed based on how a property is used and the owner's residency status, and is entirely separate from the federal FHSA program. Using an FHSA to fund a home purchase does not create any exemption from, or additional obligation under, the Speculation and Vacancy Tax; a buyer's liability is determined by the BC Ministry of Finance based on property use and ownership criteria. Buyers purchasing in a designated area should review their Speculation and Vacancy Tax obligations independently of their FHSA use.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
Can a BC licensee collect or store a buyer's FHSA account information, and what privacy rules apply?

If a BC real estate licensee collects personal financial information from a client, including details about an FHSA, that information is subject to BC's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), which requires that personal information be collected only for identified purposes, with consent, and be protected from unauthorized use or disclosure. Licensees regulated by the BCFSA under RESA must handle client personal information in a manner consistent with PIPA obligations. FHSA account details are sensitive financial information and should be collected only to the extent genuinely necessary for the real estate transaction.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
What happens to a BC resident's FHSA on death, and are there estate implications under BC law?

On the death of an FHSA holder, the federal Income Tax Act governs how the account is treated, generally allowing a tax-free transfer to a surviving spouse's or common-law partner's FHSA or RRSP if the proper designation is in place, or otherwise resulting in the account value being included in the deceased's income in the year of death. In BC, the distribution of estate assets — including registered account proceeds not passing by designated beneficiary — is governed by the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA). Individuals should ensure their FHSA beneficiary designations and estate plans are coordinated with a qualified legal and tax professional familiar with both federal FHSA rules and BC's WESA.

Verify with: Canada Revenue Agency
