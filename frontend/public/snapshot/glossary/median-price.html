Median Price

Median price is a statistical measure used to describe property values within a defined market area and time period. All recorded sale prices are arranged in ascending order; the value that falls exactly in the middle is the median. When the total number of sales is odd, the median is the single middle figure. When the total is even, the median is the arithmetic midpoint between the two central values. Because the median is unaffected by extreme high or low outliers, it is generally considered a more stable indicator of typical market conditions than a simple average. Real estate boards and bodies such as CMHC commonly publish median price data to help buyers, sellers, and licensees gauge market trends. No BC statute defines median price for general market reporting purposes; verify current methodology and data sources with CMHC, BCFSA, or a licensed real estate professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between median price and average price in BC real estate?

Median price is the middle sale value when all transactions are ranked from lowest to highest, meaning half sold for more and half for less. Average price is the total dollar volume divided by the number of sales, which can be skewed by a few very high or very low sales. Neither metric is defined or regulated by a specific BC statute; they are standard market statistics compiled by real estate boards and reported to inform consumers. Verify how these figures are calculated for your local market with a BC REALTOR® or real estate professional.

Does the BC government use median price to set property transfer tax (PTT) rates or exemptions?

No. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, PTT rates and exemption thresholds (such as the First-Time Home Buyer exemption up to $835,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current)) are set by dollar figures in the statute and regulations, not by median prices. The Additional PTT (foreign buyer tax) also applies based on statutory thresholds, not median market prices. Verify current PTT thresholds and eligibility with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before completing a transaction.

Is median price used to determine affordability for BC housing programs or grants?

BC housing programs, such as those administered under the Home Purchase Assistance Act or by BC Housing, typically use specific income and purchase-price limits set by regulation or policy, not median prices. The BC Home Owner Grant Act provides property tax relief based on assessed value thresholds and owner-occupier status, not median market prices. Verify current program eligibility criteria with BC Housing or the BC Ministry of Finance, as limits and eligibility rules change and are specific to each program (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).

Can a REALTOR® or licensee publish median price data in their marketing materials under BC rules?

Yes, provided the data is accurate, not misleading, and properly sourced. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the BCFSA Rules, licensees must ensure all representations are truthful and substantiated. Median price statistics are commonly provided by local real estate boards; licensees should cite the source, date range, and geographic area clearly. Verify compliance with current BCFSA advertising and disclosure rules before publishing market statistics.

Does median price affect the assessed value used for property taxes in BC?

No. BC Assessment determines assessed values under the Assessment Act, RSBC 1996, c. 20, using actual sales and property characteristics as of a legislated valuation date (July 1 of the prior year (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current)). Median price is a market statistic and is not a substitute for or directly used in the statutory valuation process. Property taxes are calculated by municipalities using the assessed value and applicable mill rates; verify your assessment and tax obligations with BC Assessment and your local government.

Is median price relevant to determining market value for mortgage or appraisal purposes in BC?

Appraisers and lenders assess individual properties using comparable sales, property condition, location, and other factors; median price is a broad market indicator but not a substitute for a professional appraisal. CMHC and private lenders rely on appraisals that comply with professional standards (e.g., Appraisal Institute of Canada), not summary statistics. Verify the appraisal and lending requirements for your transaction with your lender or a qualified appraiser (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).

Can median price be used to calculate the Home Flipping Tax in BC?

No. Under the Home Flipping Tax Act, SBC 2024 (effective January 1, 2025 — verify current), the tax applies to the income (gain) realized on a residential property sale that occurs within a specified holding period, calculated using the actual sale price and acquisition cost, not median market prices. The taxable income is subject to rates that decline with longer holding periods. Verify your filing obligations and applicable rates with a licensed tax professional or the BC Ministry of Finance before selling a residential property acquired after the effective date.

Does the Speculation and Vacancy Tax in BC use median price to determine liability?

No. Under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, liability is based on the property's assessed value (as determined by BC Assessment), the owner's residency and occupancy status, and location within a designated taxable region. Median price is not a factor in calculating the tax, which ranges from 0.5% to 2% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) of assessed value depending on owner classification. Verify your declaration and exemption eligibility annually with the BC Ministry of Finance.

Is median price a mandatory disclosure in a BC Contract of Purchase and Sale?

No. The Contract of Purchase and Sale in BC (typically the standard forms prepared by the BC Real Estate Association) requires disclosure of the agreed purchase price, deposit, conditions, and other transaction-specific terms, but does not require disclosure of market statistics such as median price. Under RESA and the BCFSA Rules, licensees must disclose all material latent defects and conflicts of interest, but median price is market context, not a material fact about the specific property. Verify disclosure obligations for your transaction with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional.

Can strata corporations in BC rely on median price to set special levies or contingency fund contributions?

No. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the Strata Property Regulation, strata corporations must base operating fund and contingency reserve fund (CRF) contributions on budgeted expenses, a depreciation report (if required), and strata lot unit entitlement, not on median market prices. Special levies are approved by resolution for specific expenses and apportioned by unit entitlement. Verify the strata's financial planning and levy requirements with the strata council, strata manager, or a BC lawyer familiar with strata law (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).
