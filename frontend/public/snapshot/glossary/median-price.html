Median Price

A median price is the middle transaction when all sales within a defined period are ordered from lowest to highest. Half of sales occur below this point and half occur above it. If there is an odd number of sales, the median is the exact middle sale. If there is an even number of sales, the median is calculated as the midpoint between the two central values.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 'median price' mean in the context of BC real estate market statistics?

The median price is the middle transaction value when all sales in a defined period are arranged from lowest to highest, meaning exactly half of sales occurred below that figure and half above it. If there is an odd number of sales, the median is the exact middle sale price; if there is an even number, it is the midpoint between the two central values. This measure is widely used by BC real estate boards and the BC Real Estate Association to report market conditions.

How does the median price differ from the average (mean) price in BC housing market reports?

The average price is calculated by adding all sale prices together and dividing by the number of transactions, making it sensitive to extremely high or low outliers. The median price, by contrast, is simply the middle value in a ranked list of sales, so a handful of very expensive luxury sales in markets like West Vancouver will not skew it as dramatically as they would the average. For this reason, analysts and BC real estate boards often favour the median when describing what a 'typical' buyer paid in a given period.

Is a BC real estate licensee required by RESA or BCFSA rules to disclose the median price to a client?

The Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and its Rules, administered by the BCFSA, require licensees to act honestly, with reasonable care and skill, and in the best interests of their clients, which includes providing relevant market information. While RESA does not specifically mandate disclosure of median price data as a standalone figure, a licensee's duty of competence and the requirement to disclose known material information means that relevant market context — which may include median pricing — should be shared when it would materially affect a client's decision. Licensees should consult current BCFSA guidance for specifics on their disclosure obligations.

Can the median sale price affect how Property Transfer Tax (PTT) is calculated on a BC home purchase?

Property Transfer Tax under BC's Property Transfer Tax Act is calculated on the fair market value of the property being transferred, not on any regional median price figure. The standard PTT tiers are 1% on the first $200,000 of fair market value, 2% on the portion from $200,000 to $2,000,000, 3% on the portion from $2,000,000 to $3,000,000, and an additional 2% on any residential value above $3,000,000. Median price data is a market-analysis tool only and has no direct role in determining a buyer's PTT liability.

How is the median price of strata lots in a BC development typically calculated, and which legislation governs strata ownership?

When reporting median prices for strata lots, analysts rank all strata unit sales in the defined period from lowest to highest and identify the middle value, applying the same mathematical method used for any real estate asset class. Strata ownership in BC is governed by the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), which defines strata lots, common property, and limited common property, but that Act does not prescribe how market pricing statistics are compiled or reported. Median price data for strata units is produced by real estate boards and third-party data providers outside the Strata Property Act framework.

Does the median price of farmland in BC's Agricultural Land Reserve reflect the restrictions placed on that land?

Median sale prices for properties within the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) typically reflect the market's assessment of the land under the use restrictions imposed by the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36), administered by the Agricultural Land Commission (ALC). Because non-farm uses, subdivision, and non-adhering residential development within the ALR are significantly restricted, ALR land generally trades at median prices different from comparable non-ALR parcels of similar size. Buyers and sellers of ALR land should consult the ALC directly for current rules, as permitted uses affect value.

If a BC estate is being settled and the executor needs to establish fair market value, is the regional median price sufficient evidence?

Under BC's Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA), an executor has a duty to administer estate assets in the best interests of beneficiaries, which typically requires establishing the fair market value of real property through a qualified appraisal rather than relying solely on regional median price statistics. Median price figures reflect aggregated market data and do not account for the specific condition, location, and attributes of the individual property being valued. Executors should obtain a formal appraisal and seek independent professional advice to fulfil their obligations under WESA.

Can a BC real estate licensee use median price data in marketing emails to prospective clients, and does CASL apply?

Sending commercial electronic messages containing market data such as median prices to prospective clients constitutes a commercial electronic message under Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL), meaning the licensee must have express or implied consent, include identification information, and provide a functional unsubscribe mechanism. In addition, BC's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) requires that personal information used to send such communications was collected with appropriate consent and for an identified purpose. Licensees should ensure their email marketing practices comply with both CASL and PIPA before distributing median price reports.

How does the First-Time Home Buyers' Program threshold relate to median prices in BC?

The First-Time Home Buyers' Program under BC's Property Transfer Tax Act provides a full PTT exemption for eligible first-time buyers purchasing a property with a fair market value at or below $835,000, with a partial exemption available up to a higher threshold — consult current BC Ministry of Finance guidance for the precise partial-exemption ceiling. Whether a property is priced above or below the median for a given area is a separate market-analysis concept and does not itself determine eligibility for the exemption. Eligibility is assessed on the individual property's fair market value and the buyer's qualifications, not on how the price compares to regional median figures.

Why can the median price for a BC neighbourhood change significantly from one quarter to the next even if underlying home values have not shifted?

The median price is sensitive to the composition of sales in any given period: if a quarter sees an unusually high proportion of smaller condos or entry-level homes sell, the median will fall even if individual property values remain stable, and vice versa when larger or higher-value homes dominate the sales mix. This compositional effect is particularly pronounced in smaller BC markets where the total number of transactions is limited, causing the median to shift based on which specific properties happened to change hands. Interpreting median price trends therefore requires considering sales volume, property type mix, and other contextual factors alongside the headline figure.
