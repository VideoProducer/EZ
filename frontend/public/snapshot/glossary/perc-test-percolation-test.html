Perc Test (Percolation Test)

A perc test (percolation test) measures how quickly soil absorbs water to determine whether a site can support an on-site sewage system and, if so, what type. In British Columbia, on-site sewage systems are regulated under provincial authority; verify current requirements with BC's Ministry of Health or the relevant local health authority. Properties without municipal sewer service typically require a satisfactory perc test result before a sewage system permit can be issued. Buyers of rural or acreage properties should treat a perc test as essential due diligence, as a failed or marginal result may significantly affect a property's usability and value. Confirm current testing standards, approval processes, and any associated costs with a qualified professional and verify current details with a BC lawyer or licensed professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a perc test and why is it required in BC?

A percolation test measures how quickly water drains through soil to determine whether a site can support a septic system and what design is feasible. In BC, properties not connected to municipal sewer typically require approval under local government bylaws (authorized under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1) and must meet Provincial sewage disposal standards before a building permit can be issued. Verify current sewage system approval requirements with your local health authority and municipal planning department before purchasing rural or acreage property.

Who is authorized to perform a perc test in British Columbia?

Perc tests and soil evaluations for on-site sewage systems must be conducted by qualified professionals as specified by local health authorities and in accordance with provincial sewage system standards. The specific qualifications (for example, a professional engineer or registered onsite wastewater practitioner) vary by regional health authority and municipal bylaw adopted under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Verify the current professional requirements with the health authority and building department in the jurisdiction where the property is located before commissioning a test.

Verify with: Government of BC
Is a perc test required for all rural properties in BC?

A perc test or soil evaluation is typically required only if the property lacks municipal sewer connection and an on-site sewage disposal system (septic) must be installed or upgraded. Properties already served by an approved, functioning septic system may not need a new test unless expansion or replacement is planned; local bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, govern these requirements. Verify whether a current perc test or sewage disposal permit exists, and whether re-testing is needed, with the local health authority and building department before finalizing your purchase.

Verify with: Government of BC
Can a property fail a perc test, and what happens if it does?

Yes, a site can fail a perc test if the soil drains too slowly, too quickly, or is otherwise unsuitable for a conventional septic system. In such cases, alternative systems (for example, mound, sand filter, or advanced treatment systems) may be required if permitted by local bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, though these systems are typically more expensive. If no compliant sewage disposal system can be approved, the property may be unbuildable; verify feasibility and obtain written confirmation of sewage system approval before removing subject conditions in a purchase contract.

Verify with: Government of BC
How much does a perc test cost in BC?

Perc test and soil evaluation costs vary widely depending on site accessibility, required testing depth, laboratory analysis, and the professional's fees; no statutory fee schedule exists under BC law. As a general market observation (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), costs may range from several hundred to several thousand dollars, but you must obtain written quotes from qualified practitioners in your area. Verify current pricing and required scope of work with local registered onsite wastewater practitioners or engineers before budgeting for due diligence.

Verify with: Government of BC
Should a perc test be a subject condition in a BC real estate contract?

Including a condition that allows the buyer to obtain, at the buyer's expense, a satisfactory perc test or sewage system approval is common prudent practice on rural properties without municipal sewer, though contract terms are a matter of negotiation. The Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, requires licensees to advise clients competently but does not prescribe mandatory contract clauses. Verify the appropriateness and wording of any subject condition with your lawyer or notary and ensure sufficient time (often 10–30 days) is allowed to complete testing and obtain health authority approval before the condition expires.

Verify with: Government of BC
Does the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) affect perc test or septic requirements?

Land within the ALR, governed by the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, is subject to both ALC restrictions on non-farm use and residential construction, and to the same local health authority sewage disposal standards as non-ALR land. A perc test and sewage system approval are still required if a dwelling is to be built, but ALC permission may also be needed for residential use or soil removal associated with septic installation (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify both ALC and local health authority/building permit requirements with the Agricultural Land Commission, regional health authority, and your local government before proceeding.

Verify with: Government of BC
How long is a perc test valid in BC?

There is no single province-wide expiry period for perc tests; validity depends on the policies of the local health authority and whether site conditions (soil disturbance, grading, or hydrology) have changed since the test was performed. Some jurisdictions may accept reports from recent years if conditions are unchanged, while others require fresh testing if a building permit has not been issued within a specified time (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the current validity and acceptance of any existing perc test report with the health authority and building department in the property's jurisdiction before relying on it.

Verify with: Government of BC
What is the difference between a perc test and a soil or site evaluation?

A traditional percolation test measures water drainage rate in a test hole, while a comprehensive site evaluation (now commonly required in BC) assesses soil texture, depth, water table, slope, and other factors to determine the appropriate sewage system design. Many jurisdictions in BC have moved toward site evaluations conducted by qualified professionals rather than simple perc tests, as required under local bylaws adopted under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the specific testing and reporting requirements with your local health authority and building department, as terminology and standards vary by region.

Verify with: Government of BC
Can I sell a BC rural property 'as-is' without a perc test?

Yes, properties may be sold 'as-is' without a current perc test, and neither the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, nor the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, mandates a perc test as a condition of sale. However, RESA Rules require licensees to disclose known material latent defects, and buyers often insist on a perc test or sewage approval condition to protect their ability to build; failure to disclose known soil or septic issues may expose the seller to misrepresentation claims. Verify disclosure obligations and contract risks with your lawyer or notary, and ensure any 'as-is' language is clear and legally sufficient before listing or purchasing.
