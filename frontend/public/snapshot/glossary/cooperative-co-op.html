Title & Ownership

Cooperative (Co-op)

A housing cooperative (co-op) is a form of residential ownership in which a corporation holds title to the land and building under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, while residents hold shares in that corporation entitling them to occupy a specific unit under a proprietary lease rather than owning a strata lot directly under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. Because the resident owns shares rather than real property, financing arrangements differ materially from conventional mortgages and lenders may apply different qualification criteria; verify current lending terms with a licensed mortgage professional. Share transfers typically require board approval, and the co-op's bylaws govern resale processes in ways that have no direct equivalent in strata or fee simple ownership. Property transfer tax obligations under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378 may apply differently to co-op share transfers than to title transfers; verify current treatment with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a housing cooperative (co-op) in British Columbia, and how does ownership differ from strata or fee simple?

In a BC housing cooperative, residents own shares in a corporation that holds title to the entire building, rather than owning individual strata lots or fee simple land. Each shareholder typically receives an exclusive right to occupy a specific unit under the cooperative's bylaws and occupancy agreement. This structure is distinct from strata ownership under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, where each owner holds a separate legal title to their strata lot. Verify financing, transfer, and tax implications with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before purchasing co-op shares.

Are BC housing cooperatives governed by the Strata Property Act?

No. Housing cooperatives in British Columbia are corporations governed by the Cooperative Association Act, SBC 1999, c. 28, not by the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. Co-ops have their own articles, bylaws, and occupancy agreements that set out rules, approval processes, and governance. Because the regulatory framework differs materially from strata corporations, prospective co-op purchasers should verify all governance, transfer, and approval requirements with a BC lawyer or notary before committing to a purchase.

Verify with: Cooperative Housing
Can I get a traditional mortgage to buy shares in a BC housing cooperative?

Obtaining conventional mortgage financing for co-op shares in BC can be more difficult than for fee simple or strata properties, because the purchaser does not hold legal title to real property—only shares in a corporation. Some lenders, including Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC)–approved lenders, may offer co-op share loans, but terms, rates, and down-payment requirements often differ from standard mortgages. Verify current lending options and approval criteria with a mortgage broker or lender experienced in co-op financing, and consult a BC lawyer or notary regarding the legal structure of the share purchase.

Do I pay BC Property Transfer Tax (PTT) when I buy shares in a housing cooperative?

Generally, no. The purchase of shares in a cooperative housing corporation does not typically constitute a transfer of real property that triggers Property Transfer Tax under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378. However, if the cooperative itself acquires real property, the cooperative may be liable for PTT on that transaction. Verify the specific tax treatment of your co-op share purchase with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before closing, as circumstances and ministerial interpretations can vary.

Are co-op share purchases subject to BC's Home Flipping Tax?

The Home Flipping Tax Act, SBC 2024 (effective January 1, 2025 — verify current), applies to certain dispositions of residential property held for less than two years (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Because co-op shares are not legal title to real property, the application of the Home Flipping Tax to co-op shares is uncertain and may depend on ministerial interpretation or future amendments. Verify the current treatment of co-op share sales under the Home Flipping Tax Act with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before selling.

Can a housing cooperative in BC refuse to approve a prospective purchaser of shares?

Yes. Most BC housing cooperatives have bylaws and occupancy agreements that give the co-op board discretion to approve or refuse prospective share purchasers, subject to human rights protections under the Human Rights Code, RSBC 1996, c. 210. Approval criteria may include financial suitability, references, interviews, and compatibility with the co-op's values or mandate. Because approval processes vary widely among co-ops, prospective purchasers should review the co-op's bylaws and consult a BC lawyer or notary to understand approval requirements and timelines before entering into a contract of purchase and sale.

Are BC housing cooperatives subject to the Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, applies to owners of residential property in designated taxable regions of BC. Because co-op shareholders do not hold legal title to real property, individual shareholders are generally not directly liable for the Speculation and Vacancy Tax; instead, the cooperative corporation itself, as the registered owner, may be subject to the tax if the property is in a designated area and not exempt. Verify the tax status of a specific co-op property with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional, as exemptions and interpretations may apply.

Can I rent out my unit in a BC housing cooperative to a third party?

This depends entirely on the cooperative's bylaws and occupancy agreement. Many BC housing cooperatives restrict or prohibit rentals to preserve the cooperative's character, occupancy eligibility, and mandate, while some co-ops permit limited rentals with board approval. The Cooperative Association Act, SBC 1999, c. 28, allows co-ops to set their own membership and occupancy rules, subject to the Human Rights Code, RSBC 1996, c. 210. Verify rental restrictions and approval processes with the co-op board and a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing shares if you anticipate needing to rent the unit.

Verify with: Cooperative Housing
What happens to my co-op shares if I die? Can they be transferred to my estate like other real property?

Co-op shares are personal property, not real property, and are governed by the cooperative's bylaws, articles, and occupancy agreement, as well as the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA), SBC 2009, c. 13. Many co-ops require the estate or beneficiaries to apply for board approval to occupy the unit or transfer the shares, and some bylaws impose time limits or mandatory sale provisions. Because co-op succession rules vary widely and may differ from fee simple or strata succession, executors and beneficiaries should consult a BC lawyer or notary experienced in co-op law and estate administration to verify transfer procedures and timelines.

Verify with: Cooperative Housing
Are BC housing cooperatives exempt from municipal property taxes, and do residents pay property tax directly?

Housing cooperatives in BC are generally subject to municipal property taxes on the land and buildings they own, assessed under the applicable local government taxation bylaws authorized by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. The cooperative corporation, as the registered owner, is responsible for paying the property tax, and co-op residents typically contribute their proportionate share through monthly housing charges or maintenance fees, not through direct individual tax bills. Some non-profit or limited-equity co-ops may qualify for property tax exemptions under local bylaws; verify the tax status and your housing charge breakdown with the co-op board and a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.
