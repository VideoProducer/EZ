Home Buyer Rescission Period (HBRP)

The Home Buyer Rescission Period (HBRP) is a statutory right under the Property Law Act (RSBC 1996, c. 290) that allows a buyer of eligible residential property in British Columbia to rescind an accepted contract of purchase and sale within a prescribed number of business days of acceptance, without providing reasons. Exercise of the right requires the buyer to pay a rescission fee calculated as a percentage of the purchase price; verify the exact period, fee percentage, and effective date with a BC lawyer or notary, as these figures must be confirmed against current regulations. The period runs in business days, excluding weekends and statutory holidays, as defined under the applicable legislation. The right cannot be contracted out of or waived by either party. This right is distinct from the separate rescission right available to purchasers of presale units under the Real Estate Development Marketing Act; verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Home Buyer Rescission Period (HBRP) in British Columbia?

The HBRP is a statutory 3-business-day cooling-off period under Section 42 of the Property Law Act (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) that allows a residential buyer to rescind an accepted offer to purchase by paying a rescission fee of 0.25% of the purchase price (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). It came into effect on January 3, 2023 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The period excludes weekends and statutory holidays, and the right cannot be waived by the parties.

When did the Home Buyer Rescission Period start in BC?

The HBRP came into force on January 3, 2023 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), pursuant to Section 42 of the Property Law Act. It applies to contracts of purchase and sale for residential real property entered into on or after that date. Verify the current application date and any amendments with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

How much does it cost to rescind a contract under the HBRP?

The rescission fee is 0.25% of the purchase price (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), as set out in Section 42 of the Property Law Act. For example, on a $1,000,000 purchase, the fee would be $2,500 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). This fee is paid to the seller and is separate from any deposit; verify the calculation and payment mechanism with a BC lawyer or notary.

Can a buyer waive the Home Buyer Rescission Period in BC?

No. Section 42 of the Property Law Act (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) expressly provides that the HBRP cannot be waived or contracted out of by the parties. Any contract term purporting to waive or shorten this statutory right is void. Verify the enforceability of any clause with a BC lawyer or notary.

How are the 3 business days calculated under the HBRP?

The 3-business-day period under Section 42 of the Property Law Act (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) excludes Saturdays, Sundays, and statutory holidays as defined in BC law. The count begins the day after the contract is accepted. Because calculation details can vary depending on contract timing and holidays, verify the exact deadline with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Is the HBRP the same as the 7-day rescission period for presale condos?

No. The HBRP under Section 42 of the Property Law Act (3 business days, 0.25% fee, effective January 3, 2023; as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) is separate from the 7-day rescission right for presale strata units governed by the Real Estate Development Marketing Act (REDMA). Different rules, timelines, and fees apply to each; verify which rescission right (if any) applies to your transaction with a BC lawyer or notary.

Does the HBRP apply to commercial real estate purchases in BC?

Section 42 of the Property Law Act (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) applies to contracts for the purchase and sale of 'residential real property.' The statute defines what qualifies as residential property. If the property is purely commercial, the HBRP likely does not apply, but verify your specific property classification and contract with a BC lawyer or notary before relying on any exemption.

What happens to the buyer's deposit if they rescind under the HBRP?

Under Section 42 of the Property Law Act (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), if the buyer properly rescinds within the 3-business-day period and pays the 0.25% rescission fee (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), the contract is void and any deposit paid must be returned to the buyer. The seller retains only the rescission fee. Verify the exact return mechanism and timing with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: BCFSA — Home Buyer Rescission Period
Can a seller refuse to accept a rescission under the HBRP if the buyer pays the fee?

No. Section 42 of the Property Law Act (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) grants the buyer an unconditional statutory right to rescind within the 3-business-day period by paying the 0.25% fee (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The seller has no discretion to refuse a valid rescission notice accompanied by payment of the prescribed fee. If a seller refuses, verify your remedies with a BC lawyer or notary.

Does the HBRP apply to assignments of presale contracts in BC?

The application of Section 42 of the Property Law Act (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) to assignment contracts depends on whether the assignment is treated as a 'contract of purchase and sale' of residential real property under the statute. Case law and regulatory interpretation on this point are still developing. Verify whether the HBRP applies to your specific assignment transaction with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.
