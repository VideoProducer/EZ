Income Verification

Income verification is the documentation a lender requires to qualify a borrower for a mortgage — typically recent pay stubs, T4 slips, Notices of Assessment from the Canada Revenue Agency, and an employment letter. Self-employed and commission-income borrowers usually provide 2 years of T1 General returns plus business financials. Lenders require originals or e-signed copies and commonly confirm employment directly with the employer. Income-verification standards for federally regulated lenders are set under OSFI Guideline B-20.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents does a lender in British Columbia typically require for income verification when applying for a mortgage?

BC lenders commonly require recent pay stubs, T4 slips, a current employment letter confirming position and salary, and Notices of Assessment (NOAs) from the Canada Revenue Agency. Federally regulated lenders such as banks must follow OSFI Guideline B-20, which sets minimum underwriting standards for income documentation. Lenders may also verify employment details directly with the borrower's employer before approving the mortgage.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions — Guideline B-20 · OSFI — Guideline B-20
How does income verification work differently for self-employed borrowers in British Columbia?

Self-employed borrowers in BC typically cannot rely on pay stubs or T4 slips, so lenders instead require two years of T1 General income tax returns along with corresponding CRA Notices of Assessment and, in many cases, corporate or business financial statements. This two-year history allows the lender to assess income stability and calculate an average qualifying income. OSFI Guideline B-20 requires federally regulated lenders to apply consistent, documented underwriting policies when assessing self-employed income.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions — Guideline B-20 · OSFI — Guideline B-20
Does a BC real estate licensee have any obligations related to a buyer's income verification process?

Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and the rules established by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA), a licensee's duty is to act in the client's best interests and to disclose material information, but income verification itself is a lender function, not a licensee function. A licensee must not misrepresent a client's financial position to a lender, as doing so could constitute professional misconduct under BCFSA's regulatory framework. Licensees should refer clients to a mortgage broker or lender for guidance on specific documentation requirements.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions — Guideline B-20 · OSFI — Guideline B-20
Can commission-based employees in British Columbia use their commission income to qualify for a mortgage?

Yes, commission income can be used to qualify for a mortgage in BC, but lenders typically require a two-year history of commission earnings documented through T4 slips, T1 General returns, and CRA Notices of Assessment to demonstrate consistency and sustainability. Federally regulated lenders applying OSFI Guideline B-20 must use documented, verifiable income and may average commission earnings over the two-year period rather than using the most recent year's figure alone. Variable or declining commission income may be assessed conservatively.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions — Guideline B-20 · OSFI — Guideline B-20
Are there privacy rules in British Columbia governing how lenders and licensees handle income verification documents?

Yes, the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) of BC governs how private-sector organizations — including lenders and real estate licensees — collect, use, and disclose personal information such as income documents. Under PIPA, organizations must collect only the personal information necessary for the identified purpose, obtain appropriate consent, and protect the information with reasonable security safeguards. Clients have the right to request access to their personal information held by an organization and to request corrections where information is inaccurate.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions — Guideline B-20 · OSFI — Guideline B-20
What is OSFI Guideline B-20, and why does it matter to BC homebuyers seeking mortgage approval?

OSFI Guideline B-20 is the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions' residential mortgage underwriting guideline, which applies to all federally regulated lenders such as chartered banks operating in BC. It sets minimum standards for income verification, requiring lenders to use documented and sustainable income — not stated or unverified income — when assessing a borrower's ability to repay. Because most BC buyers finance through federally regulated institutions, B-20 effectively defines the income verification standards they will encounter during mortgage qualification.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions — Guideline B-20 · OSFI — Guideline B-20
Can a buyer in BC use rental income from an investment property to help qualify for a mortgage?

Rental income may be considered by a lender when qualifying a BC borrower, but the lender's policies — shaped by OSFI Guideline B-20 for federally regulated institutions — typically require documented rental income from lease agreements and, in many cases, confirmation on the borrower's T1 General returns or NOA. Lenders often apply a vacancy factor or use only a portion of the gross rental income to calculate net qualifying income. Borrowers should consult their lender or mortgage broker for the specific rental-income treatment applied by that institution.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions — Guideline B-20 · OSFI — Guideline B-20
Does income verification affect eligibility for the BC First-Time Home Buyers' Property Transfer Tax exemption?

The First-Time Home Buyer exemption under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act is not directly tied to income verification — eligibility is based on factors such as residency, first-time buyer status, the property value (full exemption up to $835,000), and intended use as a principal residence. However, income verification is required separately by the mortgage lender to approve financing. Buyers should confirm current exemption thresholds and eligibility conditions with the BC Ministry of Finance, as these figures are subject to legislative change.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions — Guideline B-20 · OSFI — Guideline B-20
What happens if a borrower in BC provides falsified income documents to a lender?

Providing falsified income documents to a lender constitutes mortgage fraud under the Criminal Code of Canada and can result in criminal charges, immediate loan recall, and civil liability. If a licensed real estate professional in BC knowingly assists in or facilitates the submission of false income documents, this constitutes serious professional misconduct subject to disciplinary action by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), including fines, suspension, or licence cancellation. Lenders who discover fraud may also report the matter to law enforcement and FINTRAC.

Verify with: Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions — Guideline B-20 · OSFI — Guideline B-20
When a BC property is sold through an estate, does the executor need to provide income verification to transfer the property?

An executor administering an estate under the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA) is not required to provide personal income verification simply to transfer or sell estate property, as the executor acts in a representative capacity rather than as a borrower. However, if the estate itself is seeking financing — for example, to pay estate debts before sale proceeds are available — the lender may require documentation of the estate's assets and cash flow rather than personal income documents. Executors should obtain independent legal advice to ensure compliance with WESA and any lender-specific requirements.
