Building Envelope

The building envelope is the physical barrier separating a building's interior from the exterior environment, comprising the walls, roof, windows, doors, and foundation assembly. In BC, widespread building envelope failures — commonly referred to as the "leaky condo crisis" — affected a significant number of multi-family residential buildings, particularly strata properties constructed during the 1980s and 1990s; verify the full historical scope with a BC lawyer or building envelope engineer. Remediation of affected buildings involved substantial costs borne by strata corporations and owners; no specific aggregate dollar figure is cited here — verify current estimates through CMHC or BC government sources. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, strata corporations have obligations relating to the repair and maintenance of common property, which may include building envelope components. Buyers conducting pre-purchase due diligence should review available strata documentation, including depreciation reports and meeting minutes, for any history of envelope remediation or ongoing assessments. Consult a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed building inspector to assess envelope-related risks before completing a purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a building envelope in BC real estate?

The building envelope is the physical barrier between the interior and exterior of a building, including walls, roof, windows, doors, and foundation. It controls air, water, heat, and vapour transfer. In BC, building envelope integrity is a critical consideration in strata property due diligence, particularly for buildings constructed in the 1980s and 1990s. Verify current building code requirements and inspection standards with a BC building professional.

What was the BC 'leaky condo crisis' and how does it relate to building envelopes?

The leaky condo crisis refers to widespread building envelope failures in thousands of BC condominiums and townhouses built primarily in the 1980s and 1990s, resulting in water ingress, mould, and structural damage. Remediation costs across the province totaled billions of dollars. This history makes building envelope condition, depreciation reports under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and any prior envelope remediation work standard items in pre-purchase strata due diligence. Verify the building envelope history and available documentation with a BC lawyer or building envelope professional before purchasing.

Does BC law require strata corporations to disclose building envelope problems to prospective buyers?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation must provide Form B (Information Certificate) to a buyer or buyer's representative, disclosing known issues including special levies, litigation, and insurance claims that may relate to building envelope defects. Sellers and licensees under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and RESA Rules have disclosure obligations regarding material latent defects. Verify the scope of disclosure obligations and review all strata documents with a BC lawyer or notary before completing a purchase.

What is a depreciation report and how does it relate to building envelope condition?

A depreciation report is a long-term maintenance and funding plan required under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, s. 94 for most strata corporations, assessing the condition and remaining useful life of common property and assets, including the building envelope. The report must include repair and replacement cost estimates. Buyers should review the most recent depreciation report to understand anticipated building envelope repair or replacement timelines and associated costs. Verify current depreciation report requirements and interpretation with a BC lawyer, notary, or qualified building professional.

Verify with: BC Housing — Building Science Library
Can a strata corporation levy owners to pay for building envelope repairs?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, s. 108, a strata corporation may approve a special levy by a 3/4 vote at a general meeting to raise funds for expenses not covered by the contingency reserve fund, including building envelope remediation. Special levies are allocated to strata lot owners according to unit entitlement unless the bylaws provide otherwise. Verify the amount, payment terms, and any approved or pending special levies in the strata's Form B (Information Certificate) and minutes with a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing.

Are there BC building code standards that govern building envelope design and construction?

Yes. The BC Building Code, adopted under authority of provincial statute, sets minimum standards for building envelope design, construction, materials, and performance, including air barriers, vapour barriers, water management, and thermal resistance. Compliance is enforced at the municipal level through building permits and inspections. For new construction or major renovations, verify that the building envelope design and construction comply with current BC Building Code requirements by reviewing permits, field reviews, and engaging a qualified building envelope professional.

Verify with: BC Housing — Building Science Library
What records should a buyer review to assess building envelope condition in a strata building?

A buyer should review the strata's depreciation report, Form B (Information Certificate), engineering reports, building envelope assessments, warranty documents, insurance claims history, special levy resolutions, and meeting minutes discussing envelope condition or repairs. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, these documents form part of standard pre-purchase due diligence. Verify document completeness, interpretation, and any red flags with a BC lawyer, notary, or building envelope consultant before removing subject conditions.

Verify with: BC Housing — Building Science Library
Is a building envelope inspection required when purchasing a strata unit in BC?

BC law does not mandate a building envelope inspection as a condition of sale for resale strata units, but prudent buyers often include a subject clause allowing for such an inspection, particularly for buildings built in the 1980s and 1990s or with known envelope issues. A qualified building envelope professional can assess visible condition, review engineering reports, and identify risks. Verify inspection scope, cost, and timing with your BC real estate licensee, lawyer, or notary when drafting the Contract of Purchase and Sale.

Verify with: BC Housing — Building Science Library
Can a buyer negotiate a price reduction or repairs if building envelope defects are discovered?

Whether a buyer can negotiate depends on the terms of the Contract of Purchase and Sale, whether the defect was disclosed, and whether a subject condition (inspection clause) was included. Undisclosed material latent defects may give rise to remedies under common law or the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42. If an inspection subject is in place, the buyer may negotiate price, repairs, or cancel the contract within the subject removal period. Verify your contractual rights and remedies with a BC lawyer or notary before taking action.

Verify with: BC Housing — Building Science Library
Does building envelope remediation work require a building permit in BC?

Yes. Building envelope remediation typically involves structural alterations, re-cladding, window replacement, or waterproofing that require a building permit under the BC Building Code and municipal bylaws adopted under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Strata corporations undertaking envelope work must engage qualified professionals, obtain permits, and comply with applicable codes and standards. Verify permit status, completion certificates, and warranty documentation for any past or planned remediation work with a BC lawyer, notary, or building professional before purchasing or authorizing repairs.
