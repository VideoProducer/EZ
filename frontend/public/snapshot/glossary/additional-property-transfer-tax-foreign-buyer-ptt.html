Additional Property Transfer Tax (Foreign Buyer PTT)

An additional Property Transfer Tax (PTT) applies to certain transfers of residential property in British Columbia to foreign nationals, foreign corporations, and taxable trustees, as defined under the Property Transfer Tax Act (PTTA), RSBC 1996, c. 378. The rate is 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) of the property's fair market value and is imposed on top of the standard PTT otherwise payable. The tax applies within designated taxable regions, which have included Metro Vancouver, the Capital Regional District, the Fraser Valley Regional District, the Regional District of Central Okanagan, and the Regional District of Nanaimo; verify the current list of designated areas with BC Ministry of Finance at gov.bc.ca, as regional designations may change. Verify all rates, defined terms, and applicable regions with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before completing any transaction.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Additional Property Transfer Tax (Foreign Buyer PTT) in British Columbia?

The Additional Property Transfer Tax is a 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) tax imposed under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, on residential property transfers to foreign nationals, foreign corporations, or taxable trustees in specified regions of BC. This tax is separate from and in addition to the standard Property Transfer Tax. It applies in designated areas including Metro Vancouver, the Capital Regional District, the Fraser Valley Regional District, the Regional District of Central Okanagan, and the Regional District of Nanaimo (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify current designated areas and rates with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Who is subject to the Additional Property Transfer Tax in BC?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, the Additional PTT applies to foreign nationals (individuals who are not Canadian citizens or permanent residents), foreign corporations (corporations not incorporated in Canada or controlled by foreign nationals or foreign corporations), and taxable trustees (trustees of which a foreign national or foreign corporation is a beneficiary). The determination of who qualifies is fact-specific and depends on citizenship, residency status, and corporate control at the time of registration. Verify your specific status and eligibility with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Government of BC
Which areas of British Columbia does the Additional Property Transfer Tax apply to?

The Additional PTT applies in designated areas specified under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, including Metro Vancouver Regional District, Capital Regional District (Greater Victoria), Fraser Valley Regional District, Regional District of Central Okanagan (Kelowna area), and Regional District of Nanaimo (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The specific boundaries and any changes to designated areas are determined by regulation. Verify the current list of designated areas and whether a specific property falls within them with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Government of BC
What types of property are subject to the Additional Property Transfer Tax?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, the Additional PTT applies to residential property, which includes detached homes, townhouses, condominiums, and other forms of residential real property in the designated areas. Commercial property, agricultural land, and recreational property are generally not subject to the Additional PTT. Verify whether a specific property qualifies as residential and is subject to the Additional PTT with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Are there any exemptions from the Additional Property Transfer Tax?

The Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, provides certain exemptions from the Additional PTT, including for individuals who become permanent residents of Canada within a specified time after registration, certain nominees or trustees in limited circumstances, and transfers between spouses in some cases (verify current exemption criteria as of 2026-07-27). Each exemption has specific eligibility requirements and documentation obligations. Verify your eligibility for any exemption with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

How is the Additional Property Transfer Tax calculated?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, the Additional PTT is calculated as 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) of the fair market value of the residential property at the time of registration. This 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) is in addition to the standard PTT rates that apply to all property transfers in BC. Verify the current rate and calculation method with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Government of BC
When was the Additional Property Transfer Tax introduced in BC?

The Additional Property Transfer Tax (Foreign Buyer PTT) was introduced through amendments to the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, and came into effect on August 2, 2016 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) at an initial rate of 15% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) in Metro Vancouver. The tax was later expanded to additional regional districts and the rate was increased to 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) on February 21, 2018 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the complete legislative history and current status with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Can a foreign buyer obtain a refund of the Additional Property Transfer Tax if they become a permanent resident?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, a refund of the Additional PTT may be available if a foreign national becomes a permanent resident of Canada within a specified time period after the property transfer is registered and meets other eligibility requirements (verify current refund period and conditions as of 2026-07-27). The refund application must be made within the prescribed timelines and supported by required documentation. Verify current refund eligibility, timelines, and application procedures with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Government of BC
Does the Additional Property Transfer Tax apply if a foreign buyer purchases only a partial interest in residential property?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, the Additional PTT applies proportionally to the interest acquired by a foreign national, foreign corporation, or taxable trustee in residential property located in a designated area. If a foreign buyer acquires a 50% interest, the 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) Additional PTT applies to the fair market value of that 50% interest. Verify how the tax applies to your specific ownership structure with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Government of BC
How does the Additional Property Transfer Tax interact with the federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act?

The federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10, generally prohibits non-Canadians from purchasing residential property in Canada (currently extended through January 1, 2027 as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), while the BC Additional PTT under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, imposes a 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) tax on qualifying purchases in designated BC areas. Both laws may apply, and exemptions under one law do not automatically provide exemptions under the other. Verify the interaction of federal and provincial rules and any available exemptions with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.
