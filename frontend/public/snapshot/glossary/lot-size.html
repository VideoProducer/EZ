Lot Size

Lot size is the total land area of a parcel, typically expressed in square feet, square metres, hectares, or acres. Under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, municipal and regional bylaws set minimum lot-area standards that govern subdivision eligibility, permitted uses such as secondary suites, and density entitlements including Floor Space Ratio. The registered survey plan filed at the Land Title Office under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, is the authoritative record of lot dimensions; BC Assessment data and a current Survey Certificate may also be referenced, but listing-stated dimensions are not authoritative. Parcels within the Agricultural Land Reserve are subject to additional area restrictions under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36. Lot size directly influences permitted building envelope, setbacks, and resale value. Verify specific area thresholds, bylaw minimums, and any applicable provincial amendments with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed professional, as local zoning requirements vary and change.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is lot size in British Columbia real estate?

Lot size is the total land area of a property, expressed in square feet, square metres, hectares, or acres. In BC, lot size affects zoning entitlements such as minimum lot area for subdivision, secondary suites, and laneway houses, as well as Floor Space Ratio (FSR) limits on permitted building size and setbacks from property lines. Authoritative lot-area sources include BC Assessment, the registered survey plan filed at the Land Title Office under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, and a current Real Property Report (Survey Certificate). Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed surveyor before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Where do I find the official lot size for my BC property?

The authoritative sources are the registered survey plan filed at the Land Title Office (governed by the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250), BC Assessment records, and a current Real Property Report (Survey Certificate) prepared by a BC Land Surveyor. Listing-information lot dimensions provided by real estate licensees are not authoritative and should not be relied upon for legal or zoning purposes. Verify the registered lot area with the Land Title Office or a BC Land Surveyor before making decisions based on lot size.

Verify with: BC Assessment
How does lot size affect what I can build on my property in BC?

Lot size determines zoning entitlements under municipal bylaws enacted under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, including minimum lot area for subdivision, eligibility for secondary suites or laneway houses, and the maximum Floor Space Ratio (FSR) cap on building size. Setback requirements from property lines are also tied to lot dimensions. Verify your municipality's zoning bylaws and any applicable provincial legislation, such as the Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023 (BC Bill 44, effective July 1, 2024, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), with a BC lawyer or municipal planning department before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Can I subdivide my BC lot, and does lot size matter?

Subdivision eligibility is governed by municipal zoning bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, and requires that the lot meet minimum lot-area requirements for the zone. If the property is in the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR), the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, imposes additional restrictions. Verify the minimum lot size for subdivision in your municipality's zoning bylaw and any ALR restrictions with a BC lawyer, notary, or municipal planner before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Does BC Assessment's lot size always match the Land Title Office record?

BC Assessment records are generally based on the registered survey plan at the Land Title Office (Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250), but discrepancies can occur due to survey errors, boundary adjustments, or data-entry issues. The registered survey plan at the Land Title Office is the definitive legal record of lot area. Verify any discrepancy with a BC Land Surveyor or by ordering a certified copy of the registered plan from the Land Title Office before relying on lot-size figures for legal or financial decisions.

Verify with: BC Assessment
What is Floor Space Ratio (FSR) and how does lot size affect it in BC?

Floor Space Ratio (FSR) is a zoning regulation under municipal bylaws (Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1) that limits the total floor area of a building as a multiple of the lot size. For example, a lot of 5,000 square feet with an FSR of 0.6 permits a maximum building floor area of 3,000 square feet (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current FSR rules for your zone). Verify the applicable FSR and how it is calculated in your municipality's zoning bylaw with a BC lawyer, notary, or municipal planning department before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Can I rely on the lot size shown in a real estate listing in BC?

No. Lot dimensions and areas in real estate listings are not authoritative and may be approximate or incorrect. Real estate licensees in BC are governed by the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and BCFSA Rules, but listing data is typically sourced from non-legal records. The only authoritative sources are the registered survey plan at the Land Title Office (Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250), BC Assessment, or a current Real Property Report prepared by a BC Land Surveyor. Verify lot size with one of these authoritative sources before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Does lot size affect property taxes in BC?

Lot size can indirectly affect property taxes because BC Assessment uses lot size (along with location, improvements, and market conditions) to determine assessed value, which forms the basis for municipal property taxes. Larger lots in desirable areas typically have higher assessed values. The Home Owner Grant Act provides a property-tax reduction for eligible owner-occupiers, but verify current thresholds and eligibility with BC Ministry of Finance or a licensed tax professional (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current).

Verify with: BC Assessment
What is a Real Property Report (Survey Certificate) and why does it matter for lot size in BC?

A Real Property Report (also called a Survey Certificate) is a document prepared by a BC Land Surveyor showing the lot boundaries, dimensions, area, and the location of buildings and encroachments. It is an authoritative source of lot size and is often required by lenders or purchasers to confirm legal boundaries under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Verify whether a current Real Property Report exists or order a new survey from a BC Land Surveyor before completing a real estate transaction or development.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Does lot size affect eligibility for secondary suites or laneway houses in BC?

Yes. Minimum lot size is a common zoning requirement under municipal bylaws (Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1) for secondary suites and laneway houses. The Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023 (BC Bill 44, effective July 1, 2024, as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) requires many BC municipalities to allow secondary suites and accessory dwelling units on eligible lots, but municipalities may still impose minimum lot-size thresholds. Verify your lot's eligibility and the applicable minimum lot size with your municipality's zoning bylaw and a BC lawyer or municipal planner before acting.
