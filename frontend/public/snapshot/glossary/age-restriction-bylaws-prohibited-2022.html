Strata

Age Restriction Bylaws (Prohibited 2022)

Age restriction bylaws in strata corporations are governed by the Strata Property Act (SPA), SBC 1998, c. 43, as amended by the Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023 (BC Bill 44). Following amendments that took effect in 2022 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), strata bylaws restricting residency based on age are generally prohibited, with a narrow statutory exception permitting bylaws that restrict residency to persons aged 55 or older (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Bylaws that conflict with these provisions are unenforceable. Verify the current scope of any age-based bylaw with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before relying on it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are age-restricted strata bylaws still allowed in British Columbia?

Following amendments to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, effective November 24, 2022 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), most age-restriction bylaws are prohibited in BC. The only exception is bylaws that restrict residency to persons 55 years of age or older (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Any bylaw inconsistent with these amendments is unenforceable; verify the current Strata Property Act provisions and any exemptions with a BC lawyer or notary before relying on or enforcing an age-based bylaw.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
Can a strata corporation in BC enforce a bylaw that restricts the building to adults 19+ or 'no children under 12'?

No. Under the November 2022 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) amendments to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, bylaws restricting residency by age other than 55+ are prohibited and unenforceable. A bylaw excluding children, minors, or adults under any age other than 55 years is not valid. Verify enforcement options and current strata law with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
What is the specific age threshold that BC strata bylaws are allowed to use for age restrictions?

The Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, permits bylaws that restrict residency to persons 55 years of age or older (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). No other age-based restriction is lawful under the November 2022 amendments. Verify the precise wording, exceptions, and current statutory requirements with a BC lawyer or notary before drafting or enforcing such a bylaw.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
When did the prohibition on most age-restriction strata bylaws take effect in BC?

The amendments to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, prohibiting age-restriction bylaws (except 55+) took effect on November 24, 2022 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Bylaws inconsistent with these amendments became unenforceable on that date. Verify the exact effective date and any transitional rules with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
What happens to an existing '45 and over' or '19 and over' bylaw registered before November 2022?

Under the November 2022 amendments to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, bylaws inconsistent with the new prohibition are unenforceable, regardless of when they were registered. A '45 and over' or '19 and over' bylaw does not fall within the 55+ exception and cannot be enforced. Verify current bylaw validity and repeal procedures with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
Can a strata enforce an existing '55+' age-restriction bylaw that was in place before the 2022 amendments?

Yes, generally. The November 2022 amendments to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, permit bylaws restricting residency to persons 55 years of age or older (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). A properly registered 55+ bylaw predating the amendments remains enforceable, provided it complies with all current statutory requirements. Verify bylaw wording, registration, and any human rights or other compliance issues with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
Does the prohibition on age-restriction bylaws apply to rental buildings or only to strata corporations?

The November 2022 prohibition applies to bylaws under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, which governs strata corporations (condominiums, townhouses, etc.). The Residential Tenancy Act, SBC 2002, c. 78, governs landlord-tenant relations in rental housing and has separate rules; age-based restrictions in rental housing may be subject to the BC Human Rights Code and other legislation. Verify the application of the Strata Property Act versus the Residential Tenancy Act, and any human rights implications, with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
Can a strata corporation create a new '55 and over' bylaw after the 2022 amendments?

Yes, a strata corporation may pass a bylaw restricting residency to persons 55 years of age or older (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), as this falls within the exception under the November 2022 amendments to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43. The bylaw must comply with all Strata Property Act bylaw amendment procedures, including the required vote threshold. Verify drafting, voting, filing, and human rights compliance with a BC lawyer or notary before proceeding.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
Are there any grandfather provisions or exemptions for strata corporations that had age-restriction bylaws before 2022?

The November 2022 amendments to the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, do not create a general grandfather clause; bylaws inconsistent with the prohibition (other than 55+) became unenforceable on November 24, 2022 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Only bylaws restricting residency to 55+ remain valid. Verify whether any transitional rules, exemptions, or specific fact patterns apply to your strata with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Strata Property Amendment Act 2022
Where can I confirm the current rules on age-restriction bylaws in BC strata properties?

Review the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the Strata Property Regulation on www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca, and consult the BC Ministry of Housing or the provincial government's strata housing resources at www.gov.bc.ca. For legal interpretation, bylaw drafting, enforcement, or human rights compliance, verify current rules with a BC lawyer or notary. Do not rely on pre-2022 materials without confirming they reflect the November 2022 amendments.
