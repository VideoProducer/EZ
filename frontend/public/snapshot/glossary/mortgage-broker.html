Mortgage Broker

A mortgage broker is a licensed professional who shops a mortgage application across multiple lenders (banks, credit unions, monoline lenders, private lenders) to find competitive rates and terms. In BC, mortgage brokers are licensed and regulated by BCFSA under the Mortgage Brokers Act and the Mortgage Services Act (in force 2026). Brokers are typically paid by the lender on prime deals; on private and certain non-prime deals the borrower may pay a fee, which is subject to written disclosure to the borrower. Mortgage brokers commonly serve self-employed borrowers, applicants with complex income, second-property and investment-property buyers, and applicants whose primary bank has declined. Licence status can be verified through BCFSA's public registrant search.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who regulates mortgage brokers in British Columbia?

Mortgage brokers in British Columbia are licensed and regulated by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA). As of 2026, the governing legislation has transitioned from the Mortgage Brokers Act to the Mortgage Services Act, which sets out licensing requirements, conduct standards, and borrower protections. Licence status for any mortgage broker can be verified through BCFSA's publicly accessible registrant search.

Verify with: BCFSA — Mortgage Brokers
How is a mortgage broker in BC different from a bank's mortgage specialist?

A BC mortgage broker is an independent licensed professional who submits a borrower's application to multiple lenders — including banks, credit unions, monoline lenders, and private lenders — to compare rates and terms on the borrower's behalf. A bank's mortgage specialist is an employee of that single institution and can only offer that institution's own products. Because a mortgage broker accesses a broader lending market, they may be particularly valuable to borrowers who have been declined by their primary bank or who have complex income situations.

Verify with: BCFSA — Mortgage Brokers
Does a mortgage broker in BC charge borrowers a fee?

On prime residential deals, BC mortgage brokers are typically compensated by the lender through a finder's fee or commission, meaning the borrower pays no direct fee. However, on private-lender deals and certain non-prime transactions, a broker may charge the borrower a fee; under BCFSA requirements and the Mortgage Services Act, this fee must be disclosed to the borrower in writing before it is charged. Borrowers should always ask their broker upfront whether any borrower-paid fees apply to their specific transaction.

Verify with: BCFSA — Mortgage Brokers
How can I verify that a mortgage broker in BC is properly licensed?

You can confirm a mortgage broker's licence status by searching BCFSA's online public registrant directory, which is maintained under the Mortgage Services Act and lists all currently licensed mortgage brokers and sub-mortgage brokers in British Columbia. A licensed individual will appear with an active status; anyone offering mortgage brokering services without a valid licence is in contravention of BC law. It is advisable to perform this check before engaging any mortgage broker.

Verify with: BCFSA — Mortgage Brokers
Can a mortgage broker in BC also act as a real estate licensee on the same transaction?

Holding both a mortgage broker licence under the Mortgage Services Act and a real estate licence under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) is legally possible in BC, but it creates a dual-role situation that triggers significant disclosure obligations. Under BCFSA's rules governing RESA licensees, any conflict of interest or dual role must be fully disclosed in writing to all parties. Borrowers and buyers in such situations should carefully review the written disclosure and understand how each role is being exercised.

Verify with: BCFSA — Mortgage Brokers
What types of borrowers most commonly use mortgage brokers in BC?

Mortgage brokers in BC frequently assist self-employed individuals, borrowers with non-traditional or complex income structures, applicants purchasing second properties or investment properties, and those who have been declined by their primary bank. Because brokers can access a wide range of lenders — including private lenders for borrowers who do not qualify under standard federal mortgage stress-test rules — they can often structure solutions that a single institution cannot provide. First-time buyers seeking competitive rate comparisons also commonly use mortgage brokers.

How does BC's mortgage stress test affect what a mortgage broker can arrange?

The federal mortgage stress test, administered under Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) rules for federally regulated lenders and under CMHC guidelines for insured mortgages, requires borrowers to qualify at a rate higher than their contracted rate; this applies regardless of whether the mortgage is arranged through a broker or directly with a bank. A BC mortgage broker can access provincially regulated credit unions and some private lenders that may apply different qualifying criteria, which can be relevant for borrowers who do not pass the stress test with federally regulated lenders. Borrowers should consult current OSFI and BCFSA guidance for exact qualifying rate thresholds.

Verify with: BCFSA — Mortgage Brokers
What disclosure obligations does a mortgage broker in BC owe to a borrower?

Under the Mortgage Services Act and BCFSA's regulatory requirements, a BC mortgage broker must provide borrowers with clear written disclosure of material information, including the broker's compensation (especially any borrower-paid fees), any conflicts of interest, and the key terms of the mortgage product being arranged. These disclosure obligations are designed to ensure borrowers can make informed decisions before committing to a mortgage. Failure to meet disclosure requirements can result in disciplinary action by BCFSA.

Verify with: BCFSA — Mortgage Brokers
Does BC's privacy law apply to how a mortgage broker handles my personal financial information?

Yes. When a BC mortgage broker collects, uses, or discloses a borrower's personal and financial information — such as income documents, credit reports, and identification — they must comply with British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA). PIPA requires that personal information be collected only for identified purposes, with the individual's knowledge and consent, and that it be protected by reasonable security safeguards. Borrowers have the right under PIPA to request access to their personal information held by the broker and to request corrections.

Verify with: BCFSA — Mortgage Brokers
If a mortgage broker in BC behaves improperly, what recourse does a borrower have?

A borrower who believes a BC mortgage broker has acted improperly or in breach of their statutory obligations can file a complaint directly with BCFSA, which has the authority to investigate, impose conditions, suspend, or cancel a broker's licence under the Mortgage Services Act. BCFSA can also levy administrative penalties where contraventions are established. For disputes involving financial losses, a borrower may also have civil remedies available through the BC courts, and should seek independent legal advice regarding those options.
