Title & Ownership

Fee Simple

The most complete form of ownership of land recognized in BC, conferring perpetual rights to use, possess, and transfer the land, subject to applicable laws, registered encumbrances, and the rights of the Crown. Most non-strata residential property in BC is held in fee simple.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 'fee simple' mean in British Columbia?

Fee simple is the most complete form of land ownership recognized in BC, granting the owner perpetual rights to use, possess, mortgage, lease, and transfer the land. The ownership continues indefinitely and passes to heirs or designated beneficiaries upon death. It is subject to applicable laws, registered encumbrances on the title, and the underlying rights of the Crown, including taxation and expropriation powers.

How does fee simple ownership differ from a strata lot in BC?

A fee simple owner holds exclusive title to the land and any improvements on it, whereas a strata lot owner under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) holds title to a defined unit within a strata plan and shares an interest in common property with other strata lot owners. Fee simple owners are not subject to strata corporation bylaws or rules, strata fees, or the Form B / Form F disclosure requirements mandated by the Strata Property Act. Most non-strata residential properties in BC — detached houses, vacant lots, and bare-land parcels — are held in fee simple.

How is fee simple ownership recorded and protected in British Columbia?

Fee simple ownership in BC is recorded in the Land Title Register maintained under the Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250), administered by the BC Land Title and Survey Authority (LTSA). Registration of a fee simple transfer by way of a Form A Freehold Transfer provides indefeasibility of title, meaning the registered owner's interest is protected against most unregistered claims. Prospective buyers are encouraged to obtain a current title search through the LTSA to identify all registered charges, liens, and encumbrances affecting the property.

What Property Transfer Tax applies when purchasing a fee simple property in BC?

Under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act, PTT is calculated at 1% on the first $200,000 of the fair market value, 2% on the portion between $200,000 and $2,000,000, 3% on the portion between $2,000,000 and $3,000,000, and an additional 2% on the residential portion of the fair market value exceeding $3,000,000. Eligible first-time home buyers may qualify for a full exemption on properties up to $835,000, with a partial exemption up to a higher threshold; consult current BC Ministry of Finance guidance for the exact partial-exemption ceiling. A Newly Built Home Exemption is also available for qualifying properties up to $1,100,000.

Can a fee simple property in BC be located within the Agricultural Land Reserve?

Yes, fee simple ownership and ALR designation are separate legal matters; a parcel can be privately owned in fee simple while simultaneously being within the Agricultural Land Reserve established under the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36). Being in the ALR significantly restricts subdivision, non-farm use, and non-adhering residential uses, and owners must obtain approval from the Agricultural Land Commission before undertaking many activities. Buyers considering ALR fee simple property should consult the ALC directly to understand current use restrictions applicable to the specific parcel.

What happens to fee simple property when the owner dies in British Columbia?

Upon the death of a fee simple owner, the property forms part of their estate and is distributed according to the owner's will or, if there is no valid will, in accordance with the intestacy rules set out in the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA, SBC 2009, c. 13). The executor or administrator must typically obtain a Grant of Probate or Administration from the BC Supreme Court before the LTSA will register a transfer of the fee simple title to a beneficiary or purchaser. If the property was held in joint tenancy with a right of survivorship, the surviving owner's interest passes outside the estate without probate upon filing the appropriate documents at the LTSA.

What obligations does a real estate licensee have when representing a buyer or seller of fee simple property in BC?

Real estate licensees in BC are governed by the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and its Rules, overseen by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA). Licensees owe statutory duties including disclosure of known material latent defects, agency disclosure obligations, and the handling of deposit funds in a designated trust account in accordance with RESA Rules. Licensees must also ensure that any remuneration arrangements comply with RESA and that clients receive the required disclosure documents before entering into a representation agreement.

Can fee simple land in BC be subdivided freely?

Subdivision of fee simple land in BC is regulated and is not automatic; it requires approval under the Land Title Act and compliance with applicable municipal or regional district zoning bylaws, official community plans, and the Subdivision and Development Servicing Bylaw. If the parcel is within the Agricultural Land Reserve, additional approval from the Agricultural Land Commission is required under the Agricultural Land Commission Act (SBC 2002, c. 36), which imposes strict minimum lot size and use restrictions. Owners should consult their local authority and, where applicable, the ALC before pursuing any subdivision of fee simple property.

What is a mortgage over fee simple property, and what happens if the owner defaults in BC?

A mortgage over fee simple property is a registered charge on the Land Title Register that grants the lender a security interest in the land under the Land Title Act and the Mortgage Brokers Act. In BC, lenders do not use a power-of-sale process; instead, enforcement of a mortgage on default is carried out through a judicial foreclosure proceeding governed by the BC Supreme Court Civil Rules and the Law and Equity Act (RSBC 1996, c. 224), which gives the court discretion to order a redemption period, a sale, or an order absolute foreclosing the owner's interest. Homeowners facing default should seek independent legal advice promptly.

Does the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax apply to fee simple residential properties?

The BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax, established under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, applies to residential properties — including fee simple residential parcels — located in designated taxable regions of BC, and is levied annually on owners who do not qualify for an exemption. Exemptions are available for principal residences, certain rental arrangements, and other qualifying circumstances; consult the current BC Ministry of Finance guidance for eligible exemptions and applicable tax rates, as these can vary by owner type and residency status. Owners of fee simple residential property in a designated area must file an annual declaration even if they believe they qualify for an exemption.
