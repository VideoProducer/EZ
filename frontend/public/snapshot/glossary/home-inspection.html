Home Inspection

A home inspection is a visual examination of a property's major systems and components — typically including the foundation, roof, plumbing, electrical, heating, and insulation — conducted by a licensed home inspector. In BC, home inspectors are licensed under the Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act and its associated home inspector licensing regulation; verify current licensing requirements with Consumer Protection BC or a BC lawyer. Inspection costs and duration vary; any figures cited elsewhere should be verified current with local professionals (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Buyers commonly use a subject-to-inspection condition within their offer to purchase, allowing time to review the inspector's written report before removing subjects. The report helps buyers understand the property's condition and may inform further negotiation on price or repairs. Confirm current regulatory requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are home inspectors in British Columbia required to be licensed?

Yes. Under the Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act and the Home Inspector Licensing Regulation, BC Reg 12/2009, home inspectors in British Columbia must be licensed by Consumer Protection BC. This regulatory framework ensures minimum competency and ethical standards for anyone offering home inspection services in BC. Verify current licensing requirements and inspector credentials directly with Consumer Protection BC before engaging a home inspector.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
What is typically included in a BC home inspection?

A home inspection is a visual examination of the home's major systems and components, including foundation, roof, plumbing, electrical, heating, cooling, and insulation. The inspection does not typically include invasive or destructive testing, nor does it guarantee the condition of concealed elements. Verify the exact scope of the inspection with your licensed inspector, as services may vary.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
How much does a home inspection typically cost in British Columbia?

A home inspection in BC typically costs $400 to $600 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) and takes approximately 2 to 3 hours (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), depending on the size, age, and complexity of the property. Prices may vary by region, property type, and additional services requested. Verify current pricing and the inspector's credentials with Consumer Protection BC before proceeding.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
Is a home inspection legally required when buying a home in BC?

No. There is no statute in British Columbia that mandates a home inspection as a condition of purchasing real estate. However, it is a widely recommended practice to protect the buyer's interests and is commonly incorporated into purchase agreements through a subject-to-inspection clause. Verify whether an inspection is advisable for your specific transaction with a BC lawyer, notary, or your licensed real estate professional.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
What is a 'subject-to-inspection' clause in a BC real estate contract?

A subject-to-inspection clause is a condition in the Contract of Purchase and Sale that allows the buyer to hire a licensed home inspector and review the report before the contract becomes unconditional (firm). If the inspection reveals significant defects, the buyer may negotiate repairs, a price reduction, or remove the subject and walk away without penalty, depending on the wording of the clause. Verify the exact wording and legal effect of any subject clause with a BC lawyer or notary before signing.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
Can I rely on the home inspection report for legal claims if defects are found later?

A home inspection report is a snapshot of visible conditions at the time of the inspection and does not guarantee the absence of defects or future performance. Licensed home inspectors carry errors and omissions insurance, but liability is typically limited by the inspection contract. For legal advice on your rights under BC contract law, the Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act, or common law negligence, verify your situation with a BC lawyer before acting.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
Who pays for the home inspection in a BC real estate transaction?

By convention in British Columbia, the buyer typically pays for the home inspection, as it is conducted for the buyer's benefit and due diligence. The Contract of Purchase and Sale and any subject clauses do not generally allocate this cost to the seller. Verify the allocation of inspection costs in your specific agreement with a BC lawyer, notary, or your licensed real estate professional.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
How do I verify that a home inspector is licensed in BC?

You can verify a home inspector's licence status by contacting Consumer Protection BC directly or checking their online registry at www.consumerprotectionbc.ca. Under the Home Inspector Licensing Regulation, BC Reg 12/2009, only licensed individuals may offer home inspection services in BC. Verify the inspector's current licence and any disciplinary history before engaging their services.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
Can a seller refuse to allow a home inspection in British Columbia?

Yes. A seller is not legally required to permit a home inspection unless the Contract of Purchase and Sale includes a subject-to-inspection clause that the seller has accepted. If the seller refuses access, the buyer may be unable to satisfy the subject condition and the contract may not become firm. Verify your contractual rights and remedies with a BC lawyer or notary if access is denied.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
What happens if the home inspection reveals major defects?

If the Contract of Purchase and Sale includes a subject-to-inspection clause, the buyer may negotiate with the seller for repairs, a price reduction, or other remedies, or may choose to remove the subject and terminate the contract without penalty, depending on the clause wording. If the contract is already unconditional (firm), the buyer's remedies are limited to those available under general contract law or the common law doctrine of misrepresentation. Verify your legal options with a BC lawyer or notary based on the specific facts and contract language.

Verify with: Consumer Protection BC
