Property Tax Deferment Program

A BC government program, administered under the authority of the BC Ministry of Finance (gov.bc.ca), that allows eligible homeowners to defer payment of annual property taxes on a principal residence. Eligible groups generally include homeowners aged 55 or older, surviving spouses or relatives, and families with dependent children; however, eligibility criteria, equity requirements, and application conditions are set by the Province and may change. Deferred taxes accrue interest and become repayable when the property is sold or transferred. Verify all current eligibility thresholds, interest rates, and program details directly with the BC Ministry of Finance at gov.bc.ca or with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before applying.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Property Tax Deferment Program in British Columbia?

The Property Tax Deferment Program is a BC government initiative that allows eligible homeowners to defer payment of annual property taxes on their principal residence, with deferred amounts accruing simple interest and becoming payable when the property is sold or transferred. The program is administered by the BC Ministry of Finance under the Home Owner Grant Act. Eligibility criteria, equity requirements, and interest rates are set by the Province and subject to change. Verify current program rules and application procedures at www.gov.bc.ca or with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before applying.

Who is eligible to apply for property tax deferment in BC?

General eligibility categories under the BC Property Tax Deferment Program include homeowners aged 55 or older, surviving spouses or partners of any age, and families with children under 18 residing in the home (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Applicants must hold registered title to the property, occupy it as their principal residence, and meet equity and financial criteria set by the Province. Verify your specific eligibility, including citizenship or residency requirements and any changes to age or family status thresholds, with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC notary or lawyer before applying.

Verify with: Property Tax Deferment
What types of property taxes can be deferred under this program?

The program allows deferment of regular municipal and regional district property taxes, along with certain parcel taxes and specified area charges levied on the principal residence. It generally does not cover utilities, service charges, or fees unrelated to the annual property tax levy. Verify the specific categories of deferrable charges for your municipality and the current year by reviewing your property tax notice and contacting the BC Ministry of Finance or your local tax collector.

Verify with: Property Tax Deferment
How much equity must I have in my home to qualify for the deferment program?

The Province sets minimum equity requirements to ensure sufficient security for the deferred tax loan, typically expressed as a loan-to-value ratio or minimum home equity percentage (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The exact threshold and calculation method may vary by program stream (age 55+, surviving spouse, or families with children) and are subject to periodic revision. Verify the current equity requirement and whether your property's assessed value and existing mortgages or charges meet the threshold by contacting the BC Ministry of Finance or consulting a BC notary or lawyer before applying.

Verify with: Property Tax Deferment
What interest rate is charged on deferred property taxes, and how does it accrue?

Deferred property taxes under the BC program accrue simple interest at a rate set annually by the Province; the rate is published by the BC Ministry of Finance each year and is not compounded (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current rate). Interest begins to accrue from the date the taxes are deferred and continues until full repayment occurs. Verify the current simple interest rate and the date from which interest is calculated for your deferment year at www.gov.bc.ca or with a BC notary or licensed tax professional before applying.

Verify with: Property Tax Deferment
When and how must deferred property taxes be repaid?

Deferred property taxes, plus accrued simple interest, become due and payable when the property is sold, transferred, or when the homeowner no longer meets eligibility criteria (for example, ceases to occupy the home as principal residence or passes away, triggering repayment from the estate). The Province registers a lien against the title to secure repayment, and the deferred amount is typically paid from sale proceeds or estate assets. Verify repayment triggers, lien registration procedures, and options for voluntary early repayment with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC lawyer or notary before entering the program.

Verify with: Property Tax Deferment
Can I defer property taxes if I have an existing mortgage or home equity line of credit?

Yes, you may defer property taxes even with an existing mortgage or home equity line of credit, provided you meet the Province's minimum equity requirement after accounting for all registered charges against the property (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The deferral creates an additional charge (lien) registered on title in favour of the Province, ranking behind earlier mortgages. Verify that your combined loan-to-value ratio remains within the program's threshold and that your lender does not object to the deferral lien by consulting the BC Ministry of Finance and your mortgage lender or a BC notary before applying.

Verify with: Property Tax Deferment
How do I apply for the Property Tax Deferment Program?

Applications are submitted to the BC Ministry of Finance, typically before the annual property tax due date; the Ministry provides application forms, instructions, and deadline information at www.gov.bc.ca (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current application process). You will need to provide proof of age, family status (if applicable), title ownership, occupancy as principal residence, and financial or equity documentation as required. Verify the current application deadline, required documents, and whether you must reapply annually or only once by contacting the BC Ministry of Finance or consulting a BC notary or lawyer before your tax due date.

Verify with: Property Tax Deferment
Does participating in the deferment program affect my Home Owner Grant?

No, participation in the Property Tax Deferment Program does not disqualify you from claiming the BC Home Owner Grant, which is a separate property tax reduction administered under the Home Owner Grant Act. You may claim the grant to reduce your annual tax liability and then defer the remaining net amount owing. Verify your eligibility for both programs and the order in which credits and deferrals are applied by reviewing your property tax notice and contacting the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC notary before your tax payment deadline.

Verify with: Property Tax Deferment
What happens to the deferred tax debt if I die while enrolled in the program?

Upon the death of a program participant, the deferred property taxes and accrued simple interest become a debt of the estate, secured by the Province's registered lien on the property title. The executor or administrator must repay the deferred amount, typically from the proceeds of selling the home or from other estate assets, before distributing the estate to beneficiaries under the Wills, Estates and Succession Act, SBC 2009, c. 13. Verify the priority of the deferral lien relative to other estate debts and the process for discharge of the lien with the BC Ministry of Finance or a BC lawyer experienced in estate administration before finalizing your estate plan.
