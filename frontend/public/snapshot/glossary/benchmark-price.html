Benchmark Price

A benchmark price is a composite value published by BC real estate boards and the Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA) through the MLS® Home Price Index, designed to represent the price of a "typical" home of a specified type and area. Unlike an average or median, it is derived from statistical modelling that controls for unusual or atypical sales, aiming to reflect genuine market conditions. Because it tracks the same notional property over time, it is used as an indicator of price trends rather than a measure of individual transaction prices. Benchmark prices are not defined or regulated under BC statute; verify how this metric applies to your circumstances with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Benchmark Price in BC real estate?

A Benchmark Price is a composite price reported by BC real estate boards and by CREA's MLS® Home Price Index, intended to represent the price of a 'typical' home of a given type in a given area, controlling for unusual sales. It is calculated using statistical models rather than averaging individual transaction prices, and reflects market trends over time. The specific methodology is maintained by the boards and CREA; verify current calculation details with the relevant real estate board or MLS® Home Price Index documentation before relying on any particular Benchmark Price figure.

Verify with: Greater Vancouver REALTORS®
Is Benchmark Price defined or regulated by BC statute?

No. Benchmark Price is not defined in the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, or any other BC statute on the whitelist. It is a market-analysis tool developed and published by regional real estate boards and CREA's MLS® Home Price Index system. Verify with a BC lawyer or the issuing real estate board if you need to rely on a Benchmark Price for legal, tax, or regulatory purposes.

Verify with: Greater Vancouver REALTORS®
Can I use Benchmark Price to calculate Property Transfer Tax in BC?

No. Property Transfer Tax (PTT) under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, is calculated on the fair market value or consideration paid for the property, not on a Benchmark Price. Fair market value is determined at the time of transfer; Benchmark Price is a statistical composite that may not reflect the actual value of a specific property. Verify PTT obligations and valuation requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before filing.

Verify with: Greater Vancouver REALTORS®
Does the BC First-Time Home Buyer Exemption use Benchmark Price as a threshold?

No. The First-Time Home Buyer Exemption under the Property Transfer Tax Act uses specific dollar thresholds for the fair market value of the property, not Benchmark Price (full exemption applies up to $835,000 and partial exemption up to $860,000 as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Fair market value is the actual transacted or assessed value of the home you are purchasing. Verify current thresholds and eligibility with a BC lawyer, notary, or BC Ministry of Finance before applying.

Verify with: Greater Vancouver REALTORS®
How often is Benchmark Price updated in BC?

Benchmark Price is typically updated monthly by participating BC real estate boards and by CREA's MLS® Home Price Index, based on recent sales data and statistical models. The exact update schedule and methodology vary by board and market area. Verify the current reporting frequency and methodology with the relevant BC real estate board or at www.housepriceindex.ca before relying on any specific Benchmark Price figure.

Verify with: Greater Vancouver REALTORS®
Can BCFSA-licensed real estate professionals cite Benchmark Price in their marketing?

Yes, BCFSA-licensed real estate professionals may cite Benchmark Price in marketing, provided they do so accurately and do not mislead clients or the public, consistent with their duties under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules. They must attribute the source, not represent it as the value of a specific property, and ensure any market commentary is factual and verifiable. Verify compliance obligations with a BC lawyer or by consulting the BCFSA Rules and professional conduct guidelines.

Verify with: Greater Vancouver REALTORS®
Does Benchmark Price apply to the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

No. The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, calculates tax based on the owner's tax status, declaration, and the property's assessed value (as determined by BC Assessment), not on Benchmark Price. Benchmark Price is a market indicator and is not used to determine Speculation and Vacancy Tax liability. Verify your SVT obligations with a BC lawyer or licensed tax professional and consult www.gov.bc.ca for current rates and exemptions.

Verify with: Greater Vancouver REALTORS®
Is Benchmark Price the same as BC Assessment's assessed value?

No. Benchmark Price is a market-based composite calculated by real estate boards using recent sales data and statistical models, updated monthly. BC Assessment's assessed value is determined annually (as of July 1 each year, for the following tax year) under the Assessment Act and is used for property tax purposes. The two numbers measure value at different times, using different methods, and serve different purposes; verify which value is required for your transaction or tax matter with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Verify with: Greater Vancouver REALTORS®
Can I appeal my BC property taxes based on Benchmark Price?

No. Property tax appeals in BC are based on BC Assessment's assessed value, not Benchmark Price. If you believe your assessed value is incorrect, you may file a complaint with the Property Assessment Review Panel under the Assessment Act, using comparable sales and appraisal evidence, not a Benchmark Price composite. Verify appeal deadlines and evidence requirements with a BC lawyer or visit the Property Assessment Appeal Board's website before acting.

Verify with: Greater Vancouver REALTORS®
Does the federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act reference Benchmark Price?

No. The Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10 (federal Foreign Buyer Ban, currently extended through January 1, 2027 as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), does not reference Benchmark Price; it prohibits certain purchases by non-Canadians subject to exceptions, without regard to price or market indicators. Compliance depends on citizenship, residency status, and property type, not valuation metrics. Verify your eligibility and obligations with a BC lawyer or licensed real estate professional before purchasing residential property in Canada.
