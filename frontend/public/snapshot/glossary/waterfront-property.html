Property Types

Waterfront property

A waterfront property is a parcel of land with direct frontage on, or deeded access to, a body of water such as an ocean, lake, or river. Title to such a property is registered under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Buyers and sellers should be aware that foreshore, riparian rights, and any water-access easements are governed by provincial and federal law; verify current details regarding water boundaries and rights with a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does owning waterfront property in BC trigger any special property transfer tax (PTT) rules?

No. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, waterfront properties are subject to the same general PTT rates and exemptions (e.g., First-Time Home Buyer Exemption, Newly Built Home Exemption) as other residential properties, based on the fair market value at the time of registration. The Additional PTT of 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) applies to foreign entities and taxable trustees for residential property including waterfront property. Verify current rates and eligibility with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Who regulates real estate licensees selling waterfront property in BC?

The British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) regulates all trading and rental services licensees under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the RESA Rules. BCFSA assumed this role from the Real Estate Council of BC on August 1, 2021 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify current BCFSA Rules and licensing requirements at www.bcfsa.ca or with a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Are there restrictions on foreign buyers purchasing waterfront property in BC?

Yes. The federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10, prohibits most non-Canadians from purchasing residential property in Canada, including BC waterfront properties, through January 1, 2027 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). BC's Additional PTT of 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) applies to foreign entities and taxable trustees under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378. Verify current exemptions, extensions, and eligibility with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax apply to waterfront properties?

It may. Under the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, the tax applies to residential property in designated taxable regions (certain urban areas); many rural and remote waterfront areas fall outside these regions. Rates and exemptions depend on residency status, occupancy, and declaration filing. Verify whether your specific waterfront property location is in a designated taxable region and your exemption eligibility at www.gov.bc.ca or with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Is waterfront property in BC typically strata (condo) or freehold?

Both forms exist. Waterfront properties may be freehold (fee simple land title under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250) or strata lots governed by the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and Strata Property Regulation. Each strata corporation's bylaws and rules may impose restrictions on use, short-term rentals, or modifications. Verify the registered title, any restrictive covenants, and strata bylaws with a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Do I need special insurance for a waterfront property in BC?

Insurance requirements and availability are determined by private insurers and are not prescribed by the Property Transfer Tax Act, Strata Property Act, or Real Estate Services Act. Waterfront properties may face higher premiums or exclusions for flood, earthquake, or erosion risks. Verify coverage, exclusions, deductibles, and availability with licensed insurance brokers and your mortgage lender before purchasing.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can I claim the BC First-Time Home Buyer PTT Exemption on a waterfront property?

Yes, if you meet all eligibility criteria. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, the full exemption applies to properties up to $835,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) fair market value if the buyer is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, has never owned an interest in a principal residence anywhere, and occupies the property as principal residence within 92 days. Partial exemptions apply up to $860,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify current thresholds, residency, and occupancy requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Are there Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) restrictions on waterfront properties in BC?

Possibly. Under the Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, land within the ALR is subject to restrictions on subdivision, non-farm use, and building placement, even if it has water frontage. Permitted uses and exemptions depend on the specific ALR zone and ALC orders. Verify ALR status, permitted uses, and required ALC approvals at www.alc.gov.bc.ca or with a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing or developing waterfront property.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Does the BC Home Flipping Tax apply if I sell my waterfront property within one year?

It may. Under the Home Flipping Tax Act, SBC 2024 (effective January 1, 2025 — verify current), profits from the sale of residential property (including waterfront property) held for less than 730 days are subject to provincial income tax, with rates declining over the holding period and exemptions for life events (death, separation, disability, employment relocation, insolvency). Verify current holding period thresholds, exemptions, and tax rates with a licensed tax professional or BC lawyer before acting.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
Can a waterfront strata corporation restrict my use of the water access or dock?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation may adopt bylaws and rules governing the use of common property and limited common property, including docks, boat slips, beaches, and waterfront access, provided the bylaws are not contrary to the Act or human rights legislation. Special levies or user fees may also apply. Verify the registered strata plan, bylaws, rules, and any exclusive-use designations in the Form B Information Certificate and with a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing.

Verify with: Land Title Act (RSBC 1996, c. 250) · BC Assessment
