Strata

EV Ready Plan

A voluntary electrical plan specifying the infrastructure required to support one EV charger per strata unit. Not mandatory under the Strata Property Act but required to access certain BC Hydro rebates. Different from, and not a substitute for, an Electrical Planning Report.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an EV Ready Plan in the context of British Columbia strata properties?

An EV Ready Plan is a voluntary electrical infrastructure document that outlines the wiring, panel capacity, conduit, and related systems needed to support one electric vehicle charger per strata unit. It is not mandated by the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) but is commonly prepared by a licensed electrical engineer or contractor. Strata corporations often commission one to guide future EV charging installations and to qualify for certain BC Hydro incentive programs.

Verify with: BC Hydro
Is an EV Ready Plan legally required under the Strata Property Act in BC?

No, the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) does not require a strata corporation to prepare or adopt an EV Ready Plan. It remains a voluntary planning document that strata corporations may choose to commission. Strata corporations should consult the Act and their own bylaws when deciding how to proceed with any EV infrastructure initiative.

Verify with: BC Hydro
How does an EV Ready Plan differ from an Electrical Planning Report in BC?

An EV Ready Plan specifically addresses the infrastructure needed to support one EV charger per strata unit and is a condition of certain BC Hydro rebate programs. An Electrical Planning Report is a broader engineering assessment of a building's overall electrical capacity and upgrade needs and is a distinct document with its own scope. The two documents serve different purposes and one cannot be substituted for the other.

Verify with: BC Hydro
Can a BC strata corporation use its contingency reserve fund to pay for an EV Ready Plan?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), the contingency reserve fund is intended for expenses that occur less frequently than annually or that are not anticipated in the annual budget. Whether the cost of an EV Ready Plan qualifies as a contingency reserve fund expenditure depends on the strata corporation's budget and bylaw provisions, as well as the nature of the work involved. Strata councils should review the Act's provisions on fund expenditure and seek appropriate professional guidance before drawing on the contingency reserve fund.

Verify with: BC Hydro
What vote threshold does a BC strata corporation need to approve work arising from an EV Ready Plan?

The required vote depends on the nature and cost of the work being approved, as set out in the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and its Regulation. Significant alterations to common property or expenditures exceeding prescribed thresholds relative to the operating budget generally require a three-quarters vote or, in some cases, a resolution without a meeting. Strata corporations should consult the Act and their current bylaws to determine the correct approval threshold for the specific work contemplated.

Verify with: BC Hydro
Does an EV Ready Plan affect what a strata corporation must disclose in a Form B Information Certificate in BC?

A Form B Information Certificate, required under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), discloses prescribed financial and bylaw information about a strata corporation to prospective purchasers. While the Form B itself does not have a dedicated field for an EV Ready Plan, any approved special levies, budget items, or bylaw amendments arising from EV infrastructure decisions would need to be accurately reflected in the Form B. Sellers and strata managers should ensure the Form B captures all relevant financial obligations associated with any adopted EV infrastructure plans.

Verify with: BC Hydro
Can individual BC strata lot owners install EV chargers without a strata-wide EV Ready Plan?

A strata lot owner may apply to the strata corporation for permission to alter common property or limited common property to install an EV charger, as governed by the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and the strata corporation's bylaws. The existence of a strata-wide EV Ready Plan is not a legal prerequisite for an individual owner's application, though it may streamline electrical planning and approval. Individual installations must still comply with any applicable strata bylaws, electrical permitting requirements, and BC Safety Authority rules.

Verify with: BC Hydro
Does the preparation or adoption of an EV Ready Plan trigger any Property Transfer Tax obligations in BC?

Preparing or adopting an EV Ready Plan is an internal planning and governance activity of a strata corporation and does not in itself constitute a taxable transaction under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act. Property Transfer Tax is triggered by the registration of a transfer of an interest in land, not by infrastructure planning documents. Consult the BC Ministry of Finance for guidance on any specific transaction where EV infrastructure improvements may affect property value or title.

Verify with: BC Hydro
What role does BCFSA or RESA play when a real estate licensee discusses an EV Ready Plan with a client in BC?

When a licensee licensed under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) discusses an EV Ready Plan with a client, the licensee's obligations under RESA and the BCFSA's Rules—including duties of honesty, accurate disclosure, and acting in the client's best interests—apply to any representations made. A licensee should accurately characterize an EV Ready Plan as a voluntary document and should not overstate its legal status or guarantee access to BC Hydro rebates. Licensees who are uncertain about the technical details of EV infrastructure should refer clients to qualified electrical engineers or the relevant program administrators.

Verify with: BC Hydro
If a BC strata corporation collects owner contact information to circulate an EV Ready Plan survey, what privacy rules apply?

The collection, use, and disclosure of strata owners' personal information by a strata corporation is governed by the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) of BC, which requires that personal information be collected only for reasonable purposes and with appropriate consent. If the survey is distributed electronically for commercial or solicitation purposes, Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) may also apply to those communications. Strata corporations should ensure their data handling practices comply with PIPA and, where applicable, CASL before distributing any electronic survey to owners.

Verify with: BC Hydro
