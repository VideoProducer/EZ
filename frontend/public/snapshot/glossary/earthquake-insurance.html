Earthquake Insurance

Optional coverage, typically purchased as an endorsement to a homeowner or strata insurance policy, for damage caused by earthquakes. Earthquake coverage carries its own deductible (often expressed as a percentage of the dwelling limit) and may have specific exclusions. Important to consider in BC's seismic zones.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is earthquake insurance mandatory for homeowners in British Columbia?

Earthquake insurance is not mandatory under any BC statute for individual homeowners; it is optional coverage typically added as an endorsement to a standard homeowner's policy. However, given that much of BC lies within high-seismic-activity zones, insurers and mortgage lenders may strongly encourage it. Homeowners should review their existing policy carefully to confirm whether earthquake damage is excluded from the base coverage.

Verify with: Insurance Bureau of Canada
How does earthquake insurance work for strata corporations in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), a strata corporation is required to obtain and maintain property insurance on the buildings and common property, but the Act does not specifically mandate earthquake coverage. Many strata corporations purchase earthquake insurance as part of their master policy or as a separate endorsement, and the terms—including any percentage-based deductible—are governed by the corporation's insurance arrangements and its bylaws. Individual strata lot owners should review the strata corporation's insurance certificate and consider purchasing their own unit owner's policy to cover any gap between the strata deductible and their unit's assessed value.

Verify with: Insurance Bureau of Canada
What is a typical earthquake insurance deductible structure in BC?

Unlike the flat-dollar deductibles common in standard property insurance, earthquake deductibles in BC are frequently expressed as a percentage of the insured dwelling's replacement value—consult current insurer offerings and BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) guidance for typical percentage ranges, as these vary by insurer and seismic zone. This percentage-based structure means that for high-value properties, the out-of-pocket deductible can be substantial. Buyers and strata owners alike should carefully read their policy documents to understand the exact deductible calculation method.

Verify with: Insurance Bureau of Canada
Does BC's Strata Property Act require strata corporations to disclose earthquake insurance details to prospective buyers?

Yes. Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and its Regulation, a Form B Information Certificate—which must be provided to a prospective purchaser on request—is required to include information about the strata corporation's current insurance, including the type, coverage amount, and deductible. This means whether or not the strata carries earthquake coverage, and the size of the earthquake deductible, should be disclosed through the Form B process. Buyers should review the Form B and the attached insurance summary carefully before completing a purchase.

Verify with: Insurance Bureau of Canada
Can a real estate licensee in BC advise clients on whether to purchase earthquake insurance?

BC real estate licensees are regulated by the BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and its Rules, which govern licensee conduct and the scope of permissible services. Providing specific insurance advice falls outside the authorized scope of a real estate licensee's practice; licensees can note the existence and general importance of earthquake insurance as a consumer consideration, but clients should be directed to a licensed insurance broker or advisor for coverage recommendations. Licensees must not misrepresent facts about insurance coverage or deductibles in the course of a transaction.

Verify with: Insurance Bureau of Canada
How does a large strata corporation earthquake deductible affect individual unit owners in BC?

When a strata corporation's earthquake insurance carries a large percentage-based deductible and an insured loss occurs, the strata corporation may pass a portion of that deductible cost on to affected unit owners, subject to the strata corporation's bylaws and the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43). Individual unit owners who carry their own strata unit owner's insurance policy may be able to claim the assessed deductible contribution against their own policy, depending on their coverage terms. Prospective buyers should review both the strata corporation's insurance deductible amounts and their own personal coverage needs before purchasing.

Verify with: Insurance Bureau of Canada
Are there specific BC seismic zones that influence earthquake insurance premiums?

BC is divided into seismic hazard zones by the National Building Code of Canada, and areas such as Metro Vancouver, Victoria, and the Fraser Valley are recognized as higher-risk zones, which generally results in higher earthquake insurance premiums and potentially higher deductibles. Insurers use these seismic risk classifications, alongside factors such as building age, construction type, and soil conditions, to determine pricing. Homeowners and strata corporations in higher-risk zones should consult a licensed insurance broker for zone-specific premium information, as no single statutory schedule governs premium rates.

Verify with: Insurance Bureau of Canada
Does the BC Property Transfer Tax Act have any provisions related to earthquake insurance?

The BC Property Transfer Tax Act does not contain any provisions that relate to earthquake insurance; it governs the tax payable on transfers of interests in land, with rates of 1% on the first portion of the fair market value, 2% on the next portion, 3% on the portion above a specified threshold, and an additional 2% on the residential value exceeding $3,000,000. Earthquake insurance is a private contractual matter between a property owner and an insurer and does not affect Property Transfer Tax calculations. Buyers should keep these matters separate when budgeting for a purchase.

Verify with: Insurance Bureau of Canada
If a BC property is destroyed by an earthquake, how are insurance proceeds handled in a mortgage or estate context?

Mortgage lenders in BC typically require that they be noted as a loss payee on a property insurance policy, meaning earthquake insurance proceeds would generally be directed first toward satisfying the outstanding mortgage balance, with any surplus going to the property owner. If a property owner dies and earthquake insurance proceeds form part of the estate, those funds would be dealt with under the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA) of BC, passing according to the will or intestacy rules. The specific terms of the mortgage contract and any applicable strata bylaws will also govern how insurance proceeds are applied.

Verify with: Insurance Bureau of Canada
What should a BC buyer look for in a property disclosure or inspection regarding earthquake risk?

While no BC statute mandates a seller-completed earthquake risk assessment, the Property Disclosure Statement—a standard document in BC residential transactions—invites disclosure of known material latent defects, which could include known seismic vulnerabilities or unremedied earthquake damage. Buyers should also commission a professional home inspection and, for older buildings, consider requesting information about seismic upgrades, foundation type, and soil conditions. A licensed insurance broker can then use this information to assess insurability and premium costs under an earthquake endorsement.
