Property Types

Single Family Home

A single family home is a detached residence designed to house one household on its own lot, registered under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Historically, many BC municipalities zoned urban lots exclusively for single family use under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. The Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023 (BC Bill 44) amended zoning rules to require most urban lots to permit small-scale multi-unit housing; verify the unit allowances applicable to a specific property with the relevant municipality. A property marketed as a single family home may still lawfully contain a secondary suite or laneway house where local zoning permits. Buyers should confirm current zoning designations directly with the municipality as standard pre-purchase due diligence; verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a single family home in British Columbia?

A single family home is a detached residential dwelling designed for occupation by one household, situated on its own lot. Under BC's Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, municipalities have historically zoned many urban residential lots to permit only a single detached dwelling, though that may include accessory uses such as secondary suites or laneway houses where local bylaws allow. As of July 1, 2024 (verify current), BC's Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023 (Bill 44) requires most municipalities to permit small-scale multi-unit housing (typically 3–4 units) on lots previously zoned for single family homes, subject to specified exemptions. Confirm current zoning and permissible uses with the local municipality before purchase.

Does owning a single family home in BC trigger property transfer tax (PTT)?

Yes. Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, every transfer of land or a registered interest in land in BC is subject to property transfer tax unless a specific exemption applies. Rates are 1% on the first $200,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), 2% on the portion between $200,000 and $3,000,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), 3% on the portion between $3,000,000 and $10,000,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), and 5% on the balance over $10,000,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). First-time home buyers and purchasers of newly built homes may qualify for full or partial exemptions; verify eligibility and current thresholds with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before completing a purchase.

Verify with: BC Government — Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing
Can I have a secondary suite or laneway house in my single family home?

This depends on local municipal zoning bylaws adopted under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Many BC municipalities historically permitted one secondary suite and/or one laneway house on a single family lot by bylaw, and BC's Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023 (Bill 44), effective July 1, 2024 (verify current), now generally requires municipalities to allow small-scale multi-unit housing (typically 3–4 units) on most urban residential lots. Confirm the exact number, size, and parking requirements for additional dwelling units with the municipal planning or building department and verify compliance with zoning, building, and safety codes before construction or occupancy.

Verify with: BC Government — Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing
What is BC Bill 44 and how does it affect single family home zoning?

BC Bill 44, the Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023, amends the Local Government Act to require most municipalities to permit small-scale multi-unit housing—generally 3 units on lots under 280 m² (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) and 4 units on larger urban residential lots—on parcels historically zoned for single detached dwellings. The amendments took effect July 1, 2024 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for most municipalities, subject to specified exemptions (e.g., certain rural, island, or resort communities). Even where multi-unit housing is now permitted, property owners retain the right to maintain a single family home; the law expands options but does not mandate redevelopment. Confirm current zoning with your municipality and consult a BC lawyer or notary regarding permissible uses, development standards, and strata or subdivision implications before undertaking any construction.

Verify with: BC Government — Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing
Am I subject to BC's Speculation and Vacancy Tax if I own a single family home?

This depends on the location of the property and your residency and use of the home. The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, imposes an annual tax on residential properties in designated taxable regions (primarily Metro Vancouver, Capital Regional District, Nanaimo, Lantzville, certain Fraser Valley and Central Okanagan areas) that are not the owner's principal residence or are not occupied by a qualifying tenant for at least six months of the calendar year (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). BC citizens and permanent residents pay 0.5% of assessed value (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), while foreign owners and taxable Canadian corporations pay 2% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), unless an exemption applies. Review your annual declaration from the BC Ministry of Finance and verify eligibility for any exemption with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before the filing deadline.

Does the federal foreign buyer ban apply to single family homes in BC?

Yes. The Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10, generally prohibits non-Canadians (individuals who are not Canadian citizens or permanent residents, and foreign corporations and entities) from purchasing residential property in Canada, including single family homes, for a period currently extended through January 1, 2027 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Certain exceptions exist, including for permanent residents, work or study permit holders meeting specified criteria, and properties in rural areas with a population below 10,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify current exemptions, definitions, and the expiry or extension of the prohibition with a BC lawyer or notary before any non-Canadian purchaser enters into a purchase agreement.

What is the additional property transfer tax (Additional PTT) on foreign buyers purchasing a single family home in BC?

Under the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, an additional property transfer tax of 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) applies to residential transfers by foreign entities (individuals who are not Canadian citizens or permanent residents, foreign corporations, and taxable trustees) in designated regions of BC, including Metro Vancouver, the Capital Regional District, Fraser Valley, Central Okanagan, and Nanaimo regional districts. This additional 20% (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) is levied on top of the general PTT rates. Exemptions may apply in limited circumstances (e.g., nominees of Canadian citizens or permanent residents, certain provincial nominees); verify the current list of taxable regions, foreign entity definitions, and available exemptions with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before closing.

Verify with: BC Government — Small-Scale Multi-Unit Housing
Can a single family home be subject to the BC Home Flipping Tax?

Yes, if it is sold within a specified holding period. The Home Flipping Tax Act, SBC 2024, effective January 1, 2025 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), imposes a tax on profits from the sale of residential real property (including single family homes) held for less than 730 days (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), with the tax rate declining from 20% for properties sold within 365 days to 0% at 730 days (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Exemptions apply for certain life events (death, separation, disability, employment relocation, insolvency, or involuntary disposition) and for properties that were the seller's principal residence for the entire holding period. Verify current rates, holding periods, exemptions, and principal residence definitions with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before listing or accepting an offer.

Do I need a home inspection when buying a single family home in BC?

Home inspections are not legally required under BC law, but they are a standard component of prudent pre-purchase due diligence. Under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and the BCFSA Rules, licensed real estate professionals must advise buyers to conduct their own inspections and investigations; however, the final decision to retain an inspector rests with the buyer. A professional home inspection can identify latent defects, building code compliance issues, and maintenance concerns not visible during a showing. Engage a qualified home inspector and consult a BC lawyer or notary to include appropriate inspection and financing subjects in your Contract of Purchase and Sale before removing conditions.

What is the BC Home Owner Grant and does it apply to single family homes?

The BC Home Owner Grant, administered under the Home Owner Grant Act, provides an annual reduction in municipal property taxes for owner-occupiers of principal residences, including single family homes. The basic grant amount is $570 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) for properties with assessed values below a specified threshold ($2,150,000 as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), with higher grant amounts for seniors, persons with disabilities, and veterans. Eligibility requires that the applicant be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, occupy the property as their principal residence as of December 31 of the previous year, and meet residency requirements. Verify current grant amounts, assessed value thresholds, and eligibility criteria on the BC Ministry of Finance website or with your municipality before claiming the grant on your annual property tax notice.
