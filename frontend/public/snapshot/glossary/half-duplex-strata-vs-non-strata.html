Half Duplex — Strata vs Non-Strata

A half duplex is one of two dwelling units that share a common wall or part of the same building, with each side typically occupied or owned separately — essentially half of a detached home divided into two separate residences. In Metro Vancouver and Langley these are extremely common. A stratified half duplex is a strata lot — it has strata fees, a strata corporation, and is governed by the Strata Property Act. A non-strata half duplex is owned outright including the land under it — there are no strata fees and no strata corporation. Non-strata half duplexes are generally more desirable and easier to finance. The strata-vs-non-strata distinction appears on the registered Strata Plan and on title.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a half duplex in BC a 'strata' versus a 'non-strata' property?

A strata half duplex has been subdivided under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), meaning each unit is a separate strata lot registered on a Strata Plan filed in the Land Title Office, with a strata corporation governing the shared structure and common property. A non-strata half duplex is owned as a single freehold parcel — the owner holds title to the unit and the land beneath it outright, with no strata plan, no strata corporation, and no strata fees. The distinction is visible on the property's certificate of title: a strata lot will show a Strata Plan number (e.g., BCS or LMS prefix), while a non-strata half duplex will show a conventional lot and plan description. This registration difference has significant legal, financial, and governance consequences for buyers.

How does a buyer confirm whether a Metro Vancouver half duplex is strata or non-strata before making an offer?

The most reliable method is to search the property's certificate of title through the BC Land Title and Survey Authority (LTSA), which will show whether a Strata Plan is registered against the parcel. A licensed real estate professional regulated by the BCFSA under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) is obligated to disclose material latent and patent facts about a property, and the strata versus non-strata distinction is a material fact that must be clearly communicated to a buyer. The registered Strata Plan itself, filed at the Land Title Office, is the definitive legal document confirming strata status. Buyers can also review the MLS listing's legal description, though independently verifying title is always prudent.

What is a Form B Information Certificate, and does it apply to a strata half duplex in BC?

A Form B Information Certificate is a document required under section 59 of the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) that a strata corporation must provide upon request, disclosing key financial and legal information about the strata including current strata fees, the balance of the contingency reserve fund, any special levies, registered bylaws, and pending litigation. It applies to any strata lot, including a stratified half duplex, because that unit is a strata lot within a strata corporation — even if the strata corporation comprises only two units. A buyer purchasing a strata half duplex should always request the Form B as part of their due diligence, along with the strata's bylaws, rules, and most recent depreciation report if one exists.

Are strata fees common on half duplexes in BC, and what do they typically cover?

Yes, strata fees are payable on any stratified half duplex because the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) requires every strata corporation to establish and collect strata fees from owners to fund the operating budget and the contingency reserve fund. For a two-lot strata comprising one half duplex building, fees typically cover shared expenses such as building insurance for the structure, maintenance of common property (which may include the roof, exterior walls, and foundations shared by both units), and contributions to the contingency reserve. The amount varies significantly between stratas; buyers should review the strata's current budget, the most recent financial statements, and the Form B Information Certificate to understand the exact fee and reserve fund status.

Why are non-strata half duplexes generally considered easier to finance in BC?

Non-strata half duplexes are freehold properties where the owner holds title to both the dwelling and the underlying land, which many lenders treat similarly to a detached single-family home and typically apply straightforward residential mortgage underwriting. Strata half duplexes, by contrast, require lenders to assess strata-specific risks including the adequacy of the contingency reserve fund, pending special levies, strata litigation, and the strata corporation's financial health — factors lenders scrutinize under their internal policies and OSFI mortgage underwriting guidelines. Some lenders impose additional conditions or restrict lending on two-lot stratas they consider high-risk, particularly if the contingency reserve is underfunded. Buyers should consult directly with their lender or mortgage broker regarding the specific financing conditions that apply to their intended purchase.

Does the Strata Property Act require a two-unit strata (one half duplex building) to obtain a depreciation report?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43) and its Regulation, strata corporations with fewer than five strata lots were previously exempt from the mandatory depreciation report requirement; however, amendments to the Strata Property Act Regulation have progressively narrowed exemptions, and buyers of two-lot stratas should verify the current applicable threshold with the BC Government or BCFSA, as the rules have been subject to legislative change. Even where a two-lot strata is technically exempt or has waived the requirement, the absence of a depreciation report means there is no independent assessment of the building's long-term repair and replacement costs, which is a material consideration for buyers. If no depreciation report exists, buyers of a strata half duplex should conduct their own due diligence on the building's condition, including a professional home inspection.

How does Property Transfer Tax apply when purchasing a strata half duplex versus a non-strata half duplex in BC?

Under the BC Property Transfer Tax Act, both strata and non-strata half duplexes are subject to the standard PTT rate schedule: 1% on the first $200,000 of the fair market value, 2% on the portion between $200,000 and $3,000,000, and an additional 2% on the residential portion exceeding $3,000,000. The First-Time Home Buyer full PTT exemption applies to qualifying buyers where the property's fair market value does not exceed $835,000, regardless of whether the half duplex is strata or non-strata. The Newly Built Home Exemption under the Property Transfer Tax Act may also apply to either type if the property is newly constructed and the purchase price does not exceed $1,100,000, subject to eligibility requirements. Buyers should consult the BC Ministry of Finance for current thresholds and eligibility conditions.

If a strata half duplex owner in BC wants to change the bylaws — for example to permit short-term rentals — how does that work?

Under the Strata Property Act (SBC 1998, c. 43), bylaws of a strata corporation can be amended by a three-quarters vote at an annual or special general meeting of the strata corporation, meaning in a two-lot strata both owners would effectively need to agree in order to achieve the required majority. Once passed, bylaw amendments must be filed in the Land Title Office to be enforceable against subsequent owners, as required by section 128 of the Strata Property Act. It is also important to note that any strata bylaw permitting short-term rentals must still comply with the applicable local government zoning bylaws and, where applicable, the provincial Short-Term Rental Accommodations Act, which imposes additional restrictions in many BC municipalities. Owners should review all layers of regulation before relying solely on a strata bylaw amendment.

When a non-strata half duplex owner in BC dies, how is the property transferred to their estate?

Because a non-strata half duplex owner holds registered freehold title to their individual parcel, the property forms part of their estate and is transferred in accordance with the Wills, Estates and Succession Act (WESA, SBC 2009, c. 13), either according to the terms of a valid will or, if the owner dies intestate, under WESA's intestacy distribution rules. The executor or administrator of the estate will typically need to obtain a Grant of Probate or Administration from the BC Supreme Court before the Land Title Office will register a transfer of title to a beneficiary or purchaser, unless an exception applies. For a strata half duplex, the same WESA rules apply to the deceased owner's strata lot, and the executor would also need to manage any outstanding strata fees or levies owed to the strata corporation before or during the transfer. Parties involved in estate transfers of either property type should work with a BC notary public or lawyer.

Can a licensed BC real estate licensee collect personal information about a buyer's interest in a specific half duplex listing without their knowledge?

No — under BC's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA, SBC 2003, c. 63), a real estate licensee or brokerage may only collect, use, or disclose an individual's personal information with their knowledge and consent, except in limited circumstances prescribed by the Act. BCFSA, which regulates licensees under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), expects licensees to handle client and customer personal information in compliance with PIPA, and brokerage privacy policies must reflect these obligations. Licensees should be transparent with buyers about what personal information is collected, why it is being collected, and how it will be used or shared. For unsolicited commercial electronic communications related to property listings, Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) also applies and generally requires express or implied consent before sending commercial electronic messages.
