Heat Pump Requirements

Heat pump requirements refer to building energy efficiency standards established under the BC Building Code and the BC Energy Step Code that may, depending on the municipality and the type of construction, impose minimum performance thresholds effectively directing new buildings toward low-carbon or electric heating systems such as heat pumps. Specific requirements, applicable building types, and mandatory Step Code tiers vary by jurisdiction, as local governments may adopt higher steps than the provincial baseline. Because effective dates and technical standards change, verify current requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed building professional before relying on any specific threshold.

Frequently Asked Questions

What statute or regulation governs heat pump requirements for new residential construction in BC?

Heat pump and heating system requirements for new construction in BC are primarily governed by the BC Building Code, which is adopted under the Building Act, SBC 2015, c. 2. Some local governments also adopt the BC Energy Step Code under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, which may impose additional energy performance requirements, including restrictions on fossil fuel heating systems. Verify the specific requirements in effect in your municipality with a BC lawyer, notary, or the local building department before acting.

Are heat pumps mandatory for all new homes in British Columbia?

No, heat pump requirements vary by municipality and the BC Energy Step Code level adopted locally. Not all BC municipalities have adopted the Energy Step Code or zero-carbon heating requirements (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Some jurisdictions require electric heat pumps or zero-emission heating for new residential construction, while others do not. Verify current requirements with your municipality's building department or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Building Code
What is the BC Energy Step Code and how does it relate to heat pump requirements?

The BC Energy Step Code is a voluntary provincial standard adopted under Part 10 of the BC Building Code and enabled by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Municipalities may require compliance with specific Step Code levels (Step 1 through Step 5), and higher steps often effectively require electric heat pumps or other zero-emission heating to meet energy performance targets (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify whether your municipality has adopted the Energy Step Code and at what step level with the local building department or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Building Code
Can a municipality in BC ban natural gas heating systems in new construction?

Yes, under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, municipalities have authority to regulate energy use and greenhouse gas emissions in new construction through building bylaws and adoption of the BC Energy Step Code. Some BC municipalities have enacted bylaws effectively requiring zero-emission heating systems (such as electric heat pumps) for new residential buildings, which precludes natural gas (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the bylaws in your specific municipality with a BC lawyer, notary, or the local government before acting.

Verify with: BC Building Code
Do heat pump requirements apply to existing homes or only new construction in BC?

As of 2026-07-27 (verify current), BC Energy Step Code heat pump requirements generally apply only to new construction, not existing residential buildings. Renovations or additions to existing homes may trigger building code compliance for the new or altered portions, but whole-home heat pump retrofits are typically not mandated by the BC Building Code. Verify whether your renovation scope or local bylaws impose heating system requirements with your municipality or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Building Code
Are there exemptions or alternatives to heat pump installation for new homes in BC?

Exemptions and alternatives depend on the specific municipal bylaw and BC Energy Step Code level adopted locally. Some jurisdictions may allow compliance through alternative performance pathways (e.g., net-zero-ready construction, renewable natural gas, or district energy), while others mandate electric heat pumps outright (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify available compliance pathways and exemptions with your municipality's building department or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Building Code
When did BC municipalities start requiring heat pumps for new construction?

The timing varies by municipality, as heat pump requirements are adopted locally under the BC Energy Step Code framework enabled by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Some municipalities began requiring zero-emission heating (effectively heat pumps) as early as 2020, while others have not yet adopted such requirements (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the effective date of any heat pump or zero-emission heating requirement in your municipality with the local building department or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Building Code
Does the Property Transfer Tax Act or Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act address heat pump requirements?

No, neither the Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, nor the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, contain provisions governing heat pump installation or heating system types in residential buildings. Heat pump requirements are governed by the BC Building Code (under the Building Act, SBC 2015, c. 2) and municipal bylaws adopted under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Verify heating system compliance obligations with your municipality or a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Building Code
Are strata corporations in BC required to retrofit common property with heat pumps?

No, the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and Strata Property Regulation do not impose heat pump retrofit mandates on existing strata corporations. Heat pump requirements for new construction are imposed by the BC Building Code and municipal bylaws under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, but retrofit obligations for existing common property are not currently mandated by provincial statute (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify whether your municipality has enacted retrofit bylaws or other requirements with a BC lawyer or notary before acting.

Verify with: BC Building Code
Where can I verify whether my municipality requires heat pumps for new residential construction?

Contact your municipality's building department or planning department directly, or consult the municipality's official website for building bylaws and BC Energy Step Code adoption. The BC Ministry of Municipal Affairs and the BC Housing website may also publish lists of municipalities that have adopted the Energy Step Code (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). For legal interpretation of your obligations, verify with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed professional before acting.
