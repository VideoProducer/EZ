Agency & Disclosure

Unrepresented Buyer

An unrepresented buyer is a person who has not entered into an agency relationship with any licensee in a transaction and is therefore not a client of any brokerage. Under the Real Estate Services Act, SBC 2004, c. 42, and rules made under it, a licensee must disclose their representative capacity to an unrepresented buyer before any substantive discussion about a property. The listing licensee may assist an unrepresented buyer with factual information but cannot provide advice, advocate on that buyer's behalf, or act in that buyer's interest. The required disclosure form must be provided at the outset of the interaction; verify the specific form name, delivery timing, and governing rule number with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed real estate professional, as these requirements are subject to change.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an unrepresented buyer in a British Columbia real estate transaction?

An unrepresented buyer is a buyer who has chosen not to enter into an agency relationship with a licensed real estate professional (REALTOR®) and is therefore not a client of any licensee in the transaction. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the Real Estate Services Rules made under RESA, this buyer does not receive advice or advocacy from a licensee. The listing licensee may interact with an unrepresented buyer but owes them only limited duties, not the fiduciary duties owed to a client. Verify current obligations with a BC lawyer or notary before proceeding without representation.

Verify with: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services
Does the listing agent have to give me a form if I contact them directly as an unrepresented buyer?

Yes. Under Section 54 of the Real Estate Services Rules (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), the listing licensee must provide you with a Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services form before any substantive interaction about the property. This form explains what services the licensee will and will not provide to you, and clarifies that the licensee represents the seller, not you. Failure to provide this disclosure may constitute a breach of the Rules enforced by the British Columbia Financial Services Authority (BCFSA).

Verify with: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services
Can the listing agent give me advice if I am an unrepresented buyer?

No. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA) and the Real Estate Services Rules, a listing licensee may not provide advice or advocate on behalf of an unrepresented buyer because that buyer is not the licensee's client. The licensee's fiduciary duties—including advice and advocacy—run only to their client, the seller. The licensee may answer factual questions and facilitate the transaction, but strategic or legal advice must come from your own BC lawyer, notary, or licensed professional.

Verify with: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services
What duties does the listing licensee owe to me as an unrepresented buyer?

The listing licensee owes you limited duties under the Real Estate Services Act and Rules, including honesty, accuracy in representations, and compliance with BCFSA Rules. However, the licensee does not owe you fiduciary duties such as loyalty, confidentiality of your negotiating position, advice, or advocacy—those duties run to the seller. For a full understanding of your rights and risks, verify with a BC lawyer or notary before proceeding.

Verify with: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services
Do I save money on commission if I buy without my own agent?

Not necessarily. Commission is negotiated between the seller and the listing brokerage under the listing agreement; it is not typically reduced if a buyer is unrepresented. The listing brokerage may retain the full commission that would otherwise have been shared with a buyer's agent. Verify the specific commission arrangement and any potential savings with a BC lawyer or the seller directly before assuming cost savings.

Verify with: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services
Can I negotiate directly with the seller if I am unrepresented?

In most cases, the listing agreement requires all offers and negotiations to go through the listing licensee, even if you are unrepresented. The listing licensee acts as the conduit but represents only the seller's interests. You may wish to consult a BC lawyer or notary to review any offer or contract terms before submitting, as you will not have a licensee advocating for your position. Verify the seller's preferences and listing terms before attempting direct contact.

Verify with: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services
What risks do I face as an unrepresented buyer in BC?

Without representation, you have no licensee providing advice on price, contract terms, property condition, financing conditions, or disclosure obligations under BC law. You bear sole responsibility for due diligence, including reviewing the Property Disclosure Statement (if provided), title searches under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, strata documents under the Strata Property Act (if applicable), zoning under the Local Government Act, and other material facts. Errors or omissions may result in financial loss or legal liability; verify all aspects with a BC lawyer, notary, or other licensed professional before signing any contract.

Verify with: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services
If I am unrepresented, who prepares the Contract of Purchase and Sale?

In British Columbia, the listing licensee or the seller's lawyer typically prepares the Contract of Purchase and Sale using standard forms (such as those published by the British Columbia Real Estate Association). As an unrepresented buyer, you should have the contract reviewed by your own BC lawyer or notary before signing, as the listing licensee cannot advise you on its terms or negotiate on your behalf. The contract is a binding legal document governed by BC common law and statute; verify all clauses and conditions with independent legal counsel.

Verify with: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services
Does being unrepresented affect my property transfer tax obligations or exemptions in BC?

No—your status as unrepresented or represented does not change your obligations under the Property Transfer Tax Act (PTTA), RSBC 1996, c. 378. Eligibility for exemptions such as the First-Time Home Buyer Exemption (up to $835,000 as of 2026-07-27 — verify current) or the Newly Built Home Exemption depends on statutory criteria, not on whether you have a real estate agent. However, without a licensee to guide you, you are solely responsible for understanding and claiming any exemptions; verify eligibility and filing requirements with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before completion.

Verify with: Disclosure of Representation in Trading Services
Can I become a 'client' of the listing agent partway through the process if I start as unrepresented?

Generally, the listing licensee cannot also represent you (the buyer) in the same transaction, as this would create a conflict of interest under the Real Estate Services Act and BCFSA Rules, unless both you and the seller consent in writing to limited dual agency (which is rare and subject to strict disclosure requirements). If you wish to have representation, you would typically engage a separate buyer's agent from a different brokerage or the same brokerage (with proper disclosure). Verify the specific rules and any consent forms with a BC lawyer or the BCFSA before proceeding.
