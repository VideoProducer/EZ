Unserviced Lot

An unserviced lot is a parcel of land without connections to municipal water, sewer, or utility infrastructure. Such lots are common in rural BC, the Gulf Islands, and unincorporated areas governed under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. Buyers typically need to arrange a drilled well, a septic system approved by the relevant Regional Health Authority following a percolation test, and propane or electrical service extensions. Servicing costs vary significantly based on terrain, soil conditions, and site access; verify estimated costs with qualified contractors before making an offer, as no defensible figure can be stated here without verification. Assessing servicing feasibility and budget is a standard due-diligence step on any rural land purchase. Verify current requirements and costs with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed professional before proceeding.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an unserviced lot in British Columbia?

An unserviced lot is a parcel of land that lacks municipal water, sewer, and utility connections. It is common in rural BC, the Gulf Islands, and unincorporated areas outside municipal boundaries. Owners typically must install a private well, septic system, and arrange for propane or hydro extensions. Verify zoning, servicing requirements, and feasibility with the local government and a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing.

What approvals are required for a septic system on an unserviced lot in BC?

Septic systems in BC are governed by the Public Health Act and local Health Authority regulations, which require a permit and site assessment (including a percolation test) before installation. The Local Government Act (RSBC 2015, c. 1) also grants municipalities and regional districts the authority to set additional bylaws for on-site sewage disposal. Verify current permit requirements, percolation test standards, and approval timelines with your local Health Authority and municipal or regional district office before proceeding.

Does Property Transfer Tax apply differently to unserviced lots?

The Property Transfer Tax Act (PTTA), RSBC 1996, c. 378, applies to all taxable transactions in BC land, including unserviced lots, based on the fair market value at registration. There is no specific exemption or reduced rate for unserviced land under the PTTA (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the applicable PTT rate, any available exemptions, and current thresholds with a BC lawyer, notary, or the BC Ministry of Finance before closing.

Are unserviced lots subject to the BC Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

The Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, applies only to residential properties in designated taxable regions (primarily urban areas such as Metro Vancouver, Capital Regional District, and certain other municipalities). Many rural and unserviced lots fall outside these designated areas and are not subject to the tax (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify whether your specific property is within a designated taxable region by checking the current BC Ministry of Finance SVT maps and consulting a BC tax professional.

Can a foreign buyer purchase an unserviced lot in BC?

The federal Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act, SC 2022, c. 10, prohibits most non-Canadian citizens and non-permanent residents from purchasing residential property in Canada, including vacant land intended for residential use, through January 1, 2027 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Whether an unserviced lot is captured depends on zoning and intended use; recreational or agricultural land may be exempt. Verify eligibility, exemptions, and current extension dates with a BC real estate lawyer and review the federal regulations before making an offer.

What are typical servicing costs for an unserviced lot in BC?

Servicing an unserviced lot in BC typically involves drilling a well, installing a septic system (with Health Authority approval and percolation test), and extending propane or hydro service; costs commonly range from $30,000 to $150,000+ depending on terrain, access, depth to water, soil conditions, and distance to hydro infrastructure (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). These are general market estimates, not statutory amounts. Obtain written quotes from licensed well drillers, septic installers, and BC Hydro (or the local utility) before finalizing your purchase, and verify all costs and feasibility with qualified contractors and a BC real estate lawyer or notary.

Is an unserviced lot automatically excluded from the Agricultural Land Reserve?

No. The Agricultural Land Commission Act, SBC 2002, c. 36, designates land into the Agricultural Land Reserve (ALR) based on agricultural capability and policy, not on whether the lot is serviced or unserviced. An unserviced lot may be inside or outside the ALR; if inside, strict use restrictions apply and non-farm residential use or subdivision may require ALC approval. Verify the ALR status, zoning, and permitted uses with the local government, the Agricultural Land Commission, and a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing.

Do lenders finance unserviced lots the same way as serviced properties?

Most Canadian lenders, including those insured by Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC), impose stricter lending criteria on unserviced land, often requiring larger down payments, higher interest rates, or full servicing plans before advancing funds. Unserviced lots typically do not qualify for standard high-ratio insured mortgages until a dwelling is constructed and services are installed. Verify financing options, down payment requirements, and servicing conditions with your lender and a licensed mortgage broker before making a subject-free offer.

Must a BC real estate licensee disclose that a lot is unserviced?

Yes. Under the Real Estate Services Act (RESA), SBC 2004, c. 42, and the BCFSA RESA Rules, BC licensees owe a duty of honesty, fairness, and disclosure of all material latent defects and relevant facts. The absence of municipal water, sewer, and utility connections is a material fact that must be disclosed to buyers. Verify disclosure obligations and specific property details with your licensed BC real estate professional and a BC lawyer or notary before closing.

Can I subdivide an unserviced lot in BC?

Subdivision in BC is governed by the Local Government Act (RSBC 2015, c. 1) and municipal or regional district bylaws, which set minimum lot sizes, servicing requirements, road access, and other conditions. Many jurisdictions require full municipal servicing, a development permit, or rezoning before approving subdivision; unserviced lots in rural areas may have different rules. Verify subdivision feasibility, minimum parcel sizes, servicing obligations, and applicable bylaws with the local government planning department and a BC lawyer or notary before purchasing with subdivision intent.
