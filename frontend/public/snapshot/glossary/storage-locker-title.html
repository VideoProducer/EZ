Storage Locker Title

In BC strata buildings, a storage locker may be held in one of three configurations under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43: as part of the strata lot itself (titled space forming part of the registered strata plan), as Limited Common Property designated for the exclusive use of a specific strata lot and noted on the strata plan, or as general common property assigned by the strata corporation for locker use. Where a locker is common property, the strata corporation retains authority over its allocation and it does not automatically transfer with the strata lot on sale. Only lockers designated as Limited Common Property or forming part of the titled strata lot travel with a conveyance. The locker's status is disclosed in the Form B Information Certificate and the registered strata plan, both of which a buyer should review carefully before completion. Verify the specific configuration and any assignment terms with a BC lawyer or notary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a storage locker that is part of my strata lot and one that is Limited Common Property (LCP) in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a storage locker that forms part of your strata lot is titled space included in the legal boundaries shown on the registered strata plan and transfers automatically with the strata lot on sale. A locker designated as Limited Common Property (LCP) is common property reserved for the exclusive use of a particular strata lot under section 73 of the Strata Property Act, is registered on the strata plan, and also transfers with the strata lot. Both configurations give stable, long-term rights, but only a locker that is part of the strata lot itself is owned outright as part of the unit. Verify the specific designation on the registered strata plan and Form B Information Certificate with a BC lawyer or notary before purchase.

Can a strata corporation in BC reassign my storage locker to another unit owner?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, only storage lockers held as common property (not designated as LCP and not part of a strata lot) may be reassigned by the strata council under a short-term assignment policy. If the locker is part of your strata lot or is designated as Limited Common Property on the registered strata plan, the strata corporation cannot unilaterally reassign it. Review the Form B Information Certificate and the registered strata plan to confirm the locker's status, and verify your rights with a BC lawyer or notary if reassignment is threatened.

Where can I find out whether my storage locker is part of my strata lot, Limited Common Property, or common property in BC?

The ownership configuration of a storage locker is disclosed in the Form B Information Certificate issued by the strata corporation under section 59 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and shown on the registered strata plan filed at the BC Land Title Office under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. The strata plan will indicate whether the locker is within the boundaries of your strata lot or designated as Limited Common Property; if neither, it is common property. Verify both documents with a BC lawyer or notary during the purchase or sale process to confirm the locker's exact status and transferability.

Verify with: BC Government — Strata Housing
Will my storage locker automatically transfer when I sell my strata unit in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a storage locker that is part of your strata lot or designated as Limited Common Property on the registered strata plan will transfer automatically with the strata lot on sale. A locker held as common property and assigned by the strata council under a short-term assignment policy does not transfer automatically and may be reassigned by the strata corporation. Confirm the locker's status on the Form B Information Certificate and the registered strata plan, and ensure the contract of purchase and sale explicitly addresses the locker, with verification from a BC lawyer or notary.

What is a Form B Information Certificate, and why does it matter for storage locker ownership in BC?

The Form B Information Certificate is a document required under section 59 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, that the strata corporation must provide to a buyer or owner, disclosing the strata lot's financial and legal status, including whether a storage locker is part of the strata lot, Limited Common Property, or common property. Purchasers rely on Form B to confirm locker ownership type and transferability. Verify the Form B details against the registered strata plan and consult a BC lawyer or notary to reconcile locker assignments before completing a purchase.

Verify with: BC Government — Strata Housing
Can a strata corporation in BC charge me a fee to use a storage locker that is common property?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, a strata corporation may adopt bylaws to regulate the assignment of common property, including storage lockers, and may impose a reasonable user fee or deposit if authorized by bylaw. Section 120 permits the strata corporation to impose a user fee for exclusive use of common property if the bylaws so provide. Review the strata corporation's bylaws and consult a BC lawyer or notary to confirm whether the fee structure is authorized and whether the locker assignment may be terminated or reassigned.

If I buy a strata unit in BC and the seller claims a storage locker is included, how do I verify that claim?

Verify the claim by reviewing the Form B Information Certificate issued under section 59 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the registered strata plan filed at the BC Land Title Office under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, to confirm whether the locker is part of the strata lot, designated as Limited Common Property, or merely assigned common property. Ensure the contract of purchase and sale explicitly identifies the locker by number and ownership type, and have a BC lawyer or notary reconcile the locker assignment at completion to avoid disputes.

Verify with: BC Government — Strata Housing
What does 'Limited Common Property' (LCP) mean for a storage locker under BC strata law?

Under section 73 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, Limited Common Property (LCP) is common property designated on the strata plan for the exclusive use of one or more strata lots. A storage locker designated as LCP belongs to the strata corporation but is reserved exclusively for the owner(s) of the specified strata lot(s) and transfers with that strata lot on sale. The designation must appear on the registered strata plan; verify the plan and the Form B Information Certificate with a BC lawyer or notary to confirm LCP status and avoid reassignment risk.

Verify with: BC Government — Strata Housing
Can a strata corporation in BC change a storage locker from common property to Limited Common Property (LCP) without my consent?

Under section 74 of the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, amending a strata plan to designate common property as Limited Common Property (or vice versa) requires a resolution passed by a 3/4 vote at an annual or special general meeting and approval of the owners whose strata lots are affected by the change. The amended strata plan must then be filed at the Land Title Office under the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250. Consult a BC lawyer or notary if the strata corporation proposes such an amendment to understand your voting and consent rights.

Verify with: BC Government — Strata Housing
What happens if the storage locker number shown on the Form B differs from the locker number on the registered strata plan in BC?

Under the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, and the Land Title Act, RSBC 1996, c. 250, the registered strata plan is the authoritative legal record of strata lot boundaries and Limited Common Property designations; the Form B Information Certificate issued under section 59 of the Strata Property Act discloses the strata corporation's records, which should match the plan. A discrepancy may indicate an administrative error, an unauthorized reassignment, or an outdated plan amendment. Do not complete the purchase or sale until a BC lawyer or notary reconciles the locker number against the registered strata plan and confirms the correct ownership configuration and transferability.
