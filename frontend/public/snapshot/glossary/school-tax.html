Taxation

School Tax

A provincial tax levied on residential and non-residential property in British Columbia, authorized under provincial legislation and administered through the annual property tax notice. Rates are set each year by the Province and vary by property class and assessed value. Residential properties assessed above certain value thresholds may be subject to an additional school tax rate tier; verify current thresholds and rates at gov.bc.ca (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Confirm all current figures with the BC Ministry of Finance or a licensed tax professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the School Tax in British Columbia?

School Tax is a provincial tax levied on residential and non-residential property in BC under the School Act, RSBC 1996, c. 412, and collected annually on the property tax notice issued by municipalities. Rates are set each year by the Province and vary by property class and assessed value. An additional school tax applies to high-value residential properties above legislated thresholds (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify current rates and thresholds on the BC Government website or with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
Who collects the School Tax?

School Tax is collected by the municipality or taxing authority on the annual property tax notice, alongside other property taxes. The municipality then remits the School Tax portion to the Province of British Columbia. Verify current collection procedures with your local municipality or a BC lawyer or notary.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
What properties are subject to the School Tax?

Both residential and non-residential properties in BC are subject to School Tax, with rates set by property class under provincial legislation. The additional school tax on high-value residential properties applies only to residential property with an assessed value above provincially set thresholds (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the current property class definitions and thresholds on the BC Government website or with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
What is the additional school tax on residential property?

The additional school tax is a provincial surtax on residential property assessed above set thresholds, introduced to generate revenue from high-value residential real estate. Rates and thresholds are set annually by the Province and change each year (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the current thresholds and applicable rates on the BC Ministry of Finance website or with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
What are the current additional school tax thresholds and rates?

The additional school tax thresholds and rates are set annually by the Province and published by the BC Ministry of Finance; specific dollar thresholds and percentage rates change each year (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Because these figures are updated annually, you must verify the current thresholds and rates on the BC Government website or with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before relying on them.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
Is the School Tax the same as the Speculation and Vacancy Tax?

No, the School Tax and the Speculation and Vacancy Tax are separate provincial taxes. The School Tax is a general property tax levied on all property classes and collected annually on the property tax notice, while the Speculation and Vacancy Tax Act, SBC 2018, c. 46, is a distinct annual tax on certain residential properties in designated areas that are not the owner's principal residence or are left vacant. Verify applicability of each tax with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
Can I get an exemption or reduction from the School Tax?

General School Tax is a mandatory levy on all property; however, property owners may qualify for grants or reductions under other programs such as the BC Home Owner Grant Act, which reduces overall property tax liability for eligible principal residences (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The additional school tax on high-value residential properties does not have a statutory exemption for principal residence. Verify eligibility for any grant or reduction with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
When is the School Tax due?

School Tax is due on the same date as the annual property tax notice issued by your municipality, typically in early July each year with a discounted early-payment deadline (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Payment dates and penalties for late payment are set by the municipality under the Community Charter, SBC 2003, c. 26, or Vancouver Charter, SBC 1953, c. 55. Verify the exact due date and any discount or penalty dates on your property tax notice or with your local municipality.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
Does the School Tax apply to strata properties (condominiums)?

Yes, School Tax applies to strata lots (condominiums) governed by the Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43, in the same manner as other residential properties. Each individual strata lot is assessed and taxed separately, and the additional school tax may apply if the assessed value of the strata lot exceeds the provincially set thresholds (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Verify the assessed value of your strata lot and applicable rates on your annual property tax notice or with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
Where can I find the current School Tax rates and thresholds?

Current School Tax rates, including the additional school tax thresholds and rates, are published annually by the BC Ministry of Finance on the BC Government website at www.gov.bc.ca (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). Because rates and thresholds change each year, you should verify the current figures directly on the BC Government website or with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before relying on them for any transaction or planning.

Verify with: Provincial School Tax
