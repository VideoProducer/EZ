Property Value

BC Assessment Value

BC Assessment Value is an estimate of a property's market value as of July 1 of the prior year, established annually by BC Assessment under the Assessment Act, RSBC 1996, c. 20 — verify current legislative details with a BC lawyer or notary. Local governments use assessed values to calculate property tax levies under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1. BC Assessment uses mass appraisal methods to determine values across entire property classes rather than individual appraisals. Assessed value may differ from current market value, which reflects what a willing buyer and seller would agree upon in an open-market transaction at the time of sale.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is BC Assessment Value and how does it differ from market value?

BC Assessment Value is the estimated value of a property as of July 1 of the prior year, determined by BC Assessment (a Crown corporation) using mass appraisal methods. This assessed value is used by local governments and the province to calculate property taxes under the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, and related provincial legislation. Market value is the price a property would command in a present-day open-market sale and typically differs from assessed value because the assessment reflects a retrospective valuation date. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

What is the valuation date used for BC Assessment Value each year?

BC Assessment establishes the assessed value of a property as of July 1 of the year prior to the tax year. For example, the assessed value on your 2027 property tax notice reflects the estimated market value as of July 1, 2026 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). This retrospective valuation date is set by provincial legislation governing BC Assessment. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Is BC Assessment Value the same as the appraised value for a mortgage?

No. BC Assessment Value is a mass-appraisal estimate as of July 1 of the prior year, used for property tax purposes under provincial legislation. A mortgage appraisal is an individualized current-market valuation conducted by a qualified appraiser for lending purposes, typically reflecting present-day conditions and property-specific features. The two values often differ significantly. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
How is BC Assessment Value used to calculate my property taxes?

Local governments (municipalities, regional districts) and other taxing authorities apply their annual tax rates (mill rates) to your property's BC Assessment Value to determine the property taxes owing. For example, if your assessed value is $1,000,000 and the combined municipal and regional mill rate is 0.004 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), your base property tax would be $4,000 (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current), subject to any applicable BC Home Owner Grant or other credits. This process is governed by the Local Government Act, RSBC 2015, c. 1, and provincial taxation statutes. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Can I challenge or appeal my BC Assessment Value if I believe it is incorrect?

Yes. Property owners in British Columbia may file a Notice of Complaint with the independent Property Assessment Review Panel if they believe their assessed value is incorrect, typically by the complaint deadline of January 31 of the tax year (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). The complaint and appeal process is governed by the Assessment Act, RSBC 1996, c. 20, and administered by BC Assessment and the Property Assessment Appeal Board. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Does BC Assessment Value affect the amount of Property Transfer Tax I pay when I buy a property?

No. Property Transfer Tax (PTT) is calculated based on the fair market value of the property at the time of registration or deemed registration, not the BC Assessment Value. The Property Transfer Tax Act, RSBC 1996, c. 378, requires PTT to be paid on the greater of the purchase price or the fair market value at the time of transfer. BC Assessment Value, which reflects a July 1 retrospective valuation, is not used for PTT purposes. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Why does my BC Assessment Value sometimes increase even when local real estate prices are falling?

Because BC Assessment Value reflects market conditions as of July 1 of the prior year, not current conditions. If prices rose leading up to the July 1 valuation date but fell afterward, your assessed value may still show an increase on the following year's notice. Mass appraisal methods and the retrospective valuation date mean assessed values lag behind present-day market movements. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Does a higher BC Assessment Value automatically mean my property taxes will increase?

Not necessarily. Property tax amounts depend on both your assessed value and the tax rates (mill rates) set annually by your municipality and other taxing authorities. If all properties in your area increase proportionally, tax rates may be adjusted downward to generate the same total revenue, leaving your individual tax bill relatively stable. Conversely, if your property's assessed value rises faster than the average, your share of the tax burden may increase. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Can I use my BC Assessment Value to determine how much to list my home for sale?

BC Assessment Value is not a reliable indicator of current market listing price because it reflects market conditions as of July 1 of the prior year and is produced through mass appraisal. For pricing a property for sale, consult a licensed real estate professional, review recent comparable sales, or obtain a current independent appraisal. BC Assessment Value is intended solely for property taxation purposes. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.

Verify with: BC Assessment
Where can I find my property's official BC Assessment Value?

Your property's BC Assessment Value is published annually on your Assessment Notice, typically mailed in late December or early January. You may also view your assessed value online at www.bcassessment.ca by searching your address or legal description (as of 2026-07-27 — verify current). BC Assessment is a Crown corporation responsible for producing these valuations under provincial legislation. Verify current details with a BC lawyer, notary, or licensed tax professional before acting.
