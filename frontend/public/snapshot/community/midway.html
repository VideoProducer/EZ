About Midway

Midway is a small village located in the Boundary region of southeastern British Columbia, positioned at the eastern edge of the Similkameen Valley where it meets the Kettle River Valley. Situated along Highway 3, it lies approximately 600 kilometres east of Vancouver and roughly 35 kilometres west of Grand Forks, with the larger centre of Kelowna accessible to the northwest. The area is defined by the confluence of the Kettle River and Boundary Creek, and the surrounding landscape features rolling hills, open valleys, and the forested highlands of the Monashee and Midway Ranges. The region experiences a semi-arid continental climate, with warm, dry summers and cold winters that are somewhat moderated by valley geography, making it one of the milder inland communities in the Kootenay-Boundary area. Midway is a distinctly rural and small-scale community, with a population well under one thousand residents, lending it the character of a quiet, close-knit village. It holds a notable place in Canadian history as the western terminus of the Kettle Valley Railway, and this heritage is recognized locally and celebrated as part of the community's identity. The Trans Canada Trail passes through the area, drawing outdoor enthusiasts who appreciate the natural surroundings, and the village retains a relaxed, unhurried pace of life that is characteristic of small Boundary region communities. Despite its modest size, Midway offers a genuine sense of community rooted in its agricultural heritage and frontier history, with residents valuing the space, scenery, and slower rhythm that rural living affords. The local economy in the Midway area has historically been tied to agriculture, forestry, and resource industries, all of which continue to shape employment patterns in the region. Farming operations in the Kettle River Valley contribute to the rural character of the broader area, and forestry-related activities remain a background presence in the regional economy. Small-scale local services provide residents with basic day-to-day amenities, while a broader range of retail, healthcare, and professional services are accessed in nearby communities along the Highway 3 corridor. The area also benefits from growing interest in recreational tourism, particularly related to trail use and outdoor activities connected to the Kettle Valley Railway corridor.
