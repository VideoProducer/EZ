Heriot Bay, BC

About Heriot Bay

Heriot Bay is a small coastal community located on the northeastern shore of Quadra Island, which sits just off the eastern coast of Vancouver Island in British Columbia. The community is accessible via a short BC Ferries crossing from Campbell River, the nearest larger regional centre, which lies approximately 270 kilometres north of Vancouver. Quadra Island itself is characterized by a rugged and beautiful landscape, featuring forested hillsides, rocky shorelines, and sheltered coves along the Discovery Passage and Strait of Georgia. The area benefits from a mild West Coast maritime climate, with relatively moderate temperatures year-round, meaningful rainfall particularly in the winter months, and warm, dry summers that draw visitors and residents alike to its scenic waterways and natural surroundings. Heriot Bay is a quiet, rural community with a small permanent population, reflecting the intimate and close-knit character common to many of British Columbia's Gulf Islands communities. Life here moves at a relaxed pace, shaped by the rhythms of the ocean, the surrounding wilderness, and a strong sense of community among residents. The village serves as one of Quadra Island's more established gathering points, with a small boat harbour that has long been a hub of marine activity and a connection point to nearby Cortes Island via a second ferry route. The landscape and lifestyle attract those drawn to outdoor pursuits such as kayaking, fishing, hiking, and diving, with the surrounding waters recognized for their exceptional marine biodiversity and recreational opportunities. The local economy is rooted in a combination of tourism, commercial fishing, forestry, and small-scale marine services, all of which have historically shaped life on Quadra Island and continue to provide an employment base for residents. Seasonal tourism plays a meaningful role, bringing visitors to the area for its natural beauty, marine recreation, and relaxed island atmosphere. Essential community amenities are available locally, including general retail and services, while residents access a broader range of health, education, and commercial services by travelling to Campbell River via the ferry connection. The island also supports a small arts and crafts community, reflecting a creative culture that has developed alongside its natural and maritime heritage.
