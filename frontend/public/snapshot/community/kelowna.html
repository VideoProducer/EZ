About Kelowna

Kelowna is situated in the southern interior of British Columbia, positioned along the eastern shore of Okanagan Lake in the heart of the Okanagan Valley. It lies approximately 390 kilometres east of Vancouver and serves as the primary regional hub for the Central Okanagan area. The city is framed by the Okanagan Highlands to the east and rolling benchlands to the west, with Okanagan Lake stretching roughly 135 kilometres through the valley. The region is characterized by a semi-arid climate that delivers hot, dry summers and mild winters by British Columbia standards, with significantly more sunshine hours annually than most other parts of the province. This climate supports the surrounding agricultural land, particularly the orchards and vineyards for which the valley is internationally recognized. Kelowna is the largest city in the Okanagan region and one of the fastest-growing urban centres in British Columbia, with a population that has surpassed 150,000 residents within the city proper and a considerably larger figure across the broader metropolitan area. Despite its scale, the city retains a strong connection to its agricultural roots and outdoor recreation culture, offering residents access to lake activities, skiing, hiking, and cycling virtually year-round. The downtown waterfront along Okanagan Lake provides a vibrant focal point for community life, featuring public beaches, parks, and a lively arts and cultural scene. A mix of established neighbourhoods, newer suburban developments, and rural acreages on the surrounding hillsides gives the community a diverse residential character that reflects both its small-city charm and its growing urban sophistication. Kelowna's economy is broad and continues to diversify, with significant contributions from agriculture, tourism, technology, healthcare, education, retail trade, and construction. The wine industry anchors a thriving agritourism sector, drawing visitors from across Canada and internationally to the valley's many vineyards and farm operations. A growing technology sector has earned the region recognition as one of British Columbia's emerging innovation hubs, attracting skilled professionals and entrepreneurs. The city supports a comprehensive range of amenities, including post-secondary institutions, regional hospital services, a regional airport with direct flights to major Canadian cities, shopping centres, professional services, and a wide variety of recreational facilities. This combination of employment opportunity and quality of life continues to draw new residents from across the country.
