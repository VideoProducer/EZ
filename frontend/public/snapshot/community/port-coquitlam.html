About Port Coquitlam

Port Coquitlam is located in the northeastern portion of Metro Vancouver, situated along the confluence of the Pitt and Fraser Rivers in British Columbia's Lower Mainland. The city sits approximately 30 kilometres east of downtown Vancouver, sharing borders with Coquitlam to the west and northwest, and Pitt Meadows across the Pitt River to the east. The surrounding landscape is defined by river valleys, agricultural floodplains, and the dramatic mountain backdrops characteristic of the region. The Coast Mountains rise prominently to the north, while the Fraser River lowlands extend to the south and east. The climate is temperate and mild, with warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters typical of the Pacific Northwest, though the inland location means slightly more pronounced seasonal temperature variations than communities closer to the coast. Port Coquitlam, affectionately known locally as "PoCo," functions as a mid-sized suburban city with a population of approximately 65,000 residents. It carries a distinct community identity separate from its neighbouring municipalities, maintaining a small-city feel characterized by established residential neighbourhoods, accessible green spaces, and a network of recreational trails, including connections to the popular Traboulay PoCo Trail that encircles much of the city. The community has deep roots in BC's industrial and railway history, as the city developed significantly as a rail hub in the early twentieth century. Hyde Creek and the Coquitlam River wind through the city, adding natural character and supporting recreational opportunities for residents. Port Coquitlam's economy reflects its position within the broader Metro Vancouver region, with a diverse employment base spanning light industrial and manufacturing operations, warehousing and logistics, retail and service sectors, and small-to-medium enterprises. The city supports a range of everyday amenities including shopping areas, dining, recreation centres, parks, and healthcare and educational services, allowing residents to meet most daily needs locally. Many residents also commute to neighbouring Coquitlam, Burnaby, or Vancouver for employment, supported by regional transit connections including the West Coast Express commuter rail service that links Port Coquitlam directly to downtown Vancouver. The city continues to attract businesses suited to its accessible transportation corridors and relatively affordable commercial and industrial land within the Metro Vancouver context.
