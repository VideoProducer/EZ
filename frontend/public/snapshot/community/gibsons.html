About Gibsons

Gibsons is a small coastal town located at the southeastern tip of the Sunshine Coast in British Columbia, positioned along the northern shore of Howe Sound. It sits approximately 50 kilometres northwest of Vancouver as the crow flies, though access by land requires travelling north through Squamish and around Howe Sound, making the BC Ferries route from Horseshoe Bay in West Vancouver the primary and most practical connection to the Lower Mainland. The town is framed by the waters of Howe Sound to the south and east, with the forested slopes of the Coast Mountains rising behind it to the north. The climate is among the mildest on the BC coast, benefiting from a rain shadow effect that delivers more sunshine and less precipitation than many surrounding areas, with warm summers, mild winters, and relatively low snowfall. Gibsons is a small town with a population of approximately 5,000 residents, though the broader surrounding area supports a larger community. It carries a distinctly relaxed, arts-friendly coastal character that blends a working waterfront heritage with a vibrant creative and retirement community. The historic lower town, known as Gibsons Landing, is a charming harbour-front district recognized by many Canadians as the filming location of the long-running television series The Beachcombers, which gave the area national recognition over several decades. The community attracts those drawn to a quieter pace of life without full rural isolation, offering walkable neighbourhoods, a scenic marina, and a strong sense of local identity rooted in both its maritime history and its natural surroundings. The local economy draws from a mix of tourism, marine services, retail, construction, and the arts. Commercial fishing and boat-related industries have historically anchored the waterfront economy, and that maritime tradition remains part of the community's identity today. Tourism plays a meaningful role, with visitors drawn to the natural scenery, outdoor recreation opportunities, and the appeal of small-town coastal living. Gibsons also serves as a regional service centre for surrounding Sunshine Coast communities, supporting a range of general amenities including shopping, dining, healthcare services, and educational facilities. A growing population of remote workers and retirees has contributed to the local economy in recent years, broadening the employment and commercial base.
