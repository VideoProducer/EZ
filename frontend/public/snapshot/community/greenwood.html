About Greenwood

Greenwood is situated in the Boundary Country of southeastern British Columbia, nestled in the Kettle River Valley within the broader Kootenay region. The city lies approximately 100 kilometres west of Trail and around 600 kilometres east of Vancouver, making it relatively remote from major urban centres. The surrounding landscape is defined by rolling forested hills, the Kettle River, and the rugged terrain of the Monashee Mountains, which provide a dramatic and scenic backdrop to the valley. The climate is a semi-arid continental variety, characterized by warm, dry summers and cold winters with moderate snowfall, offering four distinct seasons that appeal to those who enjoy outdoor recreation year-round. Greenwood holds the distinction of being the smallest city by population in Canada, a title that speaks to its intimate, close-knit character. With a population of only a few hundred residents, the community offers a profoundly rural lifestyle with a strong sense of history and heritage. The city's roots run deep in the mining era of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, and much of its original architecture has been preserved, lending Greenwood a charming and authentic historic atmosphere. The pace of life is quiet and unhurried, attracting those drawn to wide open spaces, natural surroundings, and a genuine small-town community spirit. Heritage buildings, a modest downtown core, and proximity to crown land and wilderness areas shape daily life and recreation for residents. The economy of Greenwood and the surrounding Boundary region has historically been tied to mining and forestry, industries that continue to play a role in the regional employment base. Ranching and agriculture also contribute to the local economic fabric, taking advantage of the valley's relatively fertile land and open terrain. While Greenwood itself offers only basic amenities given its small size, residents have access to a broader range of services in nearby communities such as Grand Forks, located roughly 30 kilometres to the west, which provides shopping, medical services, schools, and other essential resources. Outdoor recreation, including hiking, fishing, hunting, and snowmobiling, supports a modest tourism sector and reflects the lifestyle that draws many people to this part of the province.
