About Sicamous

Sicamous is a small district municipality located in the Shuswap region of British Columbia's Southern Interior, positioned at the confluence of Shuswap Lake and Mara Lake along the Trans-Canada Highway corridor. Situated roughly 70 kilometres east of Salmon Arm and approximately 110 kilometres west of Revelstoke, the community occupies a strategic position between the Thompson Valley and the Columbia Mountains. The surrounding landscape is defined by expansive lake systems, forested mountain slopes, and the Eagle River valley, which runs southward toward the Monashee Mountains. Sicamous experiences a semi-arid continental climate tempered by its lakeside setting, bringing warm, dry summers and moderately cold winters with reliable snowfall in the surrounding highlands. With a population of approximately 3,000 residents, Sicamous carries the character of a small lakeside town with a relaxed, outdoor-oriented lifestyle. The community's location on the narrows between Shuswap Lake and Mara Lake has long shaped its identity as a hub for boating and water recreation, earning it the informal designation as the "Houseboat Capital of Canada." The downtown core retains a modest small-town feel, and the surrounding area offers access to hiking, snowmobiling, skiing, and fishing throughout the seasons. The community draws both year-round residents and seasonal visitors, giving it a blend of permanent neighbourhood character and recreational resort energy. The local economy is driven by several key pillars, including tourism and recreation, which generate significant seasonal activity, along with transportation and logistics supported by the community's position on a major highway and rail corridor. Forestry and related industries have historically contributed to the employment base in the broader region. Retail, hospitality, and service industries cater to both residents and the substantial volume of travellers and vacationers passing through or staying seasonally. Sicamous provides access to essential everyday amenities including grocery and general retail services, medical and healthcare facilities, recreational infrastructure, and community services sufficient for day-to-day living. Regional centres such as Salmon Arm and Kamloops offer expanded amenities within reasonable driving distance.
