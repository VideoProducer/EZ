About Falkland

Falkland is a small unincorporated community located in the Shuswap-Okanagan region of British Columbia's southern interior, situated along Highway 97A between the cities of Kamloops to the northwest and Vernon to the southeast. The community rests within the gentle valley of Salmon River, a tributary that winds through the surrounding hills and agricultural lowlands. The landscape is characterized by rolling benchlands, open range country, and forested hillsides typical of the interior plateau, with the elevated terrain of the Highland Valley and surrounding uplands providing a scenic backdrop. Falkland experiences a semi-arid interior climate, with warm, dry summers and cool winters that bring moderate snowfall, making it a four-season community well suited to the rural lifestyle of the region. Falkland is a distinctly rural community with a small, close-knit population numbering in the hundreds, reflecting the character of a quiet agricultural village rather than an urban or suburban centre. The community has a long history rooted in ranching and farming, and that heritage remains evident in the working landscapes and traditional character of the area. Falkland is perhaps best known for its annual Stompin' Tom Days celebration, a community event that draws visitors from across the region and reflects the town's strong sense of local pride and rural tradition. Life in Falkland moves at an unhurried pace, with residents enjoying access to the surrounding natural environment for outdoor recreation including hiking, horseback riding, and fishing along the Salmon River corridor. The local economy has historically been anchored in agriculture, particularly cattle ranching and mixed farming, which continue to define the land use and employment patterns of the surrounding area. Forestry and related resource industries also contribute to the regional employment base. While Falkland itself offers a modest range of everyday amenities including general retail, fuel services, and basic community facilities, residents typically travel to Vernon or Kamloops for a broader selection of shopping, healthcare, and professional services. Both centres are within comfortable driving distance, allowing residents to access full urban amenities while enjoying the quieter pace of rural living. The community's location along a well-traveled interior highway corridor also supports a modest degree of through traffic and associated small-scale commercial activity.
