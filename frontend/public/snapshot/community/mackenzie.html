About Mackenzie

Mackenzie is a small town located in the Northern Interior of British Columbia, situated approximately 183 kilometres south of Fort St. John and roughly 1,200 kilometres northeast of Vancouver. It lies at the southern end of Williston Lake, the largest reservoir in British Columbia and one of the largest in North America by surface area, created by the construction of the W.A.C. Bennett Dam on the Peace River. The town is surrounded by the rugged landscape of the Rocky Mountain Foothills and the Hart Ranges of the Rocky Mountains, offering dramatic terrain and boreal forest in every direction. Mackenzie experiences a subarctic continental climate, characterized by cold, snowy winters and warm, relatively short summers, with long daylight hours during the summer months that are a notable feature of life at this latitude. Mackenzie is a small, close-knit resource town with a population of roughly 3,000 to 4,000 residents, giving it a strong community character defined by its relative remoteness and its connection to the surrounding natural environment. The town has a rural and somewhat remote feel, with residents who tend to embrace outdoor recreation, including boating on Williston Lake, hiking, snowmobiling, fishing, and hunting. Despite its modest size, Mackenzie supports a range of community facilities and services that reflect a functional and self-sufficient northern town. The community has a proud heritage tied to its origins as a planned resource town, established in the 1960s to support the forestry and hydroelectric development of the region, and this history remains a meaningful part of its identity. The economy of Mackenzie has historically been driven by the forestry industry, which remains a central part of the local employment base through logging, sawmilling, and related operations. The construction and ongoing operation of major hydroelectric infrastructure in the region, including projects associated with the Peace River corridor, has also contributed significantly to local economic activity. Mining and energy sector developments in the broader Northern BC region provide additional employment opportunities for residents. General amenities in Mackenzie include grocery and retail services, medical facilities, recreational infrastructure, and community services appropriate to a small northern town, making day-to-day life manageable without requiring frequent travel to larger centres.
