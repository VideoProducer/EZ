About Masset

Masset is located on the northern tip of Graham Island, the largest island in the Haida Gwaii archipelago, situated off the northwest coast of British Columbia. The community lies approximately 1,100 kilometres northwest of Vancouver and is accessible primarily by ferry from Prince Rupert or by air service connecting to the mainland. The landscape surrounding Masset is shaped by the Pacific Ocean, with the community sitting along Masset Inlet and fronting Dixon Entrance to the north. The area features a rugged coastal environment of old-growth forests, wetlands, and sandy beaches, with the nearby Naikoon Provincial Park offering expansive natural scenery. The climate is temperate oceanic, characterized by mild, wet winters and cool summers, with significant rainfall throughout the year and relatively rare frost or snowfall at sea level. Masset is a small, remote community with a population of approximately 900 to 1,000 residents, making it one of the larger communities on Haida Gwaii. It sits adjacent to Old Massett, a neighbouring Haida First Nation village, and together the two communities form a closely connected social fabric rooted in both Indigenous heritage and a broader settler history. The area carries deep cultural significance as part of the traditional territory of the Haida people, whose art, language, and governance have shaped the identity of the region for thousands of years. The community has a small-town, close-knit character where residents often know one another and where a strong sense of place and connection to the natural environment is central to everyday life. A former Canadian Forces Base contributes to the local built environment and history. The economy of Masset and the broader northern Haida Gwaii area has historically been anchored in commercial fishing, forestry, and government services. Fishing remains a cultural and economic cornerstone, with both commercial operations and sport fishing drawing activity to the area. Tourism related to wildlife viewing, sport fishing, hiking, and cultural experiences contributes meaningfully to the local economy, particularly during summer months. Government, health, and social services provide stable public sector employment for residents across the region. The community offers essential amenities including grocery and general retail options, health care services, and community recreation facilities, though residents occasionally travel to Prince Rupert or larger centres for more specialized goods and services.
