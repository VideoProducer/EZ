About Telegraph Creek

Telegraph Creek is a small, remote community located in northwestern British Columbia, situated along the banks of the Stikine River in the Stikine Country region. It lies approximately 115 kilometres south of the Yukon border and roughly 1,000 kilometres northwest of Prince George, the nearest major regional hub. The community is nestled within a dramatic landscape shaped by the Stikine River canyon, surrounding mountain ranges, and expansive wilderness. The area experiences a subarctic to continental climate, characterized by cold winters with significant snowfall and warm, relatively dry summers. The Stikine River, one of the last major undammed river systems in North America, is among the most defining geographic features of the region. Telegraph Creek is a deeply rural and remote community with a very small population, historically numbering only in the hundreds of residents. It is one of the oldest non-Indigenous settlements in northern BC and holds considerable historical significance as a former supply hub during the Klondike Gold Rush era and as part of the Collins Overland Telegraph project from which the community takes its name. The area is home to a significant Indigenous population, particularly members of the Tahltan Nation, whose cultural presence and traditions are woven into the fabric of daily life. The community exudes a tight-knit, frontier character shaped by its isolation, rich heritage, and close relationship with the surrounding natural environment. The economy of Telegraph Creek and the broader Stikine region is driven primarily by resource industries, including mining exploration and activity in the surrounding mineral-rich terrain, as well as trapping, hunting, and fishing. Tourism, particularly wilderness adventure tourism, plays a growing role given the region's extraordinary natural landscapes and world-class river systems. Government services and administration associated with the Tahltan Nation also represent a significant component of the local employment base. Basic amenities are available within the community to support daily living needs, though residents regularly travel to larger centres such as Dease Lake for additional services and supplies. The remoteness of Telegraph Creek means that self-sufficiency and a strong community spirit are important aspects of life here.
