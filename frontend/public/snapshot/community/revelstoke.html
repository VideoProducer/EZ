About Revelstoke

Revelstoke is located in the Columbia Mountains of British Columbia's Southern Interior, positioned along the Trans-Canada Highway where it meets the confluence of the Columbia and Illecillewaet rivers. The community sits roughly 565 kilometres east of Vancouver and approximately 150 kilometres west of Golden, placing it within a region defined by dramatic mountain terrain, broad river valleys, and glacier-fed waterways. The Selkirk and Monashee mountain ranges frame the area, and Revelstoke Lake — a reservoir formed by the Columbia River — stretches to the south of town. The climate brings heavy snowfall in winter, which is a defining characteristic of the region's identity, while summers are warm and relatively dry, offering a distinct seasonal contrast throughout the year. Revelstoke is a small mountain town with a population of approximately 8,500 to 9,000 residents, embodying a close-knit, outdoor-oriented community character. Despite its modest size, the town carries a strong sense of place, with a compact and walkable downtown core that reflects its railway heritage and early twentieth-century history. Designated heritage buildings and a historic streetscape give the town centre a distinctive character. The surrounding wilderness supports a lifestyle deeply connected to the outdoors, with skiing, hiking, backcountry adventure, and water recreation all central to daily life for many residents. The community balances a small-town atmosphere with a growing international profile, drawing visitors and newcomers attracted by both its natural setting and its recreational amenities. The economy of Revelstoke has historically been rooted in forestry, hydroelectric power generation, and rail transportation, all of which continue to play a role in the local employment base. In recent decades, tourism and mountain recreation have emerged as increasingly significant economic drivers, with a major ski resort operating nearby and a growing hospitality and service sector supporting visitor activity year-round. The community offers a reasonable range of amenities for a town of its size, including retail services, dining, healthcare facilities, recreational infrastructure, and local governance services. Transportation links via the Trans-Canada Highway and rail corridor support both commerce and connectivity to larger regional centres. The local economy reflects a blend of resource industry tradition and a newer, recreation-driven growth trajectory that continues to shape the character of the community.
