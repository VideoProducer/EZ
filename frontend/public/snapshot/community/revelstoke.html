About Revelstoke

Revelstoke is located in the Columbia Mountains of British Columbia's Southern Interior, positioned along the Trans-Canada Highway where it meets the confluence of the Columbia and Illecillewaet rivers. The community sits roughly 565 kilometres east of Vancouver and approximately 150 kilometres west of Golden, placing it within a region defined by dramatic mountain terrain, broad river valleys, and glacier-fed waterways. The Selkirk and Monashee mountain ranges frame the area, and Revelstoke Lake — a reservoir formed by the Columbia River — stretches to the south of town. The climate brings heavy snowfall in winter, which is a defining characteristic of the region's identity, while summers are warm and relatively dry, offering a distinct seasonal contrast throughout the year. Revelstoke is a small mountain town with a population of approximately 8,500 to 9,000 residents, embodying a close-knit, outdoor-oriented community character. Despite its modest size, the town carries a strong sense of place, with a compact and walkable downtown core that reflects its railway heritage and early twentieth-century history. Designated heritage buildings and a historic streetscape give the town centre a distinctive character. The surrounding wilderness supports a lifestyle deeply connected to the outdoors, with skiing, hiking, backcountry adventure, and water recreation all central to daily life for many residents. The community balances a small-town atmosphere with a growing international profile, drawing visitors and newcomers attracted by both its natural setting and its recreational amenities. The economy of Revelstoke has historically been rooted in forestry, hydroelectric power generation, and rail transportation, all of which continue to play a role in the local employment base. In recent decades, tourism and mountain recreation have emerged as increasingly significant economic drivers, with a major ski resort operating nearby and a growing hospitality and service sector supporting visitor activity year-round. The community offers a reasonable range of amenities for a town of its size, including retail services, dining, healthcare facilities, recreational infrastructure, and local governance services. Transportation links via the Trans-Canada Highway and rail corridor support both commerce and connectivity to larger regional centres. The local economy reflects a blend of resource industry tradition and a newer, recreation-driven growth trajectory that continues to shape the character of the community. ☀️ Weather & Climate in Revelstoke

Revelstoke sits in a dramatic mountain valley at the confluence of the Columbia and Illecillewaet rivers, giving it a humid continental climate with strong alpine and maritime influences. Winters are long, snowy, and relatively mild for the interior, with valley temperatures typically ranging from around -15°C to 0°C, though cold snaps can push temperatures lower during Arctic outflow events. The community is renowned for receiving some of the deepest snowpacks in Canada, with surrounding mountain terrain regularly accumulating several metres of snow annually, making it a celebrated destination for backcountry and resort skiing. Summers in Revelstoke are warm and pleasant, with valley temperatures generally ranging from roughly 10°C to 28°C, accompanied by moderate precipitation that keeps the landscape noticeably greener than many Southern Interior communities. Wildfire smoke from regional fires can periodically affect air quality during July and August. The surrounding mountains create distinct microclimates, and valley fog is a common feature during shoulder seasons. Spring and fall bring transitional precipitation with a mix of rain and snow, and the region's positioning between mountain ranges means weather systems can shift quickly throughout the year.
