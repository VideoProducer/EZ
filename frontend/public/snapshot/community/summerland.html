About Summerland

Summerland is a district municipality located on the western shore of Okanagan Lake in the South Okanagan region of British Columbia. It sits approximately 15 kilometres north of Penticton, the nearest regional hub, and roughly 400 kilometres east of Vancouver. The community is nestled between the lake to the east and the rolling benchlands and mountains of the Okanagan Highlands to the west, creating a landscape of considerable natural beauty. The surrounding terrain features forested hillsides, fruit-bearing benches, and open vineyard country that has come to define the regional identity. Summerland benefits from the Okanagan's semi-arid climate, characterized by hot, dry summers, mild springs and falls, and relatively modest winters, making it one of the more temperate communities in the province. Summerland is a small town with a population of approximately 13,000 residents, offering a relaxed, community-oriented atmosphere that sits comfortably between rural and suburban in character. The community has a well-established heritage rooted in fruit growing and early agricultural settlement, and that history remains visible in its heritage commercial district, heritage rail excursion, and its long-standing agricultural character. Life in Summerland tends to move at a measured pace, with residents enjoying easy access to Okanagan Lake for boating, swimming, and recreation, as well as the many hiking and cycling trails that wind through the surrounding hills. The community's compact size means neighbours are often familiar with one another, contributing to a strong sense of local identity and civic pride. The economy of Summerland has traditionally been anchored in agriculture, most notably tender tree fruits such as peaches, cherries, and apples, along with a growing viticulture and winery sector that has expanded significantly in recent decades. The broader economy is supported by tourism, which draws visitors to the area's wineries, lakefront, and outdoor recreational opportunities throughout the year. Local government and public services form a steady part of the employment base, and residents also commute to Penticton for work and access to a wider range of regional amenities. Within Summerland itself, residents have access to essential services including grocery retail, medical and dental care, parks, recreation facilities, and a variety of local shops and dining establishments.
