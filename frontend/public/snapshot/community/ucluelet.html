About Ucluelet

Ucluelet is a small coastal community located on the western edge of Vancouver Island, British Columbia, positioned along the Pacific Rim at the southern tip of Clayoquot Sound. The town sits approximately 310 kilometres northwest of Victoria and roughly 200 kilometres west of Nanaimo, making it one of the more remote communities on Vancouver Island accessible by road. The surrounding landscape is dramatic and rugged, defined by the open Pacific Ocean, ancient temperate rainforest, rocky headlands, sheltered inlets, and the Broken Group Islands scattered across Barkley Sound. Ucluelet shares the peninsula with the neighbouring community of Tofino to the north, connected by Pacific Rim Highway. The climate is mild but notably wet, with heavy annual rainfall that sustains the dense old-growth forest ecosystems characteristic of the region. Ucluelet is a small, close-knit town with a population of approximately 1,700 residents, carrying a distinctly rural and remote character while maintaining a strong sense of community identity. Life here is deeply shaped by the natural environment, with outdoor recreation, wildlife viewing, and ocean access woven into everyday living. The area is well known for world-class surfing, whale watching, sport fishing, and hiking along the Wild Pacific Trail, a celebrated oceanfront trail that winds along dramatic coastal bluffs. Ucluelet has invested in cultural and heritage recognition connected to its Indigenous and maritime history, and the community has experienced growth in tourism-related development while retaining its small-town atmosphere. The harbour remains a central feature of the community, reflecting the town's enduring connection to the sea. The local economy has traditionally been anchored in commercial fishing and forestry, industries that continue to play a meaningful role in the community's employment base. Tourism has grown significantly over recent decades, driven by the area's exceptional natural attractions, and now represents a major economic pillar supporting accommodation, hospitality, and outdoor recreation services. Retail and professional services are available within the community to meet everyday needs, and residents have access to essential health and emergency services locally, with more specialized services accessible in larger centres further along the island. The arts and creative economy have also found a place in Ucluelet, reflecting a community that values both its working traditions and its cultural development.
