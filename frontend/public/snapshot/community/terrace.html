Terrace, BC

About Terrace

Terrace is situated in the Skeena Country of northwestern British Columbia, approximately 1,450 kilometres north of Vancouver and roughly 90 kilometres east of Prince Rupert on the coast. The community occupies a broad valley carved by the Skeena River, one of BC's most significant salmon-bearing rivers, and is surrounded by dramatic mountain ranges that rise steeply on all sides. The nearby Coast Mountains contribute to a notably wet climate by interior BC standards, with moderate temperatures, substantial rainfall in the lower elevations, and reliable snowfall that accumulates in the surrounding peaks each winter. The landscape is defined by dense coniferous forest, glacier-capped ridges, and the powerful river systems that wind through the valley floor. Terrace functions as the regional hub for a wide area of northwestern BC, serving surrounding smaller communities and First Nations villages across the Skeena and Nass valleys. With a population of approximately 12,000 to 13,000 people, it carries the character of a mid-sized northern town — self-sufficient in most respects, with a strong sense of community identity tied to the land, the river, and the outdoor lifestyle that the region naturally encourages. Residents enjoy access to world-class fishing, hiking, skiing, and wildlife viewing virtually at their doorstep, and the area holds deep cultural significance for the Tsimshian and Gitxsan peoples, whose heritage is woven into the identity of the broader region. The community supports a modest downtown core alongside established residential neighbourhoods spread across the valley. The economy of Terrace has traditionally been anchored in forestry, which remains a foundational industry for the surrounding region. Mining and mineral exploration have grown in relevance given the area's considerable resource potential, and the community also serves as an administrative and service centre for regional government, health, and social services. The Terrace-Kitimat Airport connects the region to larger urban centres, supporting both business travel and the movement of goods and workers. Retail, hospitality, and trades services are well represented given Terrace's role as a supply and service hub for a large geographic catchment area. The nearby port infrastructure at Prince Rupert and industrial development activity in Kitimat further influence regional employment and economic patterns. General amenities including healthcare facilities, education options, recreational infrastructure, and retail services are available within the community to support day-to-day living. ☀️ Weather & Climate in Terrace

Terrace sits in a temperate rainforest climate shaped by its position in the Skeena River valley, where Pacific moisture streams inland through coastal mountain gaps. Winters are notably mild for Northern BC, with temperatures typically ranging from around -5°C to +3°C, though cold Arctic outflow events can periodically push readings well below -15°C. Snowfall is substantial, often exceeding 200 centimetres seasonally, and the region also receives significant winter rainfall at lower elevations. Freezing rain can occur when milder Pacific air overrides colder valley air, creating hazardous conditions. Summers in Terrace are warm and moderately wet, with average temperatures generally ranging from 12°C to 24°C, occasionally climbing higher during inland heat events. The valley corridor funnels consistent winds off the Coast Mountains, influencing local conditions considerably. Precipitation remains present through summer, though drier stretches do occur and wildfire smoke from interior BC fires can periodically affect air quality. Terrace is notably one of the wettest communities in Northern BC, with annual precipitation among the highest in the province outside of coastal areas, and its lush surrounding landscape reflects that distinction.
