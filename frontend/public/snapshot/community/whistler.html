About Whistler

Whistler is located in the Sea-to-Sky corridor of southwestern British Columbia, approximately 125 kilometres north of Vancouver along Highway 99. Nestled between the Coast Mountains, the resort municipality sits at an elevation of roughly 670 metres and is surrounded by dramatic alpine terrain, including Whistler Mountain and Blackcomb Mountain, which together form one of the largest ski areas in North America. The area is also characterized by a chain of interconnected lakes — most notably Alta Lake, Alpha Lake, and Green Lake — as well as the Fitzsimmons and Cheakamus river systems. The climate features cool, snowy winters with significant snowfall accumulation at higher elevations and mild, pleasant summers, making the area a year-round destination for outdoor recreation. Whistler is a resort municipality with a resident population of approximately 12,000 to 14,000 people, though that number swells considerably with seasonal workers and visitors throughout the year. Despite its international profile, the community retains a distinct village character, centered on the pedestrian-friendly Whistler Village, which offers a lively mix of cultural events, festivals, and public gathering spaces. The broader municipality encompasses several distinct neighbourhoods and surrounding areas, each with its own residential character ranging from ski-in, ski-out developments to quieter lakeside settings. Whistler hosted events during the 2010 Winter Olympics and Paralympic Games, a distinction that shaped much of its world-class infrastructure and international identity. The economy of Whistler is driven predominantly by tourism and hospitality, with skiing, snowboarding, mountain biking, and outdoor adventure activities forming the foundation of year-round visitor activity. A significant portion of the local workforce is employed in accommodation, food and beverage services, recreation, and retail, with many residents also working in professional services, construction, and property management. The municipality supports a well-developed amenity base that includes grocery and specialty retail, arts and cultural facilities, fitness and wellness services, recreational infrastructure, and a range of dining and entertainment options. Healthcare and educational services are available within the community, with more specialized services accessible in Squamish and the greater Vancouver area. The blend of world-class recreation and a tight-knit community culture makes Whistler a distinctive place to live and work within British Columbia's Sea-to-Sky region.
