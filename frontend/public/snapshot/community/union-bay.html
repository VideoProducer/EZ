About Union Bay

Union Bay is a small unincorporated community located on the eastern coast of Vancouver Island, in the Comox Valley area of British Columbia. Situated along the shores of Baynes Sound, it lies approximately 15 kilometres south of the city of Courtenay and roughly 200 kilometres north of Victoria. The community enjoys a scenic coastal setting, with views across Baynes Sound toward Denman Island and the broader Strait of Georgia. The surrounding landscape is characterized by a mix of oceanfront, forested hillsides, and agricultural land. Like much of the east coast of Vancouver Island, Union Bay benefits from a mild, temperate climate with relatively low precipitation compared to the island's western side, warm summers, and gentle winters that see little to no snowfall. Union Bay is a small, rural community with a quiet, village-like character and a population numbering only in the hundreds. Its pace of life is relaxed and intimate, drawing residents who appreciate a close connection to the natural environment and a slower rhythm than what is found in nearby urban centres. The community has a notable coal mining heritage, having served as a significant coal shipping port in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and this history continues to shape its local identity. Heritage buildings and waterfront areas reflect this past, adding a sense of historical depth to what is otherwise a modest and unhurried coastal setting. Residents often look to Courtenay and Comox for a broader range of services and amenities while enjoying the tranquility that Union Bay's rural character provides. The local economy has evolved considerably from its coal-era roots, and today the area's employment base is supported by a combination of small-scale commerce, agriculture, trades, and service industries. Many working residents commute to the larger Comox Valley urban centres for employment opportunities in healthcare, retail, education, and public services. The surrounding region supports farming operations, and the coastal location sustains ongoing interest in marine-related activities. Within the community and its immediate surroundings, residents have access to basic local amenities, while the nearby Comox Valley provides a more comprehensive range of shopping, healthcare, recreational facilities, and community services. Outdoor recreation plays a significant role in daily life, with opportunities for boating, fishing, hiking, and wildlife viewing readily accessible from the community's doorstep.
