About Roberts Creek

Roberts Creek is a small coastal community located on the Sunshine Coast of British Columbia, situated along the eastern shoreline of the Strait of Georgia on the Sechelt Peninsula. It lies between the communities of Gibsons to the south and Sechelt to the north, accessible from the Lower Mainland via BC Ferries from Horseshoe Bay to Langdale, a journey that places Roberts Creek roughly an hour and a half from Vancouver when travel and ferry time are combined. The area is characterized by a rugged yet picturesque natural setting, with the coastal mountains forming a dramatic backdrop, the ocean providing sweeping western views, and a landscape of second-growth forest, small streams, and pockets of agricultural land. The climate is among the mildest in British Columbia, benefiting from a rain shadow effect that brings warmer summers and relatively moderate winters compared to many other parts of the province. Roberts Creek has the feel of a quiet, rural village with a strong sense of community identity and a notably artistic, free-spirited character. The permanent population is small, numbering in the low thousands, and the pace of life reflects the area's rural surroundings and close connection to the natural environment. The community is known for its commitment to local culture, creative expression, and environmental stewardship, and it has long attracted artists, craftspeople, and those seeking a more intentional way of life. A community hall serves as a social hub, and the area's heritage as an early settler community on the Sunshine Coast adds depth to its local identity. The foreshore, with its public pier and expansive ocean views, is a beloved gathering point for residents and visitors alike. The economy of Roberts Creek and the broader Sunshine Coast region draws from a diverse mix of industries, including forestry, construction trades, marine-related activities, and a growing base of remote workers who have relocated from urban centres. Small-scale agriculture, artisan production, and tourism also contribute to local economic activity. The community is served by a modest collection of local amenities including small retail and service businesses, community gathering spaces, and recreational facilities, while a broader range of services, healthcare, and commercial options are available in the nearby regional centre of Sechelt. The area's natural setting supports outdoor recreation opportunities year-round, from hiking and kayaking to cycling and beach access, which plays an increasingly important role in drawing residents and visitors to the region.
