About Langley Township

Langley Township is located in the eastern portion of Metro Vancouver, in the Lower Mainland of British Columbia. It sits approximately 50 kilometres southeast of downtown Vancouver, bordered by Surrey to the west, Abbotsford to the east, and the United States border to the south. The Fraser River forms the township's northern boundary, and the Nicomekl and Salmon rivers wind through portions of the municipality. Much of the land is characterized by broad, fertile agricultural plains protected under the Agricultural Land Reserve, giving the area a distinctly rural feel in many sectors despite its proximity to the city. The climate is mild and temperate, with warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters, consistent with the broader Lower Mainland region. Langley Township is a large, diverse municipality that blends suburban neighbourhoods, rural acreages, and small community centres across its considerable land base. With a population approaching 160,000 residents, it is one of the more populous municipalities in Metro Vancouver, yet much of its character remains rooted in an open, spacious way of life. The township is home to equestrian properties, hobby farms, and country estates alongside growing residential subdivisions and established family neighbourhoods. The area has a strong agricultural heritage that remains visible in its working farms, farm markets, and rural roads, and a number of heritage sites and community landmarks contribute to a sense of local identity and history. Residents generally enjoy a quieter, more spread-out lifestyle compared to the denser urban centres to the west. The economy of Langley Township is broad and varied, reflecting its mixed urban-rural character. Agriculture remains a foundational industry, with berry farming, dairy operations, equestrian facilities, and market gardening all playing significant roles across the municipality. The township also supports a robust commercial and light industrial sector, with business parks, manufacturing operations, and distribution facilities concentrated along major transportation corridors. Retail and service industries are well established in the area's commercial nodes, providing residents with access to everyday amenities including grocery, dining, medical services, recreation facilities, and community programs without the need to travel to Vancouver. Employment opportunities span a wide range of sectors, making the township increasingly self-sufficient as a regional centre.
