About North Vancouver District

North Vancouver District is a large municipal district located on the north shore of Burrard Inlet, directly across from the City of Vancouver in the Greater Vancouver region of British Columbia. It surrounds but is distinct from the City of North Vancouver, and together these two municipalities form what is commonly referred to as North Vancouver. The district sits at the base of the Coast Mountains, giving it a dramatic landscape defined by steep forested slopes, glacier-fed rivers, and deep valleys. Notable geographic features include Grouse Mountain, Lynn Canyon, and the Seymour and Capilano river systems, as well as several reservoirs that serve as primary water sources for the region. The district's western boundary meets West Vancouver, while its eastern edge borders Coquitlam and Port Moody. The climate is mild and temperate, with warm, dry summers and wet winters, and the higher elevations receive substantial snowfall, supporting winter recreation throughout the colder months. North Vancouver District is predominantly suburban in character, with a mix of established residential neighbourhoods, semi-rural pockets, and wilderness areas at its outer edges. The population sits in the range of approximately 90,000 to 100,000 residents, making it one of the more populous municipalities on the North Shore. Community life here is strongly shaped by outdoor recreation and a deep connection to the natural environment, with hiking, mountain biking, skiing, and kayaking all readily accessible. Despite its proximity to downtown Vancouver, accessible via the Lions Gate Bridge and the Second Narrows Bridge, the district maintains a distinct community identity with a quieter, nature-oriented pace of life. Heritage industrial sites along the waterfront, cultural facilities, and community parks contribute to the area's well-rounded character. The economy of North Vancouver District has historically been rooted in forestry, shipping, and marine industries, with the working waterfront along Burrard Inlet remaining an important employment hub. Over time, the economy has diversified to include construction, technology, public administration, and professional services. The district benefits from strong regional connectivity and access to Vancouver's broader employment market, with many residents commuting to the city for work. Locally, residents are served by a range of retail, dining, healthcare, and recreational amenities concentrated in several community centres and commercial nodes throughout the municipality. Outdoor tourism also plays a growing role in the local economy, driven by the district's world-class mountain and wilderness attractions.
