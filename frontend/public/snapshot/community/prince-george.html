Prince George, BC

About Prince George

Prince George is situated in the geographic heart of British Columbia, at the confluence of the Fraser and Nechako Rivers in the province's central interior. It lies approximately 780 kilometres northeast of Vancouver and serves as the primary regional hub for northern BC, making it a natural crossroads between the coast, the Peace Country, and the far north. The surrounding landscape is defined by boreal forest, rolling hills, and river valleys, with the Rocky Mountains visible to the east. The climate is continental, characterized by cold, snowy winters and warm, relatively dry summers, with the region experiencing four distinct seasons throughout the year. Prince George is the largest city in northern British Columbia, with a population of approximately 70,000 to 75,000 people, giving it a decidedly urban character compared to most communities in the region. The city functions as a full-service centre, offering the amenities and infrastructure typically associated with larger urban environments while retaining a strong sense of community identity rooted in its history as a fur trading post and later a forestry and railway hub. Its downtown core, cultural institutions, parks system, and established residential neighbourhoods reflect decades of steady development, and the city supports a diverse and active population drawn from across the province and beyond. The economy of Prince George has historically been anchored in the forestry sector, including lumber production, pulp and paper manufacturing, and related resource industries, which continue to play a significant role in the regional employment base. In recent decades, the city has diversified considerably, with growth in post-secondary education, health care services, retail trade, construction, and government administration. As the administrative and commercial centre of northern BC, Prince George hosts a wide range of regional services, including hospitals, post-secondary institutions, government offices, and a broad selection of retail and professional services that draw residents from communities across a vast surrounding area. Transportation infrastructure is well developed, with the city served by a regional airport, major highway corridors, and rail connections that support both industry and passenger travel.
