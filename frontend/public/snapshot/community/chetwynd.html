Chetwynd, BC

About Chetwynd

Chetwynd is a small town located in the foothills of the Rocky Mountains in northeastern British Columbia, situated along Highway 97 in the Peace River region. It lies approximately 1,100 kilometres northeast of Vancouver and roughly 300 kilometres west of Dawson Creek, which serves as one of the nearest regional service hubs. The town sits near the confluence of the Sukunka and Murray rivers, and the surrounding landscape is defined by rolling forested hills, mountain terrain, and the broad agricultural lands characteristic of the Peace Country. The climate follows a continental pattern, with cold, snowy winters and warm summers, offering residents four distinct seasons throughout the year. Chetwynd has a population of approximately 2,500 to 3,000 residents and carries the character of a small, close-knit northern community. Life here is decidedly rural in feel, with a pace and atmosphere shaped by the region's natural surroundings and resource-based heritage. The town is widely known for its collection of outdoor chainsaw carvings, which have become a celebrated local tradition and a point of community pride, drawing visitors from across the province. Residents tend to value outdoor recreation and a strong sense of community belonging, with the surrounding wilderness providing ample opportunity for hunting, fishing, snowmobiling, and hiking throughout the year. The economy of Chetwynd has historically been driven by the forestry and coal mining industries, both of which continue to play significant roles in local employment. The area also benefits from natural gas and energy sector activity, reflecting the broader resource economy of northeastern British Columbia. Agriculture contributes to the regional economy as well, supported by the fertile Peace Country lands nearby. The town provides residents with essential community amenities including grocery and retail services, healthcare facilities, recreational programs, and educational institutions serving students across grade levels. A regional airport and highway access help connect Chetwynd to larger centres, supporting both residents and the resource industries that sustain the local economy.
