Harrison Hot Springs, BC

About Harrison Hot Springs

Harrison Hot Springs is a small village located at the southern tip of Harrison Lake in the eastern Fraser Valley, approximately 120 kilometres east of Vancouver and roughly 30 kilometres north of Chilliwack. Nestled within a striking natural setting, the community is framed by the peaks of the surrounding Coast Mountains and sits at the edge of one of British Columbia's largest freshwater lakes, which stretches nearly 60 kilometres to the north. The Harrison River connects the lake to the Fraser River to the south, contributing to a landscape defined by water, forest, and mountain terrain. The area enjoys a relatively mild climate compared to many interior communities, with warm summers that draw visitors from across the province and cool, wet winters typical of the broader Fraser Valley region. Harrison Hot Springs functions as a small resort village with a population of just under 1,500 permanent residents, though that number swells considerably during peak tourist seasons. The community has a distinctly relaxed, small-town atmosphere shaped by its longstanding identity as a destination getaway. The natural hot springs from which the village takes its name are among its most defining features, having attracted visitors for well over a century. Harrison Hot Springs is also recognized internationally as the home of the World Championship Sand Sculpting Competition, an annual event that draws competitors and spectators from around the globe. The village sits along the shores of Harrison Lake, giving it a scenic lakefront character that anchors much of its social and recreational life. The economy of Harrison Hot Springs is driven predominantly by tourism and hospitality, with the community's lakeside setting, geothermal hot springs, and outdoor recreation opportunities forming the foundation of local commerce. Boating, fishing, swimming, hiking, and wildlife viewing are among the activities that attract visitors throughout the year, particularly during summer months. The surrounding Fraser Valley provides access to a broader regional economy rooted in agriculture, logistics, and manufacturing, and residents who require a wider range of employment opportunities or urban services typically commute to Agassiz, Chilliwack, or the Greater Vancouver area. Local amenities within the village itself are modest and focused on serving both residents and tourists, including dining, accommodation, and recreational services. For a more complete range of healthcare, retail, and professional services, residents generally travel to neighbouring communities.
