Houston, BC

About Houston

Houston is a small town located in the Bulkley Valley of north-central British Columbia, situated along Highway 16 — commonly known as the Yellowhead Highway — approximately 300 kilometres east of Prince Rupert and about 180 kilometres west of Prince George. Nestled between the Bulkley and Morice river valleys, the community is surrounded by the Telkwa Range and the Hazelton Mountains, offering a dramatic backdrop of forested peaks and river corridors. The nearby Morice River and Morice Lake are prominent natural features of the area, and the broader landscape is characterized by boreal and sub-boreal forests typical of the interior north. Houston experiences a continental climate with warm, dry summers and cold, snowy winters, making it a region with genuine four-season character. Houston is a rural small town with a population of approximately 3,000 residents, embodying the tight-knit, self-sufficient character common to many northern BC communities. Life here is closely connected to the natural environment, with outdoor recreation playing a central role in daily living — from fishing and hunting to snowmobiling and hiking in the surrounding wilderness. The community has a welcoming, unpretentious feel, and residents tend to take pride in the town's heritage rooted in the forestry and resource industries that shaped its development through the twentieth century. Houston is also recognized for its strong connection to fly-fishing culture, particularly on the Morice River system, which draws visitors and enthusiasts from across the province and beyond. The economy of Houston has historically been anchored in the forest industry, and forestry-related operations continue to play a significant role in local employment. Mining activity in the broader region also contributes to the economic base, with several mineral exploration and development projects operating in the surrounding area. Agriculture, though less dominant than in other parts of the Bulkley Valley, exists on a small scale. The town offers a reasonable range of everyday amenities for its size, including grocery and retail services, medical and health care facilities, community recreation infrastructure, and educational institutions serving local families. While residents travel to larger centres such as Smithers or Prince George for more specialized services, Houston functions as a self-sustaining community for day-to-day needs.
