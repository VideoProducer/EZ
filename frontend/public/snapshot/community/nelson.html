About Nelson

Nelson sits in the West Kootenay region of southeastern British Columbia, nestled along the western arm of Kootenay Lake where it meets the Kootenay River. The city is approximately 650 kilometres east of Vancouver and roughly 100 kilometres north of the Canada–United States border. The surrounding landscape is defined by the rugged Selkirk Mountains, which rise steeply on all sides and create a dramatic alpine backdrop. Kootenay Lake itself is one of the largest natural lakes in British Columbia, stretching over 100 kilometres in length and contributing to both the scenic character of the area and its recreational appeal. Nelson experiences a humid continental climate moderated somewhat by the lake, with warm summers, colourful autumns, and snowy winters that support nearby mountain recreation. Nelson is a small city with a population of approximately 10,000 residents, though its surrounding area draws in a somewhat larger regional population. It has a well-established urban core that feels distinctly historic and walkable, shaped in large part by an exceptionally well-preserved collection of late-Victorian and Edwardian architecture. The downtown streetscape reflects a heritage-rich identity that has made Nelson one of the most architecturally notable small cities in British Columbia. The community has a vibrant arts and culture scene, a strong sense of local identity, and a lifestyle orientation that balances outdoor recreation with creative and civic engagement. Residents have access to hiking, skiing, kayaking, and lake activities throughout the seasons, and the city has long attracted those drawn to an active, community-minded way of life. The Nelson economy has historically been rooted in the forestry and mining industries that drove growth throughout the Kootenay region, and while these sectors remain part of the broader regional economy, the local employment base has diversified considerably. Health care, education, government services, retail, and tourism now represent significant sources of employment within the city. Nelson serves as a regional service hub for the West Kootenays, offering a broad range of amenities including health services, post-secondary education, professional services, and a variety of retail and dining options that draw visitors and residents from surrounding communities. The local tourism sector benefits from the city's heritage appeal, its proximity to a ski area, and its position along established recreational and cultural travel routes.
