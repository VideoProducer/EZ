About Pemberton

Pemberton is a small community located in the Sea-to-Sky region of British Columbia, approximately 30 kilometres north of Whistler and roughly 160 kilometres northeast of Vancouver. Situated at the northern end of the Pemberton Valley, the area is defined by a broad agricultural flood plain carved by the Lillooet River and framed by dramatic mountain ranges on all sides. The valley floor opens into some of the most fertile farmland in the region, while glaciers, alpine peaks, and dense boreal forests surround the community. Pemberton experiences a continental climate, characterized by warm, dry summers and cold, snowy winters, offering a distinct four-season experience compared to the milder coastal conditions found closer to Vancouver. Pemberton is a small, rural community with a population of several thousand residents, carrying the character of a tight-knit village that has retained much of its agricultural and ranching heritage while gradually evolving alongside the broader Sea-to-Sky corridor. The pace of life here is noticeably quieter than in neighbouring Whistler, attracting those drawn to wide open spaces, outdoor recreation, and a strong sense of community. The area serves as a trailhead for mountain biking, hiking, skiing, and river activities, and its proximity to Lillooet Lake and the surrounding wilderness makes it a natural base for outdoor enthusiasts. The community maintains a genuine small-town atmosphere rooted in its history as a farming and ranching settlement, with that rural identity remaining an important part of local culture. The Pemberton economy has historically been anchored in agriculture, with the valley's rich soils supporting seed potato farming, hay production, and livestock operations that have defined the area for generations. Tourism and outdoor recreation have grown increasingly significant, with visitors drawn to Pemberton as both a destination and a gateway to backcountry terrain. The construction and trades sectors support ongoing development activity in the corridor, and a range of local amenities including grocery services, dining establishments, recreational facilities, and community services provide residents with essential day-to-day conveniences without requiring frequent trips to larger urban centres. Healthcare and educational services are available locally, supplemented by more specialized services accessible in Whistler or the Lower Mainland.
