Tofino, BC

About Tofino

Tofino is located on the western edge of Vancouver Island, at the tip of the Long Beach Peninsula on the Pacific Rim of British Columbia. The nearest regional centre is Port Alberni, roughly 130 kilometres to the east via Highway 4, while Victoria is approximately 330 kilometres away and Vancouver is accessible by a combination of highway and ferry. The community sits within a dramatic coastal landscape defined by the open Pacific Ocean, ancient temperate rainforest, long sandy beaches, and the rugged peaks of the surrounding mountain ranges. Clayoquot Sound, a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, wraps around much of the area, encompassing islands, inlets, and old-growth forests. Tofino experiences a mild, wet oceanic climate with relatively warm summers and cool, frequently rainy winters, and is well known for powerful surf and dramatic storm systems that draw visitors year-round. Tofino is a small, remote coastal town with a population of approximately 2,500 permanent residents, a figure that swells considerably during the busy summer tourism season. Despite its modest size, the community has a vibrant and distinctive character shaped by its Indigenous heritage, deep connection to the natural environment, and a culture that attracts artists, surfers, outdoor enthusiasts, and conservation-minded individuals from across Canada and beyond. The Tla-o-qui-aht First Nations have long-standing ties to this territory, and Indigenous culture and history are woven meaningfully into the fabric of the community. The town itself has a walkable, village-like feel, with a waterfront setting and a strong sense of place that sets it apart from most communities of comparable size. The economy of Tofino is driven primarily by tourism, which supports a broad range of hospitality, food service, and outdoor recreation businesses. Surfing, whale watching, kayaking, fishing, and ecotourism are central to the visitor experience and provide substantial seasonal employment. Commercial fishing and aquaculture also contribute to the local economy, reflecting the community's longstanding relationship with the sea. Year-round residents have access to general amenity services including grocery options, medical facilities, emergency services, and community programming, though residents often travel to larger centres for more specialized needs. The arts and creative industries have a meaningful presence in Tofino, contributing to the cultural and economic life of the community. While the remote location presents certain logistical considerations, the extraordinary natural setting and quality of life Tofino offers continue to make it a compelling place to call home.
