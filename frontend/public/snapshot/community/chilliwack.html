About Chilliwack

Chilliwack is located in the eastern Fraser Valley, approximately 100 kilometres east of Vancouver along the Trans-Canada Highway, making it one of the more accessible communities in BC's interior. The city is situated along the south bank of the Fraser River and is framed by the Coast Mountains to the north and the Cascade Mountains to the south, creating a visually striking setting. The Vedder River winds through the area, and Cultus Lake and Chilliwack Lake are prominent natural features nearby. The surrounding lowlands are defined by some of the most productive agricultural land in British Columbia, with wide open fields and dairy farms contributing to the region's rural character. The climate is mild and temperate, with warm summers, moderate winters, and notably higher precipitation than the Lower Mainland, which supports lush greenery throughout the year. Chilliwack is a mid-sized city with a population of approximately 90,000 to 100,000 residents, making it one of the larger urban centres in the Fraser Valley. It carries a blend of small-city convenience and semi-rural character, with established residential neighbourhoods, a historic downtown core, and a strong connection to the agricultural and outdoor landscape that surrounds it. The community has a notably independent spirit and a deep local identity rooted in its history as an agricultural hub. Residents have access to a range of recreational opportunities including hiking, fishing, lake activities, and mountain trails, and the area features heritage sites and community spaces that reflect the city's long-standing role in regional development. Chilliwack's economy has traditionally been anchored in agriculture, with the fertile Fraser Valley land supporting dairy, poultry, berry, and vegetable farming. This agricultural foundation continues today alongside a growing base in manufacturing, logistics, and distribution, with the city's highway and rail connectivity making it an attractive location for light industrial and warehousing operations. The service sector has expanded significantly in recent decades, and residents have access to a full range of amenities including retail shopping, healthcare facilities, educational institutions, and recreational services. A regional hospital serves the broader eastern Fraser Valley, and the city functions as a commercial and service hub for surrounding rural communities. Employment opportunities continue to diversify as Chilliwack grows, with construction, trades, and public sector work contributing meaningfully to the local workforce. ☀️ Weather & Climate in Chilliwack

Chilliwack experiences a humid oceanic climate, shaped by its position in the eastern Fraser Valley between coastal influence and the Coast Mountain range. Winters are mild and wet, with average temperatures typically ranging from about 0°C to 6°C, though periodic Arctic outflow events can drive temperatures well below freezing and bring significant snowfall to valley floors. Freezing rain and ice fog are occasional winter hazards, and the surrounding mountains frequently receive heavy snowfall while the valley bottom sees predominantly rain. Summers in Chilliwack are warm and relatively dry, with average temperatures ranging from roughly 18°C to 27°C, occasionally climbing into the low 30s during heat events. July and August are the driest months, and the community can experience hazy conditions from regional wildfire smoke drifting through the valley corridor. Spring and autumn bring notable fog, particularly morning valley fog that can persist through cooler months. The Fraser Valley's geography creates distinct wind patterns and micro-climates, with Chilliwack sitting at a transitional zone where maritime moisture meets the stronger continental influences of the interior.
