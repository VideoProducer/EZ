About Clearwater

Clearwater is a small community located in the Thompson-Nicola Regional District of British Columbia's Southern Interior, situated along the North Thompson River approximately 125 kilometres north of Kamloops and roughly 400 kilometres northeast of Vancouver. The area lies at the gateway to Wells Gray Provincial Park, one of BC's largest and most celebrated wilderness parks, and is surrounded by an impressive landscape of forested mountains, river valleys, and cascading waterfalls. The Clearwater River, after which the community is named, flows through the area and joins the North Thompson nearby. The climate is characterized by warm, dry summers and cold, snowy winters, with the surrounding mountains and river valleys contributing to the region's scenic and sometimes dramatic seasonal character. Clearwater is a small rural community with a population of roughly 2,000 residents in the village proper, though the broader surrounding area encompasses a more dispersed regional population. The community has a distinct small-town character shaped by its close connection to the natural environment, outdoor recreation, and a heritage rooted in forestry and agriculture. The proximity to Wells Gray Provincial Park, known for its dramatic waterfalls including Helmcken Falls, brings a steady presence of visitors and outdoor enthusiasts to the area year-round, contributing to a lifestyle that balances rural quiet with tourism activity. The community offers a welcoming, tight-knit atmosphere typical of smaller Interior BC towns, where residents enjoy ready access to exceptional backcountry landscapes and recreational opportunities including hiking, fishing, kayaking, and wildlife viewing. The economy of Clearwater has historically been anchored in forestry and related industries, which continue to play a significant role in local employment. Agriculture, including ranching and farming operations in the surrounding valley, also contributes to the regional economic base. Tourism has become an increasingly important component of the local economy, driven by the area's position as the primary access point to Wells Gray Provincial Park and the broader appeal of the North Thompson corridor for nature-based travel. The community is served by essential amenities including grocery and retail services, dining establishments, healthcare facilities, and community recreation infrastructure, providing residents with core day-to-day services while maintaining the rural character that defines the area. Larger urban amenities and specialized services are accessible in Kamloops to the south. ☀️ Weather & Climate in Clearwater

Clearwater sits in the North Thompson River valley at the southern edge of British Columbia's sub-boreal interior, giving it a humid continental climate with distinct seasonal contrasts. Winters are cold and snowy, with average temperatures typically ranging from around -5°C to -12°C, though cold snaps can push well below -20°C during Arctic outflow events. Snowfall is reliable and substantial, often accumulating to considerable depths through December and February, with the surrounding Cariboo and Monashee mountain ranges contributing to enhanced valley snowpack. Freezing fog can settle in the valley bottom during calm, cold periods. Summers in Clearwater are warm and relatively dry, with average temperatures generally ranging from 18°C to 26°C and occasional heat events pushing above 30°C. July and August bring the driest conditions of the year, and the community sits within a region increasingly affected by wildfire smoke during peak summer months. Spring and fall are marked by notable precipitation as Pacific weather systems move inland, and the valley's orientation along the North Thompson corridor can channel winds and moisture, creating localized weather patterns that distinguish Clearwater from surrounding highland areas.
