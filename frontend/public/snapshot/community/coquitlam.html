About Coquitlam

Coquitlam is located in the northeastern corner of Metro Vancouver, British Columbia, positioned along the north shore of the Fraser River and bordered by the municipalities of Burnaby, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody, and Maple Ridge. It sits approximately 25 kilometres east of downtown Vancouver, making it one of the more accessible communities within the broader regional district. The geography of Coquitlam is notably varied, featuring the forested slopes of Burke Mountain to the north and the expansive Coquitlam Lake reservoir within its boundaries, which serves as a key water source for the region. The Coquitlam River winds through portions of the city before meeting the Fraser, and significant green space and natural terrain define much of its northern landscape. The climate is temperate and coastal, with mild, wet winters and warm, relatively dry summers typical of the Lower Mainland. Coquitlam is a fully incorporated city with a population of approximately 150,000 residents, making it one of the largest cities in Metro Vancouver and the fifth most populous municipality in British Columbia. It has a distinctly suburban character, though its established town centre has taken on an increasingly urban feel in recent decades, supported by a rapidly developing high-density core near its regional transit hub. The community is diverse and family-oriented, with a broad mix of long-established neighbourhoods and newer planned developments. Coquitlam's identity is shaped in part by its extensive network of parks, trails, and recreational facilities, as well as a strong multicultural demographic that contributes to a vibrant local culture. The city balances natural surroundings with modern urban amenities, giving it a livable quality that appeals to a wide range of residents. The economy of Coquitlam is diversified, with employment spread across retail and commercial services, healthcare, education, construction, and public administration. Its town centre serves as a significant regional commercial and service hub, drawing residents from surrounding communities for shopping, dining, and professional services. The presence of a major transit exchange connecting SkyTrain rapid transit lines has further stimulated commercial development and increased the city's economic activity in recent years. Residents also benefit from proximity to employment centres throughout Metro Vancouver, with commuter access making Coquitlam a practical base for those working elsewhere in the region. The city offers a comprehensive range of amenities including recreational facilities, community centres, libraries, medical services, and educational institutions spanning all age groups.
